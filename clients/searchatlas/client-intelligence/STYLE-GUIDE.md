## 1. Brand & Audience

- **Company:** Search Atlas — agentic AI marketing platform. Founded by Manick Bhan.
- **Primary audience:** agencies, in-house marketing teams, and brands that want to grow across every channel without adding headcount or stitching together a dozen tools.
- **Secondary audience:** SEO professionals and operators evaluating agentic vs. point-tool approaches.
- **Brand pillars:** does the work for you, autonomous/self-healing, lives in your workspace, technical authority, data-backed claims.

## 2. Positioning (lead with this)

- **The line:** *The growth engine that runs your marketing while you sleep.*
- **What we are:** an agentic AI marketing platform that runs SEO, AEO, Google Ads, Meta Ads, content, and site health on its own, fixes what breaks through self-healing loops, builds the assets, and reports back in Slack, Teams, and ClickUp through the Search Atlas Coworker.
- **The principle:** sell the outcome, not the machine. Lead with the result the customer wants, not the mechanism that delivers it. The customer is the hero; Search Atlas is the guide that does the work.
- **The wedge:** competitors stop short of doing the work — Semrush hands you SEO data, Jasper/Scalenut generate content but won't run ads or fix your site, AI-visibility platforms (Profound, Peec, Scrunch) show you where you slip in AI answers but don't act, Ploy runs your website and stops there. Search Atlas runs every channel and repairs its own work. That is the point of difference.

### Messaging pillars (advance at least one in any piece)

1. **It runs while you sleep** — works in the customer's absence, overnight, unasked.
2. **It's self-healing** — notices what slipped (a ranking, a broken page, a leaking ad set, a lost AI citation) and fixes it on its own. Keep "self-healing loops" alive where it fits.
3. **It lives in your workspace** — the Search Atlas Coworker posts in Slack, Teams, and ClickUp, and it speaks first. Always show it surfacing something on its own, never the customer prompting it.
4. **Every channel, and it builds the assets** — writes the content, builds the site, designs the ads, ships the press release. Breadth proves it replaces a team.
5. **Priced like a coworker, not a tool stack** — compare to a hire or agency, not a software bundle. "Runs every channel from $99/month" beats "cheaper than your tools."

## 3. Voice & Tone

- Voice: a sharp human on the Search Atlas team — direct, concrete, with a point of view. Sounds like a teammate told you what it did, not like a brand announced a feature.
- Register: confident operator-to-operator. Technical where warranted, never hypey, never condescending.
- Reliable test: read it aloud. If you wouldn't say it to a colleague at their desk, rewrite it.

## 5. Brand vocabulary

### 5.1 Words that are ours
runs · fixes · ships · builds · on its own · autonomously · self-healing · coworker · while you sleep · overnight · every channel

### 5.2 Never call it a tool
Search Atlas and its features are never "tools." Use platform, coworker, growth engine, channel, or skill. "Tools" is allowed only for the fragmented stack we replace (e.g., "you bought 15 tools"). **Manick is firm on this.**

### 5.3 Avoid command-and-control framing
We promise the product works *for* the customer, so don't put them back at the controls. Cut "command," "take control," "manage," and "in 1 click" unless they sit right next to the autonomy promise.

### 5.4 Banned AI filler
delve, leverage, harness, robust, seamless, elevate, unlock, supercharge, game-changing, cutting-edge, best-in-class, navigate (figurative), realm, landscape (figurative), tapestry, boasts, testament, pivotal, ever-evolving, "in today's world," "when it comes to," "that being said," "it's worth noting," "dive in," foster, empower, streamline, holistic, synergy.

## 6. Voice rules (anti-slop)

1. **No antithesis.** Never "it's not X, it's Y," "not just X but Y," or "doesn't just X, it Y." State the claim straight. If X is wrong, don't even raise it.
2. **No reflexive triads.** Three parallel adjectives or clauses is the machine cadence. Use one strong word or an uneven list.
3. **Go easy on em-dashes.** Most can be a comma or a period.
4. **Vary the rhythm, use connectives.** Put a long sentence next to a short one. Join related clauses with but, so, because, while. Don't overcorrect into a row of clipped sentences (the staccato trap is its own tell).
5. **Cut the AI filler.** See §5.4.
6. **No setup-payoff scaffolding.** No "Here's the thing:", "The result? X.", or editorializing -ing tails ("..., making it the obvious choice").
7. **Don't over-structure.** Not every answer is headers and bullets. If three sentences do it, write three sentences.
8. **Have a point of view, and be concrete.** React to facts; name the number, the channel, the exact thing it did.
