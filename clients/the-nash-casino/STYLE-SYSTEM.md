## 2. Voice & Tone

Voice: confident, energetic, local, inviting. Content should read like it was written by a knowledgeable local who knows the venue well — not an AI, not a corporate copywriter. The Nash speaks to guests like friends.

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Confident | State facts clearly. Avoid hedging language ("might," "could"). |
| Energetic | Active verbs. Punchy, forward-moving sentences. |
| Inviting | Write in second person ("you"). Bring the reader into the experience. |
| Social & fun | Highlight shared experiences, group activities, live energy. |
| Approachable | Explain games and concepts in plain, friendly language. No jargon without explanation. |
| Community-proud | Reference Nashua, Southern NH, and local giving naturally — not as an afterthought. |

### 2.1 Voice in practice

Do this:
- "The energy around a craps table is electric." — immediate, sensory, specific
- "Whether you're learning how to play live craps or looking to master live craps strategy, understanding proven betting approaches can transform your experience from confusion into calculated confidence." — second person, forward-moving
- "From day one, The Nash Casino has embraced its role as a community partner." — confident, declarative

Not this:
- "Win big at The Nash" — implies financial outcome; non-compliant
- "Experience the thrill of our world-class gaming floor" — generic superlative, corporate
- "The Nash Casino is committed to providing an exceptional entertainment experience for all guests" — AI filler, no meaning

### 2.2 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Paragraphs: 1–4 lines maximum. One idea per sentence.
- First sentence of every section directly answers the heading — no throat-clearing.
- No em dashes — never, under any circumstances. Use a period or restructure.
- No colons in running text (colons in tables and lists are fine).
- Do not use constructions like "it's not about X, it's about Y."
- Do not begin a paragraph or section with a hyperlink.
- Do not use unclear pronouns — name the subject.
- Short factual introduction after the H1. No filler. No "In this article we will explore..."

### 2.3 Banned language

| Category | Banned |
|---|---|
| Slots terminology | slots, slot machines, play the slots, slot floor, slot titles (see §3 — critical rule) |
| Win/outcome language | win big, guaranteed strategy, beat the house, make money playing, best way to win |
| Generic superlatives | world-class, unmatched, premier, best-in-class, state-of-the-art, exceptional (when vague) |
| AI filler | in the world of, as we know, it's worth noting, delve into, dive into, the fact that, furthermore, in conclusion |
| Aggressive sales hooks | Don't miss out, Limited time, Act now, Book today before it's too late |
| Corporate tone | committed to providing, leveraging, synergy, stakeholder, value proposition |

## 3. Terminology — Critical Rules

### 3.1 Slots — NEVER use

**Never use the words "slots" or "slot machines" in any content for The Nash.** The machines operate on Historical Horse Racing (HHR) technology — not traditional random number generators. The terminology distinction is required under New Hampshire charitable gaming regulations.

| Never use | Always use instead |
|---|---|
| slots | gaming machines |
| slot machines | gaming machines |
| play the slots | play the gaming machines |
| slot floor | gaming floor |
| slot titles | gaming machine titles |

**What are HHR machines?** Historical Horse Racing machines draw outcomes from a database of past horse races using an algorithm. Unlike traditional slot machines (which use a random number generator), HHR machines blend horse racing data with modern gaming mechanics. This distinction is what allows charitable gaming in New Hampshire. Writers may briefly explain this when relevant — it is a genuine differentiator.

## 4. Responsible Gaming — Compliance Rules

All content must comply with gaming marketing regulations. These are legal and ethical requirements, not optional style choices.

### 4.1 Mandatory rules

- Never use language that suggests guaranteed winnings or implies a strategy will produce consistent profits.
- Never frame gaming as a financial strategy or income source.
- Always frame gaming as entertainment.
- Include responsible gaming messaging in longer-form content, strategy pieces, and any content that discusses betting outcomes.
- Avoid aggressive gambling hooks, misleading claims, or urgency-based language.

### 4.2 Required responsible gaming message

Include this message (or a close paraphrase with all elements present) wherever responsible gaming language is required:

> **GAMING PROBLEM?** If you are struggling with problem gambling, contact the NH Council on Problem Gambling: Info@NHProblemGambling.org | NHProblemGambling.org | **24/7 Free Confidential Helpline: 603-724-1605 | 1-800-GAMBLER (426-2537)**

### 4.3 Language swap reference

| Never use | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Win big at The Nash | Experience the excitement at The Nash |
| Guaranteed strategy for craps success | Strategies that may improve your craps experience |
| Make money playing blackjack | Enjoy blackjack at one of our 37 table games |
| Beat the house with these tips | Play smarter with these helpful tips |
| The best way to win at The Nash | The best way to enjoy your visit to The Nash |

### 4.4 Priority order when rules conflict

1. Legal and terminology compliance (no "slots," no guaranteed-win language, responsible gaming)
2. Voice and banned language rules
3. SEO structure and internal linking
4. Everything else

## 10. Internal Linking Rules

**Placement — what is forbidden:**
- Do not add an internal link to the first word of a sentence
- Do not add an internal link to the first sentence of a paragraph
- Do not add an internal link to the first line of a paragraph
- Do not add an internal link before establishing context and explaining the connection between source and target page

**Anchor text:**
- Use descriptive anchor text that matches the main entity in the relevant heading
- Never use "learn more," "click here," or similar generic anchors
- Add 1 internal link per heading area maximum (unless links appear in an HTML list, which counts as one)

**Context rule:** Build context around the linked concept first. Explain what it is, why it matters, and how it connects — then link. A reader should understand the relevance before clicking.
