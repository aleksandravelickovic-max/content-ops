- Do not use em dashes. Restructure or use a period.
- Do not start sections or paragraphs with a hyperlink.

### 2.3 Anti-slop banned list

When any of these appears in a draft, replace with a specific mechanism, capability, or outcome.

## 3. Regulatory Language — Critical Rules

These rules are compliance-critical. A violation in this section is a pipeline failure.

### 3.1 TMS: "FDA-cleared" only

TMS is **FDA-cleared** — not "FDA-approved." These are distinct regulatory designations. "Cleared" applies to medical devices via 510(k) pathway; "approved" applies to drugs via NDA/BLA pathway. Using "FDA-approved" for TMS is a factual regulatory error.

| Correct | Never write |
|---|---|
| "FDA-cleared TMS therapy" | "FDA-approved TMS therapy" |
| "FDA-cleared for depression, OCD, and anxiety" | "FDA-approved for depression" (re: TMS) |

Spravato (esketamine) is **FDA-approved** — use that term specifically for Spravato.

### 3.2 No outcome guarantees

Never guarantee treatment outcomes. No form of guarantee language is acceptable.

| Acceptable | Never write |
|---|---|
| "Significantly reduces depressive symptoms in many patients" | "Cures depression" |
| "Many patients experience meaningful reduction in symptoms" | "Eliminates depression" |
| "TMS may reduce anxiety symptoms" | "Resolves anxiety" |
| "Results vary by individual" | "Guaranteed results" |

### 3.3 No invented success rates

No specific success rate, response rate, or remission percentage has been confirmed from the site. Do not state any numeric figure. The approved qualifier for outcomes is: "significantly reduces depressive symptoms in many patients" or equivalent qualified language.

### 3.4 "Treatment-resistant depression" — clinical term

"Treatment-resistant depression" is an established clinical term meaning the patient has not responded to at least one (and commonly two or more) adequate antidepressant trials. Use it only in the correct clinical context. Do not apply it loosely to all patients seeking help with depression.

### 3.5 Spravato: REMS and in-clinic administration

Every piece of content discussing Spravato must note:
1. Spravato is FDA-approved (correct term for Spravato)
2. The clinic is REMS-certified for Spravato
3. Spravato is administered in-clinic under medical supervision — not self-administered at home

The REMS program (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy) is an FDA requirement for Spravato. It mandates in-clinic-only administration and patient monitoring. This is not optional — it must be noted whenever Spravato is discussed substantively.

### 3.6 "Drug-free alternative" — scope

"Drug-free alternative" or "non-pharmaceutical option" refers to the TMS procedure itself being non-medication-based. It does not mean TMS replaces all medications for all patients. Some patients use TMS alongside ongoing psychiatric medications. Do not imply TMS requires discontinuing medication.

### 3.7 Crisis situations

The 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is referenced on the clinic's site. TMS and Spravato are not emergency or crisis interventions. Every blog post and mental health content piece must include a reference to 988 for crisis situations. Do not imply that contacting the clinic is appropriate for acute psychiatric crises.

Required crisis note: "If you are in crisis, call or text 988."

### 3.8 Staff credentials — exact usage

Use credentials exactly as confirmed. Do not add, change, or omit designations.

| Staff member | Confirmed credentials | Use exactly |
|---|---|---|
| Dr. Michael Vines | Medical Director; MD; board-certified psychiatry; 25+ years experience | "Dr. Michael Vines, MD, board-certified psychiatrist with 25+ years of experience" |
| Alyssa Gentile | Medical Provider; PMHNP-BC, MSN | "Alyssa Gentile, PMHNP-BC, MSN" |
| Sarah Ann Nguyen | Director of Compliance; Clinical Therapist; MS, LPC | "Sarah Ann Nguyen, MS, LPC" |

The site also references unnamed licensed psychiatrists, psychiatric nurse practitioners, licensed therapists, and TMS technicians. These may be acknowledged generically. Do not invent names or titles.

### 3.9 No cross-promotion with sister entities

Scottsdale TMS Therapy is a standalone mental health clinic. Do not reference The Hope House, Arizona IOP, Scottsdale Detox, or any affiliated entity. Content must treat this clinic as a fully independent practice.

### 3.10 TMS side effects — seizure risk required

When listing TMS side effects in educational content, the full confirmed side effect list must include seizure risk. Omitting seizure risk from a side effects list is a compliance failure.

Confirmed side effects:
- Scalp discomfort
- Headaches
- Lightheadedness
- Muscle twitching
- Seizures (rare; higher risk in patients with seizure or epilepsy history)

Seizure risk is rare but must not be omitted. It may be noted as "rare" — but it must appear.

### 3.11 Cost range — required qualifiers

The confirmed cost range is $2,000–$12,000. When this figure is referenced:
- Always note "per full course" (not per session)
- Always note that insurance coverage varies
- Note that major insurers increasingly recognize TMS benefits

Approved phrasing: "A full course of TMS therapy typically ranges from $2,000 to $12,000. Insurance coverage varies — many major insurers now recognize TMS as a covered benefit."
