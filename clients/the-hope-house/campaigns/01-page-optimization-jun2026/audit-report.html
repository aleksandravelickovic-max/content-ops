What Happens During Alcohol Withdrawal

Alcohol withdrawal occurs because the central nervous system, long suppressed by alcohol's sedative effects, rebounds when alcohol is removed. The severity depends on how long a person has been drinking heavily and how much.

Mild symptoms — anxiety, tremors, sweating, elevated heart rate — typically begin 6 to 12 hours after the last drink. Moderate symptoms including nausea, vomiting, and increased blood pressure peak around 24 to 48 hours. Severe withdrawal can include seizures and delirium tremens. DTs involve confusion, hallucinations, and autonomic instability. They typically appear 48 to 72 hours after the last drink and require immediate clinical intervention.

Medical detox does not prevent withdrawal. It manages it. The goal is to reduce severity, monitor for complications, and keep the patient safe throughout the process.

What Is Alcohol Use Disorder?

Alcohol use disorder is defined by compulsive alcohol use that continues despite serious consequences. It is characterized by three physiological changes: tolerance (requiring more alcohol to achieve the same effect), physical dependence (producing withdrawal when alcohol is stopped), and sensitization (making the brain hypersensitive to anything associated with past alcohol use).

These are not character flaws. They are the neurological reality of chronic alcohol exposure, and they explain why stopping requires more than deciding to stop.

Signs of Alcohol Use Disorder

The DSM-5 defines alcohol use disorder based on 11 behavioral, psychological, and physical criteria. Common signs include drinking more than intended or for longer than planned, repeated failed attempts to cut back, continued use despite harm to health, work, or relationships, intense cravings, and spending significant time obtaining or recovering from alcohol use.

Severity is classified as mild (2 to 3 criteria), moderate (4 to 5 criteria), or severe (6 or more criteria). Treatment approach is calibrated to severity and individual circumstances.

What happens during alcohol detox?

Alcohol withdrawal can be medically serious. Symptoms range from anxiety and tremors to seizures and delirium tremens in severe cases. Medically supervised detox is strongly recommended before entering residential treatment for anyone with a history of heavy alcohol use. What TMS Is and How It Works

TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) is a non-invasive brain stimulation procedure that uses magnetic pulses to activate specific regions of the brain involved in mood regulation. The primary target is the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex — a region consistently underactive in patients with major depressive disorder.

Unlike antidepressants, TMS does not travel through the bloodstream or produce systemic effects. It acts directly at the targeted cortical area. Sessions last 19 to 37 minutes. Patients remain awake and alert throughout, drive themselves to appointments, and return to normal activity immediately afterward.

TMS is FDA-cleared for major depressive disorder, OCD (2018), smoking cessation (2020), and anxious depression (2021).

Side effects: The most common side effect is mild scalp discomfort at the treatment site during early sessions. Headache is occasionally reported. Seizures are a rare but known risk of TMS treatment. What Spravato Is and How It Works

Spravato (esketamine) is FDA-approved for treatment-resistant depression and for major depressive disorder with suicidal ideation. It acts on NMDA glutamate receptors rather than the monoamine pathways targeted by antidepressants, which is why it works for some patients who have not responded to SSRIs, SNRIs, or other conventional treatments.

Spravato is administered in our clinic under direct observation, as required by the FDA's REMS program. Patients remain in the clinic for at least two hours following each dose. It is not a take-home medication.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is TMS therapy FDA-approved?

TMS is FDA-cleared, not FDA-approved. The distinction reflects the regulatory pathway: TMS received 510(k) clearance, which applies to medical devices. TMS is FDA-cleared for major depressive disorder, OCD, smoking cessation, and anxious depression.

Is Spravato FDA-approved?

Yes. Spravato (esketamine) is FDA-approved for treatment-resistant depression and for major depressive disorder with suicidal ideation. It is administered under clinical observation per the FDA's REMS program requirements.

Does Scottsdale TMS Therapy treat addiction?

No. Scottsdale TMS Therapy is a psychiatric treatment clinic, not a substance use disorder treatment facility. We treat depression, anxiety, OCD, and PTSD. Patients with active substance use disorders require addiction medicine treatment.
