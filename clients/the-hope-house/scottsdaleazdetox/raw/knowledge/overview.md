## Clinical protocols and withdrawal timelines (confirmed from site content)

### Alcohol detox

Overall span: approximately 1-2 weeks with medication adjustments throughout.

Benzodiazepine protocol within alcohol detox: used in early stages, tapered over 4-7 days, continued through day 14.

Phase-by-phase timeline:

| Phase | Timeframe | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Early | 6-12 hours | Restlessness, anxiety |
| Early-mid | 12-24 hours | Elevated heart rate, tremors |
| Mid | 24-48 hours | Hallucinations, confusion, fever |
| Peak | 48-72 hours | Intense shaking, mood changes |
| Improving | 3-5 days | Improvement, fatigue |
| Post-acute | 5+ days | Psychological cravings remain |

### Opioid / heroin / fentanyl detox

Phase-by-phase timeline:

| Phase | Timeframe | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Early | 6-12 hours | Anxiety, muscle aches |
| Peak | 1-3 days | Peak symptom intensity |
| Late | 3-5 days | Symptom reduction |
| Post-acute | Weeks to months | Mood and craving fluctuations |

MAT (buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone) reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms throughout.

### Benzodiazepine detox

Protocol: gradual dose reduction, approximately 25% per week. Process may extend 25+ weeks. Careful tapering with potential medication substitution.

Success rate reference (confirmed cited on site): "success rates of withdrawal after 12 months ranged from 24% to 51%."

RESTRICTED USE: This statistic is for benzo withdrawal specifically, at the 12-month mark. Use only in educational context with the full "after 12 months" qualifier. Never as a standalone brand success claim.

## Medical risk notes (required context for content)

Alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal can be life-threatening without medical supervision. Content about these substances must:
1. State that unsupervised withdrawal is dangerous
2. Position medical supervision as a safety measure

Opioid withdrawal is typically not immediately life-threatening but causes significant distress and carries risk of relapse and overdose during the post-acute phase. Frame appropriately.

Detox does not guarantee sobriety or successful long-term recovery. It achieves stabilization. Long-term recovery requires continued treatment.
