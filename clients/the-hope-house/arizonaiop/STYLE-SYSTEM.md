## 1. Brand & Audience

Arizona IOP is an Intensive Outpatient Program facility in Scottsdale, Arizona. It serves adults seeking structured addiction and mental health treatment while maintaining their daily lives — job, family, and housing.

Website: https://arizonaiop.com/

**What Arizona IOP is:**
- A structured outpatient treatment program for addiction and co-occurring mental health conditions
- A clinical facility offering multiple levels of care below residential, including IOP, PHP, and standard outpatient
- One of the few centers nationwide providing simultaneous treatment for addiction and mental health (confirmed site claim)

**What Arizona IOP is NOT — never position as:**
- An equivalent to residential treatment or medical detox — IOP is for people who do not need 24-hour medical supervision
- An emergency crisis stabilization facility
- A detox facility

**Primary audience:** Adults seeking addiction treatment or mental health support who need structured care but can maintain daily responsibilities. Also: family members researching options for a loved one.

**Secondary audience:** Professionals and executives who need treatment that does not require leaving work or family.

## 2. Voice & Tone

**Register:** Compassionate, supportive, professional, empowering, accessible.

Content should feel like it is speaking to someone who is afraid, uncertain, or reaching out for the first time. The tone never condescends, never minimizes, and never dramatizes. It is medically grounded without being cold. It is encouraging without making promises.

| Goal | Not this | Use this |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge difficulty | "Recovery is a journey" (filler) | "Something has changed. You may not be ready to name it yet." |
| Describe treatment | "Holistic care for your whole self" | "Structured group therapy with 12 to 15 clients — a fraction of the Arizona average of 29" |
| Prompt action | "Don't wait to get help" | "Same-day placement is available. You don't have to decide alone." |
| Frame outcomes | "We'll get you clean" | "Arizona IOP gives you the structure, clinical support, and tools to work through addiction" |
| Reference fear | "There's nothing to be afraid of" | "You can keep your phone, your job, and your home while you get care" |

### 2.1 Blog voice

Blog posts use a question-to-answer heading structure. Headings name the concern, the body answers it directly. Use short punchy statements for emphasis; longer explanatory sentences for medical context.

Contact prompts appear naturally after sections addressing fear or uncertainty — not as a separate sales block.

Humanizing phrases (confirmed as on-brand from site and blog):
- "Something has changed."
- "It's rarely one isolated issue."
- "You don't have to decide alone."
- "Confront Your Anxiety, Depression, or Addiction Today."

### 2.2 Tone in practice

Do this:
- "Intensive Outpatient Program (IOP) at Arizona IOP is structured around your schedule — not the other way around."
- "Groups stay small. Arizona IOP runs 12 to 15 clients per group; the Arizona industry average is 29."
- "Masters Level Licensed Therapists work with each client across more than 20 therapeutic modalities."

Not this:
- "Our revolutionary holistic program will transform your life." — multiple banned terms; outcome guarantee implied
- "We guarantee our program works." — outcome guarantee, banned
- "Our dedicated team of counselors is ready to help you battle addiction." — "battle" is banned; team framing without credential specificity
- "Dr. [Name] leads your treatment." — no specific staff names are confirmed; do not invent

### 2.3 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Paragraphs: 2 to 4 sentences maximum. One idea per sentence.
- First sentence of every section directly answers the heading.
- No em dashes. Restructure or use a period.
- Lead with the client concern or clinical fact; follow with the mechanism or outcome.
- Do not start sections or paragraphs with a hyperlink.

## 4. Clinical Accuracy Standards

- Do not minimize the difficulty or risk of addiction or withdrawal.
- Results vary. Do not present outcomes as guaranteed or typical.
- All clinical claims must trace to confirmed site content only. Do not invent session formats, schedules, or session counts.
- Never invent therapeutic modalities beyond "over 20 therapeutic modalities" — the site confirms the count, not the specific names.
- "One of the few centers nationwide to provide simultaneous treatment for both addiction and mental health" — this is a confirmed claim and may be used in this framing.
- IOP is explicitly for clients who do not need 24-hour medical supervision. Never imply otherwise.
- Detox is a distinct level of care. Arizona IOP is not a detox facility.

## 10. Page Structure

### 10.1 Blog posts

Target length: 800 to 1,500 words.

Structure:
1. Meta title (55 to 60 characters): primary keyword near front; include "Arizona IOP" where natural
2. Meta description (140 to 160 characters): primary keyword, core outcome or differentiator, brand or location
3. 3 primary keywords defined
4. H1: direct, keyword-forward; addresses the reader's concern without clickbait
5. Short intro (2 to 3 sentences): acknowledge the concern; state what the piece covers; no filler
6. H2 sections: each a discrete concern or question; first sentence directly answers the heading
7. H3 for subsections; no H4 unless reference content
8. Contact prompt: placed naturally after a section addressing fear or uncertainty
9. Closing CTA: specific action step (call, verify insurance, schedule assessment)

### 10.2 Service pages

Structure:
1. H1: primary keyword; outcome-grounded framing
2. Problem paragraph (2 to 4 sentences): what the client is dealing with
3. How Arizona IOP addresses it (2 to 4 sentences): mechanism, not just claims
4. Key features or differentiators (3 to 5 bullets or a table)
5. Program details (levels of care, group size, clinical credentials)
6. Accreditations and trust signals where relevant
7. CTA: specific next step (call, verify insurance, schedule assessment)
