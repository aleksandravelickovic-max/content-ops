**What The Hope House is:**
- A licensed residential addiction treatment center with a clinical foundation
- A luxury setting where low client-to-staff ratios support individualized care
- A dual-diagnosis provider addressing both addiction and co-occurring mental health conditions

## 2. Brand Voice & Tone

Voice: professional, compassionate, hopeful, and client-centered. The Hope House speaks to people at a serious moment in their lives. Content should feel like an informed guide — not a hard sell, not clinical coldness, and not cheerful recovery marketing.

Luxury is present without being the headline. The clinical offer leads; amenities support and differentiate.

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Professional | Accurate, credential-grounded, never vague |
| Compassionate | Acknowledge difficulty without minimizing it |
| Hopeful | "Recovery is possible" framing — never outcome guarantees |
| Client-centered | Individualized care is the recurring theme; avoid generic group language |
| Aspirational, not flashy | Describe quality; do not gush |

### 2.1 Tagline

**"Personalized drug & alcohol rehab"**

This is the confirmed site tagline. Use it in brand-level contexts where the positioning of individualized care is relevant. Do not force it into every piece.

### 2.2 Key brand phrases (confirmed on site)

These phrases are cleared for use and reflect the site's own language:

- "Confront addiction at the source"
- "Heal their mind and body"
- "Personalized care"
- "Evidence-based treatment options"
- "Masters-level clinicians"
- "20+ forms of therapy" (use with this exact qualifier — not "over 20" in running text)
- "10 clients at a time" (confirmed differentiator — use precisely; never inflate)

### 2.3 Voice in practice

Do this:
- "The Hope House limits each facility to 10 clients at a time — well below the Arizona average of 29 — so every person receives focused, individual attention from masters-level clinicians." — accurate, differentiating, specific
- "Recovery looks different for every person. Treatment at The Hope House is built around that reality." — client-centered, honest
- "The dual diagnosis program addresses mental health and addiction at the same time, because treating one without the other rarely produces lasting results." — clear mechanism, no guarantee

Not this:
- "Our transformative, holistic approach will change your life." — banned puff language; guarantee framing; "holistic" without modality reference
- "Our team of expert therapists will help you win your battle against addiction." — "battle" is off-brand; outcome guarantee; vague claim
- "At The Hope House, we leverage cutting-edge, evidence-based therapies..." — anti-slop violations
- "Our group network includes Scottsdale Detox..." — do not reference affiliated companies by name

### 2.4 Metaphor and framing rules

- Use "confront" and "heal" — the brand's confirmed language
- Do not use "battle" or "fight" as metaphors for addiction
- Do not use "journey" as a standalone recovery noun (it is overused in this space and carries no meaning)
- "Recovery is possible" is approved; "you will recover" is not
- Results vary; individual outcomes differ — this must be implicit whenever outcomes are discussed

### 2.5 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Paragraphs: 2–4 lines maximum. One idea per sentence.
- First sentence of every section directly answers the heading.
- No em dashes. Restructure or use a period.
- Lead with the clinical offer or client outcome; follow with setting and amenities.
- Do not start sections or paragraphs with a hyperlink.

### 2.6 Banned language

**Anti-slop list (universal — applies to all clients):**

| Banned term | Why |
|---|---|
| utilize | Use "use" |
| leverage | Use "use" or describe the mechanism |
| robust | Vague intensifier |
| seamless | Vague promotional language |
| comprehensive (as puff) | Only use if specifically enumerating what is included |
| cutting-edge | Vague superlative |
| holistic (as puff) | Banned unless followed by a specific modality reference |
| synergy | Corporate filler |
| transformative | Vague; describe the actual change |
| innovative | Vague; describe what is actually different |
| game-changing | Prohibited |
| empower (as puff) | Vague; describe what specifically happens |
| revolutionary | Prohibited |

**Behavioral health specific — banned:**

| Banned term | Why |
|---|---|
| battle (for addiction) | Off-brand; brand uses "confront" |
| fight (for addiction) | Off-brand; brand uses "confront" |
| cure | Addiction is a chronic condition — no cure framing |
| guaranteed recovery | Cannot guarantee outcomes |
| will recover | Outcome guarantee |
| success rate (uncited) | Not confirmed on site — do not invent |
| treatment-resistant | Clinical language reserved for TMS; do not apply here |
| holistic (without modality) | Must be paired with a specific therapy name or practice |
| journey (as standalone noun) | Overused; replace with specific process language |

## 3. Clinical Accuracy Rules

**These are non-negotiable.** Inaccurate behavioral health content can cause harm and expose the client to regulatory risk.

- The only approved credential reference is "masters-level clinicians"
- "24/7 addiction specialists" is an approved supporting claim (confirmed on site)
- Do not claim degrees, certifications, or named credentials beyond the above

- Do not make claims about MAT efficacy beyond what the site states

## 6. Page Templates

### 6.1 Blog posts

Target length: 900–1,500 words.

Structure:
1. Meta title (55–60 characters): primary keyword near front; include "The Hope House" where natural
2. Meta description (140–160 characters): primary keyword, client benefit, brand name
3. 3 primary keywords defined
4. H1: direct, keyword-forward; addresses the reader's real question without sensationalism
5. Short intro (2–3 sentences): acknowledge the situation; state what the piece covers; no filler
6. H2 sections: each a distinct question or topic; first sentence directly answers the heading
7. H3 for subsections; no H4 unless deep reference content
8. Closing CTA: specific, compassionate, action-based — never a hard sell
9. Compliance check required before publishing (see §3 and COMPLIANCE.yml)

**Tone note for blog posts:** The audience may be a person in crisis or a family member in distress. Write with care. Do not minimize difficulty; do not manufacture optimism. State what is true and what options exist.

### 6.2 Program and service pages

Structure:
1. H1: program name or primary keyword; client-outcome framing
2. Problem paragraph (2–4 sentences): what the client is dealing with; acknowledge difficulty
3. What the program provides (2–4 sentences): mechanism, not just claims
4. Key elements or differentiators (3–5 bullet points)
5. Insurance / access information (where relevant)
6. CTA: specific next step — "Call Now," "Check Coverage," or "Download Brochure"

## 9. Behavioral Health Content Standards

These apply to every piece of content produced for The Hope House.

- Symptoms, withdrawal timelines, and medication protocols must not be invented — only what is confirmed on the site
- Do not minimize the difficulty or risk of addiction or withdrawal
- Do not present recovery as linear or guaranteed
- "Results vary" and "individual outcomes differ" must be implicit in all outcome language
- Never use stigmatizing language (addict, junkie, alcoholic as identity labels) — use "person with addiction," "person in recovery," or equivalent person-first language
- Sensitive populations: some readers may be in active crisis. Content should direct them toward action (call, contact) rather than prolonged decision-making content
