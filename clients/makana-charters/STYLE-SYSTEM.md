## 2. Voice & Tone

Voice: warm, grounded, heritage-proud, ocean-literate. Makana Charters speaks like a family that has spent 200 years on these waters — not like a tour company that rents boats and hires seasonal staff.

Content should feel like a conversation with someone who has lived this coast their whole life and wants to share it. Never corporate. Never generic resort-marketing. Never a checklist of amenities.

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Heritage-first | Ground the tour in the 200-year family legacy before listing amenities |
| Place-specific | Name the actual coastline features: Kalalau Valley, Honopu Archway, Pirates Cave, Polihale |
| Culturally respectful | Hawaiian terms are used accurately and with context, never decoratively |
| Conversational | "Our backyard" framing — the crew lives here, not just works here |
| Warm but not gushing | Pride in the place, not promotional superlatives about it |
| First-person plural | "We depart from," "our captains," "we built this boat for the Na Pali Coast" |
| Second person | "Your tour," "what you'll see," "when you're on the water" |

### 2.1 Voice in practice

Do this:
- "Makana means Gift. Our family has been giving this gift — the Na Pali Coast, its sea caves, its waters — for over 200 years." — grounds the brand name in meaning
- "We leave from Kikiaola Harbor because it's the closest point to the coast. Less time in transit, more time on the cliffs." — specific, functional, earned confidence
- "Every captain on our boats grew up on Kauai. When they're not on the water with you, they're on the water on their own time." — shows authenticity through behavior, not claims
- "The Honopu Archway is where you'll understand why film crews kept coming back." — place-specific, lets the fact speak

Not this:
- "Experience the breathtaking beauty of the Na Pali Coast with Makana Charters!" — promotional filler
- "Our world-class crew delivers exceptional service on every tour." — vague superlatives
- "Unlock an unforgettable adventure on Hawaii's most stunning coastline." — AI cliché
- "We are passionate about sharing the spirit of Aloha." — hollow; show it, don't state it

### 2.2 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Paragraphs: 2–4 lines. One main idea per paragraph.
- First sentence of every section directly answers the heading or states the central fact.
- H2 sections open with a short paragraph that naturally includes the primary keyword.
- Short factual intro after the H1 before the first H2. No "in this article."
- No unclear pronouns. Name the boat, the location, the person.
- Use specific place names, boat names, and sea cave names — not generic terms like "the area" or "various spots."

### 2.3 FAQ sections

- Every service page ends with a FAQ block (H2: Frequently Asked Questions About...).
- Each question is an H3.
- For yes/no questions: answer "Yes" or "No" first, then expand. Two to three sentences max.
- Match FAQ questions to real search queries tourists type before booking a Na Pali Coast tour.

### 2.4 Banned language

| Category | Banned |
|---|---|
| Generic superlatives | world-class, breathtaking (as filler), stunning, amazing, incredible, unforgettable (as a standalone claim), once-in-a-lifetime (without context) |
| Promotional clichés | unlock, elevate, transform your experience, immersive adventure, paradise awaits |
| Corporate/tour-industry filler | industry-leading, best-in-class, premier tour operator, state-of-the-art vessels |
| AI tells | delve into, dive into (non-literal), furthermore, in conclusion, it's worth noting, as we know, in today's world |
| Wildlife guarantees | "you will see dolphins," "guaranteed sightings" — always use "commonly seen," "it's rare we don't see them," or similar qualified language |

## 4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

- All pricing cited must match the fleet table in raw/knowledge/client-overview.md. Do not guess or estimate pricing.
- Age minimums vary by boat: 4+ for Na Pali Kai III and Amelia K; 9+ for Makana, Seiko 1/2, Leialoha.
- Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed. Use qualified language: "commonly seen," "guides know the tides and habitats," "it's rare that we don't see dolphins."
- Sea caves are entered "as long as ocean conditions allow." Do not promise cave access.
- Pregnancy policy: summer months only (June, July, August); up to 22 weeks gestation; doctor's note required, submitted by the doctor directly to Makana Charters. Do not simplify or omit any of these conditions.
- Weather cancellation policy: reschedule, credit, or full refund — confirm current terms before publishing.
- Alcohol policy: guests may bring their own but may consume it only after snorkeling. Not served on board.
- Handicap accessibility: Makana boat is not accessible; Na Pali Kai III is the recommended alternative for guests who can be lifted aboard.
- Departure point matters: Kikiaola Harbor (primary, most boats); Port Allen (Amelia K only). Do not conflate the two.
- Do not invent captain names beyond those confirmed: Mike, Rob, Darrel, Chris, Dave, Phil.

## 5. Content Rules by Page Type

### 5.1 Tour pages (standard + private charter)

Purpose: Convert visitors who have landed on a tour-specific page and are comparing operators.

Structure:
1. Meta title (55–60 characters): "[Tour Name] | Makana Charters — Kauai"
2. Meta description (140–160 characters): primary tour keyword + key differentiator + call to book
3. H1: Primary tour name (e.g., "Na Pali Coast Catamaran Tour")
4. Short intro paragraph: name the coast, mention the family heritage briefly, state what this tour covers
5. H2 — What you'll see: route highlights, sea caves by name, wildlife commonly seen
6. H2 — What's included: gear, lunch, cultural narration — be specific
7. H2 — Boats: which boats serve this tour, capacity, who each is suited for
8. H2 — How to book: departure point, pricing, age minimums, CTA
9. H2 — FAQ (minimum 4 questions)

**Departure note:** Always specify Kikiaola Harbor as the departure point on tour pages. Mention proximity advantage explicitly at least once: closer to the coast than Port Allen competitors.

### 5.2 Private charter pages

Purpose: Convert groups and families who want an exclusive experience.

Structure:
1. H1: "Private Na Pali Coast Charter"
2. Lead with the personalization angle: your group, your pace, your boat
3. H2 — Boats available for private charters: Makana (up to 12); Na Pali Kai III (call for 12+)
4. H2 — What's customizable vs. fixed
5. H2 — Pricing and booking guidance
6. H2 — FAQ (minimum 3 questions)

### 5.3 Destination/information pages

Purpose: Rank for informational queries about the Na Pali Coast and Kauai to capture early-funnel visitors.

Structure:
1. Answer the core question directly in the first paragraph
2. Use place names, geography, and historical context authentically
3. Work in Makana's heritage context where relevant — not forced
4. CTA at end: book the tour, learn more

**Examples:** "What is the Na Pali Coast?", "Best time to visit Na Pali Coast", "Na Pali Coast sea caves", "Things to do in Kauai"

### 5.4 Blog posts

Target length: 800–1,400 words. Lead with a specific place or moment, not a generic intro. Reference actual place names, boat names, and crew knowledge throughout.

## 6. Brand Differentiators

State these concretely. Do not inflate into superlatives.

| Differentiator | How to say it |
|---|---|
| 200-year family legacy | "Our family has been sailing these waters for over 200 years" / "documented history going back to the 1800s" |
| Native Hawaiian ownership | "Native Hawaiian-owned" — state it as a fact, not a marketing angle |
| Closest departure point | "We depart from Kikiaola Harbor — the closest point to the Na Pali Coast. That means up to an hour more on the coast compared to boats leaving from Port Allen." |
| Smaller groups | "Our tours carry 12 to 32 guests — smaller than most operators on the island" |
| Sea cave access | "Our catamarans are built to enter the sea caves safely. Most larger boats can't." |
| Crew roots | "Every captain has lived on Kauai for years — most their entire life. When they're not on the water with guests, they're on it on their own time." |
| Cultural narration | "You'll hear the Hawaiian history of the coast from people whose family is part of that history." |
| 10,000+ five-star reviews | Use as social proof; do not lead with it |
| Fast power catamarans | "New, fast power catamarans built specifically for the Na Pali Coast" |

**Never say:** "the best tour on Kauai" without substantiation. State what makes it different; let the reader conclude.

## 10. Hawaiian Language Rules

- Never use Hawaiian terms as decorative filler. Use them when the term is more precise or culturally specific than the English equivalent.
- On first use in a page, define terms that a mainland US tourist would not know: "honu (Hawaiian green sea turtle)," "Makana — the Hawaiian word for gift."
- Do not manufacture Hawaiian phrases or attempt translation. Use only terms confirmed from the website or client materials.
- Treat place names as proper nouns: capitalize and spell consistently (Kalalau, Polihale, Nualolo Kai, Waimea, Kekaha, Kikiaola).
- "Na Pali" = "the cliffs" — acceptable to note in context but do not turn it into a tagline.
