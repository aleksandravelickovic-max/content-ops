Loti Labs is a US-based supplier of research peptides, research liquids, and pre-measured research capsules. The company positions itself as a boutique, quality-first alternative to commodity peptide retailers — small team, transparent third-party testing, premium products, and personal customer service. **Two distinct audiences:**

1. **Research peptide buyers** — sourcing compounds (BPC-157, MK-677, sermorelin, IGF-1 LR3, tadalafil, clenbuterol, anastrozole, epitalon, liothyronine, letrozole) for laboratory research. Scientific, technical, values precision and sourcing transparency.

## 2. Voice & Tone

Voice: professional, scientific, warm. Think researcher-to-researcher — not salesperson-to-prospect. The brand is explicitly not a "massive operation." Copy can sound human and boutique, especially in About, FAQ, and customer service contexts.

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Authoritative without academic | Confident in the science, plain in the language. Explain technical terms at first use. |
| Precise, not promotional | Specific numbers (1 PM EST, 1–2 hour response, 30ml, 99.91%) beat vague superlatives. |
| Boutique and personal | Small team. The service commitment is a genuine differentiator — name it specifically. |
| Compliant by default | Research-only positioning is not a disclaimer. It is built into how every compound is described. |
| Researcher-to-researcher | Treat the reader as a qualified professional. Do not condescend or pad with marketing fluff. |

### 2.1 Voice in practice

Do this:
- "Every batch is tested by an independent third-party lab in Orlando, FL. COAs are available on the product page." — specific, verifiable, trust-building
- "Liquid tadalafil is studied for vascular and PDE-5 research applications." — research-only framing, accurate
- "Orders placed before 1 PM EST (M–F) ship the same day." — concrete, believable
- "The founders bring 9+ years of prior experience in research supply." — defensible tenure language

Not this:
- "Trust the experts at Loti Labs for unmatched quality you can count on!" — generic superlative
- "Liquid tadalafil helps with ED and improves circulation." — therapeutic claim, non-compliant
- "With 14+ years of proven expertise, Loti Labs has been the trusted leader…" — tenure inconsistency, propagates unresolved issue
- "Lightning-fast shipping you can rely on." — filler; the 1 PM EST cutoff is the claim

### 2.2 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Every paragraph answers one specific question (explicit or implied by the heading).
- The first sentence of any paragraph must be a direct answer or direct statement — no throat-clearing.
- Paragraphs: 2–5 lines. One main idea.
- Do not use dashes — never, under any circumstances, in any form (em dash, en dash, hyphen-as-separator). Use a period or restructure the sentence.
- Use active voice. Passive only where the subject is unknown or irrelevant.
- Each H2 section begins with a short introductory paragraph that includes the primary keyword (or close variation) and previews what the section covers. Do not repeat the H3 content verbatim in the intro.

### 2.3 Banned language

| Category | Banned |
|---|---|
| Generic superlatives | unmatched quality, lightning-fast, trusted leader, industry-leading, world-class, best-in-class, cutting-edge |
| AI filler | the unsung heroes of, in the world of, as research shows, at the forefront of, a cornerstone of |
| Vague quality claims | high quality, premium quality, top-tier, superior, state-of-the-art (unless naming a specific facility attribute) |
| Therapeutic framing | helps with, treats, improves, reduces, boosts, supports (as a health claim), enhances (as a health claim) |
| Promotional padding | truly, simply, really, incredible, exciting, game-changing, revolutionary |
| Tenure speculation | 14+ years, a decade of expertise, decade-old company, over a decade (see §11 for the unresolved tenure issue) |

## 3. Terminology

### 3.1 Required terms and framing

**For research compounds — always frame as studied for, not claimed to do:**

| Compound | Avoid | Use |
|---|---|---|
| BPC-157 | "Helps heal injuries and reduce inflammation." | "Studied in research models for tissue repair and gastrointestinal healing pathways." |
| MK-677 | "Boosts growth hormone for muscle gain." | "Investigated in research for its activity on growth hormone secretagogue receptors." |
| Copper peptides | "Reduces wrinkles and firms skin." | "Studied in cosmetic research for roles in collagen synthesis and skin matrix support." |
| Tadalafil | "Treats erectile dysfunction." | "Studied in PDE-5 inhibition research and vascular models." |
| Sermorelin | "Anti-aging hormone therapy." | "Investigated in research for growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog activity." |

**Compliance language:**
- First mention on any page: "for laboratory research use only" (full phrase)
- Second or later mention in the same piece: "RUO only" is acceptable shorthand
- Always include full compliance disclaimer (see §4) on every product page and category page

**Chemical specification format:**
When describing a compound, anchor to: chemical class or family, CAS number, molecular weight, chemical formula, available form at Loti Labs, and what it is studied for in research contexts. Do not define compounds by their effects on humans.

**Abbreviations — define at first use:**
- COA (Certificate of Analysis)
- GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) — see §11 for unresolved status
- RUO (Research Use Only)
- HPLC (High-Performance Liquid Chromatography)
- PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5)
- IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1)
- GHRH (growth hormone-releasing hormone)
- GHS-R1a (growth hormone secretagogue receptor 1a)

### 3.2 Formatting rules for lists

Use numbered lists for: steps, processes, sequences.
Use bullet points for: types, features, attributes, compound specifications.
Keep list grammar consistent within each list.

## 4. Compliance

This is the highest-priority section. Compliance rules override all other rules. Every piece of content for Loti Labs must be built around these constraints — they are not disclaimers to add at the end, they are the frame for how every compound is described.

### 4.1 What Loti Labs is and is not

Loti Labs is a chemical supplier. It is not a compounding pharmacy or chemical compounding facility as defined under 503A of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. It is not an outsourcing facility as defined under 503B of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. All products are sold for research, laboratory, or analytical purposes only and are not for human consumption.

### 4.2 Required compliance disclaimer

Every product page and category page must include the following language, prominently placed — not buried in a footer-only treatment. Paraphrase freely, but all elements must be present:

> All products sold by Loti Labs are strictly for laboratory research use only. They are not intended for human consumption, veterinary use, or any purpose outside of a controlled research setting. Statements on this site have not been evaluated by the US Food and Drug Administration. Products are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease. Loti Labs is a chemical supplier and is not a compounding pharmacy or outsourcing facility under sections 503A or 503B of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.

### 4.3 What writers must never do

- Make therapeutic, medical, or human-benefit claims about any product ("helps with weight loss," "improves recovery," "reduces wrinkles").
- Provide reconstitution, dosing, administration, or research methodology guidance. Loti Labs' position is that buyers are qualified researchers with their own training.
- Compare products to FDA-approved drugs in a way that implies equivalent therapeutic effect.
- Frame products as supplements, nutraceuticals, or cosmetics for personal use.
- Use before/after imagery or testimonials describing physical effects on the body.
- State a specific company founding year. (See §11 — unresolved.)
- Assert GMP compliance without confirmation. (See §11 — unresolved.)
- Assert 99%+ purity as a universal blanket claim across all products. State per-batch purity with a COA link, or use "typically high-purity — see COA for batch-specific results."

### 4.4 Priority order when rules conflict

1. Compliance (research-use-only, no therapeutic claims, required disclaimers)
2. Clarity and usefulness
3. Accuracy and alignment with verified information
4. Structure and formatting
5. AEO / entity optimization

## 7. Page Templates

### 7.1 Product page

Structure in this order:
1. H1 with primary keyword in natural phrasing (e.g., "BPC-157 Peptide for Research Use")
2. Opening paragraph (40–80 words): what the compound is, what it is studied for, research-use-only statement
3. Specs block: CAS number, molecular weight, chemical formula, purity statement with COA link, form, concentration
4. "Why buy from Loti Labs" — 3–5 specifics, not generic (Orlando lab, US-manufactured, same-day shipping, money-back guarantee)
5. "What [compound] is studied for" — research-context framing only; no therapeutic claims
6. Storage and handling notes
7. FAQ block (3–6 product-specific questions)
8. Required compliance disclaimer (prominent, not footer-only)
9. Related products / cross-links to category page and 2–3 related products

### 7.2 Category page

Each category page (Peptides, Liquids, Capsules) must have its own copy. No duplicated value-prop blocks across categories.

Structure:
1. H1 with primary category keyword
2. Opening paragraph (60–120 words): what the category is, who it is for, what makes Loti Labs' offering in this category distinct
3. Category-specific value props (3 items, different framing per category):
   - Peptides: lyophilized stability, batch-verified purity, COAs on every product page
   - Liquids: HPLC and Mass Spec verification, pre-formulated convenience, suspension handling note
   - Capsules: pre-measured precision, sealed for stability, no preparation required
4. Sub-category navigation (for Peptides: collagen, antimicrobial, growth factor, custom synthetics)
5. Featured products
6. Trust signals: COA link, US-manufactured, Orlando testing, money-back guarantee
7. Educational footer content (200–400 words) addressing common research-context questions. This carries most of the SEO weight — make it specific.
8. Required compliance disclaimer

### 7.3 Blog article

Target length: 1,500–2,500 words.

Structure:
1. H1 with primary keyword. Title signals framing (comparison, guide, deep dive).
2. Intro (80–150 words): what the reader will learn; acknowledge research-only context where relevant
3. H2 sections mapping to common sub-questions. Comparison articles: include a comparison table early.
4. At least one factual data point per major section (molecular weights, mechanism summaries, sourcing standards)
5. Internal links to relevant product and category pages — at least 3–5 per article
6. Conclusion (80–120 words): key takeaways without human-use claims
7. Author byline with credentials
8. FAQ block (3–5 questions) — captures long-tail queries, improves LLM citation surface area

**Blog clusters:**

Cluster A — Skincare / Beauty (~36,400 combined monthly volume): What Are Peptides in Skincare?, Peptides vs. Retinol, Best Peptide Serum 2026 Guide, Copper Peptides: Deep Dive, Collagen vs. Peptides for Skin, Peptide Skincare Beginner's Guide

Cluster B — Research peptides (~33,800 combined monthly volume): BPC-157: Research Overview 2026, What Are Research Peptides?, Matrixyl vs. Argireline Peptides, How to Source Research-Grade Peptides, MK-677 vs. Sermorelin: Comparison, Research Peptide Purity Standards

## 8. AEO and Entity Optimization

Use entity-based definitions when relevant — not in every paragraph.

Entity format: [Entity] is [clear definition + key attributes + why it matters]

Repeat the full entity name at least twice before using pronouns. Define abbreviations at first mention (see §3.1).

For question-based headings: answer immediately (Yes/No when applicable), then expand.

For compound definitions, anchor to:
- Chemical class or family
- CAS number, molecular weight, chemical formula
- What the compound is studied for in research contexts
- Form available at Loti Labs (lyophilized peptide, research liquid, capsule)

Do not define compounds by their effects on humans or comparison to FDA-approved drugs as treatments.

## 11. Unresolved Issues — Flag Before Publishing

These are inconsistencies or unconfirmed claims in existing site copy. Do not propagate them. Resolve with the project lead before publishing any content that touches them.

| Issue | Status | Recommended handling |
|---|---|---|
| Company tenure | Existing copy states "14+ years," "9 years," "over a decade," and "A Decade of Purity & Performance" inconsistently. Actual founding year not confirmed. | Frame as founder experience only: "founders with 9+ years of prior experience in research supply." Do not assert company age. |
| GMP-compliant manufacturing | Appears in homepage copy. GMP is a regulated, auditable standard — unclear if this is a formal certification or description of internal practice. | Do not use "GMP-compliant" until confirmed by project lead. Use "controlled manufacturing conditions" instead. |
| 99%+ purity as universal claim | Stated across product categories as a blanket claim. Not all COAs may support this for every product. | State per-batch purity with a COA link. Use "see COA for batch-specific results" rather than a universal percentage. Where verified (e.g., MK-677 at 99.91%), state the specific figure with the source. |
| Free shipping | Appears on the Capsules page. Not consistently stated elsewhere. | Do not claim free shipping universally. Verify current threshold ($99?) and apply only where confirmed. |
| "Pre-mixed, stable formulations" | Appears on the Peptides category page in existing copy. Lyophilized peptides require reconstitution — this claim is false for that category. | Use only for Liquids and Capsules. Remove from any Peptides page copy. |
| Duplicated value-prop blocks | "Unmatched Purity / Research-Ready / Fast, Secure Shipping" appears identically on Peptides, Liquids, and Capsules pages. | Each category page must have its own value proposition wording. Facts can be shared; framing must be unique per category. |
