\#\# \*\*Brand voice\*\*

The voice is professional, scientific, and warm — never breezy, never aggressive, never "buy now" hard sell. Think of Loti Labs' ideal reader as a credentialed researcher who values clarity and accuracy over hype.

\- \*\*Authoritative without being academic.\*\* Confident in the science, plain in the language. Avoid jargon for jargon's sake; explain technical terms when they appear.

\- \*\*Precise, not promotional.\*\* Specific numbers (1 PM EST, 99%+, 1–2 hour response) beat vague superlatives ("fast," "high quality," "the best").

\#\# \*\*Things writers must never do\*\*

\- Make therapeutic, medical, or human-benefit claims about any product (e.g., "helps with weight loss," "improves recovery," "reduces wrinkles").

\- Provide reconstitution, dosing, administration, or research methodology guidance. Loti Labs explicitly does not do this — their position is that buyers are qualified researchers with their own training.

\- Compare products to FDA-approved drugs in a way that implies equivalent therapeutic effect.

\- Use before/after imagery or testimonials describing physical effects on the body.

\- Frame products as supplements, nutraceuticals, or cosmetics for personal use.

\#\# \*\*Product page template\*\*

Each individual product page (e.g., /bpc-157/, /mk-677/) should include the following elements, in roughly this order:

\- H1 with primary keyword in natural phrasing (e.g., "BPC-157 Peptide for Research Use")

\- Short opening paragraph (40–80 words) describing what the compound is, what it's studied for, and that it's research-use-only.

\- Specifications block: CAS number, molecular weight, chemical formula, purity statement (with link to COA), form (lyophilized / liquid / capsule), concentration.

\- Why buy from Loti Labs (3–5 specifics, not generic) — e.g., third-party tested in Orlando FL, US-manufactured, same-day shipping before 1 PM EST, money-back guarantee.

\- "What \[compound\] is studied for" section — research-context framing only. No therapeutic claims.

\- Storage and handling notes (pulled from FAQ).

\- FAQ section with 3–6 product-specific questions.

\- Required compliance disclaimer (research-use-only, FDA, 503A/503B).

\- Related products / cross-links to relevant category page and 2–3 related products.

\#\# \*\*Category page template\*\*

Each category page (Peptides, Liquids, Capsules) needs its own copy — no duplicated value-prop blocks.

\- H1 with primary category keyword.

\- Opening paragraph (60–120 words) describing the category, who it's for, and what makes Loti Labs' offering in this category distinct.

\- Category-specific value props (3 items) — different framing from other categories. For example, Peptides emphasizes lyophilized stability and purity; Liquids emphasizes pre-formulated convenience and HPLC/Mass Spec verification; Capsules emphasizes pre-measured precision and protective packaging.

\- Sub-category navigation — for Peptides, surface the four sub-types (collagen, antimicrobial, growth factor, custom synthetics) with short descriptions.

\- Featured products grid.

\- Trust signals: COA link, US-manufactured, third-party tested in Orlando, money-back guarantee.

\- Educational footer content (200–400 words) addressing common research-context questions for this category. This is where most of the SEO copy weight lives — make it specific and useful, not filler.

\- Required compliance disclaimer.

\#\# \*\*Blog article template\*\*

Blog posts in the planned content clusters should aim for 1,500–2,500 words and follow this general structure:

\- H1 with primary keyword. Title should signal the article's framing (comparison, guide, deep dive, etc.).

\- Intro (80–150 words) that states what the reader will learn and acknowledges the research-only context where relevant.

\- Clear H2 sections that map to common sub-questions on the topic. Comparison articles should use a comparison table early.

\- At least one factual data point per major section — molecular weights, study references, mechanism summaries, sourcing standards. Specifics drive both SEO and LLM citation.

\- Internal links to relevant product pages and category pages — at least 3–5 per article, threaded naturally.

\- Conclusion (80–120 words) that summarizes key takeaways without making claims about human use.

\- Author byline with credentials. (Author development is part of the 6-month plan.)

\- FAQ block (3–5 questions) at the end — captures long-tail queries and improves LLM citation surface area.
