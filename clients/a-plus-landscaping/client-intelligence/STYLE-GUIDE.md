## 2. Voice & Tone

Voice: warm, experienced, unhurried, place-specific. A Plus speaks like someone who has worked in these towns for twenty years and knows the terrain, the winters, and what homeowners in Central PA actually want from their outdoor space.

Content should feel human and practical, not promotional. Never corporate. Never generic.

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Narrative | Open sections with a scene or human moment before stating company capabilities |
| Place-specific | Reference the town's character, soil conditions, local winters, community feel — not just the service |
| Warm | Speak to the reader as a homeowner who has thought carefully about their property |
| Direct | Once the scene is set, be clear and efficient about what A Plus does and how |
| First-person plural | "We build," "we serve," "we design" — A Plus speaks as a team |
| Second person | "Your backyard," "what you want," "where you live" |

### 2.1 Voice in practice

Do this:
- "Mechanicsburg is the kind of place where people put down roots and mean it." — place-specific, warm, not generic
- "There's a version of a Tuesday evening that has nothing to do with errands or screens or anywhere to be." — human moment first
- "A retaining wall is often the first project. The one that makes the patio possible." — specific mechanism, no filler
- "We've spent nearly twenty years working in communities like Hershey." — experience stated concretely

Not this:
- "A Plus Landscaping is committed to providing exceptional outdoor living solutions." — AI filler, corporate
- "Our world-class team of experts delivers innovative landscaping services." — generic superlatives
- "Transform your outdoor space into a stunning oasis." — promotional cliche
- "We take pride in our craftsmanship and attention to detail." — vague authority claim

### 2.2 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Paragraphs: 2–5 lines. One main idea per paragraph.
- **No dashes. Never use a dash — em or en — under any circumstances.** Restructure or use a period.
- No colons in running text (colons in tables and lists are fine).
- First sentence of every section directly answers the heading or opens with the relevant scene.
- H2 sections must open with a short introductory paragraph that naturally includes the primary keyword and provides a clear overview of what follows. This intro guides the reader without repeating every H3 in miniature.
- Short factual intro after the H1 before the first H2. No "in this article we will explore."
- No unclear pronouns. Name the subject.

### 2.3 FAQ sections

- Every page ends with a FAQ block (H2: Frequently Asked Questions About...).
- Each question is an H3.
- For yes/no questions: answer "Yes" or "No" first, then expand. One to three sentences max per answer.
- FAQ questions should match real search queries related to the page topic and service area.

### 2.4 Banned language

| Category | Banned |
|---|---|
| Dashes | All dashes (em dash, en dash) — never, under any circumstances |
| Generic superlatives | world-class, unmatched, premier, state-of-the-art, exceptional (when vague), stunning, beautiful, gorgeous, amazing |
| Promotional clichés | transform your outdoor space, oasis, dream backyard, unlock your potential, take your yard to the next level |
| Corporate tone | committed to providing, leveraging, synergy, solutions-oriented, best-in-class |
| AI filler | in the world of, as we know, it's worth noting, delve into, dive into, furthermore, in conclusion, it's worth noting |

## 3. Content Rules by Page Type

### 3.1 Location landing pages

Purpose: Serve homeowners searching for landscaping in a specific Central PA city. These are the main SEO pages.

Structure:
1. Meta title (55–60 characters): "Landscaping [Service] in [City], PA | A Plus Landscaping"
2. Meta description (140–160 characters): primary service, city, brand name, key differentiator
3. H1: "[Service type] in [City], PA"
4. Short narrative intro paragraph before H2s — no filler, place-specific
5. H2 — Services section: what A Plus builds (can use a bullet list)
6. H2 — Why choose section: differentiators, process, 20 years, certified installers
7. H2 — CTA paragraph: phone number + contact link
8. H2 — FAQ (minimum 4 questions)

**Tone note:** Lead every location page with a paragraph that names the town's character. What kind of place is it? Why do people choose it? Then connect the outdoor space to that identity. Reference local geography (terrain, grade, neighboring towns, county) where accurate.

**Warranty rule:** Do not mention the limited lifetime warranty on location pages. Mention it only on the patio service page.

### 3.2 Service pages

Purpose: Target buyers researching a specific service (retaining walls, patios, outdoor kitchens, etc.).

Structure:
1. Meta title (55–60 characters): "[Service] in Central PA | A Plus Landscaping"
2. Meta description (140–160 characters): service, geography, key differentiator
3. H1: "[Service] in Central PA" or "[Service] Installation in Central PA"
4. Short intro paragraph — lead with the problem the service solves, then A Plus credentials
5. H2 — What we build: types and materials within the service category
6. H2 — How we work: the A Plus process (site visit, Design Professional, 3D renderings, certified installers)
7. H2 — Why A Plus: specific differentiators relevant to this service
8. CTA: phone + contact link
9. H2 — FAQ (minimum 4 questions)

**Warranty rule:** Mention the limited lifetime warranty only on the patio service page, nowhere else.

### 3.3 Blog posts

Target length: 700–1,200 words. Not yet an active content type — add approved examples as they are published.
