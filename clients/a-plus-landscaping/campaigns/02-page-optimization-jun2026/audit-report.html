How long does a stone deck last?

A properly installed stone deck with an adequate base can last several decades with minimal maintenance. The primary maintenance requirement is occasional joint resealing or re-sanding to maintain the surface and prevent weed growth in the joints. Natural stone does not rot, warp, or require refinishing.

How does a stone deck compare to a wood or composite deck?

Stone decks sit lower to the ground and integrate more naturally with surrounding hardscape — patios, retaining walls, walkways. Wood and composite decks sit higher and are better suited for elevated applications or homes where the grade requires a raised structure. Stone handles freeze-thaw better than most decking materials and does not need to be refinished. Wood requires more ongoing maintenance; composite is lower maintenance but comes with higher upfront cost than stone in many configurations.

What is the cost of a stone deck in Central PA?

Stone deck costs vary by size, material selection, site conditions, and design complexity. A Design Professional from A Plus will assess your specific site and provide pricing after the site visit. Financing is available through HFS Financial for qualifying projects.

Can a stone deck be built adjacent to an existing patio?

Yes. Connecting a stone deck to an existing patio is a common project for A Plus. The design process accounts for grade, drainage, and material compatibility between the new deck surface and the existing hardscape. The Design Professional will assess the existing conditions during the site visit.

Does A Plus handle both the design and the installation?

Yes. A Plus is a design-build company — the same team handles the design and builds the project. There is no handoff between a design firm and a separate contractor.

Concrete Overlays in Central PA

A concrete overlay resurfaces an existing concrete slab — patio, driveway, pool deck, or walkway — without removing and replacing the underlying structure. When the concrete base is sound but the surface is cracked, stained, or worn, an overlay is typically a fraction of the cost of a full tear-out while producing a finished result that looks new. A Plus Landscaping installs concrete overlays for homeowners across Central Pennsylvania as part of the broader hardscaping and outdoor living work the company has been doing in this region since 2006.

When Concrete Overlay Is the Right Call

heading renamed — was "Good Bones, Worn Surface" which was poetic but non-descriptive and contained no searchable language An overlay works when the existing slab has structural integrity but surface problems. If the concrete is cracked on the surface from weathering, freeze-thaw, or normal aging — but the cracks are shallow and the slab is not heaving, shifting, or delaminating from the base — an overlay can bond to that surface and produce a clean, finished result.

An overlay is not the right call when the underlying slab has structural failure: major heaving, widespread deep cracking, soft spots, or drainage problems that would continue to push water under the new surface. A Plus will assess the existing slab during the site visit and be direct about whether an overlay will hold or whether the concrete needs to come out.

If the surface has significant structural issues, pavers over a rebuilt base is typically the more durable long-term option.

Overlay Finishes

each finish now has a brief experiential descriptor — was a bare specification list A Plus installs several overlay finish types depending on the project goal and the existing surface:

  • Stamped overlay — replicates the look of stone, brick, or slate at a fraction of the material cost. Well suited for a patio that needs to visually match an adjacent natural stone area without a full replacement.
  • Exposed aggregate overlay — a textured surface with small stones or pebbles set into the finish. Provides traction, handles wet conditions well, and works naturally in more casual or naturalistic outdoor designs.
  • Broom finish — a classic, linear texture that provides grip underfoot. Standard for driveways and functional surfaces where appearance is secondary to performance.
  • Micro-topping — an ultra-thin, smooth application that gives the surface a refined, almost polished look. Common in contemporary designs and interior-adjacent outdoor spaces where a clean, minimal finish fits the aesthetic.
  • Color overlays — integral color or stained finishes that update the tone of an existing slab. Can be applied alone or combined with a texture finish.

Frequently Asked Questions About Concrete Overlays in Central PA

FAQ heading now includes topic — was bare "Frequently Asked Questions"

How thick is a concrete overlay?

Most concrete overlays are applied at 1/4 inch to 1/2 inch thickness, depending on the product and the condition of the existing surface. Micro-topping applications can be as thin as 1/16 inch. The thickness is determined by the finish type and what the existing surface requires for a good bond.

How long does a concrete overlay last?

A properly installed overlay on a sound base can last 10 to 15 years with basic maintenance. Sealing the surface every few years extends the life and protects the finish from freeze-thaw damage. The lifespan is largely determined by the condition of the underlying slab — overlays on structurally compromised concrete will fail faster.

Can you overlay a cracked concrete slab?

Yes, in most cases. Surface cracking from weathering or shrinkage does not prevent an overlay if the cracks are stable and the slab is not moving. The overlay system includes crack-repair steps before the finish coat is applied. A Plus will assess the cracks during the site visit and advise on whether the existing condition is appropriate for an overlay.

What is the difference between a concrete overlay and replacing with pavers?

A concrete overlay resurfaces the existing slab. Replacing with pavers involves removing the concrete, preparing a new base, and installing individual pavers. Overlays are less disruptive and typically cost less upfront. Pavers provide more flexibility for long-term maintenance (individual pavers can be replaced), better drainage in permeable configurations, and a different aesthetic. The right choice depends on the condition of the existing slab and the homeowner's long-term goals for the space.

Does A Plus seal the overlay after installation?

Yes. A Plus applies a sealer to concrete overlay installations as part of the standard process. Homeowners are advised to reseal the surface every two to three years as routine maintenance, particularly given Central PA's freeze-thaw conditions.
