## Intro

Zellige, natural stone, cement tile, and ceramic each have distinct installation requirements. Adhesive type, substrate preparation, sealing timing, and grout selection all differ by material — and the differences are consequential. Zellige calls for medium-bed thin set mortar and a pre-installation soak for glazed tiles; cement tile carries a sealing requirement that applies before installation begins, not only at the end; natural stone demands a fully cured, dimensionally stable substrate; ceramic with a matte finish requires a grout-release seal before grouting. Matching the correct specification to the correct material is what allows an installation to perform as intended.

These guides cover each of Zia Tile's thirteen collections individually: surface and substrate requirements, adhesive and grout specifications, sealing schedule and products, overage recommendation, and how to prepare and distribute variation before the first tile is set.

The guides are written for review with the installation team before the project begins — not as a reference mid-installation. Each addresses the needs of the skilled installer and the homeowner reviewing scope together.

## Natural Stone
### Marble · Limestone · Cantera · Roman Mosaics

Marble, limestone, and cantera volcanic stone share core installation requirements: a fully cured, level substrate (minimum 30 days), medium-bed thin set mortar, 1/8" grout joints, and sealing before and after grouting. An uncoupling membrane such as DITRA is often advisable for floor installations. Variation in tone, veining, oxidation markings, and surface texture is inherent to each material; blending from multiple boxes during the dry layout is required.

Roman Mosaics — Zia's marble mosaic collection — follow the same substrate and sealing requirements as marble slab installations, with one critical addition: the sheets ship with a transparent film covering the tile face. The film must remain in place through cutting and setting. It is removed only after the thin set has fully cured — a minimum of 48 hours after setting — and before sealing or grouting begins. Removing the film before the thin set cures is the most common installation error with this collection.

Standard overage across natural stone: 15–20%. For a more uniform distribution of variation, where the installer is selecting and arranging pieces during the dry layout, order 20–25%.

## Handmade Clay
### Zellige · Zellige Mosaics · Cotto · Cotto Allende

Zellige, Cotto, and Cotto Allende are all fired clay tile, each hand-produced and carrying inherent variation in color, shape, and thickness. Installation requirements differ within this group.

Glazed zellige requires soaking in room-temperature water for 1–2 minutes before setting. Unglazed natural zellige must not be soaked — soaking prevents adhesion and can cause efflorescence. Chips, pits, and crazing are production characteristics of zellige, not defects; grout fills and blends these features. Zellige Mosaics share the same preparation and adhesive requirements as full-format zellige. Both zellige and Cotto Allende are not suitable for freeze/thaw climates; individual guides state climate suitability in the surface requirements section.

Cotto and Cotto Allende use medium-bed thin set and 1/8" grout joints. All Cotto and Cotto Allende installations require sealing. Each piece will have tonal and textural variation; blending from all boxes before setting is required.

Standard overage: 15–20% for zellige (20–25% for a curated, arranged installation); 15% for Cotto and Cotto Allende.

## Cement & Terrazzo
### Cement · Terrazzo

Cement tile and terrazzo tile are both handmade materials with sealing requirements and subfloor specifications that align more closely with stone than with ceramic. The subfloor must be even and fully cured to a minimum of 30 days before installation begins on either material; an uncoupling membrane is advisable. Sealing applies to both.

Cement tile uses 1/16" spacers — the tightest joint width across Zia Tile's collections. Grout joint width for terrazzo is 1/8". Terrazzo guide notes that dark or contrasting grout can spread grout haze across the surface and should be avoided; a neutral Mapei Flexcolor CQ is the standard recommendation. Cement tile carries a DCOF rating greater than 1; terrazzo has a DCOF rating of 0.51. For commercial and wet-area installations, slip resistance specifications should be reviewed with a licensed installer before the project begins.

Standard overage: 15% for both cement tile and terrazzo.

## Glass Mosaics & Ceramic
### Glass Mosaics · Ceramics · Field Trip: Japan

Glass Mosaics require epoxy thin set mortar and epoxy grout — both differ from the medium-bed thin set and sanded or unsanded grout used across stone and clay tile. All mosaic sheets arrive on paper backing, which is removed as part of the installation process after the adhesive beds. Waterproof underlayment is required in wet areas. No post-installation sealing is required for glass.

Ceramic tile from Zia's Ceramics and Field Trip: Japan collections carries finish-dependent sealing requirements. Matte finishes — Agave, Citron, Iconic Blue, Ironwood, Linen, Pink Dahlia, and Portland Grey — require a grout-release application of 511 Porous Plus Sealer before grouting begins. Satin and gloss finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. Grout joints for ceramic are 3/16".

Standard overage across glass mosaics and ceramic: 10–15%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do all Zia Tile materials require sealing?

No. Natural stone (marble, limestone, cantera), cement tile, terrazzo, Cotto, and Cotto Allende all require sealing before and after grouting. Glass mosaics do not require sealing. Ceramic tile in matte finishes requires a grout-release seal applied before grouting; satin and gloss finishes do not. Zellige guides address sealing requirements specific to glazed and unglazed variants.

### How much overage should the installation team order?

Natural stone and zellige: 15–20% standard, with 20–25% for installations where the team is selecting and arranging tiles during the dry layout for a more uniform result. Cotto, Cotto Allende, cement tile, and terrazzo: 15%. Ceramic tile and glass mosaics: 10–15%. Overage accounts for cuts and the material available for selection during installation — not all tiles in the order will be set.

### Which materials are not suitable for freeze/thaw climates?

Zellige — both glazed and unglazed natural — and Cotto Allende are not suitable for freeze/thaw climates. Each guide states climate suitability clearly in the surface requirements section.

### What thin set is required for glass mosaics?

Glass Mosaics require epoxy thin set mortar. All other Zia Tile collections use high-quality medium-bed thin set mortar. Epoxy grout is also required for Glass Mosaics — this requirement does not apply to other collections.

### What grout joint width is standard for each material group?

| Material | Grout joint |
|---|---|
| Marble, Limestone, Cantera, Roman Mosaics | 1/8" |
| Cotto, Cotto Allende | 1/8" |
| Terrazzo | 1/8" |
| Zellige | Leveling spacers — refer to individual guide |
| Cement tile | 1/16" |
| Ceramics, Field Trip: Japan | 3/16" |
| Glass Mosaics | Refer to individual guide |

### What should the installation team do before setting the first tile?

Every guide specifies blending the full order before installation begins — tiles from all boxes mixed and laid out across the installation area to distribute color, tone, and texture variation evenly. This step is required across all materials, not optional. For zellige and stone, the dry layout is also the point at which the installer and client confirm the arrangement before adhesive is applied.
