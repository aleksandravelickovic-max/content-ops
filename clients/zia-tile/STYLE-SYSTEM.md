## 1. Brand & Audience

## 2. Voice & Tone

Voice: trusted senior design editor -- authoritative, warm, precise. The bar is Architectural Digest and Wallpaper*, not generic SaaS SEO.

**Litmus test:** Does this read like an expert talking, or a marketer selling?

### 2.1 Core voice rules

| Rule | Do this | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Polished and confident without being stiff or corporate | Warm and aspirational, as a lifestyle or luxury brand would be |
| Authority | Earned through specificity and accurate technical detail | Claimed through adjectives: "stunning," "beautiful," "exquisite," "charming" |
| Atmosphere | Created by precise nouns and active verbs | Created by stacking descriptors before a noun |
| Sentence length | Short to medium. One idea per sentence. Active voice. | Long, clause-heavy constructions that bury the point |
| Filler words | Remove: "truly," "really," "simply," "of course," "unique," "charming" | Allow filler to soften or pad sentences |
| Fragments | Never -- complete sentences only throughout body copy | Standalone fragments for rhythm or stylistic effect |
| Emotional register | Matter-of-fact appreciation of craft; let the detail carry the feeling | Sentimental or romantic overlay on top of practical information |
| Prepositions | Restructure sentences to avoid ending on a preposition | "the place it came from" -- rewrite as "from which it came" |
| Person | No second person (you/your) anywhere in the piece | Addressing the reader directly |

### 2.2 The core tension -- shown in practice

Jamie's most consistent correction was removing aspirational or vague language and replacing it with plain, declarative statements grounded in specific facts.

| Category | Avoid | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Generic opener | "where families have been pressing clay into molds and firing it the same way for generations" | "which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques" |
| Vague closer | "is one of those materials that looks better the longer it is in place" | "ages beautifully" |
| Filler atmospheric | "add to the room rather than date it" | "enhances the room" |
| Visible aging | "benefit from the visible aging of clay surfaces" | "benefit from the lived-in character of aging clay surfaces" |
| Authenticity | "build atmosphere and authenticity over time" | "build atmosphere and character" |
| Vague framing | "reads as handmade rather than printed" | "reads as handmade" (remove "rather than printed" -- Jamie flagged "printed" as meaningless) |
| Sub-line framing | "glazed sub-line" | "glazed terra cotta tile" |
| Runs to | "The collection runs to 18 colorways" | "The collection features eighteen colorways" |
| Format framing | "Cotto's format range" | "The range of Cotto shapes and sizes" |
| Colonial | "colonial courtyard" | "Spanish courtyard" |
| Module | "four-tile module" | "four-tile set" |
| Adjective order | "a deep, traditional warm red" | "traditional, deep warm red" |
| Patina framing | "which is part of how the material ages" | "bringing beauty to the material's aging process" |
| Aging closer | "Expect tonal deepening over time. This is the material doing what it is supposed to do." | "Expect the tone to deepen over time -- that patina is the point." |
| Light exposure patina | "Foot traffic and light exposure deepen the tone rather than diminish it" | "Use and time deepen the tone -- the installation is a starting point, not a forever state" (no light-exposure claim) |

### 2.3 The purposeful test

Every sentence must deliver information or atmosphere -- not both at once, and never neither. Before submitting, apply this test to each sentence: if you removed it, would the reader miss anything? If no, cut it.

Cut:
- Clauses that restate what was just said in different words
- Phrases that describe what the customer already knows
- Adjectives that could apply to any product in the category
- Qualifications that add no useful information

### 2.4 Sentence formula (design-forward signature)

**[Specific actor] + [verb of making/placement] + [named material with origin] + [physical/spatial detail].**

> "Hand-chiseled in Fez, each zellige tile carries a chamfered edge that catches light unevenly across a wall."

### 2.5 Editorial voice benchmarks

These patterns, drawn from Architectural Digest, Wallpaper*, Remodelista, and Domino, define the register Zia content should occupy.

**Leads:** Anchor on person + place, or a single specific material detail. Never open with a generic category claim.

**Sentence rhythm:** Medium-length, one to two clauses. Long enough to carry a specific detail, short enough to scan. Subordinate clauses only when they earn their weight with a second specific.

**Vocabulary palette (use naturally, never force):** clad, wrapped, stacked, fluted, sculpted, carved, layered, muted, drippy, chalky, matte, glossy, hand-molded, hand-cut, hand-glazed, irregular, undulating, cascading, monolithic.

**Specificity standard:** Name the maker, the origin city, the material by type, the dimensions. Place geographically. Date historically. Quote the designer or homeowner, not the brand.

**Closers:** End on the maker's intent, the homeowner's words, or a practical aside. Never a CTA or generic takeaway.

**What these publications do NOT do:**
- No "transform your space." Not a single instance.
- No rule-of-three openers ("Bold, beautiful, and timeless...").
- No generic intensifiers (stunning, luxurious, powerful, robust).
- No undefined "luxury" or "elevate."
- No "in conclusion / to wrap up" closers.
- No category headers like "Why X is great for your home."
- No SEO-style headings ("10 Reasons to Love Zellige Tile").
- No marketing hedges ("Some say...," "experts agree...").
- No em-dash rhetorical pivots ("it's not just tile -- it's a statement").

## 4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

**Non-negotiable:** if a capability claim isn't verifiable against the product spec or Installation Guide, do not write it. Limitations must be stated explicitly and affirmatively.

### 4.1 Freeze/Thaw -- material-specific rules

These are hard rules that differ by material. Do not apply one material's rules to another.

| Material | Outdoor / Freeze-Thaw Rule |
|---|---|
| Zellige (glazed) | NOT suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates. State this plainly and affirmatively. |
| Unglazed Cotto | Suitable for exterior use in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions. This is a selling point. |
| Cotto Allende (glazed) | NOT suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates. Same rule as glazed zellige. |
| Marble | Can be used outdoors in appropriate climates. Do not state that it cannot. |
| Cement tile | Suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only. |

### 4.2 Pool and Spa -- Cotto vs. Cotto Allende

This distinction must be applied consistently everywhere pools or spas are mentioned.

- **Unglazed Cotto:** NOT suitable for pools or spas (full submersion). Suitable for outdoor floors, patios, and courtyards in any climate.
- **Cotto Allende (glazed):** Approved for pools and spas. NOT suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates.

> Exact language from Jamie's edit: "Glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools." Use this framing when comparing both products in the same section.

### 4.3 Sealing Requirements

Cotto and Cotto Allende have different sealing requirements. They must never be combined into a single mixed instruction set.

**Cotto (unglazed):**
- Seal before grouting with 511 Porous Plus.
- After grouting, apply Fila Matte Wax for interior; second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior.

**Cotto Allende (glazed):**
- Only requires post-grout sealing with 511 Porous Plus in areas exposed to continuous moisture.

**General sealing rules:**
- Sealing applies in all installations, including dry spaces.
- Sealing guidance must appear **inline within the specific usage bullet** (shower, pool, spa) -- not only in a section-end catch-all.
- Section-end "Sealing Required" line is a backup, not a substitute.
- Direct readers to the Installation Guide for sealer selection. Never name a specific product in customer-facing pages (sealer names above are for internal reference only).
- **Dry spaces also require sealing.** Use this exact wording: *"Always seal [tile name] according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers."*

### 4.4 Slip Resistance

When recommending tile for wet floor applications, always provide a minimum grout joint specification.

- Cotto DCOF: **0.98** -- well above the 0.42 threshold for level interior floors.
- For wet commercial floors: grout joints should measure at least **1/8"**, plus anti-slip treatment after installation.
- Slip resistance language belongs under the shower/wet area section, not under general interior floor bullets.
- Never just "consult your installer."

### 4.5 Installation Section -- required elements

Every Installation and Finishing section must include all four:
1. **PPE + Prop 65 warning** -- protective face/eye coverings when working with zellige, thin set, sealants, grout. Link to ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings.
2. **Edge / finishing options** -- drywall flush + mitered edges + Schluter strips. Never "trim pieces."
3. **Sealing for all installations** -- sealing required per Installation Guide; specifically pools, spas, showers. Include dry spaces.
4. **Contact info** -- info@ziatile.com and 310-844-1170 for installer questions.

### 4.6 Contact Line -- required on every piece

Jamie noted she keeps having to add the contact line herself on every piece. It must be included without being asked.

- Email: **info@ziatile.com**
- Phone: **310-844-1170**
- Both must appear together in the closing section and in FAQ answers where relevant -- especially the quantity/overage FAQ and the installation FAQ.

### 4.7 Usage Chart Standards

- Usage bullets are **standardized at the category level** (e.g. Glazed Zellige). Do **not** write usage bullets from scratch per SKU.
- Substitute only SKU-specific details: format dimensions, orientation, finish-specific notes.
- Structure, wording, and bullet order must be identical across all SKUs in the same category.
- Approved category templates are maintained by Alex. Request before starting a new category.

### 4.8 Content Placement Accuracy

Each usage bullet must contain only information **relevant to that specific use case**. Misplaced content is a factual error.

- Shower / wet-area slip resistance -> **Shower Walls + Floors** only.
- Interior Floors -- High Traffic -> kitchens, entryways, primary bathrooms used as **dry floors only**.
- If unsure, flag for Alex; do not guess.

### 4.9 Unglazed Zellige

Unglazed zellige is a **separate category** from glazed zellige. Different language for sealing, surface variation, finish-specific characteristics. Do **not** apply glazed zellige usage template or sealing copy to unglazed pages. Request category-specific guidance from Alex first.

### 4.10 Unverified Technical Claims

- Kiln type: do not specify wood-fired, gas-fired, or any specific kiln method without written confirmation from Zia's team.
- Light exposure and patina: do not claim that UV or light exposure deepens Cotto's tone. Patina framing should reference use and time only.
- Any climate or application claim (indoor/outdoor, freeze/thaw, wet areas) must be cross-referenced against the PDP usage chart before submission.

### 4.11 Variation Language by Material

- **Cotto (unglazed):** tone, shape, edge profile, thickness. No crazing.
- **Zellige (glazed):** chips, pits, and crazing -- always as a trio.
- Never apply zellige surface language to an unglazed tile page.

## 5. Cotto Product Knowledge

This section is the authoritative reference for all Cotto and Cotto Allende content. Review it before writing or editing any Cotto blog post, collection page, or product page. If anything here conflicts with the live site, flag it to Alex before writing.

### 5.1 What Cotto is

Cotto is Zia's terra cotta line. Terra cotta is the broader material category. Cotto is not a subcategory or superior variant of terra cotta -- it is simply Zia's name for their terracotta collection. Establish this relationship early in any piece: "Cotto is Zia's terra cotta line" or "Zia's Cotto is terra cotta."

Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried in open courtyards for one to two weeks, and high-fired in traditional kilns. It is unglazed. The surface develops a natural patina with use and time.

### 5.2 Colorways

Cotto comes in two groups -- three classic hues and three unexpected tones. Do not call them "traditional" and "contemporary" in the same section where "traditional" has already appeared -- use "classic range" and "unexpected tones" per Jamie's edits.

| Colorway | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Adobe | The lightest, a soft sun-bleached tone | Classic range |
| Fired Earth | Mid-range classic warm terra cotta. Wood ash applied during firing produces distinct textured coloration. | Classic range -- note: "distinct textured" not "distinctive" |
| Red Clay | The traditional, deepest red, closest to the raw material itself | Classic range -- note: Jamie added "traditional" before "deepest" |
| Blanco | A whitewashed finish that brightens and softens the body's natural tone | Unexpected tone -- note: Jamie added "brightens and" |
| Madera | A deep cocoa, one of the few dark brown terra cotta tiles produced anywhere | Unexpected tone -- remove "bestselling" per Jamie's edit |
| Oscura | A matte black terra cotta, rare in unglazed clay | Unexpected tone |

### 5.4 Cotto Allende

Cotto Allende is Zia's glazed terra cotta tile line -- not a "sub-line." It begins with the same clay foundation as Cotto, hand-hewn and high-fired, then hand-glazed. The name references both its origin near San Miguel de Allende and the Spanish word for "beyond" -- because the glazes introduce color and texture beyond the spectrum of natural clay.

- Eighteen colorways (write out "eighteen," not "18") with names referencing Mexican geography and flora: Sayulita, Cacao, Creosote, Peyote, Arroyo, Condesa, Pedregal, and others.
- Four shapes: 4x4 square, 4x8 rectangle, triangle, and the 1.5x8 mini bar (narrowest format, useful for linear or stack-bond layouts).
- Finishes range from matte to glossy with speckling that varies by colorway and subtle crazing and color shifts.
- Approved for pools and spas. Not suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates.

### 5.5 Saltillo -- how to frame it

Saltillo is a related clay tile from Saltillo, Coahuila, in northern Mexico -- a different region and a different process from Cotto. Zia does not sell Saltillo.

> Jamie's direct comment: "My concern is that Saltillo is positioned as a fine tile that we do not sell. Shouldn't we steer this a bit more generic/mass/negative, in order to comment on why we don't sell it? Why would we want someone to leave our site and go elsewhere for a tile we do not produce?"

- Do not position Saltillo as a quality tile that Zia simply doesn't carry. The comparison must subtly favor Cotto.
- Frame Saltillo's characteristics as distinctions that explain why Cotto performs differently -- lighter body, more porous, warmer mineral variation.
- If there is no editorial reason to include Saltillo in a piece, consider removing it entirely. Confirm with Alex before including any Saltillo reference.
- Never reference products or tile types that Zia does not sell without a clear strategic reason. Confirm with Alex first.

## 6. Blog Post Standards

### 6.1 Opening paragraphs

The opening paragraph sets the tone for the entire piece. Do not open with a production description or a process sequence. Lead with the material, the place, or the feeling.

> Jamie's approved Blog Post 4 opener: "Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques. The result is a terra cotta defined by tonal variation, surface character, and the inherent details that give handmade tile its appeal. Over time, the unglazed surface develops a natural patina that reflects the material's connection to place, process, and age."

- Lead with what the material is, where it comes from, and what makes it distinctive before describing how it is made.
- Do not open with "families pressing clay into molds" or similar process-first framing. Jamie flagged this as generic and present in every terra cotta brand's copy.
- The standard: fix the entry point, keep what works.

### 6.2 Introducing the collection early

Blog posts should introduce Zia's specific collection -- and name it -- in the opening or first body section. Do not make the reader wait several sections to understand what Zia's specific collection looks like.

> Jamie's addition (placed inside "What Is Cotto Tile?", not a separate colorways section near the end): "Zia's Cotto line was born from our three traditional tones: Adobe, Fired Earth, and Red Clay, while our newer whitewashed Blanco, deep brown Madera, and matte black Oscura round out the earthy palette."

### 6.3 Structure and redundancy

- Each H2/H3 section should approach the material from a different angle: history, performance, application, care, design context. Do not repeat the same information in different words.
- Do a word audit before submitting. If the same noun or descriptor appears more than twice in a section, find a variation.
- Every blog post needs a proper concluding paragraph. Do not end abruptly.
- Each section header should introduce a new dimension, not restate the intro.

### 6.4 Person and voice consistency

Choose first person ("our Cotto") or third person ("Zia's Cotto") and hold it for the entire piece. Do not switch within the same paragraph or between sections. This has been flagged in every review session.

### 6.5 Patina and aging language

Patina is a feature, not a warning. Frame it as something desirable and intentional. Reference use and time as the drivers of patina -- do not reference light exposure specifically unless confirmed by Zia's team.

| Category | Avoid | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Patina framing | "Foot traffic and light exposure deepen the tone rather than diminish it" | "Use and time deepen the tone -- the installation is a starting point, not a forever state" |
| Aging closer | "Expect tonal deepening over time. This is the material doing what it is supposed to do." | "Expect the tone to deepen over time -- that patina is the point." |

### 6.6 Applications sections

Each application section should reference specific Zia shapes or formats in context. Generic application descriptions that don't mention actual product names are a recurring gap.

- **Interior floors:** name the formats that suit the space -- 13x13 squares, Big Alcazar for open layouts; Stars & Cross and 6x6 Alcazar for compact rooms.
- **Exterior:** specify that unglazed Cotto performs in any climate including freeze/thaw. Note that Cotto Allende is the right choice for pools and wet spaces.
- **Bathrooms:** require proper sealing. Remove language that positions Cotto Allende as the lower-maintenance alternative -- Jamie deleted this from the blog post.
