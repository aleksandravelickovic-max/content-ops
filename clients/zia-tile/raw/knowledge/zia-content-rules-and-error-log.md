## Part 1 — Hard Gates (Run Before Any Output)

These apply to every content type: product pages, collection pages, blog posts, articles.

1. **Identify the client.** Every task targets a specific client. Infer from context or ask — never guess.
2. **Load STYLE-SYSTEM.md.** Do not write from memory. Read it in the current session.
3. **Load UNIVERSAL-RULES.md.** Baseline rules for all content.
4. **Client rules override universal rules.** Where they conflict, STYLE-SYSTEM.md wins.
5. **No invention.** Do not invent product capabilities, pricing, testimonials, statistics, colorway names, or source claims. If data is not in `raw/` or STYLE-SYSTEM.md, flag the gap — do not fill it.
6. **No placeholders in output.** Any unfilled placeholder — bracket-style, parenthetical, or inline — blocks publication. Verify all figures are present before output.

## Part 2 — Voice & Tone Rules

### 2.1 Register
- Write like a trusted senior design editor. The benchmark is Architectural Digest and Wallpaper*, not SEO blog copy.
- Authority is earned through specificity and accurate technical detail, not adjectives.

### 2.2 Sentence rules
- One idea per sentence. Active voice.
- No fragments.
- No sentence ends on a preposition.

**Error observed:** "the inviting sheen zellige is coveted for" — ends with "for."
**Correct:** "the inviting sheen for which zellige is specified"

### 2.3 No second person
- Never use "you" or "your" anywhere in the piece.

**Error observed:** "consult with your licensed installer before proceeding" / "discuss your specific location and climate"
**Correct:** "consult a licensed installer" / "discuss the specific location and climate"

### 2.4 Person consistency
- Choose first person ("our Cotto") or third person ("Zia's Cotto") and hold it for the entire piece.
- Never switch within the same paragraph or between sections.

**Error observed:** Romance copy used "our ceramics" (first person), then the About section opened with "Zia's ceramics" (third person) on the same page.

### 2.5 No filler setup sentences
- Open every section with the point. Do not ease in with an observation about the subject.
- "The names appear together often, and they describe distinct products" — flagged by Jamie as "pointless." Get to the differentiating fact immediately.

### 2.6 No external designer name-drops
- Do not reference external designers, architects, or cultural figures as authority or tie-ins.
- Flagged by Jamie: Renzo Mongiardino reference — "no tie, please don't reference."

### 2.7 Describe the space, not the spec
- Writers must describe how a material feels and reads in a room — not what its physical properties are.
- Technical accuracy is required, but it must be delivered through spatial and visual language.
- Wrong: "The dense clay body handles radiant heat."
- Right: "Cotto is a natural choice for a fireplace surround, playing off the warmth of the fire itself."

### 2.8 No unnamed attribution
- Never use "Some say," "experts agree," or any unnamed group making a claim.
- Make claims direct and declarative, or do not make them.

### 2.9 No SEO-style headings
- No question-form H2/H3 headings.
- No "10 Reasons to Love X" or listicle-style headers.
- No rule-of-three openers ("Bold, beautiful, and timeless...").

**Error observed:** "What makes Zellige tile right for kitchen backsplashes?" as an H2.
**Correct:** Declarative heading — "zellige in kitchen backsplashes"

### 2.10 Semicolons vs. em-dashes
- Parenthetical insertions use em-dashes, not semicolons.

**Correct form:** "surface variation — chips, pits, and crazing — results directly from"
**Incorrect form:** "surface variation; chips, pits, and crazing; results directly from"

## Part 4 — Technical Accuracy Rules

### 4.1 Freeze/thaw by material
Hard rules. Apply each to its specific material only.

| Material | Freeze/thaw outdoor rule |
|---|---|
| Glazed zellige | NOT suitable. State plainly and affirmatively. |
| Unglazed Cotto | Suitable for any climate including freeze/thaw. Selling point. |
| Cotto Allende (glazed) | NOT suitable for outdoor freeze/thaw. Same rule as glazed zellige. |
| Marble | Can be used outdoors in appropriate climates. Do not say it cannot. |
| Cement tile | Suitable for exterior in non-freeze/thaw climates only. |
| Limestone | Verify per SKU. French Cobblestone (Bordeaux) and Moroccan limestone have different confirmed rules. Do not apply one to the other. |

### 4.2 Pool and spa rules

| Material | Pool/spa rule |
|---|---|
| Glazed zellige | Verify per SKU before asserting — do not assume approved |
| Unglazed Cotto | NOT suitable for pools or spas |
| Cotto Allende (glazed) | Approved for pools and spas |
| Ceramics (Japanese) | Verify per SKU before asserting |
| Limestone | Verify per SKU — French Cobblestone shows ✓ but confidence is still draft |

**Rule:** If a pool/spa claim is not confirmed in the material config or STYLE-SYSTEM.md, flag it — do not assert it.

**Error observed:** Zellige pool/spa approval stated without a verified source.
**Error observed:** Ceramic pool/spa approval asserted without verification.

### 4.3 Sealing rules
- Cotto (unglazed) and Cotto Allende (glazed) sealing instructions must never be combined into a single instruction set.
- Matte ceramics require pre-grout sealing as a grout release; satin and glossy ceramics do not.
- Sealing guidance must appear inline within the specific usage bullet (shower, pool, spa) — not only in a section-end catch-all.
- Direct readers to the Installation Guide. Never name a specific sealer product in customer-facing pages.
- Dry spaces also require sealing. Required language: "Always seal [tile name] according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers."

### 4.4 Slip resistance
- DCOF threshold for wet level interior floors: 0.42 (ANSI A137.1).
- Confirmed DCOF figures: Cotto 0.98 | Matte ceramic 0.74 | Satin ceramic 0.45 | Glossy ceramic 0.30 | Limestone honed 0.50 | Limestone bush-hammered 0.56.
- Glossy ceramic: below 0.42 — approved for shower walls only, not shower floors.
- For wet commercial floors: minimum 1/8" grout joint + anti-slip treatment after installation.
- Slip resistance guidance belongs under Shower/Wet Area sections only — not under dry-floor bullets.

### 4.5 Variation language by material
Do not apply one material's variation language to another.

| Material | Correct variation language |
|---|---|
| Zellige (glazed) | Chips, pits, and crazing — always as a trio |
| Cotto (unglazed) | Tone, shape, edge profile, thickness — no crazing |
| Cotto Allende (glazed) | Color, shape, thickness, crazing, speckling |
| Ceramics (Japanese) | Tone and dimension — inherent to high-fired ceramic |
| Limestone | Tone, natural patina, honed or bush-hammered finish, natural stone variation |

**Error observed:** "Dynamic glazes" used for Cotto Allende — reserved for zellige only.
**Error observed:** Crazing applied to unglazed Cotto — banned. Crazing is a glazed-surface characteristic.
**Error observed:** Cotto Allende variation list missing "speckling" — a named characteristic per §5.4.

### 4.6 Verified production narratives
Use only verified production steps. Do not add unverified process claims.

**Zellige (verified):** Raw clay from local hillsides → soaking and filtering → hand-pressing → Moroccan sun drying → hand-shaping → glaze hand-dipping → earthen kiln.
**Cotto (verified):** Natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende → hand-pressed into molds → sun-dried in open courtyards one to two weeks → high-fired in traditional kilns.

**Error observed:** "chiseled to shape" and "manual chiseling" in zellige production copy — not in the verified narrative. Use "hand-shaped."

### 4.7 Colorway verification
Only reference colorways confirmed in STYLE-SYSTEM.md or the live catalog at ziatile.com.

**Error observed:** "Aegean" and "Absinthe" zellige colorways referenced — neither confirmed in the style system or approved catalog.

### 4.8 Installation section — four required elements
Every installation section must include all four:
1. PPE + Prop 65 warning with link to `ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings`
2. Edge finishing: drywall flush + mitered edges + Schluter strips
3. Sealing for all installations (including dry spaces)
4. Contact info: info@ziatile.com AND 310-844-1170 — both together

**Error observed (multiple pages):** Prop 65 link missing — warning text present but no hyperlink.

### 4.9 Overage rate
- Standard: 15–20%.
- Uniform result / specialty shapes: 25%.
- Both figures must be present with context for each.

## Part 8 — Material-Specific Terminology Guards

### Zellige
- Always lowercase. Never at sentence start — restructure.
- "Handcrafted Moroccan art form" required in opening.
- Chips, pits, and crazing — always as a trio, always framed as intentional characteristics.
- Dynamic glazes — zellige only.
- Not suitable for outdoor freeze/thaw.
- Production steps: hand-pressing, Moroccan sun drying, hand-shaping, glaze hand-dipping, earthen kiln. No chiseling step.

### Cotto (unglazed)
- Variation: tone, shape, edge profile, thickness. No crazing.
- Suitable for any climate including freeze/thaw (selling point).
- NOT suitable for pools or spas.
- Sealing: 511 Porous Plus pre-grout; Fila Matte Wax interior finish; second coat 511 Porous Plus exterior.

### Cotto Allende (glazed)
- Not a "sub-line" — call it "glazed terra cotta tile" or "glazed line."
- Variation: color, shape, thickness, crazing, speckling.
- Approved for pools and spas.
- NOT suitable for outdoor freeze/thaw.
- "Dynamic glazes" does not apply — zellige-only terminology.

### Ceramics (Japanese)
- High-fired in Nagoya. Practice extends back more than 1,000 years.
- Do not repeat origin detail in both romance copy and About section.
- Variation: "Inherent variation in tone and dimension is characteristic of high-fired ceramics" — never "normal."
- Matte: DCOF 0.74 (shower floors ✓). Satin: DCOF 0.45 (shower floors + walls ✓). Glossy: DCOF 0.30 (walls only).
- Satin and glossy: no pre-grout sealing. Matte: penetrating sealer required before grouting.

### Limestone
- Variation: tone, natural patina, honed or bush-hammered finish, natural stone variation.
- Honed DCOF: 0.50. Bush-hammered DCOF: 0.56.
- Sealing required — two-step protocol per Installation Guide.
- Freeze/thaw and pool/spa rules vary by SKU. French Cobblestone (Bordeaux) may have different confirmed specs from Moroccan limestone SKUs. Do not apply one to the other without verification.

### Marble
- Can be used outdoors in appropriate climates. Do not say it cannot.
- Honed finish for shower floors. Polished not recommended for wet floors.
- Variation: veining and tone inherent to the stone.

## Part 9 — Application Copy Rules (from Jamie Greenspan review, May 2026)

### 9.1 Always include commercial alongside residential
- Never list residential applications alone. Commercial context must be acknowledged.
- Wrong: "suits nearly every residential setting"
- Right: "suits nearly every residential or commercial setting"

### 9.2 Floor installations must mention drainage
- Every floor application bullet requires: "Ensure floor installations have proper drainage."
- This is a required note, not optional context.

### 9.3 Sealing must be explicit in wet application bullets
- Do not imply sealing — state it. Add "and proper sealing" alongside any wet-area application reference.
- Sealing guidance belongs inline with the specific application, not only in a separate section.

### 9.4 Don't name grout colors in recommendations
- Describe the visual outcome: "color-matching for a seamless installation" vs. "contrasting grout for graphic definition."
- Never name specific tones (buff, warm grey, bright white) as prescriptions.

### 9.5 Don't repeat production location attribution
- Once "near San Miguel de Allende" is established, do not restate "by the same artisans in San Miguel de Allende" in subsequent sections.

### 9.6 Describe colorway range, don't list names
- For Cotto Allende and other multi-colorway collections, describe the range rather than listing names.
- Right: "spanning neutrals, saturated bolds, and softly speckled finishes"
- Wrong: "across eighteen colorways: Sayulita, Cacao, Creosote, Peyote, Arroyo..."

### 9.7 Don't prescribe specific colorway pairings in application bullets
- Application bullets describe what the material is suited for, not which colorways to choose.
- Colorway pairings belong in dedicated color/design sections.

### 9.8 Introduce Zia's product name early
- When writing about a generic material category (terra cotta, Moroccan tile), introduce Zia's collection name early — do not make the reader wait.
- Terra cotta pieces must introduce "Cotto" in the first section.

### 9.9 Terra cotta is not floor-only
- Always acknowledge walls, fireplaces, and other applications. Never restrict terra cotta copy to floors.

### 9.10 "Near San Miguel de Allende" not "from"
- Geographic precision: artisans work near San Miguel de Allende, not from it.

### 9.11 Always link the Installation Guide
- Every reference to the Cotto Installation Guide or Zellige Installation Guide must be hyperlinked. Text reference without a link is incomplete.

## Part 10 — Pattern-Level Writing Failures (observed May 2026)

These are not individual errors but recurring instincts that produce weak copy across the board. They indicate a training gap, not a vocabulary gap.

### 10.1 Writers describe materials technically, not visually
Writers explain what the material does. Jamie rewrites to describe what it feels like in the space.

- Submitted: "The dense clay body handles radiant heat."
- Revised: "Cotto is a natural choice for a fireplace surround, playing off the warmth of the fire itself."

The question to ask before every sentence: *What does this look like in the room?* Not: *What does the material do?*

### 10.2 Writers are working from spec sheets, not from the room
Colorway descriptions contain factual errors (Fired Earth described incorrectly). Grout recommendations name colors from a reference list rather than describing visual outcomes. This suggests writers are pulling from documents without understanding the materials they're writing about.

Fix: Writers must look at the tile, the room, and the installation before writing the copy. Reference documents verify — they don't generate the copy.

### 10.3 Writers use qualifiers as confidence hedges
"The correct choice," "most often specified," "Classic range" are presented as information but function as hedges — ways of sounding authoritative without committing to a specific claim. Jamie removes them all.

Fix: Make the claim or don't. If a colorway is right for a context, say why specifically. Don't signal authority through ranking or categorization language.

### 10.4 Writers front-load sections with setup sentences
"The names appear together often, and they describe distinct products" → "pointless" (Jamie). Writers ease into sections with an observation about what they're about to explain, rather than leading with the explanation.

Fix: The first sentence of every section is the point of the section. Not the setup for it.
