Los Angeles-based retailer of handmade artisanal tile and stone, shipping worldwide. Collections span Morocco, Vietnam, Japan, Mexico, Italy, Spain, and France. 13 material collections, 200+ individual tiles.

5. Technical Accuracy Standards

5.1 Freeze/Thaw Rules (Material-Specific)

MaterialRuleSeverity
Zellige (glazed)NOT suitable for outdoor freeze/thaw. State plainly and affirmatively.Hard limit
Unglazed CottoSuitable for any climate including freeze/thaw. This is a selling point.Promote
Cotto Allende (glazed)NOT suitable for outdoor freeze/thaw.Hard limit
MarbleCan be used outdoors in appropriate climates. Do not state it cannot.Context-dependent
CementExterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only.Hard limit

5.2 Pool/Spa Rule (Cotto vs. Cotto Allende)

Unglazed Cotto: NOT for pools or spas (full submersion). Suitable for outdoor floors, patios, courtyards in any climate.

Cotto Allende (glazed): Approved for pools and spas. NOT for outdoor freeze/thaw.

"Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas. Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates." — Jamie's corrected version

5.3 Sealing Standards

  • Cotto and Cotto Allende have different sealing protocols. Never combine into one bullet list.
  • Sealing applies in all installations, including dry spaces.
  • Never name specific sealer products on product pages (511 Porous Plus, Fila Matte Wax belong on install guides only).
  • Required phrase: "Always seal [tile name] according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers."

5.4 Contact Line — Required on Every Piece

Email: info@ziatile.com  ·  Phone: 310-844-1170

Both must appear in the closing section and in FAQ answers. Jamie keeps having to add this herself.

6. Required Page Structure (Product Pages)

All 9 sections, in this exact order:

  1. Product Details — variation framing; 15–20% overage + 25% uniform option
  2. About — craft heritage; artisan sourcing; historical context; color spectrum
  3. Inherent Variation — 3 bullets: color/tone specifics, uniqueness/charm, full variation list
  4. Preparation and Curation — mix boxes; dry layout; disperse variation; overage numbers
  5. Installation and Finishing — PPE/Prop 65; drywall flush + mitered edges + Schluter; sealing; contact email
  6. Tile Usage — Residential + Commercial; accurate freeze/thaw; inline sealing; slip-resistance spec
  7. How It's Made — origin, process, what makes variation happen
  8. Order & Shipping — samples, full orders, pickup (appointment required), contact
  9. FAQ — minimum 6 questions covering required topics

2. The Five Themes Jamie's Edits Cluster Around

Theme 1: Plainer Than Aspirational Most frequent

Lifestyle marketing phrases replaced with declarative, fact-grounded sentences. Jamie consistently moved copy away from emotional/atmospheric language toward plain, specific prose.

Jamie RemovedJamie Replaced With
"where families have been pressing clay into molds and firing it the same way for generations""which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques"
"is one of those materials that looks better the longer it is in place""ages beautifully"
"add to the room rather than date it""enhances the room"
"benefit from the visible aging of clay surfaces""benefit from the lived-in character of aging clay surfaces"
"build atmosphere and authenticity over time""build atmosphere and character"
"reads as handmade rather than printed""reads as handmade" (drop "printed" — meaningless)

Theme 2: Anti-Filler High frequency

Words and clauses that don't earn their place get cut. Jamie applies the "purposeful test" rigorously: if removing the sentence loses nothing, it shouldn't be there.

  • "To something truly special" — removed. The sentence before it was strong on its own.
  • "These charming unglazed pieces add an earthy aesthetic" — appeared twice on same page. Cut.
  • "The local clay and traditional process introduce history in each unique piece" — meaningless. Cut.
  • "Well-suited for any climate, with sealing before and after grouting" — filler opener. Deleted.
  • "Today" as a sentence closer — removed from origin FAQ.
  • "Straightforward" for care/maintenance — banned after appearing four versions in a row.

Theme 3: Technical Precision Over Softness Critical

Vague guidance replaced with specifics. "Consult your installer" is never sufficient. Jamie expects numbers, measurements, and named products.

  • "Larger grout joints" → "minimum 1/8" grout joints"
  • Slip resistance: must include DCOF rating + grout joint spec + anti-slip treatment
  • Sealing: separate instructions for Cotto and Cotto Allende, never combined
  • Freeze/thaw: material-by-material, stated affirmatively in prose (not just a chart symbol)
  • Overage: two numbers (15–20% standard + 20–25% specialty) with context for each

Theme 4: Material Accuracy Critical

Wrong claims corrected; product distinctions enforced everywhere. Jamie doesn't tolerate borrowing language from one material and applying it to another.

  • Chips/pits/crazing on Cotto: Wrong. That's zellige only. Cotto variation = tone, shape, edge, thickness.
  • "Hand-pressed" where Zia doesn't hand-press: Removed (Henry caught Apr 28).
  • Light exposure deepens patina: Wrong. Patina is driven by use and time, not light.
  • Specific kiln types (wood-fired, gas-fired): Not approved without written confirmation.
  • Cotto vs. Cotto Allende confusion: Different materials with different application rules. Never conflate.
  • Red Clay 8x8 page shows a 13x13 tile: Imagery mismatch flagged.

Theme 5: Operational Ownership Recurring

Things Zia keeps doing — adding the contact line, killing Saltillo references, removing special-order callouts — Jamie wants the team to own without being asked.

  • Contact line: Jamie has been adding it herself every time. Email + phone on every piece.
  • Saltillo framing: "Why would we want someone to leave our site and go elsewhere for a tile we do not produce?" Never position Saltillo as a quality tile.
  • Special-order callouts: Omit or minimize. Zia prefers not to draw attention.
  • Cotto Allende as "sub-line": Call it "glazed terra cotta tile" or "glazed line."
