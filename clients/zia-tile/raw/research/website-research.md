# Zia Tile Website Research (May 2026)

Sources reviewed via WebFetch in May 2026. All quotes verbatim from public ziatile.com pages unless flagged otherwise. Two collection slugs returned 404 during review: `/collections/zellige-mosaics` and `/collections/unglazed-natural-zellige`. The actual mosaic collection slug is `/collections/zellige-tile-mosaics` (confirmed via site search), but its content was not accessible at fetch time.

## Site-wide observations

### Brand voice / register
- Tagline (every page footer / nav): "A Modern Home for an Ancient Art"
- Homepage / about-style line: "Experience the entire Zia collection in one place. Find your favorites and our newest releases, all designed in Los Angeles and crafted by artisans around the globe."
- Recurring tagline pattern: short three-word stacks paired with a longer descriptive sentence. Examples: "Hands. Heat. History." (zellige) / "Pour. Press. Patina." (cement) / "Old Glass. New Tricks." (glass mosaics) / "From Fire Comes Finery" (cantera) / "Aggregates Await" (terrazzo) / "Tradition on Trend" (Field Trip: Japan) / "Clean lines, no notes" (ceramics) / "The Timelessness of Tesserae" (Roman mosaics) / "Strong by nature, quiet by design" (limestone) / "Poetry + Precision" / "Nature's palette. Fire's finish." (Second Nature)
- Register skews atmospheric over technical on collection pages. Product pages are denser and more spec-driven (variation, sealing, DCOF, overage all listed).
- Sentences are mid-length on collection pages, short on tagline blocks, and instructional / imperative on installation guides.

### Em dash usage
Em dashes appear in product copy across nearly every category. Examples:
- Zellige: "Simultaneously traditional and on trend—our zellige tiles are handmade..."
- Unglazed Natural Zellige: "perfectly imperfect—unique"; "design decision—almost as important"
- Ceramics: "Available in matte, satin, glossy, and textured finishes—these ceramics are suited for both interior and exterior use"
- Field Trip: Japan: "Handcrafted in central Japan—where the art of ceramics has been embraced and improved over thousands of years—we present Zia's take on ceramic tiles."
- Glass mosaics: "Zia's design team created seven intentional patterns—drawing inspiration from artworks, fashion textiles, and locations like Los Angeles's own Union Station."
- Roman mosaics: "It's also sensitive to acids and abrasive cleaners—so stick to pH-neutral products."
- Cement (Kepler Set Three): "Proper installation and protection of cement tile is key – so please ensure you work with an installer..."
- Limestone: "Limestone has been used in architecture for thousands of years—from the pyramids to Mediterranean villas—and our collection carries that legacy into modern spaces."
- Terrazzo: "Equally at home in Classic, Mid-Century Modern, and contemporary minimalist interiors — from historic Italian interiors to the sunlit modernism of Palm Springs."

The Cotto Allende collection and Marble collection pages used em dashes more sparingly; hyphens dominate. Otherwise the em dash is a consistent brand convention. Note: the v3 mandate to avoid em dashes is OPPOSITE to the site convention.

### Standard recurring phrases
- Sealing recommendation: "use 511 Porous Plus Penetrating Sealer" / "511 Porous Plus sealer in thin layers until the tiles become fully saturated"
- Top-coat: "Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax" (interior); "511 Porous Plus" again for exterior
- Care: "pH neutral cleaners ONLY" / "Use of acidic cleaners will damage the [tile/stone]"
- Overage default: "minimum of 15% overage" / "15-20% overage to properly curate your installation"
- Cleaning intro: "Vacuum or sweep tile regularly... Promptly clean up any spills, especially oil and grease"
- Lead-time bucket: "Special Order" with category-specific weeks given on FAQ only
- Stock language: "In Stock", "In Transit"
- Compatible-use list categories: "Pools + Spas (Saltwater or chlorine)", "Exterior Floors – Freeze or Thaw"

Install guide silent on freeze/thaw. **CONTRADICTS v2.0 §4.1, which says zellige is NOT suitable for freeze/thaw and frames the prior approval as a factual error to be corrected.** v3 should retain v2.0 stance.

- **Pool/spa:** "Glazed zellige tiles are commonly used in swimming pools, shower walls, fountains, spas, and bathrooms."

## Cross-cuts

### 25% overage language on the public site
Confirmed: appears as the upper bound of "20-25%" on zellige (Gambit 8, Unglazed Natural, Trapezoid), marble (Carrara 6x6, Piazza), and Roman mosaic (Carrara 12x12) product pages, in the form: "We recommend adding 20-25% overage" or "20-25% to allow your installer to avoid using some of these tiles." NEVER appears as a standalone "25% for uniform look." Cement, cotto, ceramics, cantera, limestone, glass, terrazzo product pages reviewed do NOT mention 25%; they cap at 15% or 15-20%. The FAQ explicitly states "15%" and "20% for the most uniform appearance" — does not mention 25%.

### §4.2 dry-spaces sealing wording
The exact phrase "Always seal [tile name] according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers" was NOT found on any product page, collection page, or installation guide reviewed. Product pages use conditional phrasing such as "For areas exposed to constant moisture like showers, pools, and spas, use 511 Porous Plus." The mandate language is new internal wording, not site-derived.

### Lead times public-facing
Lead times appear in weeks ONLY on the FAQ page:
- Cement special order: "10-12 week lead time"
- Cotto special order: "11-15 week lead time"
- Zellige special order: "13-17 week lead time"
- In Stock: "typically ship within two business days of order placement"

They do NOT appear on collection pages, individual product pages, or installation guides reviewed.

### Glass mosaic sealing
