1. Brand Overview

Zia Tile is a Los Angeles-based retailer of handmade artisanal tile and stone, shipping worldwide. Collections span Morocco, Vietnam, Japan, Mexico, Italy, Spain, and France. Zia is a high-touch, high-value client. Writers must treat this account with strict adherence to documentation and zero tolerance for casual or generic language.

1.1 Audience

All copy must pass the two-audience test: it must respect the expertise of a senior design professional while remaining clear and useful to a first-time buyer.

AudienceWho They AreWhat They Need
Trade (55–60% of revenue)Architects, interior designers, contractors, tile installers managing projects of $20,000–$80,000+Technical precision. Correct terminology. No hand-holding. Trust that Zia knows the product as well as they do.
Homeowners & Business Owners (40–45%)Affluent individuals, ages 30–60. First-time buyers making a considered design investment.Clear guidance. Confidence-building detail. Enough technical context to feel informed without being overwhelmed.

1.2 Brand Pillars

PillarWhat It Means in Copy
AuthoritativeSpeak as the definitive expert on sourcing, design, and installation. Never hedge unnecessarily. Never use vague language when a specific term exists.
ArtisanalForeground the maker, the material, and the method. Specificity earns credibility. Romanticize through detail, not adjectives.
ApproachablePolished, not precious. Clear, not casual. The voice of a trusted senior design editor who makes complex things feel accessible.
InspirationalConvey atmosphere through precise detail — never through adjective stacking or lifestyle clichés. Show quality; never claim it.

1.3 The Editorial Benchmark

Jamie's benchmark: copy should read closer to Architectural Digest than an AI content dump. A useful test: paste a paragraph into ChatGPT, prompt it to rewrite as Architectural Digest editorial. That should be closer than what was submitted. That is the floor, not the ceiling.

2. Voice & Tone

The brand voice is that of a trusted senior design editor: authoritative, warm, and precise. Jamie's revisions consistently moved copy away from lifestyle marketing language and generic SEO phrasing toward plainer, more specific prose. The test is simple: does this sentence read like an expert talking, or a marketer selling?

2.1 Core Voice Rules

2.3 What Purposeful Means

Every sentence must deliver information or atmosphere — not both at once, and never neither. Before submitting, apply this test to each sentence: if you removed it, would the reader miss anything? If the answer is no, cut it.

  • Clauses that restate what was just said in different words
  • Phrases that describe what the customer already knows
  • Adjectives that could apply to any product in the category
  • Qualifications that add no useful information

4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

Copy that overstates what a product can do creates customer service problems and liability. All capability claims must be verified against the product spec or installation guide before submission. If you cannot verify a claim, do not write it.

4.1 Freeze/Thaw — Material-Specific Rules

These are hard rules that differ by material. Do not apply zellige freeze/thaw limitations to Cotto, and do not apply Cotto outdoor approvals to zellige.

MaterialOutdoor / Freeze/Thaw Rule
Zellige (glazed)NOT suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates. State this plainly and affirmatively.
Unglazed CottoSuitable for exterior use in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions. This is a selling point.
Cotto Allende (glazed)NOT suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates. Same rule as glazed zellige.
MarbleCan be used outdoors in appropriate climates. Do not state that it cannot.
Cement tileSuitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only.

4.2 Pool and Spa — Cotto vs. Cotto Allende

This distinction was flagged by Jamie directly across both the blog post and the product page. It must be applied consistently everywhere pools or spas are mentioned.

  • Unglazed Cotto: NOT suitable for pools or spas. Suitable for outdoor floors, patios, and courtyards in any climate.
  • Cotto Allende (glazed): Approved for pools and spas. NOT suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates.
Exact language from Jamie's edit: 'Glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools.' Use this framing when both products are being compared in the same section.

4.3 Sealing — Separate Cotto and Cotto Allende Instructions

Jamie flagged this in both the blog post and the collection page. Cotto and Cotto Allende have different sealing requirements. They must not be written as a combined, mixed instruction set.

  • Cotto (unglazed): Seal before grouting with 511 Porous Plus. After grouting, apply Fila Matte Wax for interior; second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior.
  • Cotto Allende (glazed): Only requires post-grout sealing with 511 Porous Plus in areas exposed to continuous moisture.
  • Do not mix these into a single bullet list. Write them as separate, clearly labeled sections or paragraphs.
  • Sealing applies in all installations, including dry spaces. Do not write sealing guidance that only references moisture-exposed areas.

4.4 Contact Line — Required on Every Piece

Jamie noted she keeps having to add the contact line herself on every piece. It must be included without being asked. Every blog post, collection page, and product page must contain a contact CTA.

  • Standard email: info@ziatile.com
  • Phone: 310-844-1170
  • Both must appear together in the closing section and in FAQ answers where relevant.

4.5 Slip Resistance

When recommending Cotto for wet floor applications, always provide a minimum grout joint specification. 'Consult the installer' alone is not sufficient guidance.

  • Cotto DCOF: 0.98 — well above the 0.42 threshold for level interior floors.
  • For wet commercial floors: grout joints should measure at least 1/8". An anti-slip treatment should be applied to the terra cotta after installation.
  • Slip resistance language belongs under the shower/wet area section, not under general interior floor bullets.

4.6 Unverified Technical Claims

  • Kiln type: do not specify wood-fired, gas-fired, or any specific kiln method without written confirmation from Zia's team.
  • Light exposure and patina: do not claim that UV or light exposure deepens Cotto's tone. Patina framing should reference use and time only.
  • Any climate or application claim (indoor/outdoor, freeze/thaw, wet areas) must be cross-referenced against the PDP usage chart before submission.

4.7 Usage Chart and Content Placement

Each usage bullet must contain only information accurate and relevant to that specific use case.

  • Shower slip resistance guidance belongs under Shower Walls + Floors, not under Interior Floors.
  • Usage bullets are standardized at the category level. Do not write them from scratch per SKU.
  • Do not add, remove, or reword usage bullets without explicit approval from Alex.

5. Cotto Product Knowledge

NEW IN V3 — Section 5 is new in Version 3.0.

This section is the authoritative reference for all Cotto and Cotto Allende content. Review it before writing or editing any Cotto blog post, collection page, or product page. If anything here conflicts with the live site, flag it to Alex before writing.

5.1 What Cotto Is

Cotto is Zia's terra cotta line. Terra cotta is the broader material category. Cotto is not a subcategory or superior variant of terra cotta — it is simply Zia's name for their terracotta collection. Establish this relationship early in any piece: 'Cotto is Zia's terra cotta line' or 'Zia's Cotto is terra cotta.'

Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried in open courtyards for one to two weeks, and high-fired in traditional kilns. It is unglazed. The surface develops a natural patina with use and time.

5.2 Colorways

5.4 Cotto Allende

Cotto Allende is Zia's glazed terra cotta tile line — not a 'sub-line.' It begins with the same clay foundation as Cotto, hand-hewn and high-fired, then hand-glazed. The name references both its origin near San Miguel de Allende and the Spanish word for 'beyond' — because the glazes introduce color and texture beyond the spectrum of natural clay.

  • Eighteen colorways (write out 'eighteen,' not '18') with names referencing Mexican geography and flora: Sayulita, Cacao, Creosote, Peyote, Arroyo, Condesa, Pedregal, and others.
  • Four shapes: 4x4 square, 4x8 rectangle, triangle, and the 1.5x8 mini bar (narrowest format, useful for linear or stack-bond layouts).
  • Finishes range from matte to glossy with speckling that varies by colorway and subtle crazing and color shifts.
  • Approved for pools and spas. Not suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates.

5.5 Saltillo — How to Frame It

Saltillo is a related clay tile from Saltillo, Coahuila, in northern Mexico — a different region and a different process from Cotto. Zia does not sell Saltillo.

Jamie's direct comment: 'My concern is that Saltillo is positioned as a fine tile that we do not sell. Shouldn't we steer this a bit more generic/mass/negative, in order to comment on why we don't sell it? Why would we want someone to leave our site and go elsewhere for a tile we do not produce?'
  • Do not position Saltillo as a quality tile that Zia simply doesn't carry. The comparison should subtly favor Cotto.
  • Frame Saltillo's characteristics as distinctions that explain why Cotto performs differently — lighter body, more porous, warmer mineral variation.
  • If there is no editorial reason to include Saltillo in a piece, consider removing it entirely. Confirm with Alex before including any Saltillo reference.
  • Never reference products or tile types that Zia does not sell without a clear strategic reason. Confirm with Alex first.

6. Blog Post Standards

NEW IN V3 — Section 6 is new in Version 3.0.

6.1 Opening Paragraphs

The opening paragraph is the first thing Jamie reads. It sets the tone for the entire piece. Do not open with a production description or a process sequence. Lead with the material, the place, or the feeling.

Jamie's approved rewrite of the Blog Post 4 opener: 'Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques. The result is a terra cotta defined by tonal variation, surface character, and the inherent details that give handmade tile its appeal. Over time, the unglazed surface develops a natural patina that reflects the material's connection to place, process, and age.'
  • Lead with what the material is, where it comes from, and what makes it distinctive before describing how it is made.
  • Do not open with 'families pressing clay into molds' or similar process-first framing. Jamie flagged this as generic and present in every terra cotta brand's copy.
  • The second and third sentences of the original opener were strong and preserved in Jamie's edit. The first sentence was replaced. That balance — fix the entry point, keep what works — is the standard.

6.2 Introducing the Collection Early

Blog posts should introduce Zia's specific collection — and name it — in the opening or first body section. Jamie added the full colorway introduction into the 'What Is Cotto Tile?' section rather than waiting until a dedicated colorways section.

Jamie's addition: 'Zia's Cotto line was born from our three traditional tones: Adobe, Fired Earth, and Red Clay, while our newer whitewashed Blanco, deep brown Madera, and matte black Oscura round out the earthy palette.'
  • Do not make the reader wait several sections to understand what Zia's specific collection looks like.
  • Colorways, formats, or shapes relevant to the piece should be introduced naturally and early — not saved for a dedicated product section near the end.

6.3 Structure and Redundancy

  • Each H2/H3 section should approach the material from a different angle: history, performance, application, care, design context. Do not repeat the same information in different words.
  • Do a word audit before submitting. If the same noun or descriptor appears more than twice in a section, find a variation.
  • Every blog post needs a proper concluding paragraph. Do not end abruptly.
  • Each section header should introduce a new dimension, not restate the intro.

6.4 Person and Voice Consistency

Choose first person ('our Cotto') or third person ('Zia's Cotto') and hold it for the entire piece. Do not switch within the same paragraph or between sections. This has been flagged in every review session.

6.5 Patina and Aging Language

Patina is a feature, not a warning. Frame it as something desirable and intentional. Reference use and time as the drivers of patina — do not reference light exposure specifically unless confirmed by Zia's team.

CategoryAvoidUse
Patina framing"Foot traffic and light exposure deepen the tone rather than diminish it""Use and time deepen the tone — the installation is a starting point, not a forever state"
Aging closer"Expect tonal deepening over time. This is the material doing what it is supposed to do.""Expect the tone to deepen over time — that patina is the point."

6.6 Applications Sections

Each application section should reference specific Zia shapes or formats in context. Generic application descriptions that don't mention actual product names are a recurring gap.

  • Interior floors: name the formats that suit the space — 13x13 squares, Big Alcazar for open layouts; Stars & Cross and 6x6 Alcazar for compact rooms.
  • Exterior: specify that unglazed Cotto performs in any climate including freeze/thaw. Note that Cotto Allende is the right choice for pools and wet spaces.
  • Bathrooms: require proper sealing. Remove language that positions Cotto Allende as the lower-maintenance alternative — Jamie deleted this from the blog post.

7. Collection Page Standards

NEW IN V3 — Section 7 is new in Version 3.0.

7.1 Opening Copy

Opening copy should establish what the material is, where it comes from, and what makes it distinctive in Zia's voice. It should not read like a product spec. Avoid filler closers like 'well-suited for any climate, with sealing before and after grouting' — Jamie deleted this from the Cotto collection page opening.

7.2 Repetition Across the Page

  • 'Charming unglazed pieces add an earthy aesthetic' appeared twice on the same page. Cut the second instance.
  • 'Traditional' appeared twice in consecutive sentences in the colorways section. When 'traditional' has been used once in the same passage, switch to 'classic range' per Jamie's edit.
  • Run a word frequency check before submitting any collection page. Repeated adjectives and descriptors are a consistent flag.

7.3 FAQ Standards

  • Sealing FAQ must separate Cotto and Cotto Allende instructions. Do not combine them into a mixed bullet list.
  • Do not reference Cotto Allende as 'a sub-category in this collection' — Jamie removed this framing. Write it as its own line.
  • FAQ answers should match the language and structure of Zia's actual installation guides. Do not paraphrase from memory.
  • All climate and application claims must be cross-referenced against the PDP usage charts.
  • Contact CTA must appear in the FAQ where relevant — especially in the quantity/overage FAQ and the installation FAQ.

7.4 Capitalization

  • Match capitalization exactly to the live site at ziatile.com. Cotto, Cotto Allende, Adobe, Fired Earth, Red Clay, Blanco, Madera, Oscura are all capitalized.
  • 'terra cotta' is lowercase as a general material term.
  • Verify every collection and product name before submitting.

7.5 Filler and Weak Phrases to Remove

  • 'To something truly special' — remove. The sentence before it is strong on its own.
  • 'These charming unglazed pieces add an earthy aesthetic' — remove or replace with specific descriptive language.
  • 'The local clay and traditional process introduce history in each unique piece' — meaningless. Cut or replace with something concrete.
  • 'Today' as a sentence closer — Jamie removed 'today' from the origin FAQ closing sentence.

Product page standards from V2 are preserved below, updated with new guidance from Jamie's Red Clay 8x8 edits.

8.2 Variation FAQ

The variation FAQ should not reference chips, pits, and crazing for Cotto. That language belongs to zellige (glazed). Cotto is unglazed — no crazing. Jamie flagged this directly on the product page.

  • For Cotto variation: reference tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness.
  • For zellige variation: reference chips, pits, and crazing as a trio.
  • Never apply zellige surface language to an unglazed tile page.

8.3 Cotto vs. Cotto Allende FAQ

The distinction must include the pool/spa rule. Jamie's corrected version:

'Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas. Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates.'

8.4 Overage FAQ

Do not write separate rationale sentences for standard overage and specialty overage. Be direct.

8.5 Required Page Sections

Each product page must contain all nine sections in this order: Product Details, About, Inherent Variation, Preparation and Curation, Installation and Finishing, Tile Usage, How It's Made, Order and Shipping, FAQ. Do not reorder or omit sections without explicit client approval.

10. SEO Rules

Keywords must be woven naturally into copy. If a keyword disrupts brand voice, rewrite the surrounding copy rather than force the term. Highlight SEO-critical phrases in the submitted draft for client review.

10.1 Key SEO Notes from V3 Review

  • Jamie added 'terracotta' (one word) to the Blog Post 4 keyword list alongside 'terra cotta' (two words). Both forms should be considered.
  • 'Saltillo vs Cotto' is in the keyword list but the Saltillo framing needs to favor Cotto. The keyword can stay; the copy must steer toward Zia's product.
  • Meta descriptions should include primary keyword and at least two use cases. Meta title format: [Product Name] [Format] | Zia Tile.
  • Meta title: 50–60 characters. Meta description: 140–160 characters.
