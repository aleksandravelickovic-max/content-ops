#### Order Details + Installation

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all Roman Mosaic orders to allow for cuts. For a more uniform appearance, we recommend 20 to 25%, which gives your installer the option to set aside any sheets with stronger variation. Before installation, evenly blend sheets from all boxes to distribute veining variation across the layout.

The transparent film on each sheet is critical. Do not peel it until the thinset has fully cured (48 hours minimum). Premature film removal damages the mesh; late removal makes grout haze harder to clean.

## About *[Locked]*

Each Roman Mosaic sheet is hand-clipped by Italian artisans into 5/8-inch tesserae, each piece carrying the marks of traditional craftsmanship. Tesserae are hand-placed on mesh backing with a transparent film face. Significant variation in color, tone, and veining is inherent to the material. Blend sheets from all boxes before installation.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with marble mosaic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Marble must be sealed on-site, with both pre-grout sealing and a top coat per the [Roman Mosaics Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/roman-mosaics-installation-guide). Remove the transparent film face before sealing and grouting. Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Roman Mosaic is hand-clipped by Italian artisans from the same Italian and Spanish marble blocks as Marble Solids. Each 5/8-inch tesserae is cut by hand, producing the organic edges that distinguish the line from precision-cut formats.

Tesserae are hand-placed on mesh sheets in solids, fans, and checkerboard patterns, with a transparent film face protecting the layout during shipping and installation. The film stays attached through the thinset cure and is removed only before sealing and grouting.

The technique mirrors Roman and Mediterranean mosaic traditions that date back centuries. Tesserae mosaics have been used in pool surrounds, bath complexes, and palaces since antiquity.
