## About *[Locked]*

Each cantera tile is cut from blocks of volcanic stone (lava rock) and finished by hand. Significant variation in color, oxidation markings, and mineral inclusions is inherent to the material. Blend pieces from all boxes before installation and lay them out to confirm the composition matches your preference.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cantera, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Cantera must be sealed on-site prior to grouting per the [Cantera Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cantera-installation-guide), with periodic re-sealing based on traffic and moisture. Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Cantera is a naturally porous volcanic stone sourced from quarries in Mexico, where the material has been a staple in architecture for thousands of years. Formed over millions of years from volcanic rock and ash, cantera is known for its earthy texture and organic color variation.

Each piece is cut directly from blocks of volcanic rock, then honed and finished by hand to showcase its textured surface. The line is offered in eight earthy colorways, each with a distinct mineral composition.

Cantera develops natural patina with use, similar to how marble or limestone wears with age. Sealing protects the surface and locks in the original color while allowing the material to develop character over time.
