## About *[Locked]*

Cantera is the volcanic stone that built colonial Mexico, used continuously for centuries in courtyards, fountains, and the carved facades of cathedrals and haciendas. Zia's Cantera line carries that material into a residential and commercial collection sized for both intimate rooms and broad public installations.

The line is offered in eight earthy colorways, each carrying its own mineral signature: Mesa, Caldera, Acacia, Sierra, Yuma, Sedona, Badlands, and Volcan. Formats range from 6x6 and 4x12 mosaics to 12x12, 12x24, and 24x24 floor tiles, so a single Cantera scheme can run from a textured shower wall in a primary bathroom to a hotel courtyard or restaurant patio in the same colorway.

## Inherent Variation

- Color, oxidation, and mineral inclusion shift meaningfully tile to tile within every colorway, with some pieces carrying ferrous reds or greens that read as a separate accent against the field.
- Each tile carries the hand of the maker in the cut and finish, with subtle differences in surface texture and edge profile that allow Cantera to read as the volcanic stone it is rather than as a uniformly cut material.
- Expect variation in size, edge profile, and visible aggregate or mineral pits in the body of the stone, all natural to volcanic rock, along with the soft patina that builds over years of use.

## Preparation and Curation

Open every box and blend pieces across the order before any thinset goes down. Lay out a dry run in the installation area, face up, to confirm the mineral and color variation reads the way it should across the room; Cantera has a face and an underside, and the underside may carry cut marks from finishing.

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all Cantera orders to account for cuts and the natural variation across the stone. For installations where a more uniform appearance is the priority, plan for a 25% overage so the installer can set aside the small share of pieces that drift furthest from the average.

## Installation and Finishing

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cantera, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. Use ⅛ inch tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. Verify rubber-mallet use against the install guide for your selected format; smaller mosaics generally require palm-only setting, while larger formats may tolerate light tapping.

Always seal Cantera according to our installation instructions, whether in dry rooms or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. Sealer selection and the two-step pre-grout and top-coat protocol are covered on the [Cantera Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cantera-installation-guide). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## Tile Usage *[Locked]*

Cantera is suitable for interior and exterior installations across all climates, including freeze/thaw, and is compatible with pools, spas, and continuous-water applications when sealed per the installation guide. The DCOF rating ranges from 0.53 to 0.63 depending on colorway; for wet floors and commercial wet rooms, increase grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛ inch and apply an anti-slip product after installation to raise traction further.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Cantera begins as a porous volcanic rock, formed over millions of years from cooled lava and ash. Solid blocks are pulled from quarries in Mexico, then sized down to the format dimensions of the line: 6x6 and 4x12 mosaics, 12x12 and 12x24 plank, and 24x24 large-format floor tiles.

Each piece is honed and finished by hand at the source. The finishing approach intentionally preserves the visible aggregate, mineral inclusions, and oxidation markings of the volcanic chemistry, which is what gives each colorway its individual mineral signature rather than a surface treatment that would mask it.

Once finished, the tiles are graded and packed for shipping. After installation, the stone continues to develop. Cantera takes on a soft patina with use, similar to marble or limestone, and the sealing protocol on the install guide protects the surface while still allowing the material to age into the room around it.
