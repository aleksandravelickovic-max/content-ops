## About *[Locked]*

Marble has shaped Western architecture continuously since Roman antiquity, and Marble Solids carries that lineage into a residential and commercial line built around six core colorways: Carrara, Nero Marquina, Grigio Carnico, Rosso Alicante, Giallo Reale, and Verde Alpi.

Together the six cover the full register of designed marble work, from the quiet veining of Carrara through the deep black field of Nero Marquina, the warm ochres of Giallo Reale, and the saturated reds of Rosso Alicante. The line is built to pair with the patterned compositions in Marble Patterns and the hand-clipped tesserae of Roman Mosaics, so a single project can move from a solid floor into a patterned border or a mosaic shower without leaving the marble palette.

## Inherent Variation

- Color, tone, and veining shift meaningfully tile to tile within every colorway, even between pieces cut from the same block; mineral inclusions and the geological history of each quarry produce a continuously varied surface rather than a repeating one.
- Each tile is honed by hand and arrives with subtle differences in surface character, which is what allows marble to read as a natural stone floor rather than a printed substitute for one.
- Expect variation in size, edge profile, and minor pits or fissures on the surface, all natural to quarried stone, along with the soft patina that builds over years of use.

## Preparation and Curation

Open every box and blend tiles across the order before any thinset goes down. Lay out a dry run in the installation area and arrange the pieces face up; marble has a face and an underside, and the underside may carry cut marks from the finishing process.

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all marble orders to account for cuts. For installations where a more uniform appearance is the priority, plan for a 25% overage so the installer can set aside the small share of pieces that drift furthest from the average. Matching grout color to the field of the stone helps blend any minor pits or fissures into the overall surface.

## Installation and Finishing

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with marble, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. Set tiles by hand only; rubber mallets create hairline cracks that grow over time. Use ⅛ inch tile spacers for the recommended grout joint.

Always seal marble according to our installation instructions, whether in dry rooms or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. Sealer selection and the two-step pre-grout and top-coat protocol are covered on the [Marble Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/marble-installation-guide). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## Tile Usage *[Locked]*

Marble is suitable for interior and exterior installations across all climates, including freeze/thaw, and is compatible with pools, spas, and continuous-water applications when sealed per the installation guide. The DCOF rating ranges from 0.28 to 0.62 depending on colorway and finish; for wet floors and commercial wet rooms, increase grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛ inch and apply an anti-slip product after installation to raise traction further. Carrara reads softer underfoot than the harder, denser colorways and benefits especially from the anti-slip step in wet rooms.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Marble begins as ancient metamorphic rock, compressed under heat and pressure over millions of years. Solid blocks are pulled from quarries across Italy and Spain and trucked to finishing workshops near Verona, where the rest of the production happens by hand.

Each block is sliced into slabs and then sized down to the format dimensions of the line. Stone masons hone the face by hand to the soft matte finish that defines Zia's marble, leaving the veining, mineral variation, and minor pits or fissures of the natural stone visible on the surface. The underside of every piece carries cut marks from the finishing process and is the side that bonds to the thinset.

Once finished, the tiles are graded, packed face-up, and shipped. The material continues to develop after installation: marble takes on a soft patina with use, deepening the surface over years and carrying each floor into a slightly different finish than the one it left the workshop with.
