## About *[Locked]*

Tesserae mosaics have been used in pool surrounds, bath complexes, and palaces since Roman antiquity, and Zia's Roman Mosaic line is the marble collection's translation of that tradition into a contemporary residential and commercial product. The format brings the small-scale geometry of an ancient mosaic to the same projects the rest of the marble line is built for.

The line is cut from the same Italian and Spanish marble as Marble Solids and Marble Patterns, and the seven colorways mirror the broader marble palette: Carrara, Nero Marquina, Grigio Carnico, Rosso Alicante, Giallo Reale, Verde Alpi, and Bardiglio Imperiale. Patterns include solids, Ventaglio (fan), Check, and Mini Check, each available in any of the seven colorways so a single project can run a Carrara solid floor into a Nero Marquina check border or a Giallo Reale fan in the shower without leaving the marble palette.

## Inherent Variation

- Color, tone, and veining shift meaningfully tessera to tessera within every colorway, and the small ⅝ inch piece size means each sheet carries dozens of those individual stones in close range.
- Each tessera is hand-clipped, which gives the line its distinctive organic edge and the rhythm that distinguishes hand-set Roman mosaic from precision-cut formats.
- Expect variation in piece size, edge profile, and minor surface pits or fissures across the sheet, all natural to quarried stone, along with the soft patina that builds over years of use.

## Preparation and Curation

Open every box and blend sheets across the order before any thinset goes down. Lay out a dry run in the installation area, with film face up, to confirm the veining and tonal variation read the way they should across the floor or wall.

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all Roman Mosaic orders to account for cuts, breakage in transit, and pattern alignment. For installations where a more uniform appearance is the priority, plan for a 25% overage so the installer can set aside the small share of sheets that drift furthest from the average.

## Installation and Finishing

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with marble mosaic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. The transparent film on each sheet is critical: do not peel it until the thinset has fully cured (48 hours minimum), and remove it only before sealing the tile and applying grout. Premature removal damages the mesh; late removal makes grout haze harder to clean.

Always seal Roman Mosaic according to our installation instructions, whether in dry rooms or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. Sealer selection and the two-step pre-grout and top-coat protocol are covered on the [Roman Mosaics Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/roman-mosaics-installation-guide). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## Tile Usage *[Locked]*

Roman Mosaic is suitable for interior and exterior installations across all climates, including freeze/thaw, and is especially well suited to pools, spas, and continuous-water applications because the small ⅝ inch tessera format follows the geometry of pool waterlines and curved fountain interiors. Sealing follows the marble protocol per the installation guide. The DCOF rating ranges from 0.28 to 0.62 depending on colorway and finish; for wet floors and commercial wet rooms, increase grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛ inch and apply an anti-slip product after installation to raise traction further.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Production starts from the same Italian and Spanish marble blocks used for Marble Solids. Stone masons in workshops near Verona slice each block into thin slabs sized for honing, then hone the face by hand to the same soft matte finish that defines the rest of the marble line.

Once honed, the slabs are passed to artisans who hand-clip each slab into ⅝ inch tesserae, one piece at a time. Hand-clipping is what produces the organic edges that distinguish Roman Mosaic from precision-cut formats, and it is the step that gives the line its rhythm at the joint level. The clipped tesserae are arranged by hand into the line's pattern geometries (solids, Ventaglio, Check, Mini Check) on mesh-backed sheets, then overlaid with a transparent film face that locks the layout in place.

Sheets ship film-up. The film stays attached through transit and through the full thinset cure, then is removed only before sealing and grouting the installation. That sequence is what preserves both the geometry of the mosaic and the surface of each individual tessera at the moment the floor or wall is ready for finish.
