## About *[Locked]*

Zia's terrazzo line is a contemporary read on a Venetian stone-masonry craft that has shaped architecture for over 500 years, from Renaissance Italian palazzo halls to the sunlit modernism of Palm Springs. The line is built around the same composition of cement and crushed marble or stone aggregate that defines terrazzo as a material, finished with the soft matte surface that lets the aggregate read at the floor level.

The line spans solids such as Neutra, Mirage, and Savoye and patterns including the Constellation series, Highball, Bungalow, Smoketree, and Bossa Nova Saguaro. Each is sized at 12 inches square, scaled to carry a continuous residential floor or a tighter graphic across a fireplace surround, kitchen backsplash, or commercial entryway.

## Inherent Variation

- Aggregate distribution shifts subtly tile to tile, producing a different ratio of marble flecks to cement matrix on each piece even within a single colorway.
- Each tile carries the hand of the maker in the steel mold and the press: minor crazing, slight irregularities at the edge, and small color drifts that read as a single matte field once the floor is laid.
- Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and edge profiles that benefit from blending sheets from multiple boxes before installation.

## Preparation and Curation

Open every box and blend tiles across the order before any thinset goes down. Lay out a dry run of two to three square yards in advance to confirm the aggregate distribution reads the way it should across the room.

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on every terrazzo order to account for cuts and any breakage in transit. For installations where a more uniform appearance is the priority, plan for a 25% overage so the installer can set aside the small share of tiles that drift furthest from the average.

## Installation and Finishing

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with terrazzo, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. Set tiles by hand only; rubber mallets create hairline cracks that grow over time. Use ⅛ inch tile spacers, with shims or wedge spacers as needed to maintain straight grout lines.

Always seal terrazzo according to our installation instructions, whether in dry rooms or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. Sealer selection and the two-step pre-grout and top-coat protocol are covered on the [Terrazzo Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/terrazzo-installation-guide). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## Tile Usage *[Locked]*

Terrazzo is suitable for interior installations and for exterior floors and walls in non-freeze/thaw climates only. The line is not suitable for outdoor floors that may receive snow or ice. Pools and spas are compatible with full sealing per the installation guide. The DCOF rating is 0.51; for wet floors and commercial wet rooms, increase grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛ inch and apply an anti-slip product after installation to raise traction further.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Each tile starts in a steel mold sized to the finished 12 inch format. The mold is filled with crushed marble and stone aggregate in the proportion specified for the colorway or pattern, then layered with liquid cement and a powdered concrete base.

The full assembly is hydraulically pressed in a single stroke, which compacts the layers into one composite slab and locks the aggregate into the cement matrix. The pressed tiles are cured on drying racks, then moved to the finishing station, where the surface is ground and polished by hand to expose the aggregate and bring the matte finish to its final read.

Hand finishing is what produces the subtle tonal variation, occasional crazing, and slight edge irregularity that separate hand-pressed terrazzo from machine-set composite tile. Once each tile passes a final inspection, it is graded into the box alongside others within the same tonal range so the installer receives a consistent set to mix from on site.
