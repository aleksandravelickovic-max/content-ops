## About *[Locked]*

Limestone has been used in architecture for thousands of years, from the pyramids of Giza through the Mediterranean villas that defined the European country house. Zia's Limestone line carries that lineage into a residential and commercial collection drawn from two distinct stone traditions: the warm sand and ochre tones of the Moroccan quarries near Fez and the cooler greys of the French quarries around Bordeaux.

The line includes five colorways: Buff, Monument, Basilica, French Limestone, and French Cobblestone. Each colorway is offered in two finishes, so a single project can move between them: honed for a smooth, matte surface and bush-hammered for the textured, lived-in character of an older stone floor.

## Inherent Variation

- Color and tone shift subtly tile to tile within each colorway, with the French stone carrying cooler greys and the Moroccan stone carrying warmer sand and ochre tones.
- Each tile is finished by hand and arrives with the surface character of its finish: honed pieces read as a single soft matte field, while bush-hammered pieces read as an uneven, rustic surface with visible hammer marks across the face.
- Expect variation in size, edge profile, and minor pits or fissures on the surface, all natural to quarried limestone, along with the soft patina that builds over years of use.

## Preparation and Curation

Open every box and blend pieces across the order before any thinset goes down. Lay out a dry run in the installation area, face up, to confirm the tonal variation reads the way it should across the floor; limestone has a face and an underside, and the underside may carry cut marks from finishing.

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all limestone orders to account for cuts, breakage in transit, and the natural variation across the stone. For installations where a more uniform appearance is the priority, plan for a 25% overage so the installer can set aside the small share of pieces that drift furthest from the average.

## Installation and Finishing

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. Set tiles by hand only; rubber mallets create hairline cracks that grow over time. Use ⅛ inch tile spacers for the recommended grout joint.

Always seal limestone according to our installation instructions, whether in dry rooms or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. Sealer selection and the two-step pre-grout and top-coat protocol are covered on the [Limestone Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/limestone-installation-guide). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## Tile Usage *[Locked]*

Limestone is suitable for interior and exterior installations across all climates, including freeze/thaw, and is compatible with pools, spas, and continuous-water applications when sealed per the installation guide. The DCOF rating is 0.50 for the honed finish and 0.56 for the bush-hammered finish; for wet floors and commercial wet rooms, increase grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛ inch and apply an anti-slip product after installation to raise traction further. The bush-hammered finish reads more confidently underfoot on wet floors than the honed finish.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Solid blocks are pulled from quarries near Fez, Morocco and around Bordeaux, France, then trucked to local finishing workshops. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions of the line and brought to the finishing station, where the colorway is matched to one of the two surface treatments available across the collection.

The honed finish is produced by gently smoothing the face of the stone to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is produced by striking the face with a hammer head whose textured surface uniformly pits the stone, giving it the rustic, aged character of a hand-worked floor. Both finishes leave the natural mineral signature of the quarry intact at the surface.

Once finished, each tile is graded for tonal range and packed face-up for shipping. After installation, limestone continues to develop. The material takes on a soft patina with use, deepening the surface over years and carrying the floor into the same lived-in character that has kept limestone underfoot in surviving floors from antiquity into the present.
