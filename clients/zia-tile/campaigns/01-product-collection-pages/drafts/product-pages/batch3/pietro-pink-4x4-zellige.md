## About

No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.

Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made

Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.

The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.

Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does Pietro Pink zellige look like in a room?

Zia's Pietro Pink reads as a dusty, stone-inflected pink — the kind of tone found in aged Italian plaster or travertine rather than in a paint chip. The glaze shifts between a paler blush and a more saturated rose depending on the light conditions, a result of the hand-dipping and uneven kiln firing. In a bathroom or kitchen, the colorway draws on the same earthy quality as natural stone without committing to a strictly neutral palette.

### Can Pietro Pink 4x4 zellige be used on shower walls and floors?

Yes. Zia's Pietro Pink 4x4 is suited for shower walls, shower floors, and steam showers. Sealing is required for all shower applications. On shower floors, grout joints should measure a minimum of ⅛" and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation is complete. Refer to the Zellige Installation Guide for sealing specifications, or reach out to the team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170 with any questions.

### Is Pietro Pink zellige approved for pools and spas?

Yes. Pietro Pink zellige is approved for pool waterlines, spa interiors, and fountains in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required per the installation instructions. The dusky pink tone reads well at pool surrounds paired with natural stone decking or pale plaster coping in mild-climate installations.

### What materials pair well with Pietro Pink zellige?

Pietro Pink sits at ease with travertine, limestone, marble in cream or beige tones, warm-finished plaster, and natural wood. The stone-adjacent quality of the glaze means it reads as part of a natural material palette rather than as a standalone color accent. Brass and unlacquered bronze fixtures reinforce the Italian stone reference in the colorway name. Cool-toned metals pull the glaze toward its pinker register.

### What grout suits Pietro Pink 4x4?

A grout that closely matches the blush midpoint of the Pietro Pink field allows the tonal variation across the tiles to read as a continuous surface. A warm white or cream grout introduces a lighter counterpoint that brightens the field. Avoid cool-toned grouts, which flatten the stone reference in the glaze. As with any zellige installation, evaluate the grout against actual tiles from the order rather than a chip sample.

### Does Pietro Pink vary between production runs?

Yes. The Pietro Pink colorway can shift between batches. Each run of zellige is produced by hand, and the clay, glaze, and kiln conditions change from one production run to the next. Tiles from separate orders may differ in tone and surface character. For projects requiring tiles across multiple orders, ordering the full quantity at once gives the most consistent result across the installation.
