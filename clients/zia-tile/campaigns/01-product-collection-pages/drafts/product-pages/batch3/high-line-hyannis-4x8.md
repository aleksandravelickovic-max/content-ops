## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is set. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the Hyannis pattern read in a room?

The Hyannis is a blue and white geometric repeat — a clean, structured pattern that reads as graphic and precise at scale. On a kitchen backsplash, the color contrast between the blue and white registers across the field and gives the wall a strong visual character. On a bathroom floor, the pattern introduces a formal geometry that works against neutral walls, chrome fixtures, and white stone without overwhelming the space. The 4x8 rectangular format allows the pattern to be set in a stack bond, staggered bond, or herringbone, each of which shifts how the geometry reads across the surface.

### Does Zia's High Line Hyannis cement tile require sealing?

Yes. All cement tile requires sealing before grouting and again after grouting — including in dry spaces. Follow Zia's cement installation guide for the recommended sealers and application sequence. For specific questions about sealer selection, contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### Can High Line Hyannis be used in a shower?

Yes. Zia's High Line Hyannis 4x8 is suited for shower walls and shower floors. Apply sealer before grouting and again after, following the cement installation guide. For shower floor applications, apply an anti-slip treatment after installation and maintain a minimum 1/8" grout joint.

### Can High Line Hyannis be installed outdoors?

High Line Hyannis cement tile is suited for exterior floors and walls in non-freeze/thaw climates — patios, courtyards, and covered outdoor surfaces in mild climates. It is not suitable for installation in climates where the surface may freeze.

### What materials pair well with High Line Hyannis?

The blue and white palette of Hyannis pairs well with white plaster, honed marble, and unlacquered brass. Against white surfaces, the blue geometry holds visual priority. Against natural stone or warm wood, the blue reads as a cooler accent. The pattern also works alongside other Zia cement tiles in solid white or a complementary blue for border or field combinations. Contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) to discuss pattern combinations before ordering.

### How should patterned cement tile be cleaned?

Sweep or dry-mop regularly to keep grit from scratching the matte surface. Clean with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner — avoid vinegar, citrus, and bleach, as these will damage the cement and strip the sealer. Dry the surface after mopping to prevent residue buildup. Reseal periodically per the installation guide, with frequency depending on traffic and exposure.
