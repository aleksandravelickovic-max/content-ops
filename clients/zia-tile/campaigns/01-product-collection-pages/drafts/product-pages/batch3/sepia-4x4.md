## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2×8 and 2×4 rectangles and 4×4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Zia's ceramics are produced in a potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-temperature ceramic firing has been practiced for more than 1,000 years. The collection was developed through a direct collaboration with these workshops to bring architectural-grade precision to Zia's tile range.

Each tile is molded and glazed, then fired at high temperature in a single pass. The high-firing process produces a dense, durable body with very minor variation in tone and dimension — consistent enough for tight grout joint layouts while still retaining the character of individually fired pieces.

Matte finishes are applied as a flat glaze that diffuses light evenly and requires a penetrating sealer before grouting. Satin and glossy finishes carry more reflectivity and do not require pre-grout sealing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the Sepia colorway read in a room?

Sepia is a deep, warm brown with reddish-brown undertones — the name references the warm tone of aged photography. In a room, the color reads as grounded and earthy rather than neutral. Against natural wood, it sits in the same warm register and reinforces the palette. Against plaster walls or aged metal, the reddish-brown undertone adds depth without pulling the room into a heavily saturated range. The satin finish introduces a low-level sheen that catches light without reading as gloss.

### Does Sepia 4x4 require sealing before installation?

No. The Sepia 4x4 is a satin finish and does not require a penetrating sealer before grouting. Matte ceramics require pre-grout sealing as a grout release, but satin and glossy finishes skip that step. For any installation questions, contact the team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### Can Sepia 4x4 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Zia's Sepia 4x4 is suited for shower walls and shower floors. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" to support traction on the shower floor surface. An anti-slip product can be applied after installation to increase slip resistance in high-exposure wet floor areas.

### Can Sepia 4x4 be used in a pool or spa?

Yes. Zia's satin-finish ceramics are approved for pool and spa applications, including pool waterline tile and spa interiors. The satin glaze is compatible with saltwater and chlorine environments.

### What materials pair well with Sepia?

Sepia's warm brown pairs well with natural oak and walnut, plaster walls in cream or warm white tones, and aged or blackened metal fixtures. For a grout color approach, a tone-on-tone choice in the warm brown range keeps the surface quiet; a contrasting near-white grout articulates the grid and emphasizes the tile format. The Sepia colorway is also available in a 2×8 rectangle for wall applications where a directional format is preferred.

### Is Sepia 4x4 suited for a kitchen backsplash?

Yes. The 4×4 square format in Sepia produces a clean, symmetrical grid on a backsplash that suits kitchens with warm material palettes — dark wood cabinetry, natural stone counters, and brass or bronze hardware. The satin finish is durable in kitchen conditions and does not require pre-grout sealing, which simplifies the installation sequence.
