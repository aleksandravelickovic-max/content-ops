## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is set. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the Sage colorway read in a room?

Sage is a muted grey-green — a tone that sits between green and grey rather than leaning clearly into either. In a room, it reads as a grounding neutral that contributes color without drawing the eye. Against white, it holds back and produces a quiet contrast. Against warm plaster and wood, the grey undertone comes forward and creates a cool counterpoint to the warmer materials. The 2x8 subway format gives the color a horizontal, understated field that suits contemporary and transitional interiors.

### Does Zia's Sage 2x8 cement tile require sealing?

Yes. All cement tile requires sealing before grouting and again after grouting. Sealing is required for every installation, including dry spaces. Follow Zia's cement installation guide for the recommended sealers and application sequence. For questions about sealer selection, contact the team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### Can Sage 2x8 be used in a shower?

Yes. Zia's Sage 2x8 is suited for shower walls and shower floors. Seal the tile before grouting and again after, following the cement installation guide. For shower floor applications, apply an anti-slip treatment after installation to support traction in wet conditions.

### Can Sage 2x8 be installed outdoors?

Sage 2x8 cement tile is suited for exterior floors and walls in non-freeze/thaw climates — patios, courtyards, and covered outdoor spaces in mild climates. It is not suitable for installation in climates where the surface may freeze.

### What materials pair well with Sage?

Sage pairs well with warm plaster walls, pale oak, and unlacquered brass hardware — the grey-green tone occupies the cool side of a warm palette without disrupting the warmth of the room. Against white marble or limestone countertops, Sage reads as a considered color choice that holds its ground. The Sage colorway is also available in 4x4 and 8x8 formats; mixing formats allows for different grid scales across the same installation.

### How should cement tile be cleaned?

Sweep or dry-mop regularly to remove grit from the surface. For routine cleaning, use warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid acidic cleaners — vinegar, citrus-based products, and bleach will damage the cement surface and strip the sealer. Reseal periodically following the cement installation guide, with frequency depending on traffic and exposure.
