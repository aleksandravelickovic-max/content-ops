## How It's Made

Terrazzo is produced using techniques refined by Venetian stone masons since the 15th century, when leftover marble offcuts were combined with cement to floor courtyards and palazzo halls. The same craft is preserved in the workshops where Zia's terrazzo is produced today.

Crushed marble and stone aggregates are arranged in steel molds and covered with liquid cement, then hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once cured, the surface is ground and polished to the matte finish that defines the line.

Each tile is finished by hand, which produces the subtle tonal variation, occasional crazing, and slight edge irregularity that separate hand-pressed terrazzo from machine-set composite tile. The cement matrix and the visible aggregate are what define terrazzo as a material; the press and the finish are what carry that definition into the floor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What gives Red Rocks its distinctive warm aggregate palette?

Red Rocks is built on a warm pink cement base with crushed stone aggregate in cream, beige, and earth tones distributed through the matrix. The pink base tone comes from the cement formulation; the cream and earth-tone aggregate flecks are natural crushed stone set into the wet cement before pressing. The aggregate colors — warmer and more varied than the greys found in Salt Bush — are what give Red Rocks its connection to earthy, sun-warmed material traditions.

### How does Red Rocks compare to Salt Bush in color temperature and aggregate density?

Red Rocks and Salt Bush are the two contrasting reads in Zia's terrazzo range. Red Rocks has a warm pink base with cream and earth-tone aggregate — warmer in register, more connected to Mediterranean and Southwestern palettes. Salt Bush has a cool grey base with muted stone aggregate — quieter in tone, suited to contemporary and minimalist settings. Aggregate density reads as similar across both, but the aggregate colors differ significantly: Red Rocks sits in a warm ochre-cream range while Salt Bush's aggregate is muted grey and off-white.

### On a fireplace surround versus a floor — how does the Red Rocks aggregate pattern read differently?

On a horizontal floor, the aggregate in Red Rocks distributes across a wide continuous surface and the speckled composition resolves into an even warm field at standing height. On a vertical fireplace surround, the 12x12 tiles sit at eye level, and the individual aggregate clusters and tonal shifts within each tile read at closer range. The fireplace context makes the aggregate detail — including the cream and beige flecks against the pink base — more immediately apparent. Dry-laying the surround tiles on a flat surface before setting confirms how the aggregate composition reads in the vertical plane.

### Can Red Rocks 12x12 be used on a shower floor or wall?

Yes. Red Rocks 12x12 is suited for shower floors and shower walls. Terrazzo carries a DCOF of 0.51, which meets the wet area slip resistance threshold for floor applications. Seal the tile before and after grouting per the installation guide, and size grout joints to a minimum of ⅛". An anti-slip product can be applied after installation to increase traction further in high-use wet floor applications.

### Does Red Rocks 12x12 terrazzo require sealing?

Yes. Cement terrazzo requires sealing before and after grouting. Apply the penetrating sealer in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. For interior installations, a matte wax top coat is applied after grouting. For exterior installations in non-freeze/thaw climates, apply a second coat of penetrating sealer in place of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time. Refer to Zia's terrazzo installation guide for the full sequence.

### How does the 12x12 format affect how the Red Rocks aggregate reads across an installation?

At 12x12, the warm pink field and scattered aggregate resolve into a continuous surface at enough scale for the speckled composition to register as a material rather than a series of individual tiles. Blending tiles from all boxes and dry-laying the arrangement before setting is essential — aggregate distribution varies tile to tile, and that variation becomes more apparent across a large installation. A 1/8" grout joint is recommended, with minor shimming anticipated to keep lines straight across the field.

### What does blending tiles from multiple boxes involve on site?

Each box in the order should be opened and tiles intermixed before installation begins. Aggregate distribution in terrazzo varies between individual tiles, and setting pieces drawn sequentially from a single box can concentrate similar aggregate density in one area of the floor. Dry-laying the full arrangement — or the largest portion the site allows — gives the installer visibility into how the warm pink field and earth-tone aggregate balance across the finished surface before any tile is set.

### What causes crazing on terrazzo, and should it be a concern?

Crazing is a fine network of surface-level cracks in the cement matrix that results from the pressing and curing process in hand-made terrazzo. It is visible at close range on some tiles and less apparent on others. Crazing does not affect the structural integrity of the tile or its bond to the substrate when properly installed. It is a characteristic of hand-pressed cement tile, not an indicator of a manufacturing issue.

### Can a cracked or chipped Red Rocks tile be replaced individually?

Yes. A cracked or chipped tile can be replaced individually without disturbing the surrounding field, provided overage was retained from the original order. Zia recommends keeping extra tiles from the original batch, as aggregate composition and the warm pink base tone can shift between production runs. ### Is Red Rocks terrazzo suitable for freeze/thaw climates?

No. Cement terrazzo is not rated for exterior installation in freeze/thaw climates. Water penetrating the cement matrix during freeze cycles can cause surface damage over time. In non-freeze/thaw climates, Red Rocks 12x12 is suited for exterior floors, patios, and courtyard applications with proper sealing.
