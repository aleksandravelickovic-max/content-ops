Extruded in central Japan and fired once, Zia's Toast Furrowed is a golden-brown ceramic tile in a 9.45" × 2.36" rectangle format with a furrowed surface — a pattern of raised linear ridges that run the length of the tile. The color references the warm caramel-brown of toast: a golden, amber-inflected tan that sits between honey and dark wheat, warmer than a standard beige and more grounded than a yellow. What distinguishes the furrowed version within the Toast series is the surface texture. The raised ridges catch light from an angle and produce a pattern of shadow and depth across the field that is absent in the flat version of the same tile — as light shifts across the wall, the highlights and shadows across the ridges shift with it, giving the surface a three-dimensional register that reads differently throughout the day and under different light sources. On a feature wall, the textured field is substantial without requiring another material to carry visual weight alongside it. On a kitchen backsplash or exterior wall, the furrowed texture introduces scale and warmth through the relief of the surface rather than through color contrast. The tile is 1/2" thick — slightly thicker than standard ceramic at 3/8" — which should be accounted for in transitions between materials and at finished edges. High variations in color, shade, and surface texture are natural characteristics of this extruded ceramic and are part of the material's identity. ## About

Field Trip: Japan is Zia's line of extruded Japanese ceramics — a distinct process and a distinct product category from the molded ceramics in the broader Zia ceramic collection. Where molded ceramics are pressed into a form, extruded tiles are pushed through a die-cut mold and cut to length, a process that produces a different surface character and a slightly thicker body.

The Toast series is produced in central Japan and fired once. The collection includes three finish variations — flat, furrowed, and etched — each sharing the same golden-brown colorway but differing in surface texture. High variation in color, shade, and surface texture is inherent to the extruded process and is a defining quality of the Field Trip: Japan line.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. The 1/2" body thickness of Field Trip: Japan tiles requires careful transition planning where this tile meets other materials.

## How It's Made

Field Trip: Japan tiles are produced using an extrusion process — the clay body is pushed through a die-cut mold and cut into individual tiles. This differs from the molded process used in the broader Zia ceramic collection, where clay is pressed into a mold. The extrusion process is what produces the furrowed tile's raised ridges and linear surface texture, which run as a continuous feature the length of the tile.

Each tile is fired once in central Japan, where the high-firing tradition for ceramics extends back centuries. The single firing produces a dense, durable body suited to both wall and floor applications. The furrowed finish is formed during the extrusion process before firing — the ridges are structural features of the tile body, not a glaze or surface application.

High variation in color, shade, and surface texture is inherent to extruded ceramics and is more pronounced in the Field Trip: Japan tiles than in Zia's standard molded ceramic line. Mixing tiles from multiple boxes and arranging a dry layout before setting is particularly important for this material, to distribute variation evenly across the installation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the Toast Furrowed colorway and texture read in a room?

Toast Furrowed is a golden-brown — a warm, amber-inflected tan that sits between honey and dark wheat. The furrowed surface adds a three-dimensional register to the color: the raised ridges catch light at an angle and produce a pattern of highlights and shadow that shifts as light moves across the wall. Against a raking light source — a window to the side, a directional pendant — the texture becomes more pronounced. In a room with diffuse overhead light, the ridges read as a subtle relief. The color and texture work together to give the field visual depth without requiring a contrasting material alongside it.

### How does Toast Furrowed differ from Toast Flat?

Both tiles share the same golden-brown color and are produced in the same 9.45" × 2.36" rectangle from the same extruded ceramic body. The furrowed surface has raised linear ridges that introduce shadow and depth across the field. The flat version has a smooth surface that reads as a continuous, even plane of color. On a wall, the furrowed tile is more active and textural; the flat tile is quieter and more uniform. The two can be combined in an installation — for example, a furrowed field with a flat border — but should be laid out dry first to confirm the visual relationship.

### Does Toast Furrowed require sealing?

Toast Furrowed is produced without a glaze that would require pre-grout sealing. No penetrating sealer is required before grouting for this material. For specific installation questions regarding this tile and its finish, contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### Can Toast Furrowed be used on a shower floor or wall?

Yes. Zia's Toast Furrowed is suited for shower walls and shower floors. The furrowed surface texture provides a natural surface variation underfoot that contributes to traction on the shower floor. Plan for a minimum 1/8" grout joint, and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation in high-exposure wet floor areas.

### What does the high variation in Field Trip: Japan tiles mean for installation planning?

High variation in color, shade, and surface texture is inherent to extruded ceramics and more pronounced in Field Trip: Japan tiles than in Zia's standard molded ceramic line. In practice, this means tiles from the same box may differ more noticeably in tone than standard ceramics do. Mixing tiles from all boxes thoroughly before installation and arranging a dry layout across the full installation area distributes the variation evenly across the field. This step is critical for Field Trip: Japan tiles and should not be skipped.

### How should the 1/2" body thickness of Toast Furrowed be handled at tile transitions?

At transitions to adjacent flooring or wall materials, the 1/2" body thickness — compared to the 3/8" standard for most ceramics — requires careful transition planning. Options include a Schluter strip designed for the appropriate height differential, a mitered edge where tiles meet at a corner, or bringing adjacent materials to a matching finished height. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile where the tile terminates at a wall edge.
