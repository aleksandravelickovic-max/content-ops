## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is set. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does White Stars & Cross read in a room?

White Stars & Cross produces a quiet, structured geometric field — a white eight-pointed star on a light grey ground with thin geometric outlines connecting the repeat. The contrast is measured: the pattern registers as precise geometry without strong color differentiation, which means the tile works in spaces where the composition should lead with refinement rather than boldness. Against white walls, the field reads as near-tonal and almost textural. Against warm stone, wood, or a darker grout joint, the geometry sharpens and takes on more definition.

### Does Zia's White Stars & Cross cement tile require sealing?

Yes. All cement tile requires sealing before grouting and again after grouting — in every installation, including dry spaces. Follow Zia's cement installation guide for the recommended sealers and application sequence. For specific questions about sealer selection, contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### Can White Stars & Cross be used in a shower?

Yes. Zia's White Stars & Cross is suited for shower walls and shower floors. Apply sealer before grouting and again after, following the cement installation guide. For shower floor applications, apply an anti-slip treatment after installation and maintain a minimum 1/8" grout joint.

### Can White Stars & Cross be installed outdoors?

White Stars & Cross cement tile is suited for exterior floors and walls in non-freeze/thaw climates — patios, courtyards, and covered outdoor surfaces in mild climates. It is not suitable for installation in climates where the surface may freeze.

### What materials pair well with White Stars & Cross?

The light grey and white palette of White Stars & Cross works naturally against warm materials — unlacquered brass, natural wood, waxed plaster — where the cool ground provides contrast without competing. Against white or light grey stone, the pattern integrates into a tonal field. The tile also pairs well with Zia's other Moorish-inspired cement patterns, where the interlocking geometry can create a frame around a central field or transition between two pattern zones. Contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) to discuss pattern combinations before ordering.

### How should White Stars & Cross cement tile be cleaned?

Sweep or dry-mop regularly to remove grit from the matte surface. Clean with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid vinegar, citrus-based products, and bleach — these will damage the cement surface and strip the sealer. Reseal periodically per the installation guide, with frequency depending on traffic and exposure.
