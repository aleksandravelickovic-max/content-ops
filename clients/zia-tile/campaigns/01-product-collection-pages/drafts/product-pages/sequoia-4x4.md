## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.

Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.

Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Sequoia?

Sequoia is a tranquil, muted green — a mid-tone earthy green without strong blue or yellow direction. In satin finish, the surface produces a low-sheen result that gives the color more depth than matte while stopping well short of the high reflectivity of gloss. The 4x4 square format produces an even, gridded field suited to floors, bathroom walls, backsplashes, and shower enclosures.

### What is the difference between satin and matte finish?

Satin finish sits between matte and glossy. Matte finish produces a completely flat, non-reflective surface. Satin introduces a low level of sheen that adds depth to the color without producing a high-gloss result. For slip resistance, satin ceramic has a DCOF of 0.45 — lower than matte (0.74) but higher than glossy (0.30) — making it suitable for wet floor applications when combined with appropriate grout joint sizing.

### Does Sequoia 4x4 require sealing?

No. Satin ceramic does not require pre-grout sealing. The satin glaze is dense enough that grout haze does not bond to the surface the way it can with matte ceramic. For wet installations — shower floors, shower walls, and pool surrounds — sealing the grout lines after installation is recommended to protect the grout from moisture and staining.

### Can Sequoia 4x4 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Sequoia 4x4 can be used on shower floors and shower walls. The satin finish has a DCOF of 0.45, which meets the wet area slip resistance threshold. Size grout joints to a minimum of ⅛" to support traction underfoot, and apply an anti-slip product after installation if additional slip resistance is required for the specific application.

### Can Sequoia 4x4 be installed on an exterior floor?

No. Sequoia 4x4 is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For outdoor floor surfaces, a material rated for exterior floor use is required.

### How do I clean satin ceramic tile?

To clean satin ceramic tile, wipe with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner for routine maintenance. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit and debris that can scratch the surface. Blot spills promptly. Avoid acidic cleaners — including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach — as these can damage the glaze. For heavier stains, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) is the recommended product.
