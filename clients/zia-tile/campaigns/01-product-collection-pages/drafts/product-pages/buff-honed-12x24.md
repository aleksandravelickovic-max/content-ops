## How It's Made

Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.

The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.

Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does honed limestone mean?

Honed limestone means the stone's surface has been mechanically ground with fine abrasives to produce a smooth, flat, matte finish. The process removes surface irregularities without introducing a reflective sheen, leaving the stone's natural color and markings visible. The result is a practical surface suited to a wide range of floor and wall applications.

### What is the difference between honed and bush-hammered limestone?

The difference between honed and bush-hammered limestone comes down to surface treatment. Honed limestone is ground smooth, producing a consistent, flat, matte surface. Bush-hammered limestone is finished with a high-impact tool that uniformly pits the surface, creating a rougher, more textured result. Honed finishes work well across a wide range of interior applications. Bush-hammered finishes are typically chosen where a more tactile, rustic surface is preferred.

### Is honed limestone suitable for a bathroom floor?

Yes. Honed limestone is suitable for a bathroom floor. The matte surface provides better traction than polished stone and is better at concealing water spots and minor surface wear. Sealing is required before grouting and should be maintained as often as needed, depending on traffic and moisture exposure. In wet floor applications, slip resistance can be further increased by sizing grout joints to a minimum of ⅛" and applying an anti-slip treatment.

### How do I seal honed limestone?

To seal honed limestone, clean and dry the surface thoroughly before applying 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the stone is fully saturated. Wipe off any excess before it dries — over-application can result in an uneven film on the surface. Allow the sealer to cure fully before proceeding to grouting. For interior installations, follow with a coat of Fila Matte Protective Wax. For exterior applications, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus rather than wax.

### How do I clean honed limestone?

To clean honed limestone, mop with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner for routine maintenance. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit and debris that can scratch the surface. Blot spills as soon as they occur rather than wiping them across the stone. For stubborn staining, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) is the recommended product. Acidic cleaners — including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach — should never be used on limestone as they will etch and permanently damage the surface.

### Does the 12x24 format work differently from smaller limestone formats?

The 12x24 format produces a more continuous, expansive surface than smaller formats. Fewer grout joints means the stone's tonal variation reads across a wider field, which works particularly well on large floors and floor-to-ceiling wall cladding. The format also reduces installation time on large surfaces. With a large format tile, tonal balance across the field is especially important to assess during the dry layout stage before setting begins.
