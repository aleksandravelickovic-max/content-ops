## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.

Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.

Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Linen 4x4?

Linen 4x4 is the same true ivory colorway as Linen 2x8 in a square format. The 4x4 produces equal grout joint spacing in both directions, creating an evenly gridded field across the installation. It is suited to floors, backsplashes, shower walls, and bathroom walls where a clean, neutral surface with consistent color carries the space.

### How does Linen 4x4 differ from Linen 2x8?

The colorway and finish are identical — both are true ivory in matte. The difference is the format: 4x4 produces a symmetrical square grid, while 2x8 produces a rectangular subway layout. The square format tends to read as more structured and less directional than the subway format, which has a natural horizontal emphasis that can be redirected by rotating the tile.

### Does Linen 4x4 require sealing?

Yes. As a matte ceramic, Linen 4x4 must be sealed with 511 Porous Plus before grouting to act as a grout release and prevent permanent grout haze. Apply the sealer in a thin, even layer over the tile surface after laying, allow it to cure fully, then proceed to grouting. Satin and glossy ceramics do not require this step.

### Can Linen 4x4 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Linen 4x4 can be used on shower floors and shower walls. The matte finish has a DCOF of 0.74, which meets the wet area slip resistance threshold. Seal the tile before grouting as required, and size grout joints to a minimum of ⅛". In high-exposure wet areas, an anti-slip product applied after installation can further increase traction.

### Can Linen 4x4 be installed on an exterior floor?

No. Linen 4x4 is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For outdoor floors, a material rated for exterior floor use is required.

### How do I maintain matte ceramic tile over time?

To maintain matte ceramic tile over time, clean with pH-neutral products only and address spills promptly. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit and debris that can abrade the surface. Reseal with 511 Porous Plus every one to two years depending on traffic and exposure. Avoid acidic cleaners — including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach — as these will damage the matte glaze permanently.
