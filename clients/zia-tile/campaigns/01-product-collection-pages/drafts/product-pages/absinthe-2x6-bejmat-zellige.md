## About

No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.

Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made

Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.

The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.

Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the Absinthe 2x6 Bejmat differ from the Absinthe 4x4?

The Absinthe 2x6 Bejmat and the Absinthe 4x4 share the same aqua-hued glaze and are produced using the same traditional Moroccan methods. The difference is in how the format reads across a surface. The 2x6 bejmat introduces a linear, directional quality that the square format does not produce, which is well-suited to herringbone, chevron, and stacked layouts.

### What patterns work best with the Absinthe 2x6 format?

The 2x6 format works well in several layout patterns. A stacked horizontal layout emphasizes the linear quality of the format and suits kitchen backsplashes where a clean, directional surface is the goal. A herringbone or chevron arrangement introduces more movement and is commonly used on shower walls and feature surfaces. A full dry layout before setting any tile is important with a rectangular format, as pattern direction and cut placement at the perimeter need to be resolved before thinset is involved.

### Which direction should the Absinthe 2x6 run — horizontal or vertical?

The direction depends on the surface and the intended effect. A horizontal layout emphasizes the width of the surface and works well on backsplashes and lower wall applications. A vertical layout draws the eye upward and suits shower enclosures and floor-to-ceiling installations. Neither direction is incorrect, so the key is establishing orientation with the installer before the dry layout stage, as direction affects how cuts are planned at the perimeter.

### Does the Absinthe colorway vary between orders?

Yes. The Absinthe colorway can vary between production runs. Each batch of zellige is produced by hand, and the clay, glaze, and kiln conditions shift from one run to the next. Tiles from separate orders may differ in tone and surface character. Where the project requires tiles from multiple orders, ordering the full quantity at once gives the most consistent result across the installation.

### Can the Absinthe 2x6 be mixed with other zellige formats in the collection?

Yes. The Absinthe 2x6 can be mixed with other zellige formats — including the Absinthe 4x4 — within the same installation. Each format is produced using the same traditional methods and shares consistent material character across the collection.
