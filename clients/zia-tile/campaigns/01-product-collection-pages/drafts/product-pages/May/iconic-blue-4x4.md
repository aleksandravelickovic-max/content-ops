## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.

Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.

Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Iconic Blue 4x4?

Iconic Blue is a deep, grounded blue in matte finish — a full-bodied color without strong purple or green undertones, sitting in the direct blue family with clarity and weight. The matte surface keeps the blue consistent and non-reflective across the installation, so the color reads as the same depth across the field without the tonal shifting that occurs with gloss or satin glazes. Individual tiles carry slight tonal variation, which distributes evenly across a full installation.

### Does Iconic Blue 4x4 require sealing before grouting?

Yes. Iconic Blue 4x4 is a matte finish ceramic and must be sealed with 511 Porous Plus before grouting. The sealer acts as a grout release, preventing grout haze from bonding permanently to the matte surface. Apply the sealer in a thin, even layer after laying the tile and allow it to cure fully before grouting. Satin and glossy finish ceramics do not require this pre-grout sealing step.

### Can Iconic Blue 4x4 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Iconic Blue 4x4 can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Matte ceramic has a DCOF of 0.74, which provides strong slip resistance in wet floor applications. Seal the tile before grouting as required for all matte ceramics, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". An anti-slip product can be applied after installation to further increase traction in high-exposure wet floor areas.

### Can Iconic Blue 4x4 be used in a pool?

Yes. Iconic Blue 4x4 is suitable for pool waterlines, spa interiors, and fountain installations in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Apply 511 Porous Plus as a pre-grout sealer before grouting, as required for all matte ceramics.

### Can Iconic Blue 4x4 be installed on an exterior floor?

No. Iconic Blue 4x4 is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For outdoor floor surfaces, a material rated for exterior floor use is required.

### What pairs well with Iconic Blue 4x4?

Iconic Blue's deep, grounded tone pairs well with materials that provide either warm contrast or tonal alignment. Warm white and off-white walls keep the surrounding field open and let the blue lead. White or pale grey grout creates a clean grid against the deep blue field, while a closely matched navy grout minimizes joint visibility and allows the tile to read as a continuous surface. Natural wood and light oak introduce warmth across the room. Matte black or aged brass fixtures both complement the blue well — black for a high-contrast, contemporary pairing; aged brass for a warmer, more traditional result.
