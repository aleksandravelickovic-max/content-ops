#### Order Details \+ Installation \[Locked\]

We recommend 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and tile-to-tile variation; because the Alcazar is a specialty shape, the additional cuts required during layout often warrant increasing to 20% or more. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange the layout dry to disperse variation evenly. Wash the tile immediately after cutting to prevent the cutting paste from drying on the surface.

Cotto requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout sealer applied as a grout release, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. Subfloors must be fully cured (minimum 30 days) to prevent efflorescence from depositing on the tile surface.

## About

Each Cotto tile is made from locally-sourced clays. Our handcrafted Cotto tiles are made just outside of historic San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.

The tiles' clay formulation is mixed with water and blended to the ideal consistency, then pressed in a mold to dry for 1–2 weeks until the proper moisture level is achieved for firing.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cotto, derived from the Italian terra cotta or "baked earth," is part of a long lineage of fired clays that date back to the dawn of civilization itself. From Egyptian water jugs to Chinese roof tiles to Roman bricks, terra cotta has been part of the human story for as long as we've had an alphabet. The materials in Cotto tile haven't changed much over the millennia, while the firing process has evolved: from kiln furniture to mold designs to firing method, our handmade and high-fired Cotto is our modern take on an ancient tradition.

Our Cotto is produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, where natural clay is pressed into molds, sun-dried, and fired in kilns by local artisans. The unglazed surface is what gives cotto its character at installation and over time. With proper sealing, cotto handles indoor floors, exterior floors in any climate, and freeze/thaw exposure, and the surface wears like a natural stone product as use deepens its tone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Alcazar specialty shape?

The Alcazar is a geometric specialty shape that interlocks with adjacent pieces to build a continuous patterned field across the installation. The shape resolves across tile edges, so consistent orientation during installation is essential for the pattern to read correctly. A full dry layout before setting is required to confirm how the geometry resolves across the space and to identify where cuts are needed at the perimeter. The interlocking edges also require more cuts than a standard square or rectangular tile, which is why increasing overage beyond the standard 15% is recommended for Alcazar orders.

### What color is Madera cotto?

Madera cotto is a rust-brown unglazed terra cotta with tonal movement between deep earth-brown and warm clay tones across the surface. The color comes entirely from the natural clay body and the kiln-firing process. Individual tiles carry different positions in the rust-brown range, and the distribution across the full installation produces a floor with natural tonal variation rather than uniform color.

### How do I seal Alcazar \+ Madera cotto?

To seal Alcazar \+ Madera, apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting to saturate the surface and reduce absorption. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure, apply a second coat. For interior applications, finish with Fila Matte Wax as a final protective coat. For exterior applications, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax — UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time outdoors. Periodic resealing is required to maintain resistance to moisture and staining.

### Can Alcazar \+ Madera cotto be used outdoors?

Yes. Alcazar \+ Madera cotto is suitable for exterior floors in both freeze/thaw and non-freeze/thaw climates. The high-fired clay body handles freeze/thaw cycling when properly sealed. For exterior applications, apply 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat — wax is not appropriate outdoors.

### How does the Alcazar specialty shape install?

The Alcazar specialty shape interlocks across tile edges, so the pattern resolves across the field only when each piece is oriented consistently. A full dry layout before setting is essential to confirm how the geometry reads across the space and to identify where cuts are needed at the perimeter. The additional cuts required for the interlocking edges are why increasing overage beyond the standard 15–20% recommendation is advisable for Alcazar orders.

### Can Alcazar \+ Madera be mixed with other Madera cotto formats?

Yes. Alcazar \+ Madera can be mixed with other Madera formats in the Zia Cotto collection, including the 8x8 square and other standard formats in the same colorway. Each format is produced from the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent surface character and tone. When mixing specialty shapes with standard formats, plan the full layout during the dry stage — the geometry of the Alcazar shape needs to be confirmed before any tile is set.
