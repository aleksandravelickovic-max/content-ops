## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Canvas White cement tile?

Canvas White is a warm, soft white cement tile — a clean, open neutral with enough warmth in the pigment to prevent the color from reading as stark or clinical white. It sits slightly warmer than pure white without crossing into cream or bone territory, occupying the clean end of the warm-white range. Because the pigment in lighter colorways carries less internal contrast than deeper tones, Canvas White reads as the most consistent of the pale cement colorways, with subtle tonal shifts distributing quietly across the field.

### Does Canvas White cement tile require sealing?

Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax. On a pale surface, prompt sealing and regular resealing are especially important to prevent staining.

### Can Canvas White cement tile be used in a shower?

Yes. Canvas White cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing excellent slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before and after grouting, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". For shower floor use, prompt cleanup of any residue after use helps prevent staining on the pale surface.

### Can Canvas White be used on an exterior floor?

Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Canvas White cement tile is suitable for exterior floors in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. For exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat.

### What pairs well with Canvas White cement tile?

Canvas White's warm soft white functions as a neutral field that holds alongside a wide range of materials. Natural wood and warm timber create an organic, light-filled combination. Deep navy, forest green, and charcoal introduced through textiles, cabinetry, or adjacent walls provide grounding contrast against the pale field. Aged brass and matte black fixtures both pair well — the brass reinforcing the warm undertone, the black providing clean contrast. Soft plaster finishes in greige or sand extend the warm-neutral palette naturally alongside the canvas white field.

### How do I clean Canvas White cement tile?

To clean Canvas White cement tile, use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water for routine maintenance. On a pale surface, prompt blotting of spills is especially important — absorb liquid immediately before it penetrates into the cement. Sweep or vacuum regularly to prevent grit from scratching the surface. Avoid acidic cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach. For heavier staining, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute).
