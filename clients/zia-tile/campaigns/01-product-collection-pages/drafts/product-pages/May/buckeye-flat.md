## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.

Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.

Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does Buckeye Flat look like?

Buckeye is a warm brown extruded clay tile with a flat, unglazed surface and a speckled character that comes from the natural mineral composition of the clay body. The tone sits in the warm, earthy brown family — darker than buff, with the mottled variation of clay fired at high temperature distributing across the field. In a full installation, the speckled surface reads as a complex, layered brown rather than a flat solid color, with more tonal interest visible at close range.

### What is the difference between Field Trip Japan and standard Zia ceramics?

Field Trip Japan tiles are produced using an extrusion process and die-cut mold rather than the pressed and glazed process used for standard matte, satin, and glossy ceramics. The result is a tile with three-dimensional surface texture — either Flat or Furrowed — rather than a smooth, uniform glazed finish. Field Trip Japan tiles are unglazed, so the color and character come from the clay body and firing process. Installation requirements are otherwise the same as the standard ceramics line.

### Does Buckeye Flat require sealing before grouting?

Yes. Buckeye Flat is an unglazed ceramic and requires a pre-grout penetrating sealer applied as a grout release before grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in a thin, even layer after laying the tile and allow it to cure fully before grouting. Refer to the Zia Ceramic Installation Guide for the full installation and sealing sequence.

### Can Buckeye Flat be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Buckeye Flat can be used on shower floors and shower walls. The textured unglazed surface provides good slip resistance in wet conditions. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" to further support alignment and traction. An anti-slip product can be applied after installation to further increase slip resistance in high-exposure wet floor areas.

### Can Buckeye Flat be installed on an exterior floor?

No. Buckeye Flat is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For outdoor floor surfaces, a material rated for exterior floor use is required.

### What pairs well with Buckeye Flat?

Buckeye's warm brown pairs naturally with materials that share its earthy register. Warm white and cream walls keep the surrounding field open and allow the brown's warmth to lead. Natural oak, walnut, and aged wood surfaces complement the clay brown without competing with it. Unlacquered brass and warm bronze fixtures introduce metallic warmth that reinforces the earthy palette. For contrast, soft sage or olive greens placed in adjacent areas create a warm complementary pairing that is natural alongside brown clay tones.
