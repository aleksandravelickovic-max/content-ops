## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile?

Desert Grey is a warm grey cement tile — a mid-range grey with warm undertones that read as sandy and grounded rather than cool or industrial. The warmth in the grey distinguishes Desert Grey from cooler, blue-toned grey tiles and gives it an affinity with earthy, natural materials. Individual tiles vary within the warm grey range, with the variation distributing across the field as a softly complex surface that deepens with use into natural patina.

### Does Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile require sealing?

Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax. Reseal every 1–2 years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure.

### Can Desert Grey 2x8 be used in a shower?

Yes. Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing excellent slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before and after grouting, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". An anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation for additional traction on shower floor surfaces.

### Can Desert Grey 2x8 be used on an exterior floor?

Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile is suitable for exterior floors in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. It is not rated for freeze/thaw cycling. For exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat.

### What pairs well with Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile?

Desert Grey's warm grey sits comfortably alongside both warm and cool adjacent materials, making it a versatile floor tile. Warm white and off-white walls keep the surrounding field light while the warm grey provides grounding underfoot. Natural wood and linen reinforce the desert-toned warmth of the grey. Terracotta and rust accents in adjacent tiles or decor align with the warm undertone of the grey and produce an earthy, cohesive palette. For a cooler, more minimal scheme, matte black and white function cleanly against the mid-grey field.

### What grout color works best with Desert Grey 2x8?

A warm grey or greige grout closely matched to the Desert Grey palette creates the most seamless result, allowing the tile's subtle tonal variation to read across the field without interruption. A slightly lighter warm grey grout introduces a soft grid that defines each tile's edges without creating strong contrast. Avoid cool greys and white grouts, which can conflict with the warm undertone in the cement. As with all cement tiles, grout haze removal should be done promptly — use a grout bag in areas where avoiding direct tile contact is needed.
