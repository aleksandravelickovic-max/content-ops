## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.

Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.

Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Pacific Blue 2x8?

Pacific Blue is a medium blue in satin finish — neither pale nor deeply saturated, sitting in the middle of the blue range with a clean, direct tone. The satin glaze gives the color soft luminosity, producing a surface that catches light without the strong specular highlight of a glossy glaze. In different lighting conditions, the blue reads as warmer or cooler, which is normal for satin glazes. Individual tiles carry slight tonal variation, but the reading across the full installation is even and consistent.

### Does Pacific Blue 2x8 require sealing before grouting?

No. Pacific Blue 2x8 is a satin finish ceramic and does not require pre-grout sealing. The satin glaze provides sufficient surface protection as the tile is installed — apply grout directly without a sealer step. Matte ceramics do require pre-grout sealing as a grout release; satin and glossy ceramics do not. Refer to the Zia Ceramic Installation Guide for the complete installation sequence.

### Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Pacific Blue 2x8 can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Satin ceramic has a DCOF of 0.45, which meets the wet area slip resistance threshold for floor applications. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" to further support alignment and traction. An anti-slip product can be applied after installation to increase slip resistance in high-exposure wet floor areas if desired.

### Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be used in a pool?

Yes. Pacific Blue 2x8 is suitable for pool waterlines, spa interiors, and fountain installations in both saltwater and chlorine environments. No pre-grout sealing is required for satin finish ceramics.

### Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be installed on an exterior floor?

No. Pacific Blue 2x8 is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For outdoor floor surfaces, a material rated for exterior floor use is required.

### What pairs well with Pacific Blue 2x8 ceramic?

Pacific Blue pairs well with warm neutrals that allow the blue to lead without a competing palette. Warm white and off-white walls keep the surroundings open and let the blue read clearly. Natural oak and warm wood tones introduce an earthy contrast that sits naturally alongside blue. Unlacquered brass fixtures add warmth and a period sensibility to the combination. For a cooler, more restrained scheme, grey plaster or white oak with matte black hardware work well against the medium blue field.
