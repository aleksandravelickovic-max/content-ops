## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Brighton Hyannis cement tile?

Brighton Hyannis is a coastal grey cement tile — a medium, blue-toned grey that sits in the register between the grey-blue of an overcast Atlantic sky and the weathered stone tones of coastal architecture. The blue undertone gives Brighton Hyannis a cool, quiet character that distinguishes it from warmer grey tiles, while the mid-range depth keeps it from reading as a dramatic dark tone. The tonal variation of hand-poured cement distributes across the installation as a softly complex grey-blue field that settles and deepens over time.

### Does Brighton Hyannis cement tile require sealing?

Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax. Reseal every 1–2 years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure.

### Can Brighton Hyannis cement tile be used in a shower?

Yes. Brighton Hyannis cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing excellent slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before and after grouting, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". An anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation for additional traction on shower floor surfaces.

### Can Brighton Hyannis cement tile be used on an exterior floor?

Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Brighton Hyannis cement tile is suitable for exterior floors in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. It is not rated for freeze/thaw cycling. For exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat.

### What pairs well with Brighton Hyannis cement tile?

Brighton Hyannis's cool grey-blue holds naturally alongside materials that suit a coastal or restrained minimal palette. Warm white and soft off-white walls maintain a light contrast against the grey-blue field. Natural linen, white oak, and bleached or driftwood tones introduce warmth without competing with the cool grey. Navy, deep forest green, and charcoal accents in textiles or cabinetry extend the coastal palette at a deeper register. Brushed nickel and polished chrome fixtures reinforce the cool undertone; aged brass provides a warmer metallic contrast that softens the palette.

### What grout color works best with Brighton Hyannis cement tile?

A cool grey grout closely matched to the mid-tone of Brighton Hyannis allows the field to read continuously without interruption at the joints. A slightly lighter cool grey grout introduces soft definition without creating strong contrast. Avoid warm greige and brown grouts, which can conflict with the blue undertone in the cement. As with all cement tiles, remove grout haze promptly — use a grout bag in areas requiring clean separation between grout and tile face.
