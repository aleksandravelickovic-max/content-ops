## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Atomic 4x4 cement tile?

Atomic 4x4 is a warm brown cement tile — a grounded, earthy tone that sits in the mid-brown range with warmth and depth characteristic of the deeper cement pigments. The color varies subtly tile to tile in the manner of hand-poured cement, with individual tiles carrying different positions in the warm brown range. Across a full installation, the variation within the palette distributes as a rich, complex brown field with natural tonal movement.

### Does Atomic 4x4 cement tile require sealing?

Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax. Reseal every 1–2 years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure.

### Can Atomic 4x4 cement tile be used in a shower?

Yes. Atomic 4x4 cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing excellent slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before and after grouting, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". An anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation for additional traction on shower floor surfaces.

### Can Atomic 4x4 be used on an exterior floor?

Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Atomic 4x4 cement tile is suitable for exterior floors in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. It is not rated for freeze/thaw cycling. For exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat.

### What pairs well with Atomic 4x4 cement tile?

Atomic's warm brown pairs naturally with the earthy, organic materials that complement its mid-range brown palette. Cream and warm white walls provide a light contrast that allows the brown field to lead. Natural wood tones — oak, walnut, and bamboo — sit within the same earthy register and reinforce the grounded character of the tile. Sage, terracotta, and deep olive accents complement the warm brown without competing. Aged brass fixtures reinforce the warmth of the palette with a metallic note that sits naturally alongside brown cement.

### How do I clean Atomic 4x4 cement tile?

To clean Atomic 4x4 cement tile, use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water for routine maintenance. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit and abrasive debris. Blot spills immediately rather than wiping. Avoid acidic cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach, as these will damage the cement surface. For heavier staining, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Reseal on schedule to maintain stain and moisture resistance.
