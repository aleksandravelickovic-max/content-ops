## About

No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.

Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made

Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.

The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.

Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Desert Bloom zellige?

Desert Bloom zellige is a warm pink with a glaze that shifts subtly in tone depending on light and viewing angle. The color sits in the warm pink range and reads differently under natural light versus artificial light — warmer and more saturated in direct natural light, softer under cooler artificial sources. The variation between tiles, produced by the hand-dipping process and uneven kiln heat, means no two tiles carry exactly the same tone.

### Does Desert Bloom zellige work in a small bathroom?

Yes. Desert Bloom zellige works well in small bathrooms. The glazed surface reflects light, which helps a smaller space feel less enclosed. The warm pink tones read well under the warmer artificial light typical in bathrooms, and the surface variation adds depth without overwhelming the room.

### What colors complement Desert Bloom zellige?

Neutral stone, matte ceramic, and terrazzo in off-white and buff tones sit naturally alongside Desert Bloom. In the surrounding space, unlacquered brass and natural wood complement the warm pink glaze without competing with the surface variation. Desert Bloom also pairs well alongside lighter neutrals and earthy tones within Zia's zellige collection.

### Can Desert Bloom zellige be used in a pool or spa?

Yes. Desert Bloom 4x4 is suitable for pool and spa applications in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required for all wet applications; apply sealer to all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture after grouting is complete. Consult the [Zellige Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/zellige-installation-guide) for specific sealing instructions.

### Does the Desert Bloom colorway vary between production runs?

Yes. Each batch of zellige is produced by hand, and the clay, glaze, and kiln conditions shift from one run to the next. Tiles from separate orders may differ in tone and surface character. If the project requires tiles from multiple orders, ordering the full quantity at once gives the most consistent result across the installation.

### How does Desert Bloom zellige compare to Amber zellige?

Desert Bloom and Amber are both warm-toned zellige colorways, but they sit in different parts of the warm spectrum. Desert Bloom moves into pink territory and reads more as a color statement against surrounding materials. Amber carries honey tones that read as a warm neutral, grounded enough to work alongside a wide range of palettes. The choice between the two depends on whether a warm color-forward surface or a warm neutral is the design intent.
