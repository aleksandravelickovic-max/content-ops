#### Order Details + Installation

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all terrazzo orders to account for cuts, natural variation, and breakage in transit. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange a layout to disperse aggregate distribution evenly. Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint.

Press tiles into place with the palm of your hand only; rubber mallets can create small cracks that grow over time. Subfloors must be fully cured (minimum 30 days).

## About *[Locked]*

Perhaps the most versatile tile Zia produces, with endless colorways, patterns, and shapes, our terrazzo line offers a curated mix of aggregate, color, and format for residential and commercial projects. While generally consistent in appearance, each handmade tile is inherently unique.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with terrazzo, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Terrazzo must be sealed on-site, with both pre-grout sealing and a top coat per the [Terrazzo Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/terrazzo-installation-guide). Re-seal at least every 1 to 2 years, or as often as needed depending on traffic. Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Terrazzo originated in Venice and has shaped architecture and design for over 500 years, from historic Italian interiors to the sunlit modernism of Palm Springs. Equally at home in classic, mid-century modern, and contemporary minimalist interiors, our line is produced using traditional Venetian techniques refined since the 15th century.

Zia's terrazzo tiles are formed within a steel mold, beginning with a mix of natural crushed marble and stone aggregates. These are covered in a layer of liquid cement and hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once set, the surface is ground and polished to reveal the decorative stone aggregates.

Each tile is finished by hand, which produces subtle variation in color and aggregate distribution. The cement matrix and visible aggregate combination is what defines terrazzo.
