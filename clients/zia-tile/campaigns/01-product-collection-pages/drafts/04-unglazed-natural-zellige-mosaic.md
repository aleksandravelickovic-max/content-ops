#### Order Details + Installation

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all unglazed mosaic orders to properly curate your installation. For a more uniform appearance, we recommend 20 to 25%, which gives your installer the option to set aside any sheets with stronger variation. Before installation, separate and mix sheets from multiple boxes to disperse variation evenly. Do not soak unglazed natural zellige mosaic sheets; clean dust with a damp cloth on both front and back of each sheet instead.

Mesh-mounted sheets can be installed as set or removed from the mesh for a larger, more even grout joint.

## About *[Locked]*

Each handcrafted unglazed natural zellige mosaic is wholly unique, and this innate variation defines the line's charm. Mix all boxes in your shipment before beginning your installation, and arrange an ideal layout on a flat surface before setting any sheet to confirm the color balance and variation match your preference.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for this material.

Unglazed mosaic requires pre-grout sealing across the entire layout, including any glazed tiles in mixed-mosaic sheets, per the [Zellige Mosaics Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/zellige-mosaics-installation-guide). Stains on unsealed unglazed zellige can be permanent, so sealing must be completed before the tile is exposed to spills or wear. Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Unglazed natural zellige mosaic is handmade in Morocco using the same traditional techniques as field tile, with the glaze step omitted to preserve the raw clay surface. Once fired, individual tiles are hand-placed on mesh sheets in square or trapezoid formats.

The process begins with raw clay mined from local Moroccan hillsides. Artisans soak the clay, filter it, and hand-press the paste into molds. Each tile is flattened and shaped by hand, then dried in the Moroccan sun before firing.

Tiles are fired in earthen kilns without glaze, then hand-placed on mesh backing. The unglazed surface develops natural patina with use, a defining feature of the material, similar to how natural stone wears with age.
