#### Order Details + Installation

We recommend a 15 to 20% overage on all limestone orders to ensure that you have an adequate selection to properly install your curation. Before installation, lay out all pieces and use a heavy spray to remove production dust; allow to dry thoroughly before setting. Blend pieces from all boxes to disperse variation evenly.

## About *[Locked]*

Each limestone tile is cut from solid blocks quarried near Fez, Morocco, and Bordeaux, France, then finished by stone masons in either honed or bush-hammered form. Variation in color and surface is inherent to natural stone. Blend pieces from all boxes before installation.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Limestone must be sealed on-site, with both pre-grout sealing and a top coat per the [Limestone Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/limestone-installation-guide). Different products are specified for interior and outdoor installations. Re-seal as needed, especially in high-traffic or wet areas. Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made *[Locked]*

Limestone has been used in architecture for thousands of years, from ancient pyramids to Mediterranean villas. Strong by nature and quiet by design, our line carries that legacy into modern spaces.

Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and Bordeaux, France, then finished by stone masons in either honed or bush-hammered form. The honed finish is matte and clean; the bush-hammered finish is textured and more rustic, with a slightly harder surface profile.

Each block is cut into the format dimensions and finished by hand. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. Limestone develops natural patina with use, which is part of how the material ages.
