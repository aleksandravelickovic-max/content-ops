## How It's Made — \[ACCORDION\]

Cement terrazzo tiles are made by hand, one at a time. The process begins with crushed marble and stone aggregates combined with mineral pigments placed into a steel mold. Each component is added separately, by hand, before the cement base is introduced.



Cement terrazzo tiles are not fired. There is no glaze layer on the surface. The tile derives its durability from the combination of finely dehydrated cement and a coarse sand and cement layer beneath. The pigment layer is hydraulically pressed into the surface at the time of production and becomes a permanent part of the tile.



Once the press cycle is complete, tiles cure on drying racks before the surface is ground to expose the embedded aggregates. What is visible in the finished tile is the full-body composition of the material, not a surface treatment applied after the fact. Each Bungalow 12x12 reflects the aggregate placement and conditions of that particular production run, which is why no two tiles are identical.

## Frequently Asked Questions — \[ACCORDION\]

### What is terrazzo tile?

Terrazzo tile is a composite material made by embedding chips of marble, quartz, granite, or glass aggregates into a cement binder. The mixture is hydraulically pressed into molds, cured, and then ground to expose the embedded aggregates and produce a smooth, matte surface. Cement terrazzo tiles are not fired and carry no glaze layer. The visible pattern comes from the full-body composition of the tile, not a surface treatment.

### What are the characteristics of terrazzo tile?

The characteristics of terrazzo tile come directly from its production process. Aggregate size, distribution, and color vary from tile to tile, producing a surface that is consistent in overall tone while retaining natural variation across the installation. Cement terrazzo is matte, durable, and porous, which is why sealing is required before and after grouting.

### Is terrazzo tile natural or manufactured?

Terrazzo tile is a manufactured composite. The aggregates within it, such as marble, quartz, or glass chips, are natural materials, but the tile itself is produced by binding those aggregates in cement using a hydraulic press. The result is a tile that carries the visual character of natural stone while being produced through a controlled, handmade process.

### Does terrazzo tile require sealing?

Yes, cement terrazzo tile requires sealing before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. For interior installations, finish with Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a top coat. For outdoor installations, use 511 Porous Plus only.

### How do I clean terrazzo tile?

To clean terrazzo tile, vacuum or sweep regularly to remove abrasive debris that can scratch the surface. For routine cleaning, use a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier staining, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) is the recommended product. Avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach, as these will damage the surface over time.

### How does the 12x12 format work in an installation?

The 12x12 format produces a continuous, expansive surface with fewer grout joints than smaller formats. This works particularly well on large floors and feature walls where the aggregate composition of the Bungalow tile can read across a wider field. The tradeoff is that tonal balance across the full layout requires careful curation before setting begins, as aggregate distribution is more visible at this scale. A 1/8" grout joint is recommended, and installers should plan for minor shimming and wedge spacer adjustments to maintain straight grout lines.
