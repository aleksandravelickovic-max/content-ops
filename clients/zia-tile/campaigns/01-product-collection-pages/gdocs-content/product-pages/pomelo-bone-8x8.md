## How It's Made

Our Pomelo Bone 8x8 is produced in Vietnam using the encaustic cement tile technique, refined in France and Catalonia in the 1850s. The process is hand-executed from start to finish, and the tile itself is never fired. What gives cement tile its durability and character is the combination of material composition, hydraulic pressure, and time.

  

Production begins with a steel mold fitted with a handmade metal frame that defines the pattern. Artisans fill the frame with natural pigments one section at a time, pouring the beige and white colors that make up the Pomelo Bone composition into their designated areas of the mold. A powdered concrete base is then added on top, and the full assembly is hydraulically pressed at three thousand pounds per square inch. The pressure fuses the pigment layer to the concrete body so that the pattern runs through the surface rather than sitting on top of it.

  

After pressing, each tile is soaked in water to activate the concrete, then cured on drying racks for seven days. No kiln, no firing. The final tile carries the subtle color variation and surface texture inherent to a hand-poured material, and it continues to develop a celebrated patina over time as it responds to use and environment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is encaustic cement tile?

Encaustic cement tile is a hand-poured, hydraulically pressed tile in which the pattern is formed by natural pigments layered into a mold rather than applied to the surface after firing. The technique was refined in France and Catalonia in the 1850s and has been used continuously since then. Because the pigment runs through the surface of the tile, the pattern wears with the floor rather than off it.

### Is cement tile fired like ceramic or terra cotta?

No, cement tile is not fired. Unlike ceramic or terra cotta, which depend on a kiln to develop their density and color, cement tile is hand-poured, hydraulically pressed at three thousand pounds per square inch, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. This distinction shapes both the tile's character and its care requirements, including the need for sealing before and after grouting.

### Can Pomelo Bone 8x8 be used outdoors?

Pomelo Bone 8x8 can be used for covered outdoor applications in non-freeze/thaw climates, including exterior walls and covered patios. It is not suitable for outdoor installations in freeze/thaw climates or for pool and spa applications. For exterior installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of Fila Matte Wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time.

### How do I seal a patterned cement tile?

To seal a patterned cement tile like Pomelo Bone, apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers before grouting until the tile is fully saturated, wiping off any excess before it dries. Allow the sealer to cure fully, then grout in sections no larger than 2'x2' and wipe away excess immediately. After the grout has cured, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus. For interior installations, finish with Fila Matte Wax. For exterior installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax.

### Will Pomelo Bone develop a patina over time?

Yes, Pomelo Bone will develop a patina over time. Cement tile is a living material, and the surface responds to foot traffic, moisture, and the regular application of sealers and cleaning products. The patina deepens the tonal range of the tile and softens the pattern's edges slightly, adding a lived-in quality that is part of what defines traditional cement tile. The underlying pattern does not wear away, because the pigment runs through the full surface rather than sitting on top of it.

### How do I clean Pomelo Bone cement tile?

To clean Pomelo Bone cement tile, use a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Tile and Stone Cleaner with warm water and a soft cloth or mop. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove abrasive debris that can wear down the surface over time. Avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners, which can damage the sealed cement and affect the pattern definition. Reseal every 1–2 years, or more often in moisture-exposed areas.
