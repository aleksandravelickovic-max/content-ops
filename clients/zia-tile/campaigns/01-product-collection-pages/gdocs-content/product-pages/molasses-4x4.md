## How It's Made

Our Molasses 4x4 comes from a region of Japan where ceramic production has been practiced for more than a millennium. The process begins with carefully selected raw clay, refined and formed into the precise 4x4 square format that defines the line. Once formed, tiles dry under controlled conditions before high-temperature firing.

  

Each tile is hand-finished with a glossy brown glaze applied to produce controlled surface movement and depth. The glazed tiles are then fired at high temperatures, and kiln conditions determine the final tone, surface reflection, and subtle variation across each piece. This is where the red undertones emerge within the deeper brown field, producing the layered color shifts that define the Molasses surface.

  

Minor differences in tone, glaze flow, and surface texture develop during firing. These characteristics are most visible on close inspection and resolve into a continuous field once the tiles are set together. The high-firing process also produces the density, low porosity, and long-term durability that distinguish Japanese ceramic tile from other ceramic traditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I clean Molasses ceramic tile?

To clean Molasses ceramic tile, use warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner applied with a soft cloth, sponge, or microfiber mop. Avoid bleach, vinegar, ammonia, and abrasive tools, which can damage the glaze and affect grout joints. For heavier residue, use a cleaner labeled as an acid substitute. Promptly removing grout haze during installation is critical, as hardened residue can be difficult to remove without affecting the surface once cured.

### Is Molasses 4x4 suitable for a bathroom floor?

Molasses 4x4 is suitable for a bathroom floor in low- to moderate-traffic areas such as guest bathrooms and vanity spaces. Slip resistance can be increased by sizing grout joints to a minimum of ⅛" and/or applying an anti-slip treatment after installation. Because gloss has a lower DCOF rating than matte finishes, confirm slip requirements with the installer for primary or heavily used bathrooms.

### Does Molasses 4x4 require sealing?

No, Molasses 4x4 does not require sealing on the tile surface. The high-gloss glaze is non-porous, so grout release sealing is not needed before grouting, and the surface itself does not absorb stains. Grout joints should be sealed after curing to prevent moisture absorption and staining, particularly in showers, pools, and spas where continuous water exposure requires long-term grout protection.

### What grout color works best with Molasses 4x4?

The best grout color for Molasses 4x4 depends on how the installation should read at scale. A dark brown or black grout produces a continuous surface where individual tiles recede and the glossy finish carries the visual field. A charcoal or medium grey grout defines each tile more clearly, emphasizing the 4x4 grid. Lighter grout creates higher contrast and highlights the geometry of the layout, which changes how the surface reads across a backsplash or wall.

### Can Molasses 4x4 be used in a pool or spa?

Yes, Molasses 4x4 can be used in a pool or spa, including saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing the grout joints after installation is recommended to maintain long-term performance under continuous water exposure. For pool installations specifically, epoxy grout is often specified for its durability and chemical resistance.

### Can Molasses 4x4 be mixed with other ceramic colors in the collection?

Molasses 4x4 mixes well with other colors and finishes in Zia's ceramic collection. Each tile is produced using the same Japanese production methods and shares consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the range. Molasses pairs particularly well with warm neutrals, off-whites, and deeper colorways in the collection, where the red-brown undertones sit in deliberate counterpoint against lighter fields or extend a warm palette through a fuller composition.
