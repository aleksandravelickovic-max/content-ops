## How It's Made

Our Burnt Sugar 2x6 carries the legacy of Moroccan craftsmanship in the traditional Bejmat format, produced using the same methods artisans have refined over centuries. The clay is pulled from the hillsides surrounding Fez, Morocco, soaked in water, and filtered for impurities before being worked by hand into rectangular 2x6 molds and set to dry in the Moroccan sun. Once the clay reaches the correct consistency, each tile is hand-shaped into its final rectangular profile.

  

After drying, each tile is hand-dipped in glaze. The Burnt Sugar glaze is where the dark brown tones take form, and because each dip is done by hand, coverage varies from tile to tile. That variation is what produces the layered tonal range visible across a full installation of this brown Moroccan zellige.

  

The final stage is the kiln. Earthen kilns do not distribute heat evenly, and the results of that unevenness are not incidental. The chips, pits, crazing, and shifting tones that emerge from the firing process are what make each Burnt Sugar 2x6 its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions 

### What are the characteristics of Burnt Sugar zellige?

Burnt Sugar zellige is a handcrafted Moroccan tile characterized by deep brown tones with layered tonal shifts across warm umber, maroon, and near-black variations. Each 2x6 Bejmat tile carries natural surface movement, including pits, chips, and irregular edges formed during traditional hand-shaping and glazing. 

### Is Burnt Sugar zellige good for floors?

Yes, Burnt Sugar zellige can be used on floors, though it performs best in low- to moderate-traffic residential areas. It is commonly installed in spaces where the decorative quality of the glaze is prioritized, such as guest bathrooms or interior feature zones. For a kitchen floor or other high-traffic surface, careful installation planning and appropriate sealing are required due to the handmade, glazed character of the material.

### What grout color works best with Burnt Sugar zellige?

A low-contrast grout such as taupe, chocolate, or warm brown is typically recommended for Burnt Sugar zellige to maintain a seamless visual field and emphasize surface variation. A lighter grout can be used if the goal is to highlight the Bejmat geometry and individual tile edges more distinctly. The grout choice directly influences how uniform or textured the final installation reads across the field.

### Do zellige tiles require grout?

Yes, zellige tile installations require grout, even though traditional Moroccan installations sometimes use tight or minimal spacing. A 1/16" to 1/8" grout joint is recommended for durability, edge protection, and long-term stability. Proper grouting preserves the handcrafted edges of the tile while maintaining structural integrity across the installation.

### Can I mix Burnt Sugar 2x6 with other zellige colors?

Yes, you can mix Burnt Sugar 2x6 with other colors in Zia's zellige collection. Each colorway is produced using the same traditional methods, so tiles share consistent surface character across the range. The dark brown palette of Burnt Sugar works particularly well alongside lighter neutrals and warm creams, where the colorway reads as a focal accent against a quieter surrounding field.

### How does the 2x6 Bejmat format compare to the 2x2?

The 2x6 Bejmat format and the 2x2 mosaic produce different installation characters even in the same colorway. The 2x6 carries a directional rectangular profile that supports linear running bond, vertical stack, herringbone, and basketweave patterns, where the geometry of the format shapes the final composition. The 2x2 reads as a denser, more textural field with higher grout joint density and no dominant direction. In Burnt Sugar, the 2x6 format lets the dark brown palette read as more structured, while the 2x2 produces a more atmospheric surface.
