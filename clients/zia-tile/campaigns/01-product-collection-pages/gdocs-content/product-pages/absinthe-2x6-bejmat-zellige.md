## How It's Made — \[ACCORDION\]

The clay that becomes each Absinthe 2x6 is pulled from Moroccan hillsides, soaked in water, filtered for impurities, and worked by hand until it reaches the right consistency for pressing into molds.

Once formed, the tiles dry in the Moroccan sun. Each piece is shaped and flattened by hand into the 2x6 bejmat format before being dipped individually in glaze. The aqua-hued Absinthe glaze settles differently on each tile at this stage. No two dips produce identical results, which is where the tonal range across a full installation develops.

Firing takes place in earthen kilns, where heat moves unevenly through the chamber. That unevenness is the condition that produces the chips, pits, crazing, and tonal shifts that make each Absinthe 2x6 perfectly imperfect and entirely its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions — \[ACCORDION\]

### How does the Absinthe 2x6 differ from the Absinthe 4x4?

The Absinthe 2x6 and the Absinthe 4x4 share the same aqua-hued glaze and are produced using the same traditional Moroccan methods. The difference is in how the format reads across a surface. The 2x6 bejmat introduces a linear, directional quality that the square format does not produce, and lends itself to herringbone, chevron, and stacked layouts. The 4x4 creates a more uniform, gridded field. The choice between the two depends on whether direction and pattern or an even, all-over surface is the design intent.

### What patterns work best with the Absinthe 2x6 format?

The 2x6 format works well in several layout patterns. A stacked horizontal layout emphasizes the linear quality of the format and suits kitchen backsplashes where a clean, directional surface is the goal. A herringbone or chevron arrangement introduces more movement and is commonly used on shower walls and feature surfaces. Laying out the full dry composition before setting any tile is important with a rectangular format, as pattern direction and cut placement at the perimeter need to be established before thinset is involved.

### Can the Absinthe 2x6 be used in a pool or spa?

Yes, our Absinthe zellige 2x6 functions reliably in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required for pool and spa installations. Apply 511 Porous Plus to all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture after grouting is complete. Consult the Zellige Installation Guide for full sealing instructions.

### Which direction should I run the Absinthe 2x6 — horizontal or vertical?

The direction depends on the surface and the intended effect. A horizontal layout emphasizes the width of the surface and works well on backsplashes and lower wall applications. A vertical layout draws the eye upward and suits shower enclosures and floor-to-ceiling installations. Neither direction is incorrect. The key is agreeing on the orientation with your installer before laying out the tile, as direction affects how cuts are planned at the perimeter.

### Does the Absinthe colorway vary between orders?

Yes, the Absinthe colorway can vary between production runs. Each batch of zellige is produced by hand, and the clay, glaze, and kiln conditions shift from one run to the next. Tiles from separate orders may differ in tone and surface character. If the project requires tiles from multiple orders, ordering the full quantity at once gives the most consistent result across the installation.

### Can I mix the Absinthe 2x6 with other formats in the collection?

Yes, the Absinthe 2x6 can be mixed with other zellige formats, including the 4x4, within the same installation. Each format is produced using the same traditional methods and shares consistent material character.
