## How It's Made -- \[ACCORDION\]

Each Molasses 2x8 is produced in Nagoya, Japan, where the ceramic tradition stretches back over a millennium. Raw materials are formed through an extrusion process to achieve the consistent 2x8 format before being dried and fired at high temperatures.

  

The high-firing process is what defines the tile's density and low porosity. The glossy finish develops during the glazing stage, where a controlled application produces the reflective brown surface. The depth of the Molasses tone develops fully in the kiln, where the heat transforms the glaze from raw application to finished color.

  

Minor tonal shifts emerge during firing, giving each tile a measured degree of variation while maintaining a consistent overall appearance.

## Frequently Asked Questions -- \[ACCORDION\] 

### What color is Molasses ceramic tile?

Molasses ceramic tile is a brown, glossy ceramic with a reflective surface that shifts in depth depending on the angle and light source. The brown tone reads consistently across the field at installation scale, with minor tonal variation between tiles as a natural characteristic of the high-fired production process.

### How does Molasses ceramic tile look with natural wood finishes?

Molasses ceramic tile and natural wood finishes share a warm tonal range. The gloss of the ceramic surface contrasts with the matte, textured quality of wood, providing enough distinction between the two materials to keep the pairing from feeling flat.

### How does ceramic tile differ from porcelain tile?

Ceramic tile is fired from natural clay and is well-suited to interior walls and floors, including wet areas and pools. Porcelain tile is fired at higher temperatures using denser clay, resulting in lower water absorption, which makes it suitable for exterior floors and more demanding environments.

### Can ceramic tile be cut on-site?

Yes, ceramic tile can be cut on site using a water-cooled tile saw with a diamond edge blade or a snap tile cutter. If extensive cutting is required, work outdoors or in a well-ventilated area in accordance with California Prop 65 safety guidelines.

### Which direction should I run the Molasses 2x8 -- horizontal or vertical?

A horizontal layout emphasizes the width of the surface and suits backsplashes and lower wall applications. A vertical layout draws the eye upward and works well in shower enclosures and floor-to-ceiling installations. The orientation should be agreed upon with your installer before laying out the tile, as it affects how cuts are planned at the perimeter.

### What is the difference between glossy and matte ceramic tile?

The difference between glossy and matte ceramic tile comes down to surface finish and traction. Glossy ceramic has a DCOF rating of 0.30, while matte ceramic has a DCOF rating of 0.74, making matte the more practical choice for floor applications in wet areas. Matte finish ceramics also require sealing before grouting as a grout release to prevent haze and staining, which glossy finishes do not require.
