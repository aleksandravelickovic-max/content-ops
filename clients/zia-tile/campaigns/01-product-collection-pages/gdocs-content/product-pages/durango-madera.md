  - Before installation, open and mix the contents of each box to distribute tonal and surface variation evenly across the layout.

### Installation and Finishing

  - All installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout in accordance with California Prop 65.
  - For finishing, bring the drywall flush with the finished tile surface, miter exposed edges at 45 degrees, or complete the edge with Schluter strips.

  - Plan the layout carefully. The Durango geometry depends on precise orientation during the dry layout stage to resolve across the full field. Grout joints should be sized to a minimum of 1/8".
  - Always seal Durango + Madera according to the Zia Tile Installation Guide, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as showers. Note that Madera Cotto is not suitable for pools or spas.

## How It's Made

Our Durango + Madera begins with natural Mexican clay drawn from the hills around San Miguel de Allende, a region that has produced terra cotta for centuries. The clay is refined through soaking and filtering to achieve a workable consistency that preserves the mineral composition responsible for the rust-brown tones of the Madera colorway.



Once prepared, the clay is hand-pressed into the Durango specialty mold that defines each tile's sculptural form. The curved geometry is established at the pressing stage and requires careful handling throughout the process. Each tile is then left to dry slowly over one to two weeks, allowing moisture to release gradually and stabilizing the structure before firing.



The tiles are fired in traditional kilns at high temperatures, where heat distribution shifts naturally across the chamber. This temperature variation produces subtle differences in tone and surface density across each Durango + Madera piece, which become part of the finished character rather than an inconsistency to be corrected. The unglazed finish preserves the direct connection between the clay body and the fired surface, and the rust-brown tones deepen further as the surface develops a natural patina with use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Durango + Madera cotto made of?

The Durango + Madera cotto is made from natural Mexican clay sourced near San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. The clay is refined, mixed with water, and hand-pressed into the Durango specialty mold before drying for one to two weeks. Each tile is then kiln-fired, and the heat variation and mineral interaction during firing produce the rust-brown tonal range and surface character of the finished tile. Natural variation in tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness is inherent to the material.

### What paint colors pair well with Durango + Madera?

Durango + Madera pairs with paint colors that either balance or contrast the rust-brown palette. Warm off-whites and cream tones create a neutral surrounding field that lets the sculptural shape lead. Olive, sage, and muted greens align with the earthy tonal range. Deeper colors such as charcoal, navy, or black introduce stronger contrast and shift the overall composition. The choice depends on whether the tile should lead the room or integrate into a broader palette.

### Can Durango + Madera be used in a kitchen?

Yes, Durango + Madera cotto can be used in a kitchen on both floors and walls. Proper installation and sealing are required to manage the natural porosity of unglazed terra cotta. Apply 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting to protect the surface, and maintain a minimum 1/8" grout joint. In areas exposed to grease or moisture, such as behind a range, ongoing sealing prevents absorption and staining.

### How do I clean terra cotta tile?

To clean terra cotta tile, use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water and avoid acidic or abrasive products. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove abrasive debris that can wear the surface over time. Clean with a damp cloth or mop rather than excess water, and dry the surface afterward to prevent residue buildup. Periodic resealing is required to maintain long-term performance.

### How does the Durango specialty shape install?

The Durango specialty shape is a curved, sculptural format that interlocks with adjacent pieces to build an architectural composition across the surface. Because the geometry depends on consistent orientation, a full dry layout before installation is essential to confirm how the shape resolves across the field. The shape also requires more cuts than a standard square or rectangle, which is why increasing overage beyond the standard 15% is recommended for Durango orders.

### Can Durango + Madera be mixed with other cotto colorways?

Durango + Madera can be mixed with other cotto colorways in the Zia collection. Each colorway is produced using the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent surface character across the range. The rust-brown Madera works particularly well alongside lighter cotto colorways such as Blanco or the warmer classic tones, where the tonal contrast creates a layered composition across the sculptural Durango field.
