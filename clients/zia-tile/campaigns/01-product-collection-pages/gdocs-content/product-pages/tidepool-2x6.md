## How It's Made — \[ACCORDION\]

Raw clay sourced from Moroccan hillsides is the starting point for each Tidepool 2x6, and the preparation of that clay is entirely manual. Artisans soak it in water, filter it for impurities, and work it by hand until it reaches the consistency needed for pressing into molds. Each tile is then shaped individually into the 2x6 bejmat format by hand.

Once the tiles have dried in the Moroccan sun, each piece is dipped individually in glaze, and the blue-green tones of the Tidepool colorway take form in that moment of contact between glaze and clay. Because each dip is done by hand and no two applications are identical, the tonal range that develops across a full Tidepool installation is a direct product of that.

The kiln is the final stage, and earthen kilns do not distribute heat evenly, which means the chips, pits, crazing, and tonal shifts that emerge from the firing process are not incidental byproducts but the defining characteristics of the material. Each Tidepool 2x6 carries the evidence of that firing in its surface, which is what makes it perfectly imperfect and entirely its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions — \[ACCORDION\]

### Does the Tidepool colorway vary between orders?

Yes, the Tidepool colorway can vary between production runs. Each batch of zellige is produced by hand, and the clay, glaze, and kiln conditions shift from one run to the next. Tiles from separate orders may differ in tone and surface character. If the project requires tiles from multiple orders, ordering the full quantity at once gives the most consistent result.

### How does Tidepool zellige compare to Aegean zellige?

Tidepool and Aegean share a blue tonal range, but behave differently in an installation. Where Aegean stays within a focused, deep blue, Tidepool moves more freely across the spectrum, picking up greens and seafoam tones alongside the blue, depending on the tile and the light.

### Can the Tidepool 2x6 be mixed with the Tidepool 4x4 in the same installation?

Yes, the Tidepool 2x6 and the Tidepool 4x4 can be mixed within the same installation. Both formats share the same glaze and are produced using the same traditional methods, so they carry consistent material character across the range. Mixing the two formats is a common approach for installations that combine a field tile with a more directional surface.

### Can the Tidepool 2x6 bejmat be used in a pool or spa?

Yes, our Tidepool zellige 2x6 functions reliably in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required for pool and spa installations. Apply 511 Porous Plus to all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture after grouting is complete. Consult the Zellige Installation Guide for full sealing instructions.

### Does zellige tile work on built-in architectural surfaces?

Yes, zellige tile works well on built-in architectural surfaces such as benches, planters, raised platforms, and other structural elements. The glazed surface is durable and easy to maintain, and the inherent variation in color and tone reads particularly well across three-dimensional surfaces where the glaze catches light from multiple angles. For outdoor built-in applications, zellige may only be used in non-freeze/thaw climates. Sealing is required for any surface exposed to moisture.

### How do I choose between different zellige formats for the same project?

Choosing between zellige formats depends on the scale of the surface and the design intent. Larger formats, such as the 4x4, produce a more uniform, gridded field. Rectangular formats, such as the 2x6, bejmat introduce a linear, directional quality that suits herringbone and chevron layouts. For a single project that uses multiple surfaces, mixing formats within the same colorway is a common approach.
