## How It's Made — \[ACCORDION\]

Each Absinthe 4x4 begins in the same place as all traditional zellige. Artisans source the raw clay from local Moroccan hillsides, soak it in water, filter it for impurities, and work it until it reaches a consistency suitable for hand-pressing into molds.



Once formed, the tiles dry in the Moroccan sun. Each piece is then shaped and flattened by hand before being dipped individually in glaze. The aqua-hued surface of the Absinthe develops its depth and tonal range at this stage. No two dips produce identical results.



Firing takes place in earthen kilns, where heat moves unevenly through the chamber. That unevenness is not a flaw in the process. It is the process, the condition that produces the chips, pits, crazing, and shifting tones that make each Absinthe 4x4 entirely its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions — \[ACCORDION\]

### Can Absinthe zellige tile be used in a pool or spa?

Yes, our Absinthe zellige 4x4 functions reliably in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required for pool and spa installations. Apply 511 Porous Plus to all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture after grouting is complete. Consult the Zellige Installation Guide for full sealing instructions.

### Can I mix Absinthe zellige with other colors in the same installation?

Yes, you can mix Absinthe zellige 4x4 with other colors in Zia's zellige collection. Artisans produce each colorway using the same traditional methods, so the tiles share consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the range.

### How do I calculate how much zellige tile I need?

To calculate how much Absinthe zellige tile you need, start with the square footage of the installation area. Add 15-20% overage to account for cuts, layout curation, and tiles outside your preferred tonal range. If you are working toward a more uniform look, increase that overage to 25%. For additional guidance, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

### How do I finish a zellige tile edge?

To finish a zellige tile edge, traditional trim pieces are not used with zellige. The preferred approach is to bring the drywall flush with the finished tile surface, eliminating the need for edge treatment entirely. Where an exposed edge requires finishing, mitered edges or Schluter strips are the correct options.

### What is crazing in zellige tile?

Crazing refers to the fine hairline lines that appear on the glazed surface of zellige tile during the kiln firing process. Along with chips and pits, crazing is an inherent characteristic of handmade Moroccan zellige and does not compromise the structural integrity of the tile. Matching grout color to tile color as closely as possible helps integrate these surface details into the overall composition.

### Can zellige tile be cut on site?

Yes, zellige tile can be cut on site. All cuts must be made with a water-cooled tile saw fitted with a diamond edge blade. This applies to both straight cuts and mitered edges. If extensive cutting is required, the work should be done outdoors or in a well-ventilated area in accordance with California Prop 65 safety guidelines.
