About
Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts. 
The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters. 
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.
Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made 
Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.
Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.
Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

Frequently Asked Questions 
What is crazing, and is it a defect?
Crazing is a network of fine surface cracks in the glaze, a natural result of the glaze and clay body expanding and contracting at slightly different rates during firing and cooling. In Molasses 4x4, occasional crazing is an expected characteristic of the tile, not a manufacturing defect. It does not affect the structural integrity of the tile or its suitability for standard applications.
What is the Molasses 4x4 colorway?
Molasses 4x4 is a deep brown with red undertones. In glossy finish, the high-sheen surface intensifies the depth of the color and responds to light across the installation. The 4x4 square format produces an even, gridded field suited to backsplashes, bath walls, and accent surface applications.
Can Molasses 4x4 be used on a floor?
Glossy ceramic has a DCOF of 0.30, which is below the recommended threshold for wet floor areas. Molasses 4x4 is best suited to walls, backsplashes, and dry floor applications where slip resistance is not a primary concern. For shower floors or any wet floor installation, matte ceramic (DCOF 0.74) or satin ceramic (DCOF 0.45) is the appropriate choice. Molasses 4x4 can be used on shower walls.
Does Molasses 4x4 require sealing?
No. Glossy ceramic does not require pre-grout sealing. The dense glaze surface resists grout haze without a sealer. For wet installations such as shower walls, sealing the grout lines after installation is recommended to protect the grout from moisture and staining.
Can Molasses 4x4 be installed outdoors?
Molasses 4x4 can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates only. It is not suitable for exterior floor installations in any climate. Contact info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170 to discuss your specific location and climate before ordering.
How do I maintain glossy ceramic tile?
Wipe regularly with warm water and pH-neutral cleaner. Glossy surfaces show water spots and fingerprints more readily than matte or satin finishes. Routine wiping after use in wet areas keeps the surface looking clean. For stubborn stains, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute).
