Bone Hex is a warm bone-colored cement tile in the hexagonal format. The hexagonal format produces a continuous honeycomb joint pattern at floor scale; with no single straight-through grout line running the length of the field, the geometry distributes visual texture in every direction rather than reading as horizontal or vertical registers.

About
Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.


Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.


Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.


The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Bone Hex cement tile?
Bone Hex is a warm bone-colored cement tile, positioned at the pale end of the warm-neutral spectrum between warm white and cream. 


Lighter colorways in the cement line carry less internal contrast tile to tile than deeper pigments, so individual tiles in Bone Hex shift subtly within the pale bone range rather than producing the broader tonal movement of a more saturated colorway. Those quiet shifts distribute across the installation as a soft, even field when tiles from all boxes are blended before the dry layout begins.
Does Bone Hex cement tile require sealing?
Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries; the sealer must cure fully before grouting begins. 


On a pale bone colorway, thorough saturation before grouting is critical: grout pigment absorbed into an unsealed surface leaves staining that is difficult to reverse. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the final top coat for interior installations. 


For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax: UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time outdoors. Reseal every one to two years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for the complete sealing sequence.
Can Bone Hex cement tile be used in a shower?
Yes. Bone Hex cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing strong slip resistance in wet conditions. The hexagonal joint pattern has no single straight-through grout line, which distributes surface texture evenly underfoot rather than running in a single direction across shower floors. 


Seal before grouting and apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the top coat after grout has cured for interior wet areas. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" for shower floor applications. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for the full wet-area sealing sequence.
Can Bone Hex cement tile be used on an exterior floor?
Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Bone Hex cement tile is suitable for exterior floors and walls in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. For all exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before grouting and apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat after grout has cured. Do not use Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for outdoor installations. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for the outdoor sealing sequence.
Are there special installation considerations for hexagonal cement tile?
Yes. The honeycomb geometry has no single dominant horizontal or vertical axis, which means the starting point of the field has more bearing on the final result than with rectangular formats. Establish the starting position relative to the room's centerline or primary focal wall before any tiles are set. 


Arrange the full field dry before adhesive is applied, blending tiles from all boxes to distribute tonal variation, and check the geometry against walls, thresholds, and transitions. Use 1/16" spacers consistently throughout to maintain the tight joint spacing that holds the honeycomb pattern at room scale. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for complete installation requirements.
What grout color works best with Bone Hex cement tile?
Grout color has a pronounced effect on Bone Hex because the hexagonal joint pattern is more visually active than a straight rectangular grid. A grout matched closely to the warm bone range allows the honeycomb geometry to recede into the field, reading as surface texture rather than a dominant graphic pattern. 


A contrasting dark grout makes the honeycomb grid itself the defining visual element of the installation: a deliberate design choice, but one to evaluate by previewing grout samples against the tile during the dry layout stage. For matte cement, apply grout with a bag rather than a float and do not spread over the tile face. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for grout selection and application guidance.
