We recommend ordering 15–25% overage so the installation has room for hand-selection, color blending, and the tile breakage inherent to the material. Before laying any piece, unpack every box and mix the tiles on a flat surface to plan the layout and confirm the color balance.


Zellige tile installation is labor-intensive, often likened more to masonry than traditional tiling due to the irregular nature of the handmade material. The traditional method sets pieces edge-to-edge with minimal grout, but irregular shapes mean wedge spacers help keep layouts consistent and protect chamfered edges as the tiles are set. Plan for installations 2–3 times longer than standard ceramic. Every piece needs to be sorted, soaked, arranged, and hand-adjusted for size.

About
No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.


Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

How It's Made
Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.


The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.


Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Aegean zellige?
Aegean zellige is a blue zellige with a glaze that shifts in tone depending on light and angle. The name references the Aegean Sea, and the colorway carries that association in the way the glaze moves between deeper and lighter blue tones across a full installation. Because each tile is hand-dipped individually and fired in kilns with uneven heat distribution, no two tiles carry exactly the same tone.
Does Aegean zellige work in a bathroom?
Yes. Aegean zellige is well-suited to bathroom installations. On shower walls and bathroom walls, the surface variation and tonal shifts are experienced at close range, which is where the handcrafted quality of the glaze is most visible.
Can Aegean zellige be used in a pool or spa?
Aegean zellige can be used in pool and spa applications in commercial and residential settings, where the deep blue glaze is particularly well-suited. Consult the Zellige Installation Guide and reach out to the team at info@ziatile.com with project specifics. Sealing is required for all wet-area applications; apply sealer to all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture after grouting is complete.
Can Aegean zellige be mixed with other colors in the collection?
Yes. Aegean zellige can be mixed with other colors in Zia's zellige collection. Each colorway is produced using the same traditional methods, so tiles share consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the range. Aegean pairs well with lighter neutrals in the collection, where the contrast in tone creates a layered, considered composition without competing colorways.
Does the Aegean colorway vary between orders?
Yes. The Aegean colorway can vary between production runs. Each batch of zellige is produced by hand, and the clay, glaze, and kiln conditions shift from one run to the next. Tiles from separate orders may differ in tone and surface character. Where the project requires tiles from multiple orders, ordering the full quantity at once gives the most consistent result across the installation.
How does Aegean zellige compare to Tidepool zellige?
Aegean and Tidepool are both blue-toned zellige colorways, but they sit in different parts of the color spectrum. Aegean reads as a deep, singular blue, with tonal shifts that stay within the blue range. Tidepool moves across a broader range, straddling blue-green tones. The choice between the two depends on whether a focused, deep blue or a more dynamic, color-shifting surface is the design intent.
