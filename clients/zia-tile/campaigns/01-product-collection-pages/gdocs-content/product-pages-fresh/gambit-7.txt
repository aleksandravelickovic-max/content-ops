Zellige tile installation is labor-intensive, often likened more to masonry than traditional tiling due to the irregular nature of the handmade material. About
No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.


Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

How It's Made
Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.


The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.


Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this most special material. 


To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is Gambit 7 zellige mosaic?
Gambit 7 is a hand-pressed zellige mosaic mounted on 8x8 mesh sheets. The pattern combines three colorways: Portuguese Blue, Tidepool, and Absinthe in a geometric arrangement. Each sheet is approximately 8x8 inches, and the tiles within the mosaic are removable for edge adjustments and custom fitting during installation.
Is Gambit 7 zellige mosaic good for floors?
Yes. Gambit 7 is suitable for floors, including high-traffic areas such as kitchens, primary bathrooms, and entryways. The mosaic format supports slip resistance through increased grout joint density, making it particularly well-suited to shower floors and wet-area installations. For wet floor applications, grout joints should measure at least 1/8" and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation.
Do zellige tiles require grout?
Yes. Grout is necessary between all zellige tiles to protect the edges and ensure long-term durability. Traditional installations can appear closely set, but grout is essential regardless of visual spacing. In Gambit 7, grout also shapes how the three colorways transition across the field. The grout line becomes part of how the mosaic reads as a unified composition.
Can Gambit 7 zellige mosaic be used on exterior walls?
Yes. Gambit 7 can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. The mosaic format performs reliably on vertical surfaces where the glazed finish and variation are protected from direct structural impact. It is not suitable for any exterior floor installation. Sealing is required for all exterior applications.
Can I mix Gambit 7 with other zellige colors?
Yes. Gambit 7 can be mixed with other colorways in Zia's zellige collection. Each colorway is produced using the same traditional methods, so tiles and mosaic sheets share consistent surface character across the range. The three-color Gambit 7 works well as a patterned element alongside neutral whites or softer tones.
How much Gambit 7 do I need?
Calculate the square footage of your installation and add 15 to 20% for cuts and edge adjustments. Because Gambit 7 includes three colorways in a single mosaic pattern, careful curation during layout will require removing and repositioning tiles to balance the tones across the field.
