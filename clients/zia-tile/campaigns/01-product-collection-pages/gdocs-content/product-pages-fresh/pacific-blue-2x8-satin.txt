About
Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts. 
The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters. 
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.
Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made 
Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.
Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.
Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

Frequently Asked Questions 
What color is Pacific Blue 2x8?
Pacific Blue is a medium blue ceramic in a satin finish. The satin glaze produces a surface that catches light without the strong specular highlight of a glossy glaze, giving the color presence at normal viewing distances without high reflectivity. Individual tiles carry slight tonal variation from the high-fire process, and those differences distribute evenly when tiles from all boxes are blended before the dry layout.
Does Pacific Blue 2x8 require sealing before grouting?
No. Pacific Blue 2x8 is a satin finish ceramic and does not require pre-grout sealing. Apply grout directly without a sealer step. Matte ceramics require pre-grout sealing as a grout release. Satin and glossy ceramics do not. See the Ceramic Installation Guide for the complete installation sequence.
Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be used on a shower floor?
Yes, on both shower walls and floors. Satin ceramic has a DCOF of 0.45, just above the 0.42 wet-floor threshold. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch and apply an anti-slip product after installation if additional slip resistance is required for the specific application.
Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be used in a pool?
Yes. Pacific Blue 2x8 is suitable for pool waterlines, spa interiors, and fountain installations in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Satin finish ceramics do not require pre-grout sealing. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch for all pool and spa installations.
Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be installed on an exterior floor?
No. Pacific Blue 2x8 is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For material and application questions, contact us at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
Can Pacific Blue 2x8 be mixed with other Zia ceramics?
Yes. Pacific Blue 2x8 combines with other colorways and finishes within Zia's ceramics collection. All standard ceramics share consistent dimensions within each format size group. When mixing finishes, apply the pre-grout sealer to matte tiles only and allow it to cure fully before grouting the full field. Plan any multi-colorway or mixed-finish layout during the dry stage to confirm color balance before adhesive is applied.
