About
Each Cotto tile is made from locally-sourced clays. Our handcrafted Cotto tiles are made just outside of historic San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.


The tiles' clay formulation is mixed with water and blended to the ideal consistency, then pressed in a mold to dry for 1–2 weeks until the proper moisture level is achieved for firing. At this point, wood ash is added to the surface of our 'Fired Earth' tiles to give them their distinctive surface coloration.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cotto, derived from the Italian terra cotta or "baked earth," is part of a long lineage of fired clays that date back to the dawn of civilization itself. From Egyptian water jugs to Chinese roof tiles to Roman bricks, terra cotta has been part of the human story for as long as we've had an alphabet. The materials in Cotto tile haven't changed much over the millennia, while the firing process has evolved: from kiln furniture to mold designs to firing method, our handmade and high-fired Cotto is our modern take on an ancient tradition.


Our Cotto is produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, where natural clay is pressed into molds, sun-dried, and fired in kilns by local artisans. The unglazed surface is what gives Cotto its character at installation and over time. With proper sealing, Cotto handles indoor floors, exterior floors in any climate, and freeze/thaw exposure, and the surface wears like a natural stone product as use deepens its tone.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Madera Cotto?
Madera Cotto's surface color is set by the natural clay body and the conditions in the kiln during firing. No glaze is applied, which means each tile holds its own position in the colorway's tonal range and that variation is inherent rather than incidental. The full tonal distribution becomes visible once tiles from all boxes are opened and mixed across the layout, which is why that step is essential for Alcazar installations, where the pattern alignment also needs to be confirmed before setting begins.
How do I seal Alcazar + Madera Cotto?
Sealing Alcazar + Madera requires two applications of 511 Porous Plus: one before grouting as a grout release, and one after the grout has fully cured. For interior installations, follow the post-grout 511 coat with Fila Matte Wax as a final protective layer. For exterior installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the top coat instead, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor outdoors over time. 
Is Alcazar + Madera Cotto suitable for shower walls and wet areas?
Yes. Alcazar + Madera is suited to shower wall and floor installations. Unglazed Cotto has a DCOF of 0.98, well above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. Sealing is required for all wet applications. Full sealing instructions are in the Cotto Installation Guide.
Can Alcazar + Madera Cotto be used outdoors?
Yes, in both freeze/thaw and non-freeze/thaw climates. The high-fired clay body tolerates freeze/thaw cycling when sealed before and after grouting, which is what distinguishes Cotto from most other handmade tile materials in cold-climate exterior applications. For all outdoor installations, use 511 Porous Plus as both the pre-grout sealer and the final top coat. Do not use Fila Matte Wax outdoors. 
How does the Alcazar specialty shape install?
Install the Alcazar by establishing the pattern orientation across a full dry layout before any adhesive is applied. Each piece must be oriented to align its interlocking edges with adjacent tiles. Setting without confirming the dry layout first will require backtracking once the pattern fails to resolve at a joint. Mark perimeter cut positions during the dry stage, as the Alcazar geometry produces irregular cut angles at the edges that require planning before the set begins. See the Cotto Installation Guide for full installation steps.
Can Alcazar + Madera be mixed with other Madera Cotto formats?
Yes. All Madera formats in the Zia Cotto collection share the same clay body and kiln process, so surface character and tone are consistent across the range. The 8x8 square and other standard Madera sizes can be combined with Alcazar within the same installation. The Alcazar's interlocking geometry must be resolved first before positioning any adjacent standard-format tiles. Open and mix tiles from all boxes regardless of format to distribute tonal variation evenly.
