About
Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.


Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.


Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.


The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Slate cement tile?
Slate is a deep blue-grey cement tile with tonal variation built into each tile through the hand-pouring and curing process. The deeper pigment in Slate produces broader internal contrast tile to tile compared to lighter colorways in the cement line, and those differences distribute across the installation as pieces from all boxes are blended before the dry layout.
Does Slate cement tile require sealing?
Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. The sealer must cure fully before grouting begins. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time. Reseal every one to two years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure. See the Cement Installation Guide for the complete sealing sequence.
Can Slate cement tile be used in a shower?
Yes. Slate cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing strong slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before grouting and apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the top coat after grout has cured for interior wet areas. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" for shower floor applications. 
Can Slate cement tile be used outdoors?
Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates only. Slate cement tile is suitable for exterior floors and walls in climates that do not experience freeze/thaw conditions. For all exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before grouting and apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat after the grout has cured. Do not use Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for outdoor installations. See the Cement Installation Guide for the outdoor sealing sequence.
Can Slate be mixed with other cement tile colorways?
Yes. Slate can be combined with other colorways across Zia's cement tile line. All cement tiles are produced to consistent dimensions using the same hand-pouring process, so colorways and pattern tiles combine across a single installation without sizing conflicts. When mixing colorways, plan the full layout during the dry stage and blend tiles from all boxes within each colorway before setting begins to ensure even tonal distribution. For installations where the grout color differs significantly from the tile, apply grout with a grout bag rather than a float to prevent the porous cement surface from absorbing contrasting grout and leaving a lasting haze. See the Cement Installation Guide for complete installation requirements.
How do I clean Slate cement tile?
Use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water for routine maintenance, and sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit and abrasive debris. Blot spills immediately rather than wiping, as spreading liquid can expand the stain footprint on a porous surface. 


Acidic cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach, will stain and damage the surface and must be avoided. For heavier staining, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Reseal on schedule to maintain stain and moisture resistance.
