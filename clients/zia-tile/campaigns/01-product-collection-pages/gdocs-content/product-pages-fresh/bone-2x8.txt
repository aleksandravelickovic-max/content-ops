About
Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.


Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.


Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.


The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Bone 2x8 cement tile?
Bone is a warm off-white cement tile with tonal variation built into the hand-poured pigment layer. Light colorways like Bone carry less internal contrast from tile to tile than deeper pigments in the cement line, so the variation is subtle: slight shifts within the off-white range rather than strong color movement across the field. Blending from all boxes before the dry layout distributes that variation evenly.
Does Bone 2x8 cement tile require sealing?
Yes. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off excess before it dries. Skipping this step on a light colorway like Bone significantly increases the risk of grout staining the surface. After the grout has cured, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the interior top coat. For exterior installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor outdoors. Reseal every one to two years based on traffic. See the Cement Installation Guide for the full sequence.
Can Bone 2x8 cement tile be used in a shower?
Yes. Bone 2x8 can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, exceeding the 0.42 wet-floor threshold. Seal before and after grouting, size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch, and apply an anti-slip product after installation for high-traffic shower floors. 
Can Bone 2x8 be used on an exterior floor?
Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates only. The porous cement body absorbs moisture, and freeze/thaw cycling will cause cracking. For all exterior applications, use 511 Porous Plus as both the pre-grout sealer and the final top coat. Do not use Fila Matte Wax outdoors. See the Cement Installation Guide for the outdoor sealing sequence.
How do I clean Bone 2x8 cement tile?
Vacuum or sweep regularly to remove abrasive debris. For routine cleaning, use a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier staining, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Avoid acidic cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach, as these stain and damage the cement surface, and the damage is especially visible on a light colorway. Blot spills promptly rather than wiping, and reseal when water no longer beads on the surface.
Can Bone 2x8 be mixed with other cement tile colorways or formats?
Yes. All cement tiles in Zia's collection share consistent thickness and format dimensions within each size group and are compatible across a single installation. Blend tiles from all boxes regardless of colorway or format during the dry layout, and plan any color transitions before setting begins. When placing Bone adjacent to darker colorways, confirm the visual balance reads correctly during the dry stage before adhesive is applied.
