About
Each Cotto tile is made from locally-sourced clays. Our handcrafted Cotto tiles are made just outside of historic San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.


The tiles' clay formulation is mixed with water and blended to the ideal consistency, then pressed in a mold to dry for 1–2 weeks until the proper moisture level is achieved for firing.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cotto, derived from the Italian terra cotta or "baked earth," is part of a long lineage of fired clays that date back to the dawn of civilization itself. From Egyptian water jugs to Chinese roof tiles to Roman bricks, terra cotta has been part of the human story for as long as we've had an alphabet. 


The materials in Cotto tile haven't changed much over the millennia, while the firing process has evolved: from kiln furniture to mold designs to firing method, our handmade and high-fired Cotto is our modern take on an ancient tradition.


Our Cotto is produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, where natural clay is pressed into molds, sun-dried, and fired in kilns by local artisans. The unglazed surface is what gives Cotto its character at installation and over time. With proper sealing, Cotto handles indoor floors, exterior floors in any climate, and freeze/thaw exposure, and the surface wears like a natural stone product as use deepens its tone.

Frequently Asked Questions
What variation should I expect across Toltec Red Clay Cotto tiles?
Each Toltec Red Clay tile carries tonal and textural variation from the hand-pressing and kiln-firing process: shade shifts within the red clay colorway, subtle differences in surface texture, and minor variation in shape and size are all normal for handmade terra cotta. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange the layout dry to distribute variation evenly across the field.
Is Toltec Red Clay Cotto rated for outdoor and freeze/thaw applications?
Yes. Unglazed Cotto is approved for exterior floor installations in both freeze/thaw and non-freeze/thaw climates. With proper sealing, the unglazed clay body handles freeze/thaw exposure. 


Apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer before grouting, and re-seal as needed depending on traffic and exposure. This rating applies to unglazed Cotto only; glazed Cotto Allende is not rated for freeze/thaw exterior floors.
Is Toltec Red Clay Cotto suitable for shower walls and wet areas?
Yes. Toltec Red Clay is suited to shower wall and floor installations. Unglazed Cotto has a DCOF rating of 0.98, well above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. 


For commercial wet-floor applications, a minimum 1/8" grout joint and an anti-slip product may be applied after installation if further traction is desired. Sealing is required for all wet applications; the full sealing protocol is in the Cotto Installation Guide.
Does Toltec Red Clay Cotto require sealing?
Yes. Cotto requires a two-stage sealing sequence. Before grouting, apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated, then allow it to cure. After the grout cures, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for interior installations or a second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior applications. Acidic cleaners must be avoided as they will damage the tile surface. Full instructions are in the Cotto Installation Guide.
What is the difference between Cotto and Cotto Allende?
Cotto is unglazed terra cotta; Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta produced using the same clay body with a glaze applied before firing. The glaze on Cotto Allende opens up pool, spa, and steam shower applications that unglazed Cotto is not rated for. 


In the other direction, unglazed Cotto is approved for freeze/thaw exterior floors, while Cotto Allende is not. Both are handmade in San Miguel de Allende and require sealing, but the protocols differ: 


Cotto uses 511 Porous Plus as a pre-grout sealer and Fila Matte Wax as an interior top coat; Cotto Allende follows the same sequence for wet areas with an additional post-grout sealing pass.
Can Toltec Red Clay be used in a pool or spa?
No. Unglazed Cotto is not rated for pool or spa applications. The unglazed clay body is not designed for continuous submersion or exposure to chlorinated or saltwater environments. For pool and spa applications across the Zia collection, zellige and Cotto Allende are the appropriate material categories.
Can Toltec Red Clay be mixed with other Cotto colorways or formats?
Yes. Toltec Red Clay can be combined with other Cotto colorways (Fired Earth, Adobe) within the same installation. Each piece shares the same clay body from San Miguel de Allende, so material character and thickness are consistent across the Cotto line. 


The Toltec format is one of several specialty geometric shapes in the collection; when combining with standard square or rectangle formats, confirm that the thickness and grout joint spacing align before setting.
