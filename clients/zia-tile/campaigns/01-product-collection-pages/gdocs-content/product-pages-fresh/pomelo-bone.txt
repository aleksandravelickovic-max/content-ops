About
Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.


Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.


Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.


The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Pomelo pattern and the Bone colorway?
The Pomelo pattern is a geometric design of overlapping circles in a bone and white colorway. The Bone colorway anchors the lighter end of the cement line, with the pattern reading as a crisp two-tone field once the tiles are laid. Tonal variation tile to tile is normal for hand-poured cement and softens further as the surface develops patina with use.
What variation should I expect across Pomelo Bone cement tiles?
Pomelo Bone carries the variation inherent to hand-poured cement tile: subtle shifts in the bone and white tones across pieces, slight irregularities at the edges, and small pits or air pockets in the surface. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange the layout dry to confirm the pattern alignment and color balance reads evenly across the field before setting any piece.
How does the Pomelo pattern align across the installation?
The Pomelo geometric pattern requires attention to orientation and alignment during layout. Before setting any tile, arrange the full field dry on a flat surface to confirm the overlapping circle motif reads continuously across the grout joints. Use 1/16" tile spacers throughout and work with your installer to establish the layout axis before beginning, as shifts in alignment become more visible across larger installations.
Is Pomelo Bone cement tile suitable for shower floors and wet areas?
Yes. Pomelo Bone is suited to shower wall and floor installations. Cement tile has a DCOF rating greater than 1, well above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. 


Sealing is required for all wet applications: apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer before grouting and apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax after grouting for interior installations. The full sealing protocol is in the Cement Installation Guide.
Does Pomelo Bone cement tile require sealing?
Yes. Cement tile requires a two-stage sealing sequence. Before grouting, apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated, then allow it to cure. After the grout cures, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for interior installations or a second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior applications. Acidic cleaners must be avoided at every stage as they will damage the tile surface. Full instructions are in the Cement Installation Guide.
Can Pomelo Bone cement tile be used in a pool or spa?
No. Cement tile is not rated for pool or spa applications. The pigmented cement surface is not designed for continuous submersion or exposure to chlorinated or saltwater environments. For pool and spa applications across the Zia collection, zellige and Cotto Allende are the appropriate material categories.

Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order. Plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order, and confirm the full quantity needed before ordering to avoid the additional lead time and shipping cost of a supplemental order. In-stock sample orders ship from our Los Angeles warehouse within two business days.
