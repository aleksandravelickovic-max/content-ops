About
Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta in 18 colorways (Arroyo, Baja, Albar, Puebla, Dorado, Creosote, Sayulita, Azul, and Cacao among them), across squares, rectangles, hexagons, and the same geometric forms as our unglazed Cotto line. 


The glaze sits over a handmade clay body, so expect tile-to-tile variation in shape, size, and color, plus crazing as a defining characteristic of glazed terra cotta. Lay out the tiles on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any tile.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto Allende, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. 


The full installation procedure is in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made
Cotto Allende is handmade in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, using the same clay base as standard Cotto with a glaze applied before firing. The result is a sealed surface that opens up applications such as pools, spas, and steam showers, which aren't appropriate for unglazed terra cotta.


Natural clay is pressed into molds and sun-dried, then glazed in either gloss or satin finish before high-firing. The glaze develops fine hairline cracks during cooling, known as crazing, which is a normal characteristic of glazed clay tile and reflects how the glaze contracts on the clay body as temperature drops.


Color saturation is more controlled than unglazed Cotto, while the underlying clay body still produces handmade variation across the line. The finish determines how the tile reads in light, with each formulation producing a distinct surface character.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is Cotto Allende?
Cotto Allende is a glazed terra cotta tile made from natural clay in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. The clay is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired with a glaze applied before firing. This process produces a smoother, more sealed surface than unglazed Cotto, with inherent variation in tone, subtle crazing, and speckling that develop as the glaze settles across the clay body during firing.
What color is Valverde 4x4?
Valverde is a deep green Cotto Allende with a glossy finish that shifts in depth and tone from tile to tile. The glossy finish gives the surface more reflectivity than satin terra cotta glazes, and the tonal variation in the green is more apparent as a result. Individual tiles carry different positions in the green range due to the hand-dipping process and uneven kiln heat.
Is Valverde 4x4 suitable for pools and spas?
Yes. Valverde 4x4 Cotto Allende is suitable for both saltwater and chlorine pool and spa installations. Apply 511 Porous Plus Penetrating Sealer before grouting. For pools and spas, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus after grouting is complete as the top coat. Full sealing instructions are in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide.
Is Valverde 4x4 suitable for flooring?
Yes. Valverde 4x4 is suitable for flooring in both residential and commercial settings. Cotto Allende has a DCOF of 0.48, above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. In wet floor applications, slip resistance can be increased by sizing grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch and applying an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete.
How do I clean the Cotto Allende tile?
Cotto Allende tile requires regular sweeping or vacuuming to remove abrasive debris that can scratch the glazed surface. For routine cleaning, use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water and a soft cloth. Avoid acidic or abrasive products, as these can affect the glazed surface and make crazing more visible over time. Re-seal with 511 Porous Plus in wet and high-traffic areas at least annually, or as often as conditions require.
Can Valverde 4x4 be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways?
Yes. Valverde 4x4 can be combined with other colorways in the Cotto Allende collection. Each colorway is produced using the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the line.
