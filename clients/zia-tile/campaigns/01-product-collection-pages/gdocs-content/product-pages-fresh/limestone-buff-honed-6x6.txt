We recommend 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and to give your installer the selection needed to curate the layout. Before installation, lay out all pieces and use a heavy spray to remove production dust; allow them to dry thoroughly before setting. Blend pieces from all boxes to disperse variation evenly. Limestone has a face and an underside (they are not reversible), so install the face side up.
Limestone requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout penetrating sealer, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. For floor installations, subfloors must be fully cured (minimum 30 days) to prevent efflorescence from depositing on the stone surface.

About 
Zia's limestone is sourced from two regions: Honed Limestone quarried near Fez in three colorways (Buff, Monument, and Basilica) across 6x6, 4x12, 12x12, and 12x24 formats; and French Limestone quarried in Bordeaux in 12-inch and 20-inch varying-length tiles plus French Cobblestone. Basilica is also offered in a single bush-hammered 4x12 variant. Variation in color, tone, and natural inclusions across pieces is inherent to the material; lay out the stone on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any piece.
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. The full installation procedure is in the Limestone Installation Guide. How It's Made
Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.
The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.
Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is honed limestone?
Honed limestone is ground smooth with fine abrasives to create a matte finish. The process removes surface irregularities without adding shine, leaving the stone's natural color and markings visible. The matte surface offers better traction than polished stone, making it suitable for floors and walls.
Is honed limestone good for a bathroom floor?
Yes. Honed limestone is suitable for bathroom floors. The matte finish provides better traction in wet areas than polished stone. For wet floor applications, grout joints should measure at least 1/8" and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation. Proper sealing is required before grouting and should be maintained at regular intervals.
Can honed limestone be used outdoors?
Yes. Honed limestone can be used on exterior floors and walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. It is not suitable for outdoor installations where snow or ice are present. For outdoor applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus only — do not use Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax outdoors, as UV exposure causes discoloration.
How do I maintain honed limestone over time?
Re-seal as often as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure. Clean with pH-neutral products only and address spills promptly. Over time, the stone will develop a natural patina, which is an expected characteristic of limestone. Avoid acidic cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach, as these etch the stone permanently.
What grout should I use with honed limestone?
Mapei Flexcolor CQ works well with honed limestone. A neutral grout or a color matched closely to the stone tends to produce the most cohesive result. Contrasting grout colors can stain the surface and may be difficult to remove from natural stone if excess is not wiped away promptly. Grout color is an important design decision — the same stone reads differently depending on whether the grout harmonizes with it or contrasts.
What kind of variation should I expect across honed limestone tiles?
Honed limestone carries natural stone variation in tone, veining, and surface character. The honing process reveals these characteristics rather than concealing them. Color ranges from light cream to deeper buff tones. Veining patterns are unique to each piece. Before installation, open and mix tiles from all boxes to distribute variation evenly throughout the design.
