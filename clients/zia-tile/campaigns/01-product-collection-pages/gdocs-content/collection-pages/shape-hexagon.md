# Hexagon Tile

## Six Sides, Centuries of Use

The hexagon is one of the oldest geometric formats in tile production, appearing in Roman mosaic floors and contemporary interiors across centuries. At Zia, hexagon tile is available in handcrafted zellige, cotto, and cement tile in solid and patterned compositions, and works across floors and walls, from a shower floor to a kitchen backsplash to a full feature wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the advantages of hexagon tiles?

Hexagon tile advantages are structural and visual. The six-sided geometry distributes load across more contact points than square or rectangular formats, making it particularly stable on floors. Grout joints radiate outward from each tile rather than running in long continuous lines, reducing visual grout impact and distributing substrate movement more evenly. On walls, the format adds geometric depth without requiring a decorative pattern.

### How are hexagon tiles installed?

Hexagon tile is installed from a confirmed center point outward, with chalk lines establishing the primary axes before any tile is set. Tiles from multiple boxes must be blended and laid out dry before setting to distribute any variation in color, tone, or thickness evenly across the field. For handmade hexagon formats like zellige, thinset must be applied to both the substrate and the back of each tile, and tiles should be pressed into place by palm rather than mallet to avoid cracking the angled edges.

### Small or large hexagon tiles: how do you choose?

Scale and visual intent are the deciding factors. Small hexagon tile suits compact surfaces like shower floors, niches, and kitchen backsplashes, where the geometry reads as detail work and the grout pattern contributes to the overall composition. Larger hexagon formats suit open floors and expansive walls where the shape itself anchors the design.

### Where can hexagon tiles be used?

Hexagon tile works across a wide range of residential and commercial applications, including shower floors, shower walls, kitchen backsplashes, bathroom floors and walls, fireplace surrounds, and feature walls. For exterior floors, suitability depends on the material and climate. Cotto is approved for exterior floors in both freeze/thaw and non-freeze/thaw climates. Zellige and cement tile are not suitable for exterior floors in freeze/thaw climates. For application-specific questions, contact the Zia team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### Can a hexagon tile be used in a shower?

Yes. Zellige and cotto are both approved for shower walls and shower floors, with sealing required for both materials in wet applications. On hexagon shower floors, slip resistance can be increased by keeping grout joints open to a minimum of 1/8" and applying an anti-slip treatment after installation. ### What grout color should be used with hexagon tile?

The choice depends on whether the grout joint should recede or read as part of the design. Because hexagon grout joints radiate in three directions, a contrasting grout makes the geometry explicit and adds graphic weight to the installation. A closely matched grout minimizes the joint and lets the tile surface and color carry the room. On unglazed materials like cotto, contrasting grout carries a staining risk and must be applied by hand or with a grout bag. On glazed surfaces like zellige, sealing before grouting is required in wet areas, regardless of grout color.

### Can hexagon tiles be used on a kitchen backsplash?

Yes. Zellige, cotto, and cement tile are all approved for interior wall applications, including kitchen backsplashes. Glazed zellige cleans easily with a pH-neutral product, making it well-suited to a surface exposed to heat, grease, and daily cleaning. For unglazed materials like cotto, sealing before grouting is recommended to protect against staining in a kitchen.

### What is the difference between a hexagon tile and a penny tile?

Both are small-format geometric tiles, but they differ in shape and proportion. A hexagon tile has six equal sides and is typically produced in formats ranging from 2 inches to several inches across. Penny tiles are circular or slightly oval, typically produced at around 1 inch in diameter, and arrive on mesh-backed sheets for installation. Both formats suit shower floors, bathroom floors, and detail work, but produce a different visual result.

### How much hexagon tile is needed for a project?

Measure each surface individually. Multiply length by width in feet to get the square footage, then add all surfaces together for the total. Add a minimum of 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and variation. For handmade materials like zellige and cotto, 20% is recommended, with 25% for a more uniform result.
