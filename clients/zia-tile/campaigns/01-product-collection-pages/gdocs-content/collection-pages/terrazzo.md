# Terrazzo

## Aggregates Await

Terrazzo has shaped architectural surfaces for over 500 years, from historic Italian interiors to the sunlit modernism of Palm Springs. With origins in Venetian interiors, where crushed marble and stone aggregates were set into cement to create enduring floors with natural variation, that same principle continues in Zia's collection, where crushed marble, quartz, and stone aggregates are hydraulically pressed into a cement base and ground to reveal a dense, layered surface with visible aggregate distribution across each tile.

Available in 12x12 and 24x24 formats across speckled solid tones and patterned configurations, terrazzo tile suits classic, mid-century modern, and contemporary minimalist interiors. The aggregate scale and distribution varies from tile to tile, producing subtle shifts in tone and density that define the material's character.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do terrazzo tiles come from?

Terrazzo originated in Venice, where stone fragments were embedded into cement surfaces to create durable flooring. The material evolved through Renaissance interiors and later became a defining surface in Art Deco and mid-century modern architecture. Zia's terrazzo collection continues this tradition through a modern manufacturing process that preserves the material's structural identity while refining its finish for contemporary use.

### How are Zia's terrazzo tiles made?

Zia's terrazzo tiles are produced through a controlled molding and pressing process. Crushed natural marble and stone aggregates are set into a steel mold, liquid cement is poured over the aggregate layer, and the tile is hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once cured, the surface is ground to expose the aggregate structure and polished to a smooth matte finish with visible stone composition. This process produces variation at the aggregate level, where no two tiles share identical distribution patterns.

### Does terrazzo tile need special care?

Yes. Terrazzo tiles are porous and must be sealed before and after grouting to protect the cement matrix against moisture and potential staining. For interior installations, 511 Porous Plus Sealer and Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax are recommended. For outdoor spaces, 511 Porous Plus Sealer only.

Using a pH-neutral cleaner and avoiding acidic or abrasive products will help preserve the material and prevent staining. Terrazzo should be re-sealed at least every one to two years, or as often as needed, depending on traffic and wear. For areas exposed to moisture, more frequent re-sealing may be required. Always re-seal in accordance with the sealant manufacturer's instructions.

### How do you clean terrazzo tile?

Cleaning terrazzo tile correctly starts with regular sweeping or vacuuming to prevent surface abrasion from debris. Clean spills immediately to prevent absorption into the cement matrix. For routine cleaning, use warm water with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier buildup, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Do not use bleach, vinegar, or acidic cleaners, as these dull the surface and weaken the sealer over time.

### How do I install terrazzo tile?

Installing terrazzo tile requires controlled handling to preserve alignment, finish consistency, and structural stability. Professional installation is strongly recommended due to the material's weight, rigidity, and sealing requirements.

Before installation:

  - Blend tiles from all boxes to distribute aggregate variation evenly

During installation:

  - Set tiles using a high-quality medium-bed thinset over a properly cured substrate
  - Wet the back of each tile before setting to support adhesion
  - Press tiles into place by hand only. Do not use mechanical impact tools
  - Allow full curing before sealing and grouting

For sealing and grouting:

  - Apply 511 Porous Plus Sealer before grouting
  - Grout in controlled sections using Mapei Flexcolor CQ grout and wipe clean as you go
  - Complete with Fila Matte Wax for interior applications or an additional coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior use
  - Allow all products to fully cure before moving to the next installation step

### Is terrazzo good for a kitchen floor?

Yes, terrazzo is well-suited for a kitchen floor when properly installed and sealed. The material is composed of stone aggregates bound in cement, creating a hard-wearing surface that performs consistently in high-traffic environments. Sealing before and after grouting is required to protect the cement matrix against moisture and staining, which is particularly important in a kitchen environment. Re-seal every one to two years, or more frequently depending on traffic and wear.

### How long does terrazzo last?

Terrazzo is a long-lasting architectural flooring material when correctly installed and maintained. The material is composed of stone aggregates bound in cement, which creates structural stability under regular residential and commercial use. With proper installation and a consistent sealing schedule, terrazzo flooring and wall installations maintain their surface integrity over decades of use. For detailed installation and maintenance requirements, refer to the [Terrazzo Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/terrazzo-installation-guide).

### How do I choose between 12x12 and 24x24 terrazzo?

Choosing between 12x12 and 24x24 terrazzo tile depends on the scale of the surface and the desired visual effect. The 12x12 format suits compact spaces, feature walls, and surfaces where pattern compositions such as Highball, Collins, and Bossa Nova are the focal point of the design. The 24x24 format suits open floors and expansive walls where fewer grout lines produce a cleaner, less interrupted surface.

### What is the difference between terrazzo tile and marble tile?

Terrazzo tile and marble tile are both stone-based surfaces, but differ in composition, process, and appearance. Terrazzo is produced by embedding crushed marble and stone aggregates into a cement matrix, which is then hydraulically pressed and ground to reveal the speckled aggregate surface. Marble is a naturally occurring stone, quarried and cut directly from solid blocks, with variation determined entirely by the stone's geological composition and natural veining.
