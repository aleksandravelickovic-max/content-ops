# Bathroom Tiles

Bathroom tile has to do a lot of work. It sheds water, handles humidity, and needs to hold up underfoot while still setting the tone for a room that often runs small and low on natural light. Color matters here more than in most spaces. A lighter palette opens a compact bath. A darker one gives a larger room weight and presence. At Zia, our bathroom tile options draw from our full range of collections, including handmade zellige and encaustic cement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is DCOF, and why does it matter in a bathroom?

DCOF stands for Dynamic Coefficient of Friction. It measures how much resistance a tile surface provides underfoot when wet. The higher the rating, the more traction the tile provides. In bathrooms, particularly on shower floors and wet room floors, DCOF matters because a low rating increases the risk of slipping on a wet surface. 

### How should one clean bathroom tile?

All Zia tiles should be cleaned with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier stains, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) works well. For more specific guidance, reach out at info@ziatile.com.

### Which tile is best for bathroom installations?

Any tile can work in a bathroom, so start with a vision and find the tile that best meets the design direction. Slip resistance, aesthetics, and scale will help narrow the options. Glazed tiles are easy to wipe clean, but matte cement and stone options perform well too. [Specification sheets are available](https://ziatile.com/pages/tile-specification-sheets) for a closer look at how each tile performs.

### Does bathroom tile need to be sealed?

It depends on the tile. Consult the installation guide for the specific material to find sealer selection and application guidance. Plan to reseal over time. The quickest test is to see if water beads on the surface. If it absorbs, it is time to reseal.

### What is the best tile for a shower?

Shower walls and floors have different requirements. Almost any Zia material works on shower walls: glazed zellige, honed marble, matte ceramic, and glass mosaic. Floors need more grip. Glass mosaic and small-format hexagons work well, as does matte ceramic with a DCOF of 0.42 or higher. Glazed zellige rates DCOF 0.16 and is suited for walls but not shower floors. On any shower floor, hold a minimum ⅛" grout joint and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation. Seal anything porous before and after grouting.

### Is zellige a good choice for a bathroom tile?

Yes. The hand-glazed surface handles water well, and the variation from tile to tile is a characteristic of the material, not a flaw. Zia's zellige is rated for shower walls, backsplashes, and feature walls. With a DCOF of 0.16, it is best kept off shower floors. Seal with 511 Porous Plus after grouting for wet area installations.

### What is the best marble for a bathroom?

The best marble for a bathroom decision comes down to palette and maintenance preference. Carrara has classic white and grey veining and works across most palettes. Grigio Carnico carries cooler grey tones. Nero Marquina is black with bold white veining. Every marble bathroom tile needs 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting, plus periodic resealing. 

### Can I use the same tile on bathroom walls and floors?

Yes, in most cases. The main consideration is slip resistance on the floor. A tile that works beautifully on a wall may not carry a high enough DCOF rating for a wet floor. Check the DCOF rating of the specific tile before specifying it for both surfaces. On any wet floor, hold a minimum ⅛" grout joint and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation.

### What is the difference between glazed and unglazed tile in a bathroom?

Glazed tile has a layer of glass-like coating applied before firing, which makes the surface easier to clean and more resistant to staining. Unglazed tile carries the color of the raw material itself and tends to have more natural traction underfoot. In a bathroom, glazed tile is common on walls and backsplashes. Unglazed or matte tile is often specified for floors where slip resistance is a priority.
