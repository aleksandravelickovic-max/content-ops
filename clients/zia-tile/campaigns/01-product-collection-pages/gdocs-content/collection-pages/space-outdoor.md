# Outdoor Tiles

Great design extends beyond the interior. At Zia, we have collections suited for outdoor use, from cotto and glass mosaic on freeze/thaw patios to zellige on exterior walls and cement tile on non-freeze/thaw floors. Larger format tiles cover patios with character. Glass mosaic lines pools in colorways that hold up to submersion and weather. The same care that goes into an interior installation applies outside, starting with the right material for the climate on outdoor tile.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which tiles are suitable for outdoor use?

Several Zia materials work outdoors, and the right choice depends on the climate. In freeze/thaw climates, Ceramics and Cotto are the approved options. In non-freeze/thaw climates, the range expands to include Cantera, Cement, Cotto Allende, Limestone, Terrazzo, and Zellige. Confirm the climate conditions before specifying any material for an exterior installation.

### Which tiles are best for pool surrounds?

Pool surrounds showcase tile in a major way. From blues that blend to pops of contrasting colors, make the most of any outdoor escape with these prime picks:

  - Glazed Zellige: Handmade Moroccan zellige has been used in fountains and pools in Morocco for centuries. The hand-glazed surface catches and reflects light across the water.
  - Cotto Allende: Glazed terra cotta in a range of colorways, offering a quieter alternative to zellige with its own distinctive character.
  - Ceramics: The most uniform option in the Zia range, available in matte, satin, and glossy finishes for solid or patterned pool surrounds.

### How should one clean outdoor tiles?

Sweep regularly to prevent debris buildup and potential staining. Clean with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier stains, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) works well. For more specific guidance, reach out at info@ziatile.com.

### How should outdoor tiles be installed on concrete?

Preparation varies depending on the surface, so work with an experienced installer to confirm the right approach before starting. On concrete, the installer should use mortar and grout on an even surface that has been fully cured before any tile is set.

### Can I tile outdoor furniture?

Yes, and the results can be striking. We have seen clients create beautiful furniture covered in Zia Tile. You must always use tiles approved for outdoor use in your climate and seal them properly and regularly. For furniture like tables, work with an installer who can miter the edges for a clean finish. Zia cannot guide the tiling process directly, but the [installation guides](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides) for each material are a good starting point.

### What is the best way to weatherproof and seal outdoor tile?

Weatherproofing outdoor tile comes down to the right sealer for the material. Cotto takes 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting, plus a final sealer coat for outdoor use. Cotto Allende uses 511 Porous Plus only. Cantera and limestone seal before and after grouting with an additional outdoor coat. Terrazzo and cement follow the same pre and post grout schedule and reseal every one to two years. Glass mosaic requires no sealing. Do not use Fila Matte Wax on any outdoor installation, as UV exposure discolors it. For material-specific protocols, see Zia's [Installation Guides](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides).

### How long does outdoor tile last?

Properly installed outdoor tile holds up across decades of weather exposure when the installation and sealing schedule are maintained. Cotto and glass mosaic hold their finish through freeze/thaw cycles, UV, and pool chemistry. Cantera and limestone develop a surface patina over time while the structural character of the stone remains intact. Lifespan depends more on installation than on the tile itself: subfloor cure, thinset selection, sealing schedule, and grout joint width all matter.

### Can indoor tile be installed outdoors?

Some indoor tiles work outdoors, and some do not. Before specifying any tile for an outdoor installation, confirm it is rated for the climate, moisture, and traffic of the space. For material-specific guidance, contact Zia at info@ziatile.com.

### How do I calculate how much tile I need for a patio?

To calculate, start by measuring the full area of the patio. For simple layouts, multiply length by width. If the space is irregular, break it into smaller sections and add them together. Once you have the total square footage, add 15 to 20 percent extra to cover cuts and waste. For handmade materials like cotto and cement tile, it's safer to stay closer to 20 percent.
