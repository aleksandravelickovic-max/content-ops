## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are grey tiles suitable for modern interiors?

Yes, grey tiles are suitable for modern interiors because they provide a neutral base that supports clean architectural lines and restrained material palettes. In zellige, grey introduces dynamic tones, so each tile reflects light differently due to chips, pits, and crazing. Grey also pairs directly with steel, concrete, and natural wood, making it an effective choice across kitchen backsplashes, shower walls, and fireplace surrounds.

### What patterns work best with rectangular grey tiles?

Rectangular grey tiles perform best in layouts that emphasize direction and proportion. A horizontal stacked layout reinforces long sightlines and suits modern cabinetry runs. A vertical stack increases perceived ceiling height on shower walls. A running bond introduces movement while maintaining a familiar structure. For more pattern-driven applications, a herringbone layout highlights the tonal shifts within the line, allowing the inherent variation to distribute across angles.

### What is the best grout color for grey tile?

The best grout color depends on whether the goal is contrast or continuity. A closely matched grey grout minimizes visual interruption and allows the tile surface to read as a continuous plane. A lighter grout will emphasize the layout pattern and highlight each tile's edges, while a darker grout frames each piece more distinctly. Because zellige includes tonal variation within each tile, test grout samples against multiple tiles from different boxes to confirm the final effect before installation.

### What is the difference between grey cantera, grey zellige, and grey marble tile at Zia?

Grey Cantera is a volcanic Mexican stone with visible aggregate and mineral inclusions. Grey zellige is hand-pressed and hand-glazed in Morocco, with chips, pits, and crazing as inherent characteristics of each tile. Grey marble, including Grigio Carnico, is a quarried Italian stone with soft veining and a honed surface.

### How much grey tile is needed for a project?

Measure each surface individually, multiply length by width in feet, and add all surfaces together for the total square footage. Add a minimum of 15% overage to account for cuts and variation.
