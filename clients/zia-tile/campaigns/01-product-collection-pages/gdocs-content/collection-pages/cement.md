# Cement

## Pour. Press. Patina.

Zia's encaustic cement tiles are handmade using techniques established by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s. Each tile is individually hand-poured into a steel mold with high-quality natural mineral pigments applied one layer at a time, then hydraulically pressed to form a dense cement body with a pigmented surface layer that becomes part of the tile itself. Unlike ceramic tile, cement tile is never fired — it air dries. This is a defining characteristic of the material and the reason it develops a patina over time.

The naturally matte surface and subtle color variation reflect the handmade nature of each tile. Tonal shifts and surface movement are inherent to the production process and should be expected across an order. Available in squares, hexagons, and mosaic layouts across a wide range of solid colors and geometric patterns, Zia's cement tile collection suits floors and walls in residential and commercial spaces alike.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do Zia's cement tiles come from?

Cement tiles trace their roots to 19th-century Europe and North Africa, where they were known for their durability and intricate patterned designs. Zia's cement tiles are handmade in Vietnam using traditional techniques that combine cement, marble powder, and natural mineral pigments to create timeless designs within the encaustic tile tradition.

### How are Zia cement tiles made?

Each tile is made by hand using a steel mold, natural pigments, and a hydraulic press. Unlike ceramic tile, cement tile is formed rather than fired, which is a defining characteristic of hydraulic tile production. The pattern is created by applying individual pigments into a handmade metal frame, one layer at a time. Each tile is then hydraulically pressed at approximately 3,000 PSI, soaked in water to initiate curing, and left to dry on racks for seven days. This process requires experienced artisans due to the precision of pigment placement and pressing.

### Does cement tile need special care?

Yes, cement tile is porous and must be sealed before and after grouting. This protects the surface and helps maintain long-term performance in areas such as a kitchen or other high-use environments.

Maintenance requirements include:

  - Resealing every one to two years, or more frequently in wet or high-traffic areas
  - Using a matte wax sealer indoors for added surface protection
  - Cleaning only with pH-neutral products

These steps preserve the surface as the tile ages and patinas without altering its natural matte character.

### How do you clean cement tile?

Cleaning cement tile correctly starts with regular dry cleaning using a soft broom, dust mop, or vacuum to prevent buildup without affecting the surface. For wet cleaning, use a pH-neutral tile cleaner only. Avoid acidic or abrasive products, as these can stain or dull the surface. For deeper cleaning, a heavy-duty pH-neutral solution intended for natural stone — such as Miracle Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) — can be used safely on sealed surfaces.

### How do I install cement tile?

Installing cement tile requires a professional installer experienced with porous materials.

Before installation:

  - Blend tiles from all boxes to balance natural variation
  - Confirm the layout and dry-fit design where applicable

During installation:

  - Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset
  - Lightly wet the back of each tile before setting
  - Press tiles into place by hand. Do not use mechanical impact
  - Keep the surfaces level throughout installation

Sealing and grouting:

  - Seal the tiles before grouting using 511 Porous Plus Sealer
  - Grout in small sections using Mapei Flexcolor CQ and wipe clean as you go
  - If applying a dark or contrasting grout color, use a grout bag to avoid staining
  - Apply a topcoat wax sealer such as Fila Matte Wax indoors as a final protective coat
  - For exterior installations, apply an additional coat of 511 Porous Plus instead of wax

### How much cement tile do I need for a project?

Calculating the amount of cement tile needed starts with the total square footage of the surface. Measure the length and width of each surface in feet, multiply them together, and add all sections for walls or multiple floors. From that total, add a minimum of 15% overage to account for cuts, pattern alignment, and any tiles set aside during installation. For patterned cement tile, where layout and design matching require additional material, plan for 20 to 25% overage before placing the order. ### What is encaustic cement tile?

Encaustic cement tile is a handmade hydraulic-pressed tile produced by applying natural mineral pigments into a steel mold, one layer at a time, pressing the tile at high pressure, and air-curing it rather than firing it in a kiln. The pigmented surface layer becomes part of the tile body itself during pressing, which is what gives encaustic tile its color depth and matte finish.

### Are cement tiles good for floors?

Yes, cement tiles perform well on interior floors, or outdoor areas in non-freeze/thaw climates, when properly installed, sealed, and maintained. Their density and thickness make them suitable for residential and commercial traffic. Cement tile is frequently used in entryways, living areas, retail environments, and kitchen floors where pattern and durability are both priorities. The matte finish provides natural slip resistance.

Over time, the surface develops a patina that reflects use, which is a defining characteristic of the material. Sealing before and after installation is essential to protect the surface and ensure long-term performance.

### How does cement tile compare to ceramic?

Cement tile and ceramic tile suit different design intentions and should be chosen based on the priorities of the project. Cement tile is structurally denser, formed from cement, marble powder, and natural mineral pigments, then air-cured rather than fired in a kiln. Each hydraulic tile is hand-poured and pigment-placed before pressing, producing subtle tonal shifts and surface variation that are inherent to the process. The surface develops a patina over time that reflects use.

Ceramic tile is kiln-fired and engineered for uniformity and consistent color, suiting applications where a precise, repeatable surface is the goal. The right choice depends on how much variation, pattern depth, and material character the installation calls for.
