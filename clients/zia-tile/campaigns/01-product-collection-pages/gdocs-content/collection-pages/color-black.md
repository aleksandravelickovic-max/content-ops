# Black Tile

Black is not a fixed tone in tile. It can read as soft charcoal or near-true ebony depending on the material, the finish, and how the surface reflects light. At Zia, black appears in cotto, zellige, cement, terrazzo, Roman mosaic, and marble. Each material produces a distinct surface, and that difference determines where the tile works best.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between black marble, black zellige, and Cotto Oscura at Zia?

Black marble at Zia is Nero Marquina, a quarried stone with white veining, and no two slabs read identically. Black zellige is hand-pressed and hand-glazed in Morocco, with chips, pits, and crazing as inherent characteristics of each tile and shifts in tone across the surface. Cotto Oscura is unglazed Mexican terra cotta, high in variation, carrying the deep earth tones of the fired clay itself.

### What causes variation in black tiles across a single installation?

Variation in black tile comes from the material and the making process. In black zellige tile, each tile is hand-glazed and fired in an earthen kiln where heat is uneven, producing shifts in tone and surface from tile to tile. In Cotto Oscura, the clay composition and firing determine the final color, and no two tiles come out identical. In black marble tile, geological movement and mineral deposits create veining that varies from piece to piece. Blending tiles from multiple boxes before installation distributes the variation evenly across the surface.

### What is the best grout color for black tile?

The best grout color for black tile depends on how much contrast is desired. A closely matched black or dark charcoal grout keeps the surface reading as one continuous field. A mid-tone grey softens the grid and introduces subtle definition. White grout creates the highest contrast and makes the layout graphic and visible.

### Why does black tile pair differently with warm versus cool whites?

Black tile takes on the undertone of what surrounds it. Next to a warm white, it reads softer and slightly warmer by contrast. Paired with a cool white, the same black can appear sharper and more neutral. The shift comes from contrast, not a change in the tile itself, which is why reviewing samples together in the space before finalizing is recommended.
