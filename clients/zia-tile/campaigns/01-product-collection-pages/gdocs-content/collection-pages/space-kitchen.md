## Frequently Asked Questions

### What tiles are best for kitchen floors?

Kitchens are the central gathering place for many homes, deserving of great design. From warm and welcoming Cotto to elevated yet playful patterned marble, this is an opportunity to match the mood of the room with the right foundation. Here are some ideas:

  - Cement: Available in 64 solid colorways and plenty of patterns, this is an invitation to mix and match.
  - Terrazzo: The speckled finish brings colorful notes to the project whether the design leans Mid-Century Modern, classic Italian, or contemporary.
  - Cotto: Rustic and warm, clay-based terra cotta adds character to any kitchen floor.
  - Limestone: Understated and natural, limestone brings a soft, timeless look to the room.
  - Marble: Solid or patterned, marble is a classic choice for a kitchen that calls for something more elevated.

### What tiles make the best kitchen backsplashes?

Glazed surfaces like zellige, Cotto Allende, and satin or glossy ceramics are easiest to wipe clean. Any Zia tile can work for a backsplash with the right care, so let the design direction drive the choice.

  - Zellige: Handcrafted Moroccan tile that brings texture and inherent variation to a kitchen.
  - Ceramics: The most uniform option in the Zia range, easy to work into solid or patterned layouts.
  - Marble: From veiny solids to bold patterns, smaller-format marble tiles bring something unexpected to a kitchen backsplash.
  - Patterned cement: Bold and balanced, patterned cement lets the room's personality come through.

### Should backsplash tiles be installed before or after kitchen cabinets?

For best results, always install tiles after the cabinets and countertops are in place. Working in this order lets the installer make precise cuts against finished edges, reduces waste, and ensures the tile sits flush against the surfaces it will meet. Installing tile before cabinets are set can lead to uneven reveals and unnecessary cuts that add material cost to the project.

### Is marble a good choice for a kitchen?

Marble tile can be used in kitchens with an understanding of how it performs. Carrara, Grigio Carnico, Nero Marquina, and Giallo Reale are commonly used on backsplashes and islands. Marble will etch when exposed to acidic substances such as lemon, vinegar, or wine, and the change is permanent. Seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting. On floors, honed finishes show etching less visibly than polished surfaces.

### Is zellige a good choice for a kitchen?

Zellige is well-suited to kitchen backsplashes. Once sealed, the surface handles water and grease from daily use. Each tile shows variation in tone along with chips, pits, and crazing, which are inherent characteristics of the material. Seal with 511 Porous Plus after grouting for kitchen installations.

### How do you keep kitchen tiles looking new?

Routine cleaning and sealing determine long-term performance. Wipe down backsplashes regularly to prevent grease from settling into grout joints. Use a pH-neutral cleaner and avoid acidic products such as bleach or vinegar, which can damage glaze and natural stone. Materials including cotto, cement, marble, and zellige require sealing, with resealing typically needed every one to two years, depending on use.

### Can I mix tile materials in a kitchen?

Yes, different materials are often used across kitchen surfaces. A zellige backsplash paired with a marble or cement floor is a common approach, allowing each surface to perform according to its use. You can establish one primary material, then select supporting materials that align in tone and scale. Reviewing samples together in the space helps confirm how they read under your lighting.

### How do I calculate how much tile I need for a kitchen backsplash?

Measure the total square footage of the area to be tiled, including the space between the countertop and upper cabinets. Subtract openings such as windows. Add 15–20% overage to account for cuts and waste. For handmade materials like zellige and cement tile, 20% is recommended, with up to 25% for a more uniform layout.
