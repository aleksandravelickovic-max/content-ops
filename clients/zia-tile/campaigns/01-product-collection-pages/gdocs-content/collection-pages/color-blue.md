# Blue Tile

Blue tile shifts more with light than most colors. In zellige, the glaze reflects across the surface, showing variation in tone along with chips, pits, and crazing. In ceramics, the color reads more even and controlled. Cement and terrazzo hold blue in a matte field. The same blue can read differently depending on the material, which is why it works across backsplashes, bathroom walls, pools, and floors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best blue tile for a pool?

The best blue tile for a pool depends on the material's suitability for submerged and wet environments. Pool applications require proper sealing and materials rated for that use. Some tiles are better suited to pool walls, while others can be used on surrounding surfaces with appropriate slip resistance. Always confirm the specific product's pool and spa rating before installation. For guidance, contact our team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### How do you clean and maintain blue tile?

Cleaning and maintaining blue tile requires routine sweeping and pH-neutral products. Sweep or vacuum high-traffic areas before grit scratches the surface. Clean with a pH-neutral cleaner, and avoid bleach, vinegar, and acidic products, which etch glazes and damage stone. Seal blue cement tile and zellige with 511 Porous Plus on the schedule specified in Zia's Installation Guides for each material. Glass mosaic pool tile requires no sealing.

### What causes variation and movement in blue glazed tiles?

Variation in blue glazed tiles comes from the glazing and firing process. In handmade materials like zellige, glaze is applied by hand and fired in kilns with uneven heat, which creates shifts in tone from tile to tile. The surface also shows chips, pits, and crazing. This combination produces the movement visible across the installation.

### Is blue tile a good choice for a bathroom?

Yes, blue tile works well in a bathroom across several materials. Blue zellige and blue ceramic tiles suit shower walls. On shower floors, specify a matte or textured finish and hold a minimum 1/8" grout joint for slip resistance. Apply an anti-slip treatment after installation for additional traction. Seal zellige and cement tile every one to two years, and more often in shower environments.

### How much variation should be expected in glazed blue tiles?

The level of variation in glazed blue tile depends on the material. Zellige carries the most, with shifts in tone and surface from tile to tile, along with chips, pits, and crazing as inherent characteristics. Ceramic glazes are applied more uniformly, producing a more consistent color across the surface. Cement tile sits between the two, with subtle color shifts from the hand-pouring process but without the surface variation of zellige.

### How much blue tile is needed for a project?

Start by measuring each surface individually. Multiply the length by the width in feet to get the square footage, then add all surfaces together for the total. Add a minimum of 15% overage to account for cuts and variation. For zellige, 15 to 20% overage is recommended, with 25% for a more uniform result.
