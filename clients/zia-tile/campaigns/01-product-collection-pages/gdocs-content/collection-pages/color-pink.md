# Pink Tile

Pink tile at Zia is not one material or one shade. The color ranges from soft to warm tones across zellige, ceramic, cement, and terrazzo, with tones drawn from mineral pigments and hand-dipped glaze. Each material produces a different surface, from the inherent variation of zellige, including chips, pits, and crazing, to the more controlled, matte finish of cement and terrazzo. This range allows for different applications, from a pink tile backsplash to bathroom walls and floors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the best grout color for pink tile?

The best grout color for pink tile depends on how much contrast you want to introduce. A warm white, cream, or soft blush grout will keep the surface continuous and let the tile read as a single field of color. A darker grout, such as charcoal or black, outlines each tile and brings the pattern forward.

### What color cabinets work with a pink tile backsplash?

A pink tile backsplash works with a range of cabinet finishes, but the pairing changes the tone of the room. White cabinets keep the space open and allow the tile to carry warmth. Natural wood introduces another layer of variation and keeps the palette grounded. 

### How do you clean and maintain pink tile?

To clean and maintain pink tile, routine sweeping and pH-neutral products cover most of it. Sweep or vacuum high-traffic areas before grit scratches the surface. Avoid bleach, vinegar, and acidic products, which etch glazes and damage stone. Seal pink cement tile, pink terrazzo, and zellige every one to two years, and more often in shower, pool, and spa environments.

  

### Can you use pink tile in a shower?

Yes, when the material is suited for wet areas. Glazed pink zellige tile works on shower walls but rates DCOF 0.16, so it is best kept off shower floors. Pink cement tile handles shower walls and floors with proper sealing and a minimum ⅛" grout joint. Pink Dahlia matte ceramic at DCOF 0.74 suits both shower walls and floors. On shower floors, apply an anti-slip treatment after installation for additional traction and seal every one to two years.

### Why does the same pink tile look different at different times of day?

Pink tile appears different throughout the day because lighting shifts from cool to warm, which changes how the color reads on the surface. This is more noticeable in materials like zellige, where each tile shows variation in tone along with chips, pits, and crazing.
