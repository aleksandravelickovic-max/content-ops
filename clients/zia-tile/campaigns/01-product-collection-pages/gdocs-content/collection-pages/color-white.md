# White Tile

White is not a single color in tile. It is a range of tones across stone, clay, and glaze. At Zia, white runs through every collection: handmade zellige in a light neutral glaze that shifts with the light, honed marble quarried by skilled stone masons, matte and satin ceramics produced in Japan with over 1,000 years of ceramic tradition, and cement tile hand-pressed and cured on drying racks. Each material produces a range of whites, and each suits endless applications, from a white zellige tile backsplash in a warm kitchen to Carrara white marble tile flooring throughout a light-filled primary bath to a white tile shower finished in hand-glazed ceramics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

  

### What causes the variation in white zellige tile?

Variation in white zellige is produced at every stage of the making process. Raw clay is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, hand-shaped, and hand-dipped in glaze before firing in an earthen kiln. The uneven heat of the kiln produces shifts in tone and surface from tile to tile. Chips, pits, and crazing are part of this process and are characteristics of every authentic zellige surface.

### How do you maintain white tile?

You maintain white tile through routine cleaning with the right products, though the specific routine depends on the material. Sweep or vacuum daily in high-traffic areas to carefully remove abrasive debris. Clean with a pH-neutral cleaner and avoid acidic cleaners and abrasive pads, which etch glazes and can permanently damage natural stone. Seal all tile according to the specific tile's installation guide, repeating the process per the schedule specified for each material. Wet spaces may require more frequent resealing, as in shower, pool, and spa environments, where continuous moisture accelerates wear.

### Is white subway tile the only option for a shower?

White subway tile is one of several preferred options for a shower. White hexagon tile, 4x4 zellige, marble, and terra cotta all work well on shower walls and floors. To avoid an excessively slippery shower floor, specify a matte or textured finish and consider wider grout lines to improve slip resistance. An anti-slip treatment after installation adds further traction, though we recommend a test on your tile prior to full application. On shower walls, glazed zellige, hand-glazed ceramics, and honed Carrara each produce a distinct surface: zellige for inherent variation and dynamic tones, ceramic for a consistent field, and marble for natural veining.

### What grout color works best with white hexagon tile?

The best grout color for white hexagon tile depends on the desired design direction. A closely matched warm white or off-white produces a seamless surface that foregrounds the tile itself. A contrasting dark grout, in black or charcoal, renders the hexagonal geometry graphic and emphasizes the grid. A mid-tone grey softens the pattern and conceals wear over time, a practical choice for high-traffic floors. 

### Can I mix different white tiles in the same room?

Yes, you can mix different white tiles in the same room. Commit to one dominant white and let the other materials support it. A honed Carrara floor pairs cleanly with a handmade zellige backsplash, where the stone provides a neutral base and the zellige introduces inherent variation at eye level. Order samples of each material and evaluate them in the room's light before finalizing.

Should a bathroom floor be lighter or darker than the walls?

A bathroom floor can be lighter or darker than the walls; it is important to consider the overall design. Gone are the days of rigid rules; the "right" choice depends on the size and light of the room, as well as the other elements and materials present. A darker floor grounds the space and gives a larger bath visual weight. A lighter floor expands a small bath and opens the space, particularly when paired with a white wall tile in a similar tone. In a compact space, consider matching white tile flooring to the wall tone or keep it a shade lighter. In larger baths, a darker floor can provide an interesting contrast against a white wall.
