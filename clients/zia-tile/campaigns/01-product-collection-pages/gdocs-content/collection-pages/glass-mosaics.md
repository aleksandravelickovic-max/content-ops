# Glass Mosaics

## Solid and Patterned Glass Mosaic Tile in Vibrant Colorways

Zia Tile's glass mosaics collection is the brand's first foray into glass tile, developed as its most sustainability-forward line to date. The collection was developed in Northern Spain, where production decisions are directly tied to energy use and material efficiency.

Crafted in a facility that relies primarily on renewable wind and solar energy and fired in electric kilns, these glass mosaics are made from 98% recycled glass. The resulting 12x12 grids feature vibrant colors and repeatable designs across solid and patterned formats. Zia's design team created seven intentional patterns, drawing inspiration from artworks, fashion textiles, and locations like Los Angeles's own Union Station.

## Frequently Asked Questions 

### Where do Zia's glass mosaic tiles come from?

Zia's glass mosaics are produced in Northern Spain, in a facility that prioritizes renewable energy use and circular resource management, including the recycling of water and production materials. Each glass mosaic tile is made from 98% recycled glass, built on reuse rather than raw extraction. This production environment allows consistency in color calibration and sheet formation while maintaining strict environmental standards.

### How are Zia glass mosaics made?

Zia's glass mosaic tiles begin with discarded glass, allowing for tiles composed of 98% recycled materials. The glass is melted and individually formed in a mold, then fired in electric kilns powered primarily by renewable energy. As each tile is cooled and removed from its mold, tiles are hand-placed on 12x12 grids in patterns and solid sheets. The seven intentional patterns draw inspiration from artworks, fashion textiles, and locations like Los Angeles's Union Station.

### Do glass mosaics need special care?

No. Glass mosaics do not require special care due to their non-porous surface, which makes them naturally resistant to staining and simplifies long-term maintenance. While the material itself is low-maintenance, installation quality is essential for long-term performance. Zia recommends working with detail-oriented installers experienced in glass mosaic tile systems to ensure proper substrate preparation, adhesive application, and sheet alignment.

### How do you clean glass mosaic tiles?

Cleaning glass mosaic tiles correctly starts with regular sweeping or vacuuming to remove dust and prevent abrasion. For routine cleaning, wipe floors or walls with warm water or a soft cloth and clean spills promptly to maintain surface clarity.

For deeper cleaning, a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner is recommended. For more intensive buildup, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) can be used safely on the surface. The non-porous surface is compatible with a wide range of cleaning solutions and performs well in both residential and high-traffic commercial environments.

### How do I install glass mosaic sheets?

Installing glass mosaic sheets requires precision handling and experience with small-format mesh-mounted tile systems. Zia recommends working with professional installers to ensure proper alignment, adhesion, and finish consistency.

Before installation:

  - Blend sheets from all boxes to distribute tonal and pattern variation evenly
  - Ensure substrate is clean, flat, and properly prepared

During installation:

  - Remove the paper backing on each sheet carefully
  - Apply epoxy thinset evenly in one direction to prevent air pockets
  - Press sheets firmly into place using hand pressure for full contact

After placement:

  - Allow the thinset to fully cure before grouting
  - Use epoxy grout in small sections and wipe excess grout immediately to avoid grout haze

Glass mosaics do not require sealing at any stage of installation or after completion. For full technical specifications and detailed installation steps, refer to the [Glass Mosaics Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/glass-mosaics-installation-guide).

### How are glass mosaic tiles used?

Glass mosaic tiles are used across residential and commercial applications where moisture resistance, cleanability, and visual clarity are priorities. Each mosaic arrives pre-arranged on 12x12 mesh-mounted sheets, allowing for precise spacing and alignment across walls and floors while maintaining consistency in both solids and patterns. Common applications include kitchen backsplashes, shower walls and floors, pools and spas, exterior facades, and feature walls. The small format allows the material to adapt to curved surfaces, niches, and transitions without compromising alignment.

### Can glass mosaic tile be used on a shower floor?

Yes, glass mosaic tile can be used on a shower floor. The small format of mosaic sheets increases the number of grout joints across the surface, which improves traction compared to larger-format tiles. Slip resistance can be further increased by increasing grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛" and/or applying an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete. Zia recommends working with a professional installer experienced in glass mosaic systems for wet floor applications.

### Can glass mosaic tiles be mixed with other tile materials?

Yes, glass mosaic tiles can be mixed with other tile materials in the same installation. Combining glass mosaic sheets with a larger-format field tile is a common approach in both residential and commercial projects, particularly where the mosaic defines a specific zone such as a shower floor, niche, or backsplash accent. When mixing materials, confirm that grout joint widths are consistent across the transition, that both materials are laid out dry before setting to confirm the junction reads as intentional, and that varying thicknesses across collections are accounted for.

### Can glass mosaic tiles be installed over existing tile?

Yes, glass mosaic tiles can be installed over existing tile, provided the substrate is structurally sound, fully adhered, and level to within the required tolerance. Any loose, cracked, or hollow tiles in the existing surface must be addressed before installation begins. The substrate must be clean and free of wax, sealers, or residue that would prevent proper adhesive bonding.
