# Limestone

## Sediment, Refined

Zia's limestone tiles are quarried near Fez, Morocco, and Bordeaux, France — two regions where distinct geological conditions produce stone with consistently stable neutral tones and natural mineral variation. Each tile is cut from solid stone blocks and finished by local stonemasons in honed or bush-hammered finishes, preserving the stone's natural structure while refining its surface for installation.

Available in formats from large 12x24 tiles to smaller cobblestones, limestone tiles offer warm beiges and cooler grays suited to a wide range of residential and commercial applications. Over time, limestone develops a natural patina — an expected characteristic of the material.

### How are Zia limestone tiles made?

Each tile is cut from solid blocks of natural limestone and finished by local stonemasons. Honed tiles are gently smoothed for a clean, matte surface. Bush-hammered tiles are surface-textured using a percussion process, creating a tactile, textured finish with increased traction. No two pieces are alike — variation in tone, texture, fossil marking, and surface movement is a defining characteristic of the natural material.

### Does limestone tile need special care?

Limestone is a porous material and must be sealed before and after grouting to protect its surface against moisture and staining. For interior installations, seal before grouting with a penetrating sealer, then apply a matte protective wax as a final protective coat. For exterior installations, apply an additional coat of penetrating sealer instead of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time. Reseal periodically, with extra attention to high-traffic or moisture-prone areas. Use only pH-neutral cleaners to prevent surface etching or dulling. Over time, limestone develops a natural patina, an expected characteristic of the material.

### How to clean limestone tile

Remove grit and surface debris regularly with sweeping or vacuuming. For routine cleaning, use warm water and a pH-neutral stone cleaner. Avoid acidic or abrasive products, as these will damage the surface. For deeper cleaning, use a non-acidic heavy-duty cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) applied according to manufacturer guidance.

### How do I install limestone tile?

Limestone tile requires a professional installer experienced in natural stone.

Before installation:

  - Blend tiles from all boxes to evenly distribute natural variation
  - Remove dust residue with a heavy spray of water and allow tiles to fully dry before setting

During installation:

  - Wet the back of each tile before applying medium-bed thinset mortar
  - Press tiles into place with a rubber mallet to ensure full contact
  - Set tiles with consistent pressure to maintain a level surface

Sealing and grouting:

  - Apply a penetrating sealer before grouting and allow full curing
  - Grout in small 2x2 sections using Mapei Flexcolor CQ and wipe excess grout immediately
  - For interior installations, apply a matte protective wax as a final protective coat
  - For exterior installations, apply an additional coat of penetrating sealer instead of wax

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, mortar, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. For full technical specifications, refer to the [Limestone Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/limestone-installation-guide). ### What is honed limestone tile?

Honed limestone tile is finished to a smooth, matte surface through a controlled grinding process. The result is a flat, non-reflective finish that reveals the stone's natural tonal variation and subtle fossil markings. Honed limestone has a DCOF rating of 0.50, making it suitable for floor applications. It is commonly specified for limestone flooring and bathroom walls where a matte, understated surface is preferred.

### What is bush-hammered limestone?

Bush-hammered limestone is finished using a surface percussion process that creates a textured, tactile surface with increased traction compared to honed limestone. It has a DCOF rating of 0.56, making it well-suited for high-contact areas and exterior applications. The textured finish is also a practical choice for bathroom floors where slip resistance is a priority.

### Is limestone suitable for kitchen floors?

Limestone performs well in a kitchen when properly sealed and maintained. It is durable under daily foot traffic and compatible with underfloor radiant heating systems. Sealing before and after grouting is required to protect the surface against moisture and staining. The surface develops a natural patina over time, an expected characteristic of the material. Reseal as needed based on traffic and wear.

### How much limestone tile is needed for a project?

Start with the total square footage of the surface. Measure the length and width of each surface in feet and multiply them together; for walls or multi-surface floors, add all sections. Plan for a minimum of 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts, natural stone variation, and any tiles set aside during installation. ### How long does limestone tile last?

Limestone tile is a long-lasting natural stone material when properly installed and maintained. Its dense sedimentary composition provides structural stability under regular residential and commercial use. Sealing before and after grouting protects the surface from moisture absorption and staining, and regular cleaning with pH-neutral products preserves the finish and prevents etching over time.
