# Limestone

## Strong by Nature, Quiet by Design

Zia celebrates the timelessness of limestone through two distinct lines, quarried and cut in France and Morocco. Sourced from regions near Bordeaux and Fez, the stone reflects the geological conditions of each origin, where layers of sediment have been compressed under pressure over millions of years. The natural stone is cut into tiles while the neutral color palette lets the stone settle naturally into its surroundings.

Limestone is cut from solid blocks of stone and finished by local stone masons, who refine each surface to preserve its natural structure. Offered in a range of formats, from large 12x24 tiles to smaller cobblestones, limestone tiles are available in honed or bush-hammered finishes, allowing depth and versatility in a wide range of spaces.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do limestone tiles come from?

Zia's limestone tiles are quarried near Fez, Morocco, and Bordeaux, France, where distinct geological conditions produce stone with stable neutral tones and natural mineral variation. These regions are known for their rich heritage of stonecraft and architectural stone use, with limestone forming a foundational material in historic construction across Europe and North Africa.

Limestone has been used in architecture for thousands of years, from Egyptian pyramids to Mediterranean villas. This collection continues that material lineage in contemporary applications where a natural stone tile surface is preferred for its durability and visual restraint.

### How are Zia limestone tiles made?

Each stone tile is cut from solid blocks of natural limestone and finished by local stone masons. Honed tiles are gently smoothed for a clean, matte surface, while the bush-hammered finish uses a bush hammer to create a textured, tactile surface.

No two pieces are alike — each tile is shaped by time and technique. Variation in tone and texture, fossil marking, and surface movement is a defining characteristic of the natural material rather than a defect in production.

### Does limestone tile need special care?

Yes, limestone is a porous material and must be sealed before and after grouting to protect its surface against moisture and staining. Apply 511 Porous Plus as a penetrating sealer before grouting and apply Fila Matte Wax as a final protective coat for interior applications. For exterior applications, apply an additional coat of 511 Porous Plus instead of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time. Reseal periodically, with extra attention to high-traffic or moisture-prone areas. Use only pH-neutral cleaners to prevent surface etching or dulling. Over time, limestone develops a natural patina, which is an expected characteristic of the material.

### How do you clean limestone tile?

Cleaning limestone tile correctly starts with regular sweeping or vacuuming to remove grit and surface debris. For routine cleaning, use warm water and a pH-neutral stone cleaner. Avoid acidic or abrasive products, as these will damage the surface. For deeper cleaning, use a non-acidic heavy-duty cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) applied according to manufacturer guidance.

### How do I install limestone tile?

Installing limestone tile requires a professional tile installer experienced in natural stone.

Before installation:

  - Blend tiles from all boxes to distribute natural variation evenly
  - Remove dust residue using a heavy spray of water
  - Allow tiles to fully dry before setting

During installation:

  - Wet the back of each tile before applying medium-bed thinset mortar
  - Press stone tiles into place with a rubber mallet to ensure full contact
  - Allow the thinset to fully cure before sealing and grouting

For sealing and grouting:

  - Apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting and allow it to fully cure
  - Grout in small sections using Mapei Flexcolor CQ and wipe clean excess grout immediately
  - Apply Fila Matte Wax as a final protective coat for interior applications
  - For exterior installations, apply an additional coat of 511 Porous Plus instead of wax

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, mortar, sealants, and grout in accordance with California Prop 65. For full technical specifications, refer to the [Limestone Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/limestone-installation-guide).

### What is honed limestone tile?

Honed limestone tile is a natural stone tile finished to a smooth, matte surface through a controlled grinding process. The result is a flat, non-reflective finish that reveals the stone's natural tonal variation and subtle fossil markings. Honed limestone has a DCOF rating of 0.50, making it suitable for floor applications. It is commonly specified for limestone flooring and bathroom walls where an understated, naturally consistent, matte surface is preferred.

### What is bush-hammered limestone?

Bush-hammered limestone is finished using a surface percussion process that creates a textured, tactile surface with increased traction compared to honed limestone. It has a DCOF rating of 0.56, making it well-suited for high-contact areas and exterior applications in non-freeze/thaw climates. The textured finish also makes it a practical choice for limestone bathroom floors where slip resistance is a priority.

### Is limestone good for a kitchen floor?

Yes, limestone performs well in a kitchen when properly sealed and maintained. It is durable under daily foot traffic and compatible with underfloor radiant heating systems. Sealing before and after grouting is required to protect the surface against moisture and staining in a kitchen environment, with regular resealing over time. The surface develops a natural patina over time, which is an expected characteristic of the material.

### How much limestone tile do I need for a project?

To determine how much limestone tile a project requires, measure the length and width of each surface in feet and multiply them to get the square footage. Add together all surfaces — floors, walls, or both — to reach the total. From that figure, add a minimum of 15% overage to cover cuts, natural stone variation, and any pieces set aside during installation. For complex layouts, plan for up to 25% overage. ### How long does limestone tile last?

Limestone tile is a long-lasting natural stone material when properly installed and maintained. Its durability comes from its dense sedimentary composition, which provides structural stability under regular residential and commercial use. Sealing before and after grouting protects the surface from moisture absorption and staining, along with regular resealing as needed. Regular cleaning with pH-neutral products preserves the surface finish and prevents etching over time.
