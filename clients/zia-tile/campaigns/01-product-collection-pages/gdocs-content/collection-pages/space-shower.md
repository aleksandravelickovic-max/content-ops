## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the best tiles for showers and wet spaces?

From slip resistance to maintenance, there is a strong option for every direction. Here are some to consider:

  - Zellige: Handmade Moroccan zellige has a naturally uneven surface that provides traction even on a glazed finish, making it a solid choice for shower walls.
  - Cement: Available across an extensive range of colors and patterns, cement tile brings a matte finish that works across any design direction.
  - Terrazzo: Whether the mood is ancient Italian or contemporary, an all-over terrazzo shower brings depth through each natural aggregate in the surface.
  - Stone: Marble, cantera, and limestone each bring a quiet, natural character to a shower. Seal before and after grouting and reseal every one to two years.

### Can installers tile over old tile in a shower without removing it?

This depends on the specific project and is best assessed by an experienced installer before any work begins. For heavier materials like cement and marble, tiling over existing tile adds weight that the underlying surface may not be prepared to support. If the existing tiles are clean, level, and structurally sound, and the adhesive is appropriate for the material, it may be possible. An installer can best advise on whether the substrate is ready.

### Does shower tile need to be sealed?

It depends on the material. Consult the [installation guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides) for the specific tile to find sealer selection and application guidance. Plan to reseal over time. The quickest test is to see if water beads on the surface. If it absorbs, it is time to reseal.

### What is the best tile for a shower wall?

Shower walls allow for a wider range of materials since slip resistance is not required. At Zia, zellige, marble, limestone, Cotto Allende, and Roman mosaic can be used on shower walls. Zellige introduces inherent variation in tone along with chips, pits, and crazing. Marble and limestone bring natural veining, while Roman mosaic creates texture through smaller-format pieces.

### Is natural stone safe in a shower?

Natural stone can be used in showers when properly sealed. Marble, limestone, cantera, and Roman mosaic are suitable for both shower walls and floors with sealer applied before and after grouting. Use a pH-neutral cleaner and avoid acidic or abrasive products, which can damage the surface. On shower floors, maintain a minimum 1/8" grout joint for slip resistance.

### How do I create a shower niche with tile?

A shower niche is typically finished with the same tile as the wall or with a contrasting material. The niche must be fully waterproofed before tile installation. Exposed edges should be finished with mitered edges or Schluter strips, as zellige and other materials do not use traditional trim pieces.

### Should a shower floor match the walls?

Either approach works. Using the same tile on the floor and walls creates a more continuous look, especially in smaller showers. Switching materials or formats on the floor adds definition and helps separate the two surfaces.

### What grout color works best in a shower?

Grout changes how the tile reads once installed. A close match keeps things looking more continuous. A contrasting grout makes the layout more visible. A mid-tone softens the lines without fully blending them out.

### How much tile do I need for a shower?

Start by measuring each surface separately. Multiply height by width for each wall, subtract any openings, then add the floor area. Once the total is established, add 15–20% extra to cover cuts and waste. For handmade materials like zellige, staying closer to 20% is recommended, with up to 25% for a more consistent layout.
