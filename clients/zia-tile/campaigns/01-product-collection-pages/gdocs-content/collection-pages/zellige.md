# Zellige

## Hands. Heat. History.

Handcrafted in Fez, Morocco, Zia's zellige is created by artisans using traditional techniques refined over 800 years and passed down through generations. Each tile is formed from natural clay, hand-shaped, glazed, and kiln-fired, resulting in inherent variation in color, shape, and thickness. Subtle shifts across the surface, along with chips, pits, and crazing, reflect the material's deep history and hand-formed nature.

A staple in Moroccan architecture and now embraced worldwide, zellige is coveted for its rich surface texture and inviting sheen. Traditional zellige is installed in mosaics featuring decorative geometric and floral shapes, while single tile installations work across diverse architectural styles. The surface is defined by dynamic glazes that respond to light, creating movement across the installation. Suitable for kitchen backsplashes, bathroom walls, shower walls and floors, pools, spas, and fireplace surrounds, Zia's zellige collection holds over 200 tiles to explore across colors, shapes, and mesh-backed mosaics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do zellige tiles come from?

Zia's zellige tile is a handcrafted Moroccan art form with more than 800 years of continuous production behind it. The collection is handmade in Morocco, where the material originated and has been refined through generations of skilled artisans. Historically installed in mosaics featuring decorative geometric and floral shapes within elaborately designed architectural spaces, this material remains central to Moroccan design. Zia's curated selection includes a wide range of colors, shapes, and formats used across interior surfaces worldwide, and outdoors in non-freeze/thaw climates.

### How are Zia Moroccan zellige tiles made?

Zia's zellige tiles are handmade by Moroccan artisans with generations of experience, using raw materials sourced locally. The process begins with natural clay, which is mined, soaked, and filtered to remove impurities. The refined clay is formed into molds and left to dry in the sun. Once dried, each tile is shaped and finished by hand, creating variation in size and edges. Tiles are then hand-dipped in glaze and fired in an earthen kiln.

Heat distribution within the kiln varies, producing shifts in tone and surface across each tile. As a result, zellige tile develops inherent variation in color, shape, and thickness, along with surface characteristics such as chips, pits, and crazing. These are expected features of the material and contribute to the final installation. Each zellige tile is an expression of the artisan's work — perfectly imperfect.

### Do zellige tiles need special care?

All but one colorway in the zellige line is glazed, and these tiles require skilled installers and attention to detail for the best installation results. Proper sealing and grout selection are essential, especially in moisture-exposed environments.

For unglazed natural zellige:

  - Use 511 Porous Plus Penetrating Sealer before grouting
  - Grout with Mapei Flexcolor CQ
  - After the grout cures, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax in thin layers until the tiles are fully coated, then wipe any excess before drying
  - Avoid over-sealing, as excessive application can dry unevenly and leave an undesirable film on the surface

For glazed zellige:

  - Use Mapei Ultracolor Plus FA grout
  - Apply 511 Porous Plus Sealer after grouting when installing zellige tile in showers, pools, or other wet areas

### How do you clean zellige tiles?

Cleaning zellige tile correctly starts with regular sweeping or vacuuming to prevent surface abrasion and prompt attention to spills. Floors and walls can be wiped or mopped with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner.

For deeper cleaning and potential stains, use a non-acidic cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Avoid bleach, vinegar, or acid-based products, as they can dull the glazed surface and affect long-term performance. With proper cleaning, zellige tile maintains its surface quality without intensive maintenance.

### How do I install zellige tile?

Working with a professional installer experienced with zellige tile is strongly recommended. Proper installation ensures variation is distributed correctly and performance requirements are met.

Here is an overview of the installation process:

**Standard Zellige Installation:**

  - Begin by blending tiles from every box to evenly distribute natural variation
  - Soak all glazed zellige tiles in room-temperature water for 1-2 minutes
  - Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset
  - Press tiles into place by hand. Do not use mallets
  - Let the thinset fully cure before proceeding with grout
  - Grout in small sections using Mapei Ultracolor Plus FA and wipe the surface clean as you go
  - Finish with a topcoat sealer such as 511 Porous Plus when installing in wet areas or where additional protection is required

**Unglazed Natural Zellige:**

  - Begin by blending tiles from every box to evenly distribute natural variation
  - Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset and wet the back of each tile before setting
  - Press tiles into place by hand — do not use a mallet
  - Let the thinset cure, then seal tiles before grouting using 511 Porous Plus Sealer
  - Grout in small sections using Mapei Flexcolor CQ and wipe clean as you go
  - Finish with a protective topcoat sealer or wax, depending on the application

**Zellige Mosaics:**

  - Blend tile sheets from multiple boxes before installation
  - Soak all glazed zellige tiles for 1-2 minutes. Do not soak sheets that include Unglazed Natural tiles
  - Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset
  - Press tiles into place by hand
  - Let the thinset fully cure
  - If mosaics include Unglazed Natural zellige, seal the tiles before grouting with 511 Porous Plus Sealer
  - Grout in small sections using Mapei Ultracolor Plus FA for glazed tiles and Mapei Flexcolor CQ for any mosaics where Unglazed Natural is present
  - For mosaics that include Unglazed Natural zellige, finish with a protective topcoat sealer like Fila Matte Wax
  - For glazed mosaic tiles, apply 511 Porous Plus Sealer in wet areas or where additional protection is needed

### What is a zellige tile backsplash?

A zellige tile backsplash is a surface made from handmade Moroccan clay tiles, traditionally formed, glazed, and fired in small batches. Each tile is cut by hand, resulting in uneven edges, slight shifts in thickness, and surface variation that reflects light differently across the wall. This creates a backsplash with visible movement rather than a flat, uniform finish, which is central to what defines the signature zellige aesthetic in residential and commercial interiors.

### Can zellige be used on floors?

Yes, Zia's zellige can be used on floors in select applications across interior and exterior spaces in non-freeze/thaw climates. Zellige is commonly specified for bathrooms and feature floors where variation and texture are part of the design. For shower floor installations, a minimum 1/8" grout joint is recommended to support performance and slip resistance. Zellige tile is not suitable for exterior floors in freeze/thaw climates. The handmade surface and inherent variation are part of the finished floor's character and should be distributed evenly during installation.

### How much zellige tile do I need for a project?

Start by measuring the length and width of the surface in feet and multiplying to get the total area in square footage. For a floor, measure the room's borders. For a wall, measure each section individually and add them together. Once the total square footage is established, add overage to account for cuts and variation. A 15 to 20% overage is recommended for all zellige orders. For a more uniform look, increasing overage to 25% is recommended. ### Can zellige tiles be mixed across colorways?

Yes, zellige tiles can be mixed across colorways. Because each tile is handmade using the same traditional process, zellige from different colorways shares the same format dimensions, thickness range, and surface characteristics, which makes mixed installations achievable. Tile thickness does vary across sizes, so varying shapes should be accounted for when mixing. Blending colorways works particularly well in mosaic compositions where tonal variation across the field is part of the design intent.
