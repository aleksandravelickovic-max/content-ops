Frequently Asked Questions 
Where do terrazzo tiles come from?
A staple in design for over 500 years, terrazzo originated in Venice and has swept surfaces across the globe ever since. Historically, stone fragments were embedded into cement surfaces to create durable flooring. The material evolved through Renaissance interiors and later became a defining surface in Art Deco and mid-century modern architecture. Zia’s authentic terrazzo tiles bring old-world craftsmanship to modern spaces in a carefully designed collection spanning speckled solids and bold patterns for contemporary use.
How are Zia's terrazzo tiles made?
Our terrazzo tiles are produced through a controlled molding and pressing process. Crushed natural marble and stone aggregates are set into a steel mold, liquid cement is poured over the aggregate layer, and the tile is hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once set, the surface is ground to expose the aggregate distribution, then polished to a smooth finish. This result is variation at the aggregate level, so no two tiles ever share identical distribution patterns.
Does terrazzo tile need special care?
Yes. While terrazzo’s aggregates suggest a low-maintenance finish, terrazzo tiles are porous and require specific care. Terrazzo must be sealed before and after grouting to protect the material from moisture and potential staining. For interior installations, we recommend 511 Porous Plus Sealer and Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax. For outdoor spaces, we recommend 511 Porous Plus Sealer only.
Using a pH-neutral cleaner and avoiding acidic or abrasive products will help preserve the material and prevent staining. Terrazzo should be re-sealed at least every one to two years, or as often as needed, depending on traffic and wear. For areas exposed to moisture, more frequent re-sealing may be required. Always re-seal in accordance with the sealant manufacturer's instructions.
How do you clean terrazzo tile?
Cleaning terrazzo tile correctly starts with regular sweeping or vacuuming to prevent surface abrasion from debris. Clean spills immediately to prevent absorption or staining. For routine cleaning, use warm water with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier buildup, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Do not use bleach, vinegar, or acidic cleaners, as these dull the surface over time.
How do I install terrazzo tile?
 Professional installation is strongly recommended due to the material's inherent variation and porosity. 
Before installation:
* Open all tile boxes and thoughtfully mix tiles to evenly distribute aggregate variation 
During installation:
* Set tiles using a high-quality medium-bed thinset over a properly cured substrate
* Wet the back of each tile before setting to support adhesion
* Press tiles into place by hand – do not use a mallet
* Allow full curing before sealing and grouting
For sealing and grouting:
* Apply 511 Porous Plus Sealer before grouting
* Grout in small 2’x2’ sections using Mapei Flexcolor CQ grout and wipe clean as you go
* Complete with Fila Matte Wax for interior applications or an additional coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior use
It is important to allow all products used to fully cure before moving to the next installation step. For detailed instructions, refer to the Terrazzo Installation Guide before beginning work.
Is terrazzo good for a kitchen floor?
Yes, terrazzo is well-suited for a kitchen floor when properly installed and sealed. The material is composed of stone aggregates bound in cement, creating a hard-wearing surface that performs consistently in high-traffic environments, while the dynamic surfaces are ideal for masking wear. Sealing before and after grouting is required to protect the cement tile from moisture and staining, which is particularly important in a kitchen environment. Re-seal every one to two years, or more frequently depending on traffic and wear.
How long does terrazzo last?
Terrazzo is a long-lasting architectural material when correctly installed and maintained. The material is composed of stone aggregates bound in cement, which creates structural stability under regular residential and commercial use. With proper installation and a consistent sealing schedule, terrazzo flooring and wall installations maintain their surface integrity over decades of use. For detailed installation and maintenance instructions, refer to the Terrazzo Installation Guide.
How do I choose between 12x12 and 24x24 terrazzo?
Choosing between 12x12 and 24x24 terrazzo tile depends on the scale of the surface and the desired visual effect. The 12x12 format suits compact spaces, feature walls, and surfaces where the pattern compositions, such as Highball, Collins, and Bossa Nova, are the focal point of the design. The 24x24 format suits open floors and expansive walls where fewer grout lines produce a cleaner, less interrupted surface.

What is the difference between terrazzo tile and marble tile?
Terrazzo tile and marble tile both include stone materials, but differ greatly in production process and appearance. Terrazzo is produced by embedding crushed marble and stone aggregates into a cement base, which is then hydraulically pressed and ground to reveal the speckled aggregate-dotted surface. Marble is a naturally occurring stone, quarried and then cut directly from solid blocks, with variation determined entirely by the stone's geological composition and natural veining.
