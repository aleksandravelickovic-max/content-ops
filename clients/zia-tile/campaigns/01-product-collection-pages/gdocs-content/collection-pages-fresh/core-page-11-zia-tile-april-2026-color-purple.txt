Purple tile is often placed in a backsplash, a niche, or a single wall. It brings a clear point of contrast without relying on pattern or layout to do the work. At Zia, purple appears in cement, terrazzo, and zellige, and the choice between them usually comes down to how much surface area you want to cover and how the tile fits into the rest of the palette.

Frequently Asked Questions
How do you choose between warm purples and cool purples?
The undertone is the dividing line. Warm purples carry red and pink in the base, which pulls the color toward plum and aubergine. Cool purples carry blue, which pushes them toward lavender and violet. The surrounding materials in the room shift the perception further, so ordering samples and evaluating them in the actual installation space under both natural and artificial light before deciding is recommended.
Why does purple zellige tile shift under different lighting?
Purple zellige tile shifts under different lighting because each tile is hand-glazed and fired in an earthen kiln where heat is uneven. That process produces variation in tone and surface across every tile. Warm light pulls the red out of the purple and pushes it toward plum. Cool light brings the blue forward and moves it toward violet. Chips, pits, and crazing across the surface catch and reflect light differently throughout the day, which adds to the movement.
Is purple tile a good choice for an accent wall or backsplash?
Yes, purple tile works well on accent walls and backsplashes. These applications allow the color to read as a defined element within a larger palette without covering the entire room. At Zia, cement, terrazzo, and zellige are all available in purple, and each suits wall and backsplash applications.
Is purple tile suitable for floors?
Yes, when the material is suited for floor use. Cement tile and terrazzo are both approved for interior floors. On high-traffic or wet floors, hold a minimum 1/8" grout joint and seal every one to two years, more often in shower environments.

How much purple tile is needed for a project?
Calculate your total square footage by measuring each surface (length × width in feet), then sum all areas. To that total, add overage based on your material and desired outcome. For solid-color materials like ceramics, 10 to 15% typically covers cuts. For handmade materials like zellige with higher variation, plan for 15 to 20% standard overage, or 20 to 25% if you want to hand-select for color consistency.
