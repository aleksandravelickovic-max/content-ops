Frequently Asked Questions 
Where do Zia’s marble tiles come from?
Marble is an ancient natural stone formed under intense heat and pressure, resulting in a dense, crystalline surface defined by natural veining. Our marble is quarried in Italy and Spain, regions with a long-established history of extracting and refining architectural stone. Italy, in particular, has shaped the global standard for marble through centuries of use in landmarks such as the Duomo and Michelangelo’s Statue of David.
Carrara marble is recognized for its balanced white field and subtle grey veining. It sits alongside rust-toned Rosso Alicante, golden Giallo Reale, dark Nero Marquina, grey Grigio Carnico, and rich Verde Alpi, each variety suited to stand independently or as part of a cohesive, patterned set.
How are Zia marble tiles made?
Zia's marble tiles are cut directly from solid stone blocks and finished by skilled stonemasons who preserve the integrity of the material. Honed marble creates a smooth, matte surface with a softer, architectural presence. Since marble forms through natural geological movement, no two tiles are identical. Mineral distribution and pressure patterns create variation in veining and tone that cannot be replicated, giving every installation a distinct composition.
Does marble tile need special care?
Yes, marble tiles require consistent care to maintain their surface appearance and prevent staining. As a porous natural stone, marble must be sealed before and after grouting to protect against moisture and staining. Use a penetrating sealer such as 511 Porous Plus and apply Fila Matte Wax as a protective top coat for interior applications. Reseal periodically based on traffic levels and exposure to water. Marble is sensitive to acids, so ongoing maintenance must follow stone-safe practices.
How do you clean marble tile?
Routine cleaning of marble tiles should preserve the finish without any surface damage. Use a soft cloth or mop with a pH-neutral cleaner specifically formulated for natural stone.
Please avoid the following at all times:
* Acidic cleaners, such as vinegar or citrus-based solutions
* Abrasive scrubbers or pads that can scratch or dull the tiles’ surface
For best results, wipe spills immediately to prevent absorption and staining. Consistent, gentle cleaning maintains the clarity of the stone while supporting long-term durability.
How do I install marble tile?
Installing marble tile requires precision, proper materials, and an eye for distribution and placement. Blend tiles from all boxes and dry-lay them to balance variation and veining before setting begins.
During installation:
* Cut using a water-cooled tile saw to prevent chipping
* Wet the back of each stone with a sponge before applying the adhesive to the back of each piece of marble
* Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset. Back-butter each tile and press into place by hand only to avoid damage. Maintain spacing with ⅛" spacers and remove excess thinset immediately
For sealing and grouting:
* Apply a penetrating sealer before grouting and allow full curing
* Grout in small sections and wipe immediately to prevent staining if the grout sits too long
* Complete with Fila Matte Wax for interior applications or an additional coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior use
For complete installation instructions, refer to the Marble Installation Guide before beginning any project.
How long does marble tile last?
Marble tile is a long-lasting architectural material that will wear beautifully when properly installed and maintained. Installation quality determines stability and prevents premature cracking or shifts. Sealing before and after grouting protects the surface from moisture penetration and staining. Regular maintenance with pH-neutral products preserves the finish and prevents etching or surface wear. For detailed installation and care instructions, refer to the Marble Installation Guide.
Are marble tiles good for bathroom floors?
Yes, marble tiles are a good choice for bathroom floors. Installation and care instructions should be carefully followed for the best results. Sealing before and after grouting is required to protect against moisture and staining in wet environments. For shower floors, slip resistance can be increased by increasing grout joint size to a minimum of ⅛" and/or applying an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete. Marble is also suitable for outdoor showers or exterior floor applications, in non-freeze/thaw climates.
What color grout should I use with marble?
Grout color for marble tile depends on whether the grout lines are meant to blend with the design or provide an eye-catching contrast. Light gray grout suits Carrara marble, mimicking the natural veining. White grout creates a clean, uninterrupted surface, but requires more care to keep its pristine appearance. 
Beige or warm neutrals suit warmer stones like Rosso Alicante or Giallo Reale, maintaining tonal consistency across the installation. Use Mapei Flexcolor CQ to reduce staining risk and avoid abrasive grout types that can scratch polished marble surfaces. Test grout color against the stone before full installation to confirm the choice aligns with the intended design.
What is the difference between honed and polished marble?
Honed and polished marble describe the same stone with a distinct finish. Honed marble is ground to a smooth, flat, matte finish that diffuses light rather than reflecting it. Polished marble is buffed to a high-gloss finish that enhances the depth and contrast of the veining and reflects light directly. Zia’s collection features honed marble, which requires sealing before and after grouting and ongoing care with pH-neutral products.
What is the difference between marble tile and terrazzo tile?
Marble tile and terrazzo tile both feature stone, but differ fundamentally in how they are produced and what defines their visual character. Marble is a naturally occurring metamorphic stone cut directly from solid blocks, with veining and mineral composition determined entirely by geological formation. No two marble tiles are identical because the variation is geological rather than designed. Terrazzo is a composite material produced by embedding crushed marble and stone aggregates into a cement base, hydraulically pressing the tile, and grinding the surface to reveal the aggregate.
