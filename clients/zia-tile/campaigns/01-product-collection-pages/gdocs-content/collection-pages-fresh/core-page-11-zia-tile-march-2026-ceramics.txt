Frequently Asked Questions
Where do Zia's ceramic tiles come from?
The first evidence of functional ceramics dates back to the Neolithic period — the Stone Age! Earthenware was first seen in the early Jōmon period between 10,500 and 300 BC, marking one of the earliest known traditions of structured ceramic production. Zia's Japanese ceramic tiles are designed in Los Angeles and crafted in a potters’ village outside Nagoya, Japan, where the artisans behind the line blend traditional Old-World touches with contemporary methods to achieve our modern-yet-timeless designs.
How are Zia's ceramic tiles made?
Zia's ceramic tiles are handcrafted near Nagoya, Japan, where the art of ceramic production spans thousands of years. The core ceramics begin with clay, which is molded and dried ahead of glazing and high-temperature firing. The Field Trip: Japan line follows a more precise extrusion process, where Japanese clay is passed through a die-cut mold, then cut into individual tiles. Each piece is hand-glazed and fired only once. Both sizing and coloring remain consistent throughout the collection.
Does ceramic tile need special care?
Yes, Zia's ceramic tiles require care to maintain their surface and finish over time. All matte ceramic tiles must be sealed before grouting using 511 Porous Plus Sealer as a grout release to avoid grout haze and staining. Using a pH-neutral cleaner and avoiding acidic or abrasive products will help preserve the material and prevent staining. Matte ceramics should be resealed at least every one to two years, or as often as needed, depending on traffic and wear. For areas exposed to moisture, more frequent resealing may be required. Always seal in accordance with the sealant manufacturer's instructions.
How do you clean ceramic tile?
Sweep or vacuum ceramic tile regularly to prevent surface abrasion, and promptly clean up any liquid or messier spills. Floors and walls can be wiped or mopped with warm, clean water and a pH-neutral cleaner like Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier stains, we recommend Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Avoid bleach, vinegar, or anything acidic, which can dull the tiles’ surface over time.
How do I install ceramic tile?
Ceramic tile installation is best handled by a professional installer with experience in ceramic tile, specifically. Here is an overview of the installation process:
* Blend tiles from all boxes to evenly distribute natural variation
* Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset and wet the back of each tile before setting
* Press tiles into place by hand — do not use a mallet
* Allow the thinset to fully cure
* If installing matte ceramic tile, seal the tiles before grouting using 511 Porous Plus
* Grout in small sections using a high-quality sanded grout, and wipe clean as you go
For detailed step-by-step instructions, refer to the Ceramics Installation Guide.
Is ceramic tile good for a floor?
Yes, ceramic tile flooring can be a great option for interior floors where durability, moisture resistance, and ease of maintenance are required. Please consider the tiles’ finish in wet spaces, where the slip factor is concerned. High-fired ceramic tile resists staining, surface wear, and water exposure when installed over a properly prepared substrate with consistent grout spacing. Matte finishes offer more grip in wet conditions, while satin and glossy finishes are easier to clean and maintain. 
How long does ceramic tile last?
Ceramic tile is designed for long-term performance when properly installed and maintained. High-temperature firing creates a dense tile body that maintains its structure over time, while the matte or glazed surface protects against staining and wear. Longevity depends on installation quality, substrate stability, and routine care with proper pH-neutral products. Grout and substrate condition play a critical role, as movement or improper preparation can lead to cracking or surface failure over time.
How is ceramic tile used?
Ceramic tile is frequently used in both residential and commercial applications as a wall or floor material. In kitchens, ceramic tile backsplash installations protect vertical surfaces from moisture and residue while maintaining a continuous, easy-to-clean finish. Ceramic tile bathroom walls and floors provide a stable, water-resistant surface. Ceramics are approved for shower walls and floors, pools, and spas — sealing grout lines after grouting is required in these environments. Ceramic tile is suitable for all exterior applications, regardless of climate, with the exception of outdoor floors.

How much ceramic tile do I need for my project?
To work out how much ceramic tile your project requires, begin by measuring each surface individually. Multiply the length by the width of each surface in feet to get its square footage, then add all surfaces together for your total. Add a minimum of 10 to 15% overage to account for cuts or tiles set aside during installation.
