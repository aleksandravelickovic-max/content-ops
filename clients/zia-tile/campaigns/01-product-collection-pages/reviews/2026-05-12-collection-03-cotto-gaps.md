# Collection Page 3 — Cotto — gaps vs Jamie's feedback

The Cotto collection page — a short hero ("Our Take on Terra Cotta" + two paragraphs), a product list placeholder, and a long FAQ section. Jamie's 4/30 and 5/4 calls explicitly say collection pages are closer to ready than blogs, but her 5/8 morning Cotto walkthrough applies here too — and her recurring rules about banned words, fluff, "Cotto is Zia's terracotta," and the dark-brown / matte-black leadership angle all carry over.

## Gaps

### 1. Hero leads with "visibly unique" / "inherent variation" — exact AI fluff Jamie flagged
**Jamie's ask:** "Look closely and you'll find cream, soft, gray, and something slightly different in every tile. … This sounds like AI fluff. It does not sound like you're speaking to tiles specifically. Words like variation, inherent variation. I use things like that are a little bit more common."
**Source:** 2026-03-27 Zia Tile content discussion
**Where this piece falls short:** L7 opens the only above-the-fold body copy with: "Each handmade terra cotta tile is visibly unique, with inherent variation in tone, shape, and texture that adds dimension to every design." "Visibly unique" + "inherent variation" + "adds dimension to every design" is the exact stack Jamie called out as AI-speak — and it's the lead sentence on the page.

### 2. Hero never says "Cotto is Zia's terracotta" — the explicit setup Jamie keeps asking for
**Jamie's ask:** "Kodo is a proprietary category for Zia. … I would say something along the lines of Kodo is Zia's terracotta. … You have to set up kodo a little bit more." And: "if the client gets here and they're like, wait, we're not talking about coda, we're talking about terracotta. They might not understand yet that it is our terracotta, because we haven't said that explicitly, which is again what I think we could put in that intro, which feels a little weak."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review (echoed 2026-04-08, 2026-03-11)
**Where this piece falls short:** The H2 says "Our Take on Terra Cotta" and L7 says "clay-based terra cotta tile crafted in San Miguel de Allende" — never the simple, direct "Cotto is Zia's terracotta." A reader landing here from a "terracotta tile" search has to puzzle out the brand/material relationship rather than being told it plainly above the fold.

### 3. Madera / Oscura leadership is buried in an FAQ bullet, not surfaced as a hero angle
**Jamie's ask:** "We've literally said that these are the traditional colors of terracotta. … Look. Oh, Z is doing something different. Let's call that out. … Z has pushed terracotta into this dark brown category, leading the way with terracotta production across boutique tile. … dark brown, matte black, chocolate brown, we're missing that opportunity to lean into some of these trendier keywords."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review (referenced 4–5 times in that call)
**Where this piece falls short:** The hero (L7–8) hints at "matte black Oscura, whitewashed Blanco, and cocoa-colored Madera" inside a run-on second sentence — never as a lead. Searchable keyword strings Jamie explicitly flagged — "dark brown terracotta," "matte black terracotta," "chocolate brown" — do not appear anywhere on the page as standalone phrases. Madera is also Zia's top SKU of 2026 (per 4/8 link-building review), and the page does not say so.

### 4. Generic, undifferentiated shape blurbs — no landmark / place anchoring
**Jamie's ask:** "Let's all reference this famous building that we know it" / "we know what has the most legs from a design and industry perspective. So when we marry the two, I think we'll have some, like, really nice storytelling opportunities."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** L25–31 list Stars & Cross, Alcazar, Oaxaca, Durango, Tornillo, Toltec, Zocalo — each described in one generic line. Stars & Cross gets "a Moorish pattern" with no place. Oaxaca is "an hourglass form paired with rounded octagons" — the UNESCO city is in the answer above (L13) but not used as the shape anchor. Granada, Puebla, San Miguel courtyards — all candidates Jamie has flagged for design storytelling — are not used to anchor the shape descriptions.

### 5. "Charming unglazed pieces add an earthy aesthetic" — exactly the SaaS/marketing fluff Jamie flagged
**Jamie's ask:** "Its durability comes from simple materials and straightforward maintenance. It's like, what does that mean? Like, I just don't like. It's just this feels like a slop a lot of time. … I'd like to make sure we have someone who is that line of defense before it comes to me." Plus: "There's certain words that I keep seeing, like matrix or systems. … you don't hear those words in Architectural Digest."
**Source:** 2026-05-04 Zia Tile blog posts connect; 2026-03-27 content discussion
**Where this piece falls short:** L8 second sentence reads: "These charming unglazed pieces add an earthy aesthetic to indoor and outdoor areas alike, well-suited for any climate." "Charming," "earthy aesthetic," "well-suited" stacked in a single sentence is the same generic-design register she's flagged repeatedly. There's no specificity about which pieces, which spaces, which climates.

### 6. "Cotto Allende" answer hijacks the "Where do Cotto tiles come from?" question
**Jamie's ask:** "There's another note on cement where we were talking about patterns, but it was referencing solid colored tile. … words might sound good, but like, what are we actually saying here? And is it relevant?"
**Source:** 2026-05-04 Zia Tile blog posts connect
**Where this piece falls short:** L12 asks "Where do Cotto tiles come from?" and answers with one accurate paragraph (L13) — then L14 pivots into an unrelated paragraph about Cotto Allende: "Cotto Allende begins with the same clay terra cotta base and is finished with pigmented glazes…" That belongs in a separate Q ("What's the difference between Cotto and Cotto Allende?") or in the Cotto Allende collection page. As written, it dilutes the answer to the actual question.

### 7. "Madera, a deep cocoa, one of the few dark brown terra cotta tiles produced anywhere" — buried in a bullet, not used as the page's lead-with-leadership argument
**Jamie's ask:** "Madeira color is, like, our bestseller. So I would think we would have, like, you know, Z has pushed terracotta into this dark brown category, leading the way with terracotta production across boutique tile."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review; reinforced 2026-04-08 ("That's been our top skew this year")
**Where this piece falls short:** L22 has the right framing ("one of the few dark brown terra cotta tiles produced anywhere") — but it's the third bullet of an FAQ answer, not the headline of the page. For a collection page where the goal is to convert "dark brown terracotta" / "matte black terracotta" searchers, that argument belongs at the top, not in a sub-bullet on a FAQ four scrolls down.

### 8. Saltillo / Cotto Allende / Zellige comparisons live only in FAQs — no body angle on what Cotto specifically does that others don't
**Jamie's ask:** "There's just so much more opportunity here to lean a little bit more into what we're doing and what we have to offer."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** The hero says nothing about Cotto's freeze/thaw exterior capability, its DCOF 0.98, its six-colorway range, its specialty shape catalog as differentiators against other terra cotta or against Saltillo. The Saltillo and Zellige comparisons (L56 in the FAQ) are technically correct but read like reference material, not like the brand argument the hero is supposed to make. Per Jamie's 4/30: "the highlighted intro here is the only section in a category that we really want to hyper customize."

### 9. "How much Cotto tile do I need for my project?" — second-person heavy, plus "your project" / "for project-specific quantity questions"
**Jamie's ask:** "We also don't like to use you too much and speak directly to the client in that way. So something like consider your installation or consider the installation a starting point, not a forever state."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** L54 leans on "your project" twice. L50 reads "for project-specific installation questions." Across the FAQ section ("Does Cotto tile need special care?", "How do I install Cotto tile?", "How do I seal Cotto tile?"), the "your / you" register isn't dialed back the way Jamie corrected — and there's no use of the "consider the installation" softer construction.

### 10. Hero is too short / too thin — and the "lean into the product line" call-out is missing
**Jamie's ask:** "I'm just guessing that, like, chocolate brown, our dark brown tiles, are our number one seller and there's no call out. … there's just so much more opportunity here to lean a little bit more into what we're doing and what we have to offer that, again, I don't think is buried on the site anywhere. … this top intro… that's the only section in a category that we really want to hyper customize."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review; 2026-04-30 content connect
**Where this piece falls short:** Above the [PRODUCT LIST] there are two short paragraphs (L7–8) that total ~60 words. There's no romance paragraph that names "our three traditional colors — Adobe, Fired Earth, Red Clay — and the three contemporary tones — Blanco, Madera, Oscura — that have pushed Zia into dark brown and matte black terra cotta territory." Per 4/30, this top section is the one slot per category where SKU-/collection-level "hyper customization" should land — and it currently doesn't.

### 11. "Available in six core colors, the palette includes traditional, warm neutrals alongside matte black Oscura…" — sentence stacks adjectives without saying anything specific
**Jamie's ask:** "There's words like, … even like, here's another example of a line like, Zia offers both stocked options and curated collections. What does that mean to you guys? … There's things like that where I'm reading it and I'm like, this just like, doesn't make sense."
**Source:** 2026-05-04 Zia Tile blog posts connect
**Where this piece falls short:** L8 reads: "Available in six core colors, the palette includes traditional, warm neutrals alongside matte black Oscura, whitewashed Blanco, and cocoa-colored Madera." "Traditional, warm neutrals" is doing the job that "Adobe, Fired Earth, Red Clay — Zia's three traditional terracotta colors" should be doing. The contemporary three are named; the traditional three are euphemized. Per 4/30 + 5/8, both the classics and the contemporary should be named-and-claimed on the collection page.
