# Blog Post #4 — Cotto Tile: History, Applications, and Care — gaps vs Jamie's feedback

A long-form Cotto explainer. Jamie's 5/8 morning meeting was a line-by-line walkthrough of this exact piece, supplemented by recurring editorial direction from 3/27, 4/30, and 5/4. The gaps below are everything she asked for that the current draft still does not honor.

## Gaps

### 1. Opening still leads with process, not the artisans / craft setup Jamie asked for
**Jamie's ask:** "To open by saying San Miguel, where families have been pressing clay into molds and firing it for generations… it's just a strange way of. It doesn't sound great. … The overarching we're talking about: these are families who are experts in terracotta production. It is a handmade material. The methods have been passed down for generations. So I just think that we move the pressing clay into molds and the firing into it a little bit lower."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review (Cotto blog walkthrough)
**Where this piece falls short:** The first body line (L6) opens exactly the way Jamie flagged: "Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay near San Miguel de Allende, which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques." Process verbs are still the lede. The artisans / families / "perfecting the art of terracotta over generations" framing she asked to lead with is nowhere in the first three paragraphs.

### 2. Doesn't lean into Cotto as Zia's terracotta — "Cotto is Zia's terra cotta" framing is buried and underweight
**Jamie's ask:** "Kodo is a proprietary category for Zia. … I would say something along the lines of Kodo is Z[ia]'s terracotta. … You have to set up kodo a little bit more." And: "if the client gets here and they're like, wait, we're not talking about coda, we're talking about terracotta. They might not understand yet that it is our terracotta, because we haven't said that explicitly, which is again what I think we could put in that intro, which feels a little weak."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** "Cotto is Zia's terra cotta line" appears once at L8 inside the "What Is Cotto Tile?" section — after the opening paragraph and after the headline. The intro paragraph (L6) never says "Cotto is our terra cotta." A reader landing here who doesn't know the brand still has to scroll to figure out Cotto = Zia's proprietary name for their terra cotta.

### 3. The "Zia is leading in dark brown / matte black terracotta" angle is missing
**Jamie's ask:** "We've literally said that these are the traditional colors of terracotta. … And look. Oh, Z is doing something different. Let's call that out. … Our dark brown, that Madeira color is, like, our bestseller. So I would think we would have, like, you know, Z has pushed terracotta into this dark brown category, leading the way with terracotta production across boutique tile. … dark brown, matte black, chocolate brown, we're missing that opportunity to lean into some of these trendier keywords."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review (repeated 4–5 times across that meeting)
**Where this piece falls short:** The piece names Madera ("a deep cocoa, one of the few dark brown terra cotta tiles produced anywhere") and Oscura ("a matte black terra cotta, rare in unglazed clay") — single-line color blurbs in a bulleted list at L28–29. There is no Madera/Oscura-led paragraph that positions Zia as the brand leading the way in dark brown / matte black terra cotta. The keyword phrases Jamie explicitly flagged — "dark brown terracotta," "matte black terracotta," "chocolate brown" — do not appear in the body as searchable strings.

### 4. Uses "spaces" repeatedly in body and headers — a Jamie-banned word
**Jamie's ask:** Banned-word list pulled out by Jamie includes "matrix," "systems," "full sequencing," "technical requirements," "spaces" (use "rooms"). "Like matrix or systems. And I'm like, you don't hear those words in Architectural Digest."
**Source:** 2026-05-04 Zia Tile blog posts connect; 2026-03-27 Zia Tile content discussion
**Where this piece falls short:** "Spaces" appears in section header L52 ("Exterior Floors and Outdoor Spaces"), L56 ("Sunrooms and Transitional Spaces"), L45 ("spaces where warmth, texture, and natural aging are part of the design intention"), L59 ("boutique spaces"). At least one header should be reworked to "rooms" per the editorial rule.

### 5. Closes on a CTA, not a real conclusion — and the explore section reads like SEO sign-off
**Jamie's ask:** "There's also, like, no conclusions to the blog post. It'll just sort of end, which maybe that's just the norm, and that's fine if it is. But…" Plus the broader editorial direction: don't pivot to shop-language; this is editorial, not a product page.
**Source:** 2026-05-04 Zia Tile blog posts connect
**Where this piece falls short:** L80–83 close with "Explore Zia's Cotto Collection" header followed by a 15–20% overage line and "Explore the Cotto collection, order samples, or reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170." This is exactly the shop-language closer Jamie has flagged as off-register — it's a CTA, not a conclusion. There is no closing paragraph that lands on a material observation (patina, aging, place).

### 6. Italian-terracotta framing in paragraph 2 is the lean Jamie said to soft-pedal
**Jamie's ask:** "I don't mind it like referencing that cotto is Italian for cooked. I think it just gets a little confusing to lean into Italian terracotta. So… I would take out the Italian in that second paragraph."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** L8 leads paragraph 2 with: "Cotto is Zia's terra cotta line. Terra cotta is Italian for 'cooked,' referencing a kiln-fired clay tile, traditionally unglazed, with tones determined entirely by the soil from which it came." The Italian etymology is still load-bearing in the definitional paragraph rather than reduced to a glancing aside.

### 7. No specific famous landmark / building anchor — generic "farmhouses, monasteries, villas"
**Jamie's ask:** "Let's all reference this famous building that we know it" / "we know what has the most legs from a design and industry perspective. So when we marry the two, I think we'll have some, like, really nice storytelling opportunities."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** L10 lists "farmhouses, monasteries, and villas across warmer climates" — completely generic. Granada, Puebla, Oaxaca, San Miguel de Allende's centuries-old floors — all candidates Jamie's team would supply on a 15-min product call — are not used as anchor references in the history section. (The shape blurbs at L31–37 reference Granada, Puebla, Oaxaca by name; the actual history paragraph does not.)

### 8. "Visibly unique" / generic-imperfection language Jamie has explicitly called fluff
**Jamie's ask:** "Look closely and you'll find cream, soft, gray, and something slightly different in every tile. … This sounds like AI fluff. It does not sound like you're speaking to tiles specifically. Words like variation, inherent variation. I use things like that are a little bit more common."
**Source:** 2026-03-27 Zia Tile content discussion
**Where this piece falls short:** L16 says "Variation in size, color, shade, and surface texture is a natural characteristic of terra cotta, to be expected in every Cotto order, and part of the reason a finished Cotto surface reads as handmade." Acceptable, but L6 still says the result is "a terra cotta defined by tonal variation, surface character, and the inherent details that give handmade tile its appeal" — generic intensifier phrasing. The collection page sister-doc and this blog both repeat the "visibly unique / inherent details" register Jamie flagged in 3/27 and 4/30.

### 9. Process detail Jamie flagged for verification still presented as fact
**Jamie's ask:** "Clay is pressed into mold, sun dried in courtyards and high fired in wooden gas kilns. Not quite sure they're wood and gas kilns. So I don't know if that came from our site. … I just would want our team to have eyes on that because I just don't know for sure."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** The piece replaced "wood and gas kilns" with "traditional kilns" (L11) — partial fix — but Jamie's larger concern was that production specifics need her team's verification before publishing. The piece still asserts unverified specifics ("sun-dried in open courtyards for one to two weeks") without a "Zia team to verify" flag on the draft.

### 10. Speaks directly to "you" too often in care / installation copy
**Jamie's ask:** "We also don't like to use you too much and speak directly to the client in that way. So something like consider your installation or consider the installation a starting point, not a forever state."
**Source:** 2026-05-08 morning Zia content review
**Where this piece falls short:** Care section (L62–66) and install section (L68–79) lean heavily on "you" / second-person imperative ("Clean spills promptly," "Apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting," "Skip it or rush it, and the grout will…"). Jamie's correction asked for "consider the installation" register instead of "you" — that softer construction isn't carried through the maintenance and install sequences.
