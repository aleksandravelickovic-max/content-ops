The Zellige Color Guide: How Zellige Colors Read in a Room

Zellige colors arrive as a spectrum, not a swatch. Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and fired in an earthen kiln, where variable heat gives a single colorway its range of tone and sheen. A wall of green zellige holds a dozen greens at once, and the balance among them changes as light moves through the room.

Our zellige collection features more than two hundred tiles across solid colorways and mesh-backed mosaics, each made in Morocco using techniques refined over eight hundred years. The solid range on our zellige collection page moves from the warm cream of our Casablanca through earth pinks, ambers, greens, and blues to the near-black depth of our Carbon Black.

Choosing among them is easier at the level of tone family than name by name, because within a family the glaze behaves consistently. The warm whites hold light and give it back to the room, while the deep greens and blues absorb it and register as depth.

Why Zellige Colors Shift with Light

The shifting is the work of dynamic glazes, the zellige-specific way color and sheen change as light moves across a wall. Each tile begins as clay that is hand-pressed, sun-dried, and hand-shaped before being dipped in glaze by hand and fired in an earthen kiln where the heat lands variably. No two tiles take the glaze identically, so an installed field carries innate variation in tone, surface texture, and sheen.

The surface itself does part of the work. Chips, pits, and crazing are characteristics of each zellige tile, and those small facets catch and scatter light at different angles. Direct natural light brightens the tiles, while shadowed installations appear deeper.

A colorway chosen from a single sample in a bright showroom will read differently on a north-facing bathroom wall. We recommend viewing samples in the room’s own light, through a full day if possible, before settling on a palette.

The Zellige Tone Families

The solid colorways group into six broad families. Each family shares a temperature and a way of holding light, which makes the family, not the individual name, the practical unit for building a palette.

Warm Whites and Neutrals

Our Casablanca is the classic white zellige, an off-white cream whose subtle variation keeps a large field from flattening. Its warmth settles beside plaster, pale oak, and unpolished stone without a visible seam in temperature. Our Pure White is a step brighter and lifts rooms that need more light. Small imperfections and subtle color variations in the hand-dipped glaze catch the light at different angles, so a full white wall shifts in sheen as daylight crosses it. Our Glazed Earth rounds out the family with a tan glaze and golden undertones, a neutral that leans toward the earth pinks and ambers and anchors a palette of warm materials.

Pinks and Earth Reds

This family stretches from blush to deep red. Our Desert Bloom ranges from lighter peachy-pink to deeper rose across pieces, so a full wall reads as a gradient of pink. Our Pietro Pink holds a steadier, softer warmth and settles quietly against limewash and travertine. At the saturated end, our Nana’s Lipstick leans toward coral and suits rooms where the tile is meant to lead.

Deeper still, our Terra Rosa sits firmly in earth territory as a reddish-brown, close in spirit to terra cotta. Our Cayenne splits the difference between red and orange and carries more brightness than the deep reds. Our Rouge is the richest of them, a deep red with burgundy and maroon notes that shifts noticeably as the light changes through the day.

Ambers and Browns

Brown zellige warms a room the way wood tones do, without saturating it. Our Amber is a golden-brown that falls between gold and brown and pairs naturally with walnut, leather, and unlacquered brass. Our Burnt Sugar goes darker, a deep brown under a glossy glaze that gives the color its depth, well suited to kitchens with dark cabinetry and evening light. Our Brownstone holds the middle ground, an earthy brown with less gold in it than Amber, useful where a palette needs weight without shine.

Greens from Olive to Jewel Tones

Green is the widest zellige family. Our Za’atar and our Savanna anchor the earthy end, a greenish-yellow and an olive respectively, both under matte finishes that hold light quietly instead of bouncing it. Our Jade holds the middle of the family, a medium, natural green that reads close to neutral in daylight and gains depth as the room dims.

Toward the cool end, our Prairie Green registers as a botanical, cool-to-neutral green. Our Tidepool moves into aqua territory, a subdued green that reads neutral-cool and pairs as comfortably with white marble as with warm wood. Our Racing Green is the most saturated of the family, a jewel-toned deep green that makes a shower wall or a kitchen run the room’s center of gravity.

Blues by Depth

The blue family orders itself by depth. Our Skylight is the brightest of the three, at home in a bathroom that wants color without shadow. Our Portuguese Blue deepens the register with a rich, saturated blue that falls between the other two in mood. Our Night Blue is the darkest of the three, and in shadowed light it reads closer to ink, a quality that suits moody powder rooms and fireplace walls.

Deep and Dark Tones

A few colorways stand apart from the main families and carry the darkest end of the palette. Our Plum is one of the few cool tones in the collection, a purple with real depth that turns jewel-like under evening light.

Our Sumac moves between dark burgundy and deep reddish-brown. Its matte glaze carries a coarser texture than the glossy colorways, which gives it a quieter, older presence on a wall. Our Carbon Black is the near-black anchor of the collection and the dark half of high-contrast checkerboard pairings.

Zellige Colors, Room by Room

In kitchens, a backsplash of our Casablanca or Pure White in the classic 4x4 square reflects daylight back into the work zone and holds the palette open for later changes. The glazed surface is suited to kitchen conditions, including the heat and moisture behind a range. Greens are a natural fit here. A 2x6 of our Savanna or Jade set in a running bond adds color while keeping the room grounded.

Bathrooms shift the light. Glazed zellige is suitable for shower walls, pools, and spas, and the deeper colorways gain presence in the smaller, dimmer light of a bath. Our Skylight holds its color in rooms with limited daylight, while our Night Blue and Plum turn a powder room into a jewel box. Always seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers; the zellige installation guides cover sealing and grout joints for each format.

One boundary holds regardless of color: zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates, so exterior projects in cold regions call for a different material.

Fireplace walls take the saturated end of the palette well. A hex of our Rouge or a field of our Racing Green concentrates color where the eye already goes, and the dynamic glazes animate the surface each time the light in the room changes.

Color Combinations and Grout

Two-color installations are part of the zellige tradition, and the collection includes 2x2 checkerboard mosaics on 8x8 mesh. The pairings range from the high contrast of our Carbon Black with Casablanca to the cool calm of our Tidepool with Casablanca, while warm-on-warm pairings such as our Burnt Sugar with Terra Rosa trade contrast for depth. The mesh format holds the pattern steady while each tile keeps its own variation, so the checkerboard reads handmade at close range and graphic from across the room.

Grout is the quiet third color in these decisions. A color-matched grout lets the field read as a single plane and gives the dynamic glazes an uninterrupted surface. A contrasting grout outlines each hand-shaped tile at the grout joints and pushes the installation toward pattern.

Zellige Color FAQs

What colors do zellige tiles come in?

The collection spans more than two hundred tiles across warm whites, pinks and earth reds, ambers and browns, greens, blues, and deep tones such as our Plum, Sumac, and Carbon Black. Solid colorways come in 4x4 and 2x2 squares, 2x6 rectangles, hexagons, and trapezoids, with formats varying by colorway. Each colorway carries innate variation, so the installed color is a range, not a single value.

Why does one zellige colorway vary from tile to tile?

Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and fired in an earthen kiln where heat is distributed variably, so no two tiles take the color identically. Chips, pits, and crazing are characteristics of the glazed surface, and they scatter light in ways that add to the shift in tone and sheen. That variation is inherent to zellige and is what gives a finished wall its perfectly imperfect character.

Which zellige colors suit a kitchen backsplash?

Warm whites such as our Casablanca and Pure White keep a kitchen bright and pair with nearly any counter material. Earthy greens such as our Savanna and Jade read close to neutral in daylight while adding more color than a white field. Saturated tones such as our Racing Green or Rouge suit kitchens where the backsplash is meant to carry the room.

How much zellige should an order include?

Plan for a minimum of 15% overage beyond the measured area to account for cuts and for curating the color range during layout. Move to 20% when the installation needs tighter color control, and consider 25% where a more uniform look is the goal. A dry layout lets the chips, pits, and crazing disperse evenly across the finished surface, so the overage becomes part of the design.
