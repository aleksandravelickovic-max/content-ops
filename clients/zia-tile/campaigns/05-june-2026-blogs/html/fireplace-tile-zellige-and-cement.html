Fireplace Tile: Zellige and Cement for Feature Walls

Fireplace tile turns the most watched wall in a living room into a material decision. The firebox draws the eye on its own, and the surfaces framing it, the surround at the opening, the face above the mantel, the full chimney breast, decide whether that focal point feels built into the room or merely applied to it. Zia produces two materials suited to the role: hand-glazed Moroccan zellige, whose surface moves with the firelight, and Vietnamese cement tile, matte, patterned, and never fired.

This guide covers Zia’s zellige and cement collections at the fireplace, the surfaces each one suits, and the decisions around format, color, and grout that shape how a surround or feature wall reads once the fire is lit.

What Is Fireplace Tile?

Fireplace tile is the finished surface set around a firebox: the surround that frames the opening, the face that rises toward the mantel or ceiling, and the wider feature wall or chimney breast that carries the fireplace into the room’s architecture. The firebox interior is a separate assembly, lined with firebrick or another refractory material rated for direct flame. The design question concerns the surfaces around the fire.

Heat exposure on those surrounding surfaces varies with the fireplace itself. A wood-burning firebox, a sealed gas insert, and an electric or decorative fireplace each place different demands on the wall around them, and local code sets clearance requirements near the opening. Confirming the tile specification against the fireplace type and those requirements is the first step in any working installation, and Zia’s team can advise on a specific project.

Fireplace Tile at Zia

Zia’s fireplace materials follow two paths. Glazed zellige suits a surround that should move with the light, while cement tile suits a wall that should hold a steady matte pattern. The two share handmade origins and little else, so the choice between them sets the room’s direction.

Zellige Fireplace Surrounds

Zia’s zellige is made in Morocco using techniques refined over 800 years, and the zellige collection holds more than 200 tiles across colors, shapes, and mesh-backed mosaics. Each tile is hand-glazed, and each carries the chips, pits, and crazing that define the material as intentional characteristics. A fireplace rewards that surface more than almost any wall in the house, because firelight moves. The dynamic glazes shift in color and sheen as the light changes, so a zellige fireplace reads one way at noon and another way beside an evening fire.

The colorways span a wide register. Casablanca and Pure White sit at the pale end and bounce firelight back into the room. Glazed Earth, Burnt Sugar, and Amber hold the warm middle and stay within the palette of the fire itself, while Racing Green, Tidepool, and Plum carry a surround into full color that keeps its character in low evening light. Formats include the 4x4 square, the 2x6 Bejmat, the 2x2 square, a 3.5 hex, and trapezoid shapes, along with mesh-backed mosaics.

Glazed zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates, so an outdoor fireplace in a cold region calls for a different material. Sealing is required in all zellige installations, including dry interior walls such as a surround, following the steps in Zia’s Installation Guide.

Cement Tile Fireplace Walls

Zia’s cement tile is handmade in Vietnam using a method developed by 19th-century French and Catalan artisans. The cement collection begins with mineral pigments hand-poured over a cement body and set under a hydraulic press, and the tile is never fired. The result is a naturally matte surface with subtle color variation, a finish that holds its color as the firelight moves across the room. The collection offers 68 colors in 8x8, 4x4, and 2x8 formats, with 4x8 available in select styles.

Pattern is where cement separates itself from glazed material. Solid colorways such as Elemental Blue, Midnight, and Jaipur Pink set a single matte tone across the wall, while patterned sets such as Zig Zag, Kepler, Flora, and Stars & Cross turn a chimney breast into a repeating composition. Because the surface never goes glossy, even a strong pattern stays quiet beside the light of the fire.

Cement tile is suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only, and its porous, unfired body requires sealing in all installations. The surface develops a patina with wear and time, one of the collection’s defining qualities.

Fireplace Tile Ideas for Each Surface

A fireplace offers more than one tiled surface, and scale changes the format that suits each one. A trim surround measures a few square feet and rewards a small, hand-glazed square, while a chimney breast can run floor to ceiling and hold a full composition. Zia’s formats cover both ends of that range.

The Surround and Face

The surround is the frame, and small formats keep it in proportion. Zia’s 4x4 zellige square suits the classic surround, a band of handmade surface around the opening that reads in detail at mantel distance. The 2x6 Bejmat set in a running bond brings a quieter, linear rhythm to the face above the opening, and the same tile set vertically draws a low fireplace upward.

Cement tile takes the frame in a graphic direction. An 8x8 patterned set can border the firebox as a drawn composition, while a solid colorway in 4x4 or 2x8 keeps the face matte and architectural against a plaster wall.

The Full Feature Wall

A floor-to-ceiling feature wall changes the math. At that scale, Zia’s 2x2 zellige squares and mesh-backed mosaics read as texture from across the room and resolve into individual tiles up close, which keeps a large field from going flat. A chimney breast in Casablanca or Glazed Earth becomes a surface the room’s light plays across all day, with the fire taking over that role at night.

Cement pattern sets answer the same scale with graphic order. The Kepler and Zig Zag sets repeat across an 8x8 grid, so a full chimney breast reads as one continuous composition. A patterned feature wall pairs well with plaster, wood, and unfussy furniture, because the wall itself carries the ornament.

Color and Firelight

The color decision at a fireplace is a decision about light. Pale glazes such as Casablanca and Pure White reflect the fire and brighten the wall around it, so the fireplace zone stays luminous even when the fire is low. Deep tones hold the opposite role: Plum, Racing Green, or a Midnight cement field absorb more light and frame the flames against a dark ground, a direction that reads more dramatic after dusk.

Warm middles split the difference. Zellige in Glazed Earth, Burnt Sugar, or Amber sits inside the fire’s own palette, so wall and flame read as one continuous temperature. No single direction wins on paper. The trade-off runs between a wall that amplifies the fire and a wall that recedes behind it.

Grout and Layout

Grout shapes how distinctly each tile registers. A color-matched approach, with grout close to the tile’s own tone, softens the joints so the surface reads as one handmade field, which suits zellige, where the glaze variation already carries the wall. A contrasting approach sharpens each grout joint into a drawn line and emphasizes the grid, which suits a graphic cement layout more than a variable glaze.

Layout does similar work. Zellige squares set in a straight stack read contemporary and calm, while an offset bond reads more traditional. With cement pattern sets, the layout is fixed by the pattern itself, so the decision moves to where the pattern stops, at the edge of the surround or across the full wall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can zellige tile be used on a fireplace surround?

Zellige suits the vertical surfaces around a firebox: the surround, the face, and the full feature wall. The firebox interior is a separate assembly lined with refractory material, and heat exposure on the surrounding wall varies by fireplace type and local code. Zia’s team can confirm the specification for a working fireplace at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170 before an order is placed.

Can cement tile go around a fireplace?

Cement tile suits the same surfaces, the surround, the face, and the feature wall, where its matte finish keeps pattern legible without reflection. The material is never fired, and that pressed, unfired body is what gives the tile its matte, consistent face. The same body differs from kiln-fired tile and remains porous, so sealing is required in all installations. As with any material near a working firebox, the fireplace type and clearance requirements determine the final specification.

Can tile cover a fireplace hearth?

A hearth takes different exposure than a vertical surround, including foot traffic and, in a working fireplace, proximity to embers. Neither material carries a blanket hearth rating, and the right specification depends on the fireplace type and how the hearth is used. A hearth project should be planned with Zia’s team directly at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

How much tile does a fireplace surround require?

Plan for a minimum of 15% overage above the measured area, or 20% where the layout needs tighter control around perimeter cuts. For a more uniform look across handmade tile, plan for 25%.
