# Outdoor Tile for Patios, Pool Decks and Gardens

Outdoor patio tile sets the character of a garden before the first plant arrives, and the choice begins with climate. Our unglazed Cotto, fired in San Miguel de Allende, suits exterior floors in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions. Our glazed lines, Cotto Allende and zellige, carry hand-dipped color into pools and mild-climate patios, while cement tile, Cantera stone, and limestone widen the palette wherever winters stay above freezing.

Each of these materials appears in our [outdoor collection](https://ziatile.com/pages/outdoor-collection), and each answers a different combination of climate and application, from a courtyard in Los Angeles to a farmhouse patio in Vermont.

## What Makes a Tile Outdoor-Suitable

Three things decide whether a tile belongs outdoors: the clay body, the firing, and the surface. A dense, high-fired body takes on less water than a soft one, and the surface, glazed or unglazed, shapes how the tile behaves once temperatures drop.

Freezing climates add a second test. Fired clay absorbs some moisture, and water expands as it freezes, so year-round exterior use asks for a material matched to that cycle. Our unglazed Cotto meets it in any climate. Our glazed tiles, zellige and Cotto Allende, are not suitable for freeze/thaw climates and belong outdoors only where frost never settles.

Mild climates open the field considerably. Where winters stay above freezing, cement tile, Cantera stone, and limestone join the terra cotta lines outdoors, each with a distinct surface and palette.

### Cotto

Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried in open courtyards, and high-fired. It is unglazed, and it suits exterior use in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions. The one boundary is water: Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas.

The line features six colorways, a classic range of Adobe, Fired Earth, and Red Clay alongside the unexpected tones of Blanco, Madera, and Oscura. Use and time deepen the tone, and each installation is a starting point rather than a forever state.

### Cotto Allende

Cotto Allende is our glazed terra cotta tile line, hand-hewn from the same San Miguel de Allende clay, high-fired, then hand-glazed. It features eighteen colorways named for Mexican geography and flora, Sayulita, Arroyo, and Pedregal among them, in four shapes: a 4x4 square, a 4x8 rectangle, a triangle, and a 1.5x8 mini bar.

Glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools. It is not suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates, so outdoors it belongs on patios and pool decks where winters stay mild.

### Zellige

Zellige is handcrafted Moroccan clay tile with more than 800 years of tradition behind it, hand-shaped and hand-dipped in glaze. Chips, pits, and crazing are characteristics of each tile, and the dynamic glazes shift in color and sheen as light moves across the surface.

Glazed zellige is approved for pools and spas, an application it has served in Moroccan fountains and courtyard pools for centuries. It is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates. In mild climates, colorways such as Pure White and Tidepool from the outdoor collection bring those shifting glazes into the garden.

### Cement Tile

Our cement tile is made in Vietnam by a nineteenth-century European method: mineral pigments and cement pressed hydraulically, cured rather than fired. The surface is matte across 68 colors, including the Cairo White Black pattern featured in the outdoor collection.

Cement tile is suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only. In those settings it gives a patio graphic pattern that clay lines approach differently, one flat plane of pigment at a time.

### Cantera Stone

Cantera is volcanic stone quarried in Mexico, with visible aggregate and mineral inclusions that mark each piece as stone rather than clay. It moves between indoor and outdoor settings in non-freeze/thaw climates, in earthy tones that sit naturally among planting.

Our Sierra 12x24 appears in the outdoor collection, a format broad enough to cover a garden terrace at a calm, even scale. Like the other stone lines, Cantera is not suitable for pools or spas.

### Limestone

Our limestone is quarried in solid blocks from Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France, then finished honed for a smooth face or bush-hammered for a textured one. It develops a natural patina over time, the mark of a stone floor that has been walked for years.

Limestone is suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates, and it is not a pool or spa material. Along a mild-climate path or terrace, it sets a quieter register than glazed tile.

## Pool Decks and Pools

Pool tile divides into two decisions: the submerged interior and the deck that surrounds it. For the pool itself, glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools. Glazed zellige is approved for pools and spas as well, and Moroccan fountains have carried its glazes for centuries.

The deck is an exterior floor, so the climate rules apply. In mild climates, the pairing can run a glazed line in the water and Cotto or Cotto Allende across the deck. In freeze/thaw climates, the deck calls for unglazed Cotto, which suits exterior floors in any climate but not full submersion.

A wet deck also asks for the right specification. Our Cotto carries a DCOF of 0.98, well above the 0.42 threshold for level interior floors. For wet commercial floors, grout joints should measure at least 1/8 inch, with an anti-slip treatment applied after installation.

## How Climate Narrows the Field

A project in frost country resolves quickly: unglazed Cotto is the outdoor material, in six colorways across shapes from 4x4 squares to the 13x13 Big Alcazar. That range covers patios, paths, courtyards, and pool decks, with the pool interior reserved for a glazed line where the climate allows one.

A mild-climate project chooses among all six lines, so the decision shifts from climate to surface. Glazed color comes from Cotto Allende or zellige, graphic pattern from cement tile, and quarried stone from Cantera or limestone. Wherever water fully submerges the tile, the field narrows again to the glazed lines, Cotto Allende and zellige.
