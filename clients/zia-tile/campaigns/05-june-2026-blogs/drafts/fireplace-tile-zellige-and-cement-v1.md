# Fireplace Tile: Zellige and Cement for Feature Walls

Fireplace tile turns the most watched wall in a living room into a material decision. The firebox draws the eye on its own, and the surfaces framing it, the surround at the opening, the face above the mantel, the full chimney breast, decide whether that focal point reads as handmade and warm or flat and incidental. Zia produces two materials suited to the role: hand-glazed Moroccan zellige, whose surface moves with the firelight, and Vietnamese cement tile, matte, patterned, and pressed rather than fired.

This guide covers Zia's zellige and cement collections at the fireplace, the surfaces each one suits, and the decisions around format, color, and grout that shape how a surround or feature wall reads once the fire is lit.

## What Is Fireplace Tile?

Fireplace tile is the finished surface set around a firebox: the surround that frames the opening, the face that rises toward the mantel or ceiling, and the wider feature wall or chimney breast that carries the fireplace into the room's architecture. The firebox interior is a separate assembly, lined with firebrick or another refractory material rated for direct flame. The design question concerns the surfaces around the fire.

Heat exposure on those surrounding surfaces varies with the fireplace itself. A wood-burning firebox, a sealed gas insert, and an electric or decorative fireplace each place different demands on the wall around them, and local code sets clearance requirements near the opening. Confirming the tile specification against the fireplace type and those requirements is the first step in any working installation, and Zia's team can advise on a specific project.

### Zellige Fireplace Surrounds

Zia's zellige is made in Morocco using techniques refined over 800 years, and the [zellige collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/zellige) holds more than 200 tiles across colors, shapes, and mesh-backed mosaics. Each tile is hand-glazed, and each carries the chips, pits, and crazing that define the material as intentional characteristics. A fireplace rewards that surface more than almost any wall in the house, because firelight moves. The dynamic glazes shift in color and sheen as the light changes, so a zellige fireplace reads one way at noon and another way beside an evening fire.

The colorways span a wide register. Casablanca and Pure White sit at the pale end and bounce firelight back into the room. Glazed zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates, so an outdoor fireplace in a cold region calls for a different material. ### Cement Tile Fireplace Walls

Zia's [cement tile](https://ziatile.com/collections/cement-tile) is handmade in Vietnam using a method developed by 19th-century French and Catalan artisans. Mineral pigments are hand-poured over a cement body and set under a hydraulic press, and the tile is never fired. The result is a naturally matte surface with subtle color variation, offered in 68 colors and in 8x8, 4x4, and 2x8 formats, with 4x8 available in select styles.

Pattern is where cement separates itself from any glazed material. Solid colorways such as Elemental Blue, Midnight, and Jaipur Pink set a single matte tone across the wall, while patterned sets such as Zig Zag, Kepler, Flora, and Stars & Cross turn a chimney breast into a repeating composition. Because the surface never goes glossy, even a strong pattern stays quiet beside the light of the fire.

Cement tile is suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only, and its porous, unfired body requires sealing in all installations. The surface develops a patina with wear and time, one of the collection's defining qualities.

## Fireplace Tile Ideas for Each Surface

A fireplace offers more than one tiled surface, and scale changes the format that suits each one. A trim surround measures a few square feet and rewards a small, hand-glazed square, while a chimney breast can run floor to ceiling and hold a full composition. Zia's formats cover both ends of that range.

### The Surround and Face

The surround is the frame, and small formats keep it in proportion. Zia's 4x4 zellige square suits the classic surround, a band of handmade surface around the opening that reads in detail at mantel distance. The 2x6 Bejmat set in a running bond brings a quieter, linear rhythm to the face above the opening, and the same tile set vertically stretches a low fireplace taller.

Cement tile suits a surround that should read as drawn rather than dimensional. An 8x8 patterned set can frame the firebox as a bordered composition, while a solid colorway in 4x4 or 2x8 keeps the face matte and architectural against a plaster wall.

### The Full Feature Wall

A floor-to-ceiling feature wall changes the math. At that scale, Zia's 2x2 zellige squares and mesh-backed mosaics read as texture from across the room and resolve into individual tiles up close, which keeps a large field from going flat. A chimney breast in Casablanca or Glazed Earth becomes a surface the room's light plays across all day, with the fire taking over that role at night.

Cement pattern sets answer the same scale with graphic order. The Kepler and Zig Zag sets repeat across an 8x8 grid, so a full chimney breast reads as one composition rather than many tiles. A patterned feature wall pairs well with plaster, wood, and unfussy furniture, because the wall itself carries the ornament.

## Color and Firelight

The color decision at a fireplace is a decision about light. Pale glazes such as Casablanca and Pure White reflect the fire and brighten the wall around it, so the fireplace zone stays luminous even when the fire is low. Deep tones hold the opposite role: Plum, Racing Green, or a Midnight cement field absorb more light and frame the flames against a dark ground, a direction that reads more dramatic after dusk.

Warm middles split the difference. Zellige in Glazed Earth, Burnt Sugar, or Amber sits inside the fire's own palette, so wall and flame read as one continuous temperature. No single direction wins on paper. The trade-off runs between a wall that amplifies the fire and a wall that recedes behind it.

## Grout and Layout

Grout shapes how distinctly each tile registers. A color-matched approach, with grout close to the tile's own tone, softens the joints so the surface reads as one handmade field, which suits zellige, where the glaze variation already carries the wall. A contrasting approach sharpens each grout joint into a drawn line and emphasizes the grid, which suits a graphic cement layout more than a variable glaze.

Layout does similar work. Zellige squares set in a straight stack read contemporary and calm, while an offset bond reads more traditional. With cement pattern sets, the layout is fixed by the pattern itself, so the decision moves to where the pattern stops, at the edge of the surround or across the full wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can zellige tile be used on a fireplace surround?

Zellige suits the vertical surfaces around a firebox: the surround, the face, and the full feature wall. The firebox interior is a separate assembly lined with refractory material, and heat exposure on the surrounding wall varies by fireplace type and local code. ### Can cement tile go around a fireplace?

Cement tile suits the same surfaces, the surround, the face, and the feature wall, where its matte finish keeps pattern legible without reflection. The material is never fired, so its body differs from kiln-fired tile, and sealing is required in all installations. As with any material near a working firebox, the fireplace type and clearance requirements determine the final specification.

### What size tile suits a fireplace surround?

Scale follows the surface. A trim surround suits small formats such as Zia's 4x4 zellige square or the 2x6 Bejmat, which read in detail at mantel distance. A full chimney breast suits either the 2x2 zellige mosaic, which reads as texture at room scale, or the 8x8 cement format, where patterned sets build a composition large enough to carry the wall.

### What is the difference between zellige and cement tile?

Zellige is a glazed, kiln-fired clay tile handmade in Morocco, defined by chips, pits, and crazing and by glazes that shift in color and sheen with the light. Cement tile is handmade in Vietnam from mineral pigments pressed into a cement body under a hydraulic press, and it is never fired. At a fireplace, the two differ most in surface behavior: zellige moves with the firelight, while cement holds a matte, consistent face.

### Can tile cover a fireplace hearth?

A hearth takes different exposure than a vertical surround, including foot traffic and, in a working fireplace, proximity to embers. Hearth suitability depends on the fireplace type and the material, so a hearth project should be planned with Zia's team directly at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### Can an outdoor fireplace use zellige or cement tile?

Climate decides the outdoor question. Glazed zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates, and cement tile is suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only. In a mild-winter region, cement tile can be considered for the surfaces around an outdoor fireplace once heat exposure and clearances are confirmed, while in a freeze/thaw region both materials fall outside their outdoor range.

### Does fireplace tile need to be sealed?

Sealing is required for both materials in all installations, including dry interior surfaces such as a surround or feature wall. Zellige and cement each follow the sealing steps in Zia's Installation Guide, and cement's porous, unfired body makes sealing part of the installation rather than an option.
