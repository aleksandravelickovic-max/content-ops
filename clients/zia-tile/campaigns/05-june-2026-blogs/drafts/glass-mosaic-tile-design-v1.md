# Glass Mosaic Tile: Pool, Bathroom, and Backsplash Design

Glass mosaic tile reflects light in a way no clay or stone surface can, and our glass mosaics begin in Northern Spain, where each tile is made from 98% recycled glass and hand-placed on a 12x12 grid. Seven patterns anchor the range: Vaso, Union, d'Orsay, Ells, Tesserae, Murano, and Cheque.

The material belongs in wet rooms. Glass is non-porous and suited to full submersion in pools and spas, and it performs outdoors in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions. The same qualities carry it onto shower walls and kitchen backsplashes, where the small format brings pattern and light to surfaces a larger tile would flatten.

## What Is Glass Mosaic Tile?

Glass mosaic tile is small-format glass tile mounted on a grid so an entire sheet sets at once. Our glass mosaics are hand-placed on 12x12 grids, with solid colorways in a 1x1 format and seven patterns that arrive ready to set. Because artisans assemble each grid by hand, placement shifts from sheet to sheet, and color and sheen vary across the installation. That variation is inherent to the material and gives a finished wall its depth.

Color behaves differently in glass than in glazed clay. The hue runs through the full thickness of the tile, so the palette reads especially vibrant, and the sheen shifts as daylight moves through the room. A wall of our Piscine looks different at noon than it does at dusk, and that movement is the point.

Solid colorways keep a shower floor calm underfoot, and Rye or Milk grounds a patterned wall without competing against it.

## Grout and Color Decisions

Grout decides how a glass mosaic reads from across the room. A color-matched grout lets the sheet dissolve into a single surface, so the eye registers the overall tone before the individual tiles. A contrasting grout draws the outline of each tile and makes the grid itself part of the design.

Both approaches have a place. Color-matching favors patterned grids, where the composition should carry the eye, and contrast favors solid fields, where the joint lines supply the rhythm. A dry layout before setting shows how each approach reads in place.
