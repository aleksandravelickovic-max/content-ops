## What Cement Tile Is

Cement tile is a pressed, unfired tile made from mineral pigments and cement. Artisans pour pigment into a patterned mold by hand, back it with a cement body, and form each tile under a hydraulic press. The tile then cures rather than firing in a kiln. Never fired is the defining distinction from terra cotta and ceramics, and it is the reason the surface stays naturally matte.

Because the pigment layer is part of the tile rather than a coating, pattern and color arrive with a depth that printed or glazed alternatives do not carry. Each tile shows minor variation in tone and surface character. In a commercial installation of several hundred pieces, that variation is what keeps a large patterned floor from reading as wallpaper.

The matte surface sits quietly under commercial lighting, with none of the glare that glossy floors throw across a dining room at midday. The pressed pigment carries pattern at architectural scale, so an 8x8 grid of our Cairo or Sonora can organize a large floor plan without signage or paint.

The material also ages in public gracefully. Wear and time deepen the tone, and a commercial floor develops the lived-in character that hospitality designers spend budgets trying to reproduce. A cement floor set on opening day is a starting point, not a forever state, and five years of service reads as history rather than fatigue.

## Cement Tile as Restaurant Floor Tile

Restaurant floor tile has to do two jobs at once: hold up to service and give the room its personality. Cement tile handles both because the pattern is structural rather than applied. Our 8x8 formats set in patterns like Madrid or Zig Zag can define a dining room, while a single solid colorway such as Bone or Sage keeps an open kitchen line visually calm.

### Dining rooms and pattern at scale

A patterned cement floor does the decorative work a restaurant would otherwise assign to walls and fixtures. Bold repeats such as our Monstera or Stars & Cross establish the room's register from the entry, and quieter geometries let furniture and food lead. Designers often run pattern through the dining room and transition to a coordinating solid at service corridors, which uses the same material to mark front of house from back.

### Wet zones: entries, bar areas, and restrooms

Wet commercial floors need a slip plan, and cement tile accommodates one directly. For entries, bar areas, and restrooms, grout joints should measure at least 1/8", and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation. The joints add mechanical grip underfoot, and the treatment brings the cured surface to a wet-service standard. These two specifications belong in the project documents from the first drawing, not as an afterthought at punch list.

## Sealing and Maintenance in Commercial Settings

Sealing is the single most consequential step in a commercial cement tile installation. Cement is porous and unfired, so each installation must be sealed according to the [Zia installation guides](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides), whether the space is a dry dining room or a moisture-exposed entry or restroom. Commercial projects should also plan a resealing cycle keyed to traffic, since a hotel lobby and a boutique fitting room wear at different rates.

Routine care is a matter of consistency. Floors want pH-neutral cleaner and a soft mop, never acidic or harsh chemical cleaners, which can dull the pigment. Walk-off mats at entries keep grit from working at the surface, and prompt attention to spills keeps them at the sealed surface where they wipe away. A commercial cleaning crew briefed once on these habits can maintain a cement floor for decades.

Maintained this way, the floor's evolution works in the room's favor. The tone deepens with wear, bringing beauty to the material's aging process, and the patina becomes part of the venue's character rather than a maintenance problem.

## FAQ

### Is cement tile suited to restaurant and commercial floors?

Yes. Cement tile is formed under a hydraulic press with mineral pigment integral to the body, so the color and pattern hold through years of heavy foot traffic. With sealing at installation and a pH-neutral cleaning routine, a commercial cement floor serves for decades while developing the lived-in character that suits hospitality rooms.

### How is cement tile made?

Cement tile is made by hand-pouring mineral pigments into a patterned mold, backing the pigment with a cement body, and forming the tile under a hydraulic press. The tile cures rather than firing in a kiln, which is why the surface is naturally matte. Our tiles are made in Vietnam using a method that dates to 19th-century Europe.

### Does commercial cement tile need to be sealed, and how often?

Yes, sealing is required for each installation, in dry spaces as well as areas exposed to moisture. Commercial floors should then be resealed on a cycle matched to traffic levels, with high-traffic dining rooms and lobbies on the shorter end. The [installation guides](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides) cover sealer selection and application, and our team can advise on a resealing schedule at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### Is cement tile slip resistant enough for wet commercial areas?

Cement tile can meet wet-area requirements when the installation is specified for them. For wet commercial floors such as entries, bar areas, and restrooms, grout joints should measure at least 1/8", and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation. Together the joints and treatment bring the matte surface to wet-service performance.

### How is cement tile cleaned in a commercial setting?

Commercial cement tile is cleaned with a pH-neutral cleaner and a soft mop. Acidic and harsh chemical cleaners should never reach the surface, since they can dull the mineral pigment. Walk-off mats at entrances, routine dust mopping to control grit, and quick attention to spills complete the regimen.

### Can cement tile be used outdoors at a commercial property?

Cement tile is suitable for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates only. Patios, courtyards, and outdoor dining terraces in mild-winter regions can take our cement tiles with proper sealing, while properties in freeze/thaw climates should keep the material indoors.

### How much overage should a commercial cement tile order include?

Commercial orders should include 15-20% overage to account for curation and cuts. Projects that want a more uniform look across a large floor should plan for 25%, which gives installers more pieces to select from during dry layout.
