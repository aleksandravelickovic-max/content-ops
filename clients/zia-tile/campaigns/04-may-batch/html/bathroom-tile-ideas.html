Bathroom Tile Ideas

In a bathroom where natural light enters at an angle, Zia’s zellige on a shower wall reads differently than in any other room. The chips, pits, and crazing in the hand-glazed surface hold light rather than reflect it; by evening, the same wall is different again. That quality belongs to the material, not to how it was finished. Zia carries four materials suited to bathroom settings across residential and commercial projects: zellige from Morocco, marble from quarries in Italy and Spain, limestone from Fez and Bordeaux, and high-fired ceramics from Nagoya. zellige in the bathroom

On a shower wall, Zia’s zellige produces a surface that belongs to that specific space. The enclosed geometry of a shower concentrates the material’s character: chips, pits, and crazing in the hand-glazed surface catch light from every angle, and the dynamic glazes shift in tone as the room’s light changes through the day. A colorway that reads as clean white across an open backsplash carries warmth, shadow, and depth in the contained light of a shower. No two installations read identically.

Each tile is hand-pressed into a mold, sun-dried in Morocco, hand-shaped, and fired in an earthen kiln where heat moves unevenly through the chamber. That uneven firing produces the surface variation, and it is why the material rewards a space where light changes, which is what a bathroom does from morning to evening.

Format shapes how the surface reads in a bathroom. A 4x4 square produces an even field where the glaze variation carries the room. The 2x6 bejmat runs horizontally or vertically across a shower wall, orienting the surface along a clear axis. On a smaller wall, the directional format makes the space read larger than it is.

Zia’s zellige is suited for shower walls, interior floors, and pool and spa applications. In wet areas and dry spaces alike, seal according to the zellige Installation Guide. For floor installations, apply an anti-slip treatment after installation, use a minimum 1/8-inch grout joint, and ensure proper drainage. It is not suited for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates.

The zellige collection at Zia Tile spans more than 200 colorways, from quiet neutrals through saturated bolds suited to showers, floors, and pool and spa settings.

Marble floors and walls

The veining in Carrara marble reads one way on a bathroom wall and another across a floor. At eye level, the grey veins on a white ground produce movement across a vertical surface. Across a bathroom floor, the same stone becomes a field the eye travels over rather than reads straight on, and the inherent variation is felt as well as seen.

Zia’s marble comes from four quarries: Carrara in Tuscany, Nero Marquina from the Basque Country, Grigio Carnico from the Italian Alps, Rosso Alicante from Valencia. Each stone has its own character in a space. Nero Marquina’s white veins on a near-black ground produce a contrast that makes a small bathroom read as deliberate. Rosso Alicante brings warmth that reads more Mediterranean than most stones of its tone. Grigio Carnico sits quieter, its veining in a grey field that recedes rather than drives the composition.

On a wet floor, the finish decision is structural rather than aesthetic. The honed surface sits quietly underfoot: low in reflectivity, matte in register, carrying the same veining and tone as the polished version without the gloss that creates a hazard in wet conditions. Use a minimum 1/8-inch grout joint on wet floor installations and ensure proper drainage. Polished marble belongs on walls and in dry areas, where its reflective surface amplifies the room’s light. Marble requires sealing per the Zia Installation Guide.

Zia’s marble collection is suited to interior and exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates. It is not suitable for pools or spas.

Limestone for bathroom floors

Zia’s limestone arrives as solid quarried blocks from Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France, cut to tile and finished to one of two surfaces that read entirely differently in a space.

The honed finish is quiet: matte, close-set, absorbing light evenly across the field. On a bathroom floor, it reads as a decision about the room rather than the tile, the kind of surface that holds the space without drawing attention to itself. The bush-hammered finish is deliberately worked for grip and depth. It catches light unevenly, reads closer to uncut stone underfoot, and adds a tactile quality to a bathroom floor that honed and polished surfaces do not.

Both finishes meet the wet-floor standard for level interior applications in residential and commercial settings, honed at a DCOF of 0.50 and bush-hammered at 0.56, against the 0.42 threshold. Use a minimum 1/8-inch grout joint on wet floor installations and ensure proper drainage. Sealing follows a two-step protocol per the Limestone Installation Guide. Limestone is suited for exterior use in non-freeze/thaw climates. It is not suitable for pools or spas.

Both finishes are available in the limestone collection at Zia Tile.

Japanese ceramics in the bathroom

Zia’s ceramics are high-fired in Nagoya, Japan, where ceramic craft has been practiced for more than 1,000 years. Three finishes span the visual and performance range, and the difference between them is most consequential in a bathroom, where the surface is read in wet and dry conditions under changing light.

The matte finish is chalky and dense, reading close to unglazed stone in a space. In a shower, it holds its character in wet conditions and reads as resolved rather than shiny or rough. The satin finish catches light without reflecting it directly, shifting with the angle of light in the room. On a shower wall where natural light changes through the day, satin ceramic produces a surface that moves with the light rather than flattening it. The glossy finish reads as a mirror in strong light: on a shower wall, that reflection is a design decision; on a shower floor, it is not appropriate.

Matte (DCOF 0.74) and satin (DCOF 0.45) ceramic both clear the 0.42 wet-floor threshold and are suited for shower floors and walls. Glossy ceramic, at a DCOF of 0.30, is appropriate for shower walls only, not shower floors. In areas exposed to continuous moisture, seal ceramics per the Ceramics Installation Guide. Ensure proper drainage for all floor applications.

The ceramics collection at Zia Tile is intended for interior residential and commercial use. It is not suitable for pools, spas, or exterior installation.

The register each material holds

Zia’s four bathroom materials map to distinct design registers, from zellige and marble at the classical end to high-fired ceramics at the contemporary. Limestone occupies the middle. Where a bathroom sits in that range shapes every material and finish decision that follows.

The combination of zellige on a shower wall and marble on the floor occupies the classical register. Casablanca or Pure White zellige above, Carrara or Nero Marquina below: the two materials share surface variation and hand-formed or quarried origin without competing for attention. The dynamic glazes on the wall shift with the room’s light; the marble’s veining on the floor holds still. That relationship between movement and stillness is where the classical bathroom lives in Zia’s collection.

Honed limestone on the bathroom floor reads neither classical nor contemporary. It reads as a surface decision made without reference to trend: matte, close-set, holding the space without announcing itself. Against marble or zellige it provides a counterpoint that grounds the room. Against matte or satin ceramic on the walls, it anchors the space in natural stone while the ceramics carry the contemporary register.

Matte or satin ceramic on shower walls and floors produces the clearest contemporary result in Zia’s collection. The matte surface is chalky and dense; the satin surface shifts with the room’s light without the surface variation of handmade tile. Either finish against honed limestone or bush-hammered stone underfoot places a bathroom at the contemporary end of the arc Zia’s collection spans.

A bathroom holds more of a material’s character than most rooms. In a kitchen, backsplash tile reads across a few feet of wall. In a shower, the tile closes the space and registers every change in the light. Zia’s zellige in that enclosed space, shifting from morning to evening, tile by tile, is the argument for choosing a material with something to say rather than one that covers the surface and nothing more.

Frequently asked questions

What tile works best on a shower wall? All four of Zia’s bathroom materials are suited for shower walls and produce different results in the space. Zia’s zellige shows the most surface movement: chips, pits, and crazing shift with the room’s light through the day. Marble brings quarried stone with veining that reads as composition across a vertical surface. Honed or bush-hammered limestone sits quiet and deliberate, adding texture without pattern. Matte ceramic is chalky and resolved; satin ceramic shifts with the light in a way that reads as considered rather than shiny. The material and finish together determine how the shower reads.

Which tile is appropriate for a shower floor? Matte ceramic (DCOF 0.74), satin ceramic (DCOF 0.45), honed limestone (DCOF 0.50), and bush-hammered limestone (DCOF 0.56) all meet the 0.42 wet-floor threshold for level interior wet floors. Ensure proper drainage on all floor installations. Glossy ceramic (DCOF 0.30) and polished marble fall below the threshold and are not recommended for wet floor applications. On shower floors, Zia’s zellige requires an anti-slip treatment after installation.

Can zellige be used in a pool or spa? Yes. Zia’s zellige is approved for pool and spa applications. Always seal according to the zellige Installation Guide, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture. It is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates.

Does bathroom tile need to be sealed? All four materials require sealing. Always seal zellige in dry spaces and wet areas alike. Marble requires sealing per the installation instructions. Limestone follows a two-step sealing protocol. Ceramics require sealing in areas exposed to continuous moisture. See Zia’s Installation Guides for material-specific guidance.
