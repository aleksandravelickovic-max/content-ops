# Bathroom Tile Ideas

A zellige tile held under direct light shows chips, pits, and crazing that shift color and sheen with every change in angle. In a bathroom, that surface reads differently in morning light than it does in the evening. This is a quality built into the material, not applied to it, and it is the argument for handmade tile in a room where light changes throughout the day. Zia carries four tile materials suited to bathroom settings, each from a distinct tradition and place.

---

## zellige in the bathroom

Zia's zellige is hand-shaped in Morocco from local clay, glazed, and fired in earthen kilns. On shower walls and backsplashes, its chips, pits, and crazing catch the light from every angle, with dynamic glazes shifting color and sheen as light moves across the wall. No two installations read identically.

Zia's zellige is suited for pools and spas. Always seal zellige according to the [Zia Installation Guide](https://www.ziatile.com/pages/installation), whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates.

Zia's zellige collection includes colorways from Casablanca and Pure White through deeper, more saturated options. Browse the [zellige collection at Zia Tile](https://www.ziatile.com/collections/zellige).

## Marble floors and walls

On bathroom floors and shower walls, marble introduces surface variation that only quarried stone produces. Zia's stone comes from quarries in distinct regions: Carrara in Tuscany, Nero Marquina from the Basque Country, Grigio Carnico from the Italian Alps, and Rosso Alicante from Valencia. Each has its own veining pattern and tone, and natural stone variation holds consistently across the life of the installation.

On wet floors, honed marble is the appropriate finish; use a minimum 1/8-inch grout joint. Polished marble is not recommended for wet floor applications. On walls, each finish performs reliably. Marble requires sealing per the [installation instructions](https://www.ziatile.com/pages/installation).

Zia's marble can be used outdoors in non-freeze/thaw climates. It is not suitable for pools or spas. Browse the [marble collection at Zia Tile](https://www.ziatile.com/collections/marble).

## Limestone for bathroom floors

For bathroom floors, Zia's limestone comes in two finishes that perform differently in wet settings. Quarried from Fez, Morocco, and Bordeaux, France, the stone arrives as solid blocks cut to tile dimensions and finished either honed or bush-hammered. Honed is quiet and matte; bush-hammered carries a deliberate texture that adds grip and depth.

The honed surface has a DCOF of 0.50; bush-hammered reaches 0.56, both above the 0.42 threshold for level interior wet floors. Use a minimum 1/8-inch grout joint on wet floor installations. Limestone sealing follows a two-step protocol per the [Limestone Installation Guide](https://www.ziatile.com/pages/installation).

Limestone is not suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates or for pools and spas. Browse the [limestone collection at Zia Tile](https://www.ziatile.com/collections/limestone).

## Japanese ceramics in the bathroom

Zia's ceramics are high-fired in central Japan, in Nagoya, where ceramic craft has been practiced for more than 1,000 years. The matte finish is chalky and dense; satin catches light without reflecting it directly; glossy reads as a mirror surface in strong light. Each maps to distinct bathroom applications based on DCOF rating.

Matte ceramic has a DCOF of 0.74, making it appropriate for shower floors and walls. Satin ceramic has a DCOF of 0.45, suitable for shower floors and walls. Glossy ceramic, at 0.30, falls below the wet-floor threshold and is appropriate for shower walls only, not floors. In areas exposed to continuous moisture, seal ceramics per the [Zia Installation Guide](https://www.ziatile.com/pages/installation).

The ceramics line is intended for interior use. It is not suitable for pools, spas, or exterior installation. Browse the [ceramics collection at Zia Tile](https://www.ziatile.com/collections/ceramics).

---

Each of these materials is specified for a reason. Matte and honed finishes belong on wet floors; glossy and polished on walls and dry areas. Getting that assignment right at the specification stage is what determines how a material ages in the installation rather than against it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which tile is appropriate for a shower floor?**
Matte ceramic (DCOF 0.74), satin ceramic (DCOF 0.45), honed limestone (DCOF 0.50), and bush-hammered limestone (DCOF 0.56) are all above the 0.42 wet-floor threshold. Glossy ceramic (DCOF 0.30) and polished marble are not recommended for wet floor applications.

**Can zellige be used in a pool or spa?**
Yes. Zia's zellige is approved for pools and spas. Always seal according to the [Zia Installation Guide](https://www.ziatile.com/pages/installation). zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates.

**Does bathroom tile need to be sealed?**
All four materials covered here require sealing. zellige requires sealing in dry spaces and wet areas alike. Marble requires sealing per the installation instructions. Limestone follows a two-step sealing protocol. Ceramics require sealing in areas exposed to continuous moisture. See the [Zia Installation Guide](https://www.ziatile.com/pages/installation) for guidance by material.
