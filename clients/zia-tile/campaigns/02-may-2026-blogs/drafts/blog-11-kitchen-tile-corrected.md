# Kitchen Tile: Surface, Format, and Pattern

Two surfaces in a kitchen take tile, and they ask different things. The floor carries daily foot traffic, spills, and requires measurable slip resistance under wet conditions. The backsplash faces moisture and heat. A zellige backsplash and a zellige floor are not the same project. What follows covers both: the floor materials that clear the slip resistance threshold and the backsplash materials that reward more expressive choices.

## Kitchen Floor Tile: Materials and Threshold

Residential and commercial kitchen floors carry two requirements: a DCOF of 0.42 or higher for slip resistance under wet conditions, and a material character suited to anchoring the room's design.

The floor sets the visual ground for everything above it, including cabinetry, countertops, and the backsplash. Five materials in Zia's collection meet the technical threshold; which one fits depends on surface character, format, and how much visual weight the floor should carry.

* [Ceramics](https://ziatile.com/collections/ceramics) (matte): Designed in Los Angeles, handcrafted near Nagoya. Seal before and after grouting per Zia's [Ceramic Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/ceramics-installation-guide); reseal every one to two years.

* [Cotto](https://ziatile.com/collections/cotto): Unglazed terra cotta handcrafted near San Miguel de Allende. Each piece develops natural patina over time. Seal before and after grouting per Zia's [Cotto Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cotto-installation-guide).

* [Cement tile](https://ziatile.com/collections/cement-tile): Formed rather than fired, producing a matte, pigment-driven surface. Zia's [Atomic 8x8](https://ziatile.com/products/atomic-red-cement-tile) Cement brings a high-contrast geometric pattern to a kitchen floor. Seal before and after grouting; reseal every one to two years.

* [Terrazzo](https://ziatile.com/collections/terrazzo): Ground and polished aggregate, available in 12x12 and 24x24 formats. Zia's [Tamarisk 12x12](https://ziatile.com/products/tamarisk-12x12) suits kitchens where the floor anchors the room without competing with cabinetry. Seal before and after grouting.

* [Limestone](https://ziatile.com/collections/limestone-tile): Quarried near Fez and Bordeaux, finished in honed or bush-hammered surface. Zia's [Monument 12x12 Honed Limestone](https://ziatile.com/products/monument-12x12-honed-limestone-tile) brings quiet material depth at large format. Seal before and after grouting.

Wet kitchen floors require a minimum 1/8" grout joint; anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation. Ensure floor installations have proper drainage.

## Choosing Backsplash Tile

The backsplash sits at eye level and carries the kitchen's most visible tile surface. Without a DCOF requirement, the selection opens to Zia's full material range. What guides the choice is material character: how light moves across the surface, how pattern reads in the room, and how the tile relates to the cabinetry around it.

* [Zellige](https://ziatile.com/collections/zellige): Each tile is hand-pressed, hand-glazed, and kiln-fired; chips, pits, and crazing present as signature features. The dynamic glazes shift with kitchen light throughout the day. Zia's [Burnt Sugar 4x4](https://ziatile.com/products/burnt-sugar-zellige-tile) and [Portuguese Blue 4x4](https://ziatile.com/products/portuguese-blue-zellige-tile) suit kitchen backsplash installations.

* Cement tile: Matte and pigment-driven, cement tile becomes a pattern surface at backsplash scale. [Zia's Cotton 2x8](https://ziatile.com/products/cotton-2x8-cement-tile) installed horizontally emphasizes the horizontal plane of the backsplash without interrupting the cabinetry above.

* Ceramics (satin and glossy): Satin and glossy finishes suit backsplash walls where light reflection is a design priority.

## Finish, Format, and Grout

Each variable has a distinct effect: finish affects both slip resistance and light response; format affects grout joint density and how the room reads spatially; grout color and joint width determine whether the tile's geometry amplifies or recedes.

### Finish and Slip Resistance

Matte finishes scatter light and meet the 0.42 DCOF threshold for wet floor applications. Glossy and satin finishes reflect light and suit backsplashes and walls.

Glossy ceramics fall below the 0.42 wet-floor threshold and are not suited to kitchen or shower floors. On dry residential floors, glossy ceramics are an option.

### Format and Scale

Larger-format tiles (12x12 and above) reduce grout joint count and suit open kitchen layouts. Smaller formats (2x2, 4x4) suit backsplash surfaces and add design movement.

A large-format floor tile or a horizontally set backsplash opens a smaller kitchen.

### Grout Color and Joint Width 

Grout color and joint width change the reading of any tile field. Contrasting grout amplifies the geometry of the tile; matching grout lets the surface read as a continuous material.

For cement tile, avoid contrasting grout unless using a grout bag; the surface absorbs pigment on contact. Full grout specifications by material are covered in Zia's [Installation Guides](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides).

Wear protective face and eye coverings when working with thinset, sealants, and grout. See [Proposition 65 Warnings](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). 

Finish transitions with mitered edges or Schluter strips, and bring the drywall flush with the finished tile surface. Sealing schedules and full grout specifications for each material are covered in Zia's [Installation Guides](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides). 

## The Full Kitchen Tile Selection

Kitchen tile selection divides cleanly between two surfaces, each with its own logic. For floors: DCOF above 0.42, with matte ceramics, Cotto, cement tile, terrazzo, and limestone each qualifying. 

For backsplashes: no technical threshold, which opens the full material range; zellige, cement tile, and ceramics in satin or glossy finishes each work, with material character, light response, and pattern driving the choice.

Finish, format, and grout calibrate both. Matte finishes meet the wet-floor DCOF threshold; glossy finishes do not and suit walls and backsplashes, typically. 

Larger formats suit open kitchen layouts; smaller formats suit backsplash surfaces. Grout color either amplifies the tile's geometry or lets it read as a continuous field.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Kitchen Tiles

### What is the best tile for kitchen floors? 

The best tile for kitchen floors combines a DCOF of 0.42 or higher with a finish that holds up under daily cleaning. Zia's matte ceramics (DCOF 0.74), Cotto (DCOF 0.98), and cement tile (DCOF greater than 1\) are strong choices for high-traffic kitchen floors. All three are approved for high-traffic interior use and require standard sealing and grout protocols.

### Is zellige a good choice for a kitchen? 

For kitchen backsplashes, zellige is a strong choice. The dynamic glazes, along with chips, pits, and crazing that present as signature features of each tile, make zellige a distinctly material-forward backsplash choice. Zia's [Casablanca 4x4](https://ziatile.com/products/casablanca-white-zellige-tile), Burnt Sugar 4x4, and Portuguese Blue 4x4 suit kitchen backsplash installations.

### Can I use cement tile in a kitchen? 

Yes. Cement tile is approved for high-traffic interior floors and walls. Because cement tile is formed rather than fired, it requires sealing before and after grouting per Zia's [Cement Tile Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cement-installation-guide). Reseal every one to two years, and clean daily with a pH-neutral product.

### What tile is best for a kitchen backsplash? 

Zellige, satin ceramics, and cement tile are common choices for kitchen backsplash tile. Because backsplash tile carries no DCOF requirement, the selection shifts to finish, pattern, and material character. Zia's [zellige in 2x2 squares](https://ziatile.com/collections/zellige?filter=2x2-squares) or trapezoid formats, and cement tile in geometric patterns are strong options for a kitchen backsplash installation.
