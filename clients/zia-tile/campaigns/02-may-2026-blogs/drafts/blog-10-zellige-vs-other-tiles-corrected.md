# When to Choose Zellige vs. Other Tiles: A Material Selection Guide

A handcrafted Moroccan art form, zellige tile is defined by the characteristics produced when clay is hand-shaped and individually fired: chips, pits, and crazing. Zia's zellige collection spans dozens of colorways, including Casablanca, Desert Bloom, and Burnt Sugar, handmade in Morocco using techniques in practice for more than 800 years.

## What Zellige Tile Is

A glazed clay tile hand-formed and kiln-fired in Fez, zellige's production process determines its surface characteristics and application limits.

### Clay, Glaze, and Kiln: How Each Tile Is Made

Each tile begins with raw clay from near Fez, soaked and filtered before being hand-pressed into molds and left to dry in the sun. Zellige is then hand-shaped to its final form, hand-dipped in glaze, and fired in an earthen kiln. 

The kiln's uneven heat distribution produces the color shifts and surface movement visible across a finished installation. Conditions vary from position to position within the firing, and no two tiles emerge identical.

The variation is structural, not incidental. Differences in color, shape, and thickness are produced by the process and reflect the artisan's work on each piece.

### Chips, Pits, and Crazing as Characteristics of the Material

Three surface characteristics define glazed zellige: chips, pits, and crazing. Chips are edge-level marks from hand-cutting and handling. Pits are small depressions in the glaze layer. Crazing is a network of fine cracks in the glaze surface, produced by differential cooling between the glaze and the clay body during firing.

All three are expected in authentic zellige and present in each installation. Together, they define the surface texture and the way the material interacts with light.

## Where Zellige Performs Well

The glazed surface and inherent variation of zellige perform best where light interaction and material character work in the installation's favor: walls, wet areas, and surfaces read at close range.

### Kitchen Backsplashes and Walls

The dynamic glazes of zellige, the way color and sheen shift with changes in light, read clearly on vertical surfaces. A backsplash plane in Casablanca or Burnt Sugar functions as a lit architectural surface rather than a static backdrop. Zia's [zellige collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/zellige) includes 4x4 squares, 2x6 bejmat, and trapezoid formats suited to kitchen walls, along with mesh-backed mosaics for compact installations.

### Shower Walls and Floors

Glazed zellige is approved for shower walls and floors. Sealing, per the installation instructions, is required for shower applications. On shower floors, grout joints should measure a minimum of 1/8 inch, with anti-slip treatment applied after installation. Ensure floor installations have proper drainage. Protective equipment is required during cutting and installation; see Zia's [Proposition 65 Warnings](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

### Pools, Spas, and Wet Areas

Glazed zellige is approved for pools and spas. Sealing according to Zia's [zellige installation guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/zellige-installation-guide) is required for all wet-area applications. For sealer selection and installation sequencing in pool and spa applications, consult the guide or contact Zia's team directly.

### Fireplace Surrounds and Accent Walls

The thickness variation and surface character of zellige read as tactile depth at close range. The glazed surface responds to the shifting light of a fire as it does to daylight: color and sheen shift across the plane. Vertical placement removes floor-application requirements. [Jade in 4x4 format](https://ziatile.com/products/jade-zellige-tile) holds its depth against the neutral of plaster or the texture of natural stone.

## Where Zellige Tile Has Limits

The glazed surface and inherent variation that define zellige's character also define where it cannot go.

### Outdoor Installations and Freeze/Thaw Climates

Glazed zellige is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates. Moisture penetrates the material during freeze cycles and can cause the glaze to crack and separate. In non-freeze/thaw climates, zellige can be installed outdoors on exterior walls and patios in mild regions.

For outdoor installations in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions, Zia's [Cotto](https://ziatile.com/collections/cotto), unglazed terra cotta, is the appropriate handmade tile. Cotto is suitable for exterior use in any climate, including regions with hard winters.

### High-Traffic Commercial Floors

For high-traffic interior floors, zellige is approved with proper grout joint sizing and anti-slip specification in wet applications. 

For commercial projects where dimensional consistency is the primary requirement, two Zia materials cover the range. Zia's Cotto offers a DCOF of 0.98 and suits any climate, including freeze/thaw. [Zia's cement tile](https://ziatile.com/collections/cement-tile) reaches a DCOF above 1.0 across eight formats in patterned and solid colorways.

### Projects That Require a Uniform Surface

Zellige's color, edge, and thickness variations are inherent to the material. 

For projects where a consistent surface is the design intent, [Zia's terrazzo](https://ziatile.com/collections/terrazzo) is hydraulically pressed and ground to a uniform 12x12 finish. ## Zellige vs. Cement Tile

Both zellige and cement tile are handmade and require sealing, but their visual logic differs. Zellige works through a glazed surface that responds to light; cement tile works through geometric pattern that reads at scale.

### Surface Character and Pattern Range

As a glazed surface, zellige carries color and sheen that shift with changes in light, driven by glaze chemistry and kiln conditions.

Cement tile is matte and pattern-driven; its composition reads most fully across a field of tiles at scale rather than through surface sheen. Both develop surface character over time, but through different mechanisms.

### Maintenance and Sealing Compared

Both materials require sealing. Cement tile requires sealing before grouting because the porous cement surface absorbs grout if left unprotected. Glazed zellige requires sealing in all installations, including dry spaces; sealing is applied after grouting.

Cement tile's matte surface is more vulnerable to oil and acid staining when under-sealed. Its glaze provides more surface resistance to staining, though sealing in wet applications remains required.

### When to Specify Cement Tile Instead

Cement tile is the stronger specification for floor applications where geometric pattern is the design intent, for exterior installations in non-freeze/thaw climates, and for projects where a lower per-square-foot cost is a factor. 

Zia's [cement tile collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/cement-tile) covers eight formats across patterned and solid colorways for interior and exterior applications.

## Zellige vs. Mass-Produced Lookalikes

Factory-made zellige-look tiles share the visual reference but not the material.

### What Mass-Produced Alternatives Can Replicate

Mass-produced zellige-look tiles replicate the visual texture of a glazed clay surface through molded surface relief and applied patterning. They are dimensionally consistent, frost-resistant, and available at a lower cost per square foot. 

For projects where a glazed surface with controlled variation is the priority, [Zia's ceramic collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/ceramics), high-fired in Japan and available in matte, satin, and glossy finishes, suits interior walls, floors, and wet areas across all climates.

### What They Cannot Replicate

The chips, pits, and crazing in authentic zellige are produced by hand-cutting, hand-dipping in glaze, and individual kiln conditions. Each tile's surface characteristics emerge from the process rather than being applied uniformly across a batch. 

Mass-produced alternatives apply a repeating texture pattern to a consistent body; at scale, the repetition reads differently from the genuine variation of a handmade material. The glaze in authentic zellige was applied by hand in varying thickness; the optical behavior that produces is tied to the material's process, not a print layer.

### When Authentic Zellige Is Worth the Cost

Authentic zellige earns its specification for wall and wet-area applications where the material's surface character is central to the design intent, for spaces read at close range by an experienced eye, and for projects where long-term material quality matters.

## Zellige vs. Cotto

Both zellige and Cotto are handmade clay tiles, but they come from different traditions and perform differently. Zellige is glazed and fired in Morocco; Cotto is unglazed terra cotta from Mexico. The two are often specified together on the same project, with zellige covering interior surfaces and Cotto extending to applications where zellige cannot go.

### Glazed vs. Unglazed: How the Surface Changes the Application

Of the two, zellige is glazed; Zia's Cotto is unglazed terra cotta. Zellige's glaze creates a reflective, color-variable surface. Cotto's unglazed surface develops a natural patina with wear and time, deepening in tone as the material ages.

The two materials read differently: zellige through sheen and color shift; Cotto through warmth and the visible character of aging clay. 

Sealing protocols also differ. Cotto requires sealing before grouting; glazed zellige requires sealing in all installations, including dry spaces, with sealing applied after grouting.

### Freeze/Thaw and Outdoor Performance by Material

Of the three handmade materials, only Cotto is suitable for outdoor use in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions. 

Glazed zellige and cement tile are both limited to non-freeze/thaw climates outdoors. 

For projects that span interior and exterior applications across cold climates, Cotto is the specification for the exterior and zellige for the interior wet areas. [Zia's Cotto collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/cotto) spans six colorways, from sun-bleached Adobe to matte black Oscura, across square, rectangle, hexagon, and specialty shapes suited to exterior floors, courtyards, and patios.

## Zellige vs. Marble

Both zellige and marble are often considered for the same surfaces: shower walls, kitchen backsplashes, and accent applications where material variation is the design intent. Both vary tile to tile and develop surface character over time, but they come from different origins and carry different application rules.

### Surface Variation and Aging Behavior

Both materials carry inherent variation: marble through geological veining and mineral content, zellige through glaze chemistry and kiln conditions.

Marble's variation is fixed at the quarry. The dynamic glazes of zellige continue to respond to light as the installation ages. Neither material ages poorly with proper installation and care; both develop surface character over time through mechanisms tied to their respective materials and processes.

### Where Each Material Has an Advantage

Marble is the stronger specification for durable floor applications requiring a honed or polished surface. Both marble and zellige can be installed outdoors in non-freeze/thaw climates; neither is suitable for freeze/thaw exterior applications. 

[Zia's marble collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/marble-tile) spans Carrara, Nero Marquina, and four additional stones from Italy and Spain, available honed for floor and wall applications.

For pools and spas, where marble is not suited, and for installations requiring format flexibility, zellige holds the advantage. Zia's zellige formats, including bejmat, trapezoid, 3.5 hex, 2x2, and 4x4, cover surfaces and scales where standard marble dimensions do not apply.

## Planning a Zellige Installation

The handmade character of zellige affects every stage of the installation process, from how much tile to order to who sets it.

### How Much to Order: Overage and Variation

The thickness variation of zellige means more tiles are evaluated and set aside during installation than with a dimensionally consistent material. Order with that in mind:

* A minimum overage of 15% above measured square footage for standard installations, to account for curation and cuts during dry layout.

* Up to 25% for projects requiring a more uniform appearance, allowing greater selection when distributing variation across the installation.

* Order the full quantity from a single production run. Glaze characteristics shift between batches, and a reorder that does not match the original will be visible in the finished installation.

### Grout Selection and Joint Width

The variable edge profile of zellige makes grout color a visible design decision, not just a finishing detail. Set grout joints at a minimum of 1/8 inch for all floor applications, including wet floors.

A grout close in tone to the tile reads as a continuous surface, while a contrasting grout establishes a defined grid.

### What to Look for in an Installer

Zellige requires an installer with direct experience in handmade tile. The material's chips, pits, and crazing are expected characteristics, not errors. An installer unfamiliar with the material may treat chips, pits, and crazing as errors and incorrectly adjust the installation.

Key qualifications to confirm:

* Back-butters each tile before setting.  
* Ensures the tiling surface is well prepared and leveled before installation begins.  
* Completes a full dry layout before setting begins.  
* Can provide completed zellige project references.


Finish transitions with mitered edges or Schluter strips, and bring the drywall flush with the finished tile surface. For the full installation sequence, refer to Zia's [Zellige Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/zellige-installation-guide).
