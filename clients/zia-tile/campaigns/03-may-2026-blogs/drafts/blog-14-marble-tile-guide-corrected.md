# Every Type of Marble Tile Worth Knowing

Carrara and Nero Marquina are both European marble, but they read as entirely different materials: one a soft white with feathery grey veining, the other a deep black with sharp white lines. The variety in color, veining structure, and geological origin is why specifying marble begins with understanding which marble. Zia's marble collection covers six varieties from Italy and Spain, all in honed finish, across a range of formats suited to floors, walls, and accent surfaces.

## What Makes Marble Distinctive

Marble forms when limestone is subjected to heat and pressure over geological time. The metamorphic process recrystallizes the calcium carbonate into the dense, smooth material quarried today. The veining visible across each tile is a record of mineral intrusions during that process, producing the full range of colors across the collection: from white and grey to black, green, gold, and burgundy.

### Natural Stone Variation

No two marble tiles are identical. Variation in tone, veining density, and color distribution is inherent to the material and increases the further a tile is cut from the center of the block. ## The Varieties in Zia's Marble Collection

Zia's marble collection draws from quarries in Italy and Spain. Each variety has a distinct geological character: different veining structure, color palette, and behavior in light.

### Carrara

Carrara comes from Tuscany, Italy. The background is white to soft grey, with feathery grey veining that ranges from delicate to pronounced depending on the slab. It appears in a wide range of residential and commercial interiors, from kitchens and baths to hospitality spaces.

On shower floors, Carrara requires anti-slip treatment after installation. Its DCOF falls below the threshold recommended for wet-floor applications; grout joint sizing and an anti-slip product applied to the finished installation bring it within specification.

### Nero Marquina

Nero Marquina is a Spanish marble. The base is a deep black with sharp white veining that reads as architectural geometry rather than organic movement. 

Against white walls or natural wood, it provides a strong tonal contrast; no additional patterning is needed to read as a design decision. It is available in square and rectangular formats.

### Grigio Carnico

Grigio Carnico is an Italian grey marble with a tighter, more uniform veining structure than Carrara. 

The tone sits between warm and cool grey. It suits installations where marble's character is wanted without the polarizing contrast of the darker stones.

### Rosso Alicante

Rosso Alicante is a Spanish marble with a deep burgundy base and white and cream veining. 

Honed, it reads warm and richly toned. It suits feature walls, fireplace surrounds, and floors where a deep, saturated color is the primary material choice.

### Giallo Reale

Giallo Reale comes from northern Italy. The background is warm gold to cream, with amber and rust veining. It reads as one of the warmer marbles in the collection, suited to spaces where warmth rather than contrast is the goal. 

Giallo Reale is suited to floors and walls and pairs with unlacquered brass and natural wood.

### Verde Alpi

Verde Alpi is a green marble from Italy with a dense background and fine white veining. Against light stone, plaster, or white walls, it reads as a strong material choice without requiring contrast to land. It is available honed.

## The Honed Finish

All marble in Zia's collection is honed. A honed finish is ground to a flat, smooth surface, producing a matte face that reduces light reflectivity. Honed marble shows scratches and surface etching less readily than a high-gloss surface. The finish suits every application in the collection: floors, walls, shower walls, and outdoor surfaces in non-freeze/thaw climates.

Watermarks, fingerprints, and etching are more visible on polished marble and require more frequent attention. For most floors, showers, and outdoor applications, a honed finish holds up better through daily wear without the upkeep a polished surface demands.

## The Surfaces That Suit Marble

Marble performs across a wide range of applications, including floors, walls, showers, and outdoor surfaces in mild climates.

### Floors

Marble floors suit residential and commercial spaces where the material's tonal variation and veining are meant to do design work at scale. Larger formats, 12x12 and 12x24, let each tile's variation read as part of a continuous surface; smaller formats increase visual complexity.

For wet-floor applications, Carrara requires anti-slip treatment and careful grout joint specification. Ensure floor installations have proper drainage.

### Walls and Shower Walls

Marble is approved for shower walls. Sealing, per the installation guide, is required for all wet-area applications. The honed finish suits shower walls well, reading as a calm, matte surface against tile or plaster surroundings.

For shower floors, specify grout joints and anti-slip treatment according to the variety's DCOF rating and consult Zia's [marble installation guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/marble-installation-guide) for the full sealing sequence.

### Outdoor Applications

Marble is suitable for outdoor use in non-freeze/thaw climates: patios, outdoor living surfaces, and building cladding in mild regions. It is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates, where moisture penetration during freeze cycles compromises the stone over time.

## What Marble Requires

Marble is a calcium carbonate stone. It reacts to acid, absorbs liquids when unsealed, and requires more attentive maintenance than a glazed surface. Both etching and staining are preventable with proper specification and sealing.

### Etching vs. Staining

These are two distinct forms of surface damage and require different responses. 

Etching is a chemical reaction: acidic liquids react with the calcium carbonate in marble and dull the surface in the area of contact. It cannot be removed by cleaning; it requires professional re-honing to correct. On a honed surface, light etching is less visible than it would be on a high-gloss finish.

Staining is physical: a substance penetrates the stone and leaves a residual color. It is addressed by sealing and prompt spill management. A properly sealed marble surface resists staining; an unsealed surface is vulnerable.

### Sealing and Cleaning

Marble is a porous stone that requires sealing at multiple stages of installation, not only in wet areas. The sequence matters: an unsealed surface absorbs grout, cleaning products, and spills.

Sealing:

* Seal the full surface before grouting. Consult Zia's installation guide for product selection; requirements vary by application.

* Apply a top coat after grouting. Interior and outdoor installations require different products; see the installation guide.

* Re-seal as needed based on traffic and wear. Shower floors and high-traffic surfaces require more frequent attention than dry interior walls.

* Dry interior spaces still require sealing. Marble's porosity makes unsealed surfaces vulnerable to staining even without direct moisture exposure.

Cleaning:

* Use pH-neutral cleaners for all routine maintenance.

* Avoid acidic cleaners, including vinegar and citrus-based products. Acidic contact causes etching, which dulls the surface and cannot be removed by cleaning.

* Avoid abrasive pads or scrubbing tools.

* Wipe spills promptly. Pigmented liquids penetrate under-sealed marble and leave residual staining.

### What is the difference between etching and staining on marble?

Etching is corrected by professional re-honing; staining is prevented by sealing.
