# Limestone Tile: Honed vs. Brushed Finishes and Care

Limestone tile begins as building stone, cut from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France before it becomes a floor, wall, or path. Because the tile is sliced from the block whole, each piece keeps the mineral character of its bed: soft shifts in tone, natural stone variation, and a surface that develops a natural patina over time. The finish decides how that character reads in a room. Honed limestone is ground smooth and matte. Bush-hammered limestone, the textured finish shoppers often search as brushed limestone, carries a fine, stippled grain that catches light and adds grip underfoot.

## What Is Limestone Tile?

Limestone tile is natural stone cut from sedimentary rock composed largely of calcium carbonate, the same chemistry that makes it responsive to acids and gives it a soft, chalky depth of color. Unlike ceramic or cement tile, nothing is molded or fired. [Zia's limestone collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/limestone-tile) is quarried near Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France, two regions with long histories of building in pale stone.

Formats run from 4x12 and 6x6 through 12x12, 12x24, and 24x24, with plank formats in varying lengths at 12 and 20 inches wide. Each tile differs from its neighbor in tone and surface, and that natural stone variation is inherent to quarried limestone, not a flaw to correct.

## Honed vs. Brushed Limestone Tile

The difference between honed and brushed limestone is surface texture, and it changes how the same stone looks, feels, and behaves underfoot. Honing grinds the surface flat and matte. Brushing and bush-hammering leave a worked texture that reads older, adds traction, and folds daily wear into the surface.

### Honed limestone

Honed limestone is ground with abrasives until the surface sits flat, smooth, and matte, with no reflective sheen. The finish suits contemporary interiors because it lets the tone of the stone lead: light moves evenly across a honed floor, and grout joints recede into the field. It is the finish to reach for on kitchen floors, bathroom walls, and open living areas where the stone should read calm and continuous.

A honed surface also shows the stone's chemistry most plainly. Etching from acidic spills sits visibly on a smooth field, so honed installations reward prompt cleanup and pH-neutral care. Sealed and maintained, a honed limestone floor keeps that quiet surface for decades.

### Brushed limestone and our bush-hammered finish

Brushed limestone, as most suppliers use the term, is stone worked with wire brushes until the softer material lifts away and a gentle, undulating surface remains. Our collection arrives at a similar surface by a different route: the finish is bush-hammered, struck with a multi-pointed hammer that leaves a fine, stippled grain across the face of each tile. A search for brushed limestone and our Bush Hammered listings point to the same design intent: a matte stone surface with visible relief and real grip.

The texture earns its place in entries, hallways, and any floor that sees water or grit. It also softens the stone visually, giving a new installation the settled look of a floor decades into its life. Where honed limestone reads crisp, bush-hammered limestone reads weathered in the best sense.

### How to choose between the two finishes

The room and the surface decide the finish. Honed limestone belongs where smoothness matters: kitchen and bath walls, low-traffic floors, and rooms where the design calls for an even, matte plane. Bush-hammered limestone belongs where texture matters: entries, mudrooms, exterior floors in mild climates, and wet floors where traction counts.

Many projects use both. A honed field on the walls with a bush-hammered floor keeps the palette identical while giving each surface the finish its job requires. Ordering a few samples of each finish in the same tone makes the comparison concrete before a full order ships.

Limestone flooring changes with scale. A 24x24 field gives an open room a broad, quiet plane, while a 6x6 format or a plank run gives the eye more joints, more rhythm, and more evidence of the hand setting the stone. The material suits most interior rooms and exterior settings without hard freezes.

### Kitchens and living areas

Large formats suit open rooms. The varying-length planks in 12 and 20 inch widths, set in offset rows, give a room the rhythm of a traditional stone floor. ### Bathrooms and showers

Limestone suits shower walls and bathroom floors, and sealing is what makes those installations last. Always seal limestone according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as showers. On wet floors, the geometry of the installation does some of the work: grout joints should measure at least 1/8", with an anti-slip treatment applied after installation. Smaller formats such as 6x6 work well on shower floors, where the tighter grid of joints reads as a fine texture underfoot and adds footing.

### Exteriors in mild climates

Our limestone suits exterior floors, patios, and courtyards in non-freeze/thaw climates, where the bush-hammered finish gives an outdoor surface honest texture. It is not suitable for exterior installation in freeze/thaw regions, and it is not suitable for pools or spas. In the right climate, an exterior limestone floor develops its patina in the open air.

## How to Care for Limestone Tile

Care for limestone tile is less about keeping the stone new than keeping its surface legible as it ages. Limestone is porous, so each installation is sealed before and after grouting. Our [limestone installation guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides) covers the full two-step pre-grout sealing and top-coat protocol, along with the correct materials for each setting.

Cleaning is a matter of what to avoid as much as what to do. A pH-neutral stone cleaner and a soft mop or cloth are all the routine asks, and a honed floor kept this way holds its matte surface and the soft, chalky depth of color beneath it. Acidic or abrasive cleaners, including vinegar and citrus-based formulas, etch the calcium carbonate surface and dull the finish. Sweeping or vacuuming with a soft attachment before mopping keeps grit from scratching the stone, a small habit that preserves the smooth face of a honed floor and the stippled relief of a bush-hammered one.

Resealing depends on traffic and exposure rather than a fixed calendar. When water stops beading on the surface and starts soaking in quickly, the floor is ready for a fresh coat. Between sealings, the stone quietly does what limestone does: the tone deepens with wear and time, and that patina is the point.

## Limestone Tile FAQ

### What is the difference between honed and brushed limestone?

Honed limestone is ground flat, smooth, and matte, while brushed limestone is textured with wire brushes for a soft, worked surface. ### Can limestone tile be used in a shower?

Yes, limestone works on shower walls and shower floors when sealed according to our installation instructions.
