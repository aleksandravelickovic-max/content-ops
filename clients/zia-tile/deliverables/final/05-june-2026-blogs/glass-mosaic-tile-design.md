# Glass Mosaic Tile: Pool, Bathroom, and Backsplash Design

Glass mosaic tile turns water, daylight, and small-scale pattern into one surface. Each of our glass mosaics begins in Northern Spain, where 98% recycled glass is formed into small pieces and hand-placed on a 12x12 grid. The collection marks our first foray into glass and our most sustainable material to date, produced in a facility that relies primarily on renewable wind and solar energy. It is rated for outdoor use in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions, and the same small format carries onto shower walls and kitchen backsplashes, where a larger tile would flatten the surface.

## What Is Glass Mosaic Tile?

Glass mosaic tile is small-format glass tile mounted on a grid so an entire sheet sets at once. Our glass mosaics are hand-placed on 12x12 grids, with solid colorways in a 1x1 format and seven patterns that arrive ready to set. Because artisans assemble each grid by hand, placement shifts from sheet to sheet, and color and sheen vary across the installation. That variation is inherent to the material and gives a finished wall its depth.

Color behaves differently in glass than in glazed clay. The hue runs through the full thickness of the tile, so the palette reads especially vibrant, and the sheen shifts as daylight moves through the room. A wall of our Piscine looks different at noon than it does at dusk, and that movement is the point.

## Glass Pool Tile

Glass pool tile begins as a light decision before it becomes a technical one. Our glass mosaics are rated for full submersion in pools and spas, in both saltwater and chlorine systems. The collection also belongs outdoors in freeze/thaw climates, so the same hand-placed grids that line a pool in Los Angeles can carry color at a mountain house where winters freeze hard.

## Glass Mosaics in the Bathroom

Bathrooms reward small-format glass because the room asks so much of the surface: water, light, slope, corners, and plumbing all meet in a tight footprint. The 12x12 grids cut cleanly around niches and curbs, and the glass surface makes the most of whatever daylight or task light the room offers. Glass itself requires no sealing, and only the grout takes an optional coat, a distinction that separates this collection from our clay and stone lines.

### Shower Walls

Shower walls suit glass mosaic tile from floor to ceiling. The material is non-porous, so daily water contact leaves the surface unchanged, and the grid format wraps the wall in continuous pattern. ### Shower Floors

Shower floors take grout joints of at least 1/8", with an anti-slip treatment applied after installation, and at that width the joints draw a fine grid that reads as part of the floor's design. The small format is an advantage here, because a 1x1 tile follows the slope toward the drain more gracefully than a large-format tile can manage. Solid colorways keep a shower floor calm underfoot, and Rye or Milk grounds a patterned wall without competing against it.

## Grout and Color Decisions

Grout decides how a glass mosaic reads from across the room. A color-matched grout lets the sheet dissolve into a single surface, so the eye registers the overall tone before the individual tiles. A contrasting grout draws the outline of each tile and makes the grid itself part of the design.

Both approaches have a place. Color-matching favors patterned grids, where the composition should carry the eye, and contrast favors solid fields, where the joint lines supply the rhythm. A dry layout before setting shows how each approach reads in place.

### Can glass mosaic tile be installed in a shower?

Glass mosaic tile suits both shower walls and shower floors. Walls take the material with no special provisions, because glass is non-porous.
