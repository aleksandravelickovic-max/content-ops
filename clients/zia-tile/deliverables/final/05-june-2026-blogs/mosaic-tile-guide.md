# Mosaic Tile: What It Is and How Designers Use It

Mosaic tile turns scale into the design. Individual pieces of marble, glass, or glazed clay, most measuring two inches or less, gather into a field that reads one way from across the room and another up close. Our Roman Mosaics carry the form in its oldest tradition, marble chiseled into 5/8-inch squares and set on mesh in solids, fans, and checks.

The small format is the point. Designers reach for mosaic where larger tile cannot go, on the pitched floor of a shower, along a curved wall, inside a niche, and wherever a surface should read as texture from across the room and as pattern up close. Our Glass Mosaics, hand-placed in Northern Spain, and the mesh-backed mosaics in our zellige collection extend the same logic into glass and glazed clay.

## What Is Mosaic Tile?

Mosaic tile is any tile built from small pieces, called tesserae, that combine into a larger design across a floor or wall. Most pieces measure two inches or smaller, and many are far finer than that. The scale, more than the material, defines the category, since marble, glass, ceramic, and glazed clay all appear in mosaic form.

The form dates to ancient Rome, where artisans set cut stone across villa floors and bath houses in decorative geometric and floral shapes. Each floor came together one small square at a time, and many still read clearly two thousand years later.

Modern mosaic keeps the pattern and drops the labor of piece-by-piece assembly. Sheets arrive with the tesserae already arranged on mesh backing, so an involved pattern installs sheet by sheet, with the joints between sheets disappearing into the grid.

## How Designers Use Mosaic Tile

The scale of mosaic changes how a room reads, and designers use that shift surface by surface. A field of 5/8-inch squares behaves differently from a field of 4x4s. The texture sits quieter, the grout grid runs denser, and the small pieces can follow a curve that a larger tile cannot.

### Kitchen Backsplashes

A mosaic backsplash sets pattern into the run between countertop and upper cabinets, at a scale the eye can hold from a seat across the kitchen. The tight grid suits kitchen conditions, including the heat and moisture near a range. Our Check in Carrara and Nero Marquina delivers a checkerboard at miniature scale, while a solid field in Giallo Reale or Verde Alpi reads as a single plane of color with the texture of hand-cut stone.

### Bathrooms and Shower Floors

Each small tile brings its own grout joints, and those joints give a shower floor grip underfoot. The small format also follows the slope of a shower pan toward the drain, where a larger tile would need cutting. Stone and zellige mosaics in showers require sealing per our Installation Guide, applied in wet areas and dry spaces alike. Wet commercial floors ask more of that grid: grout joints there should measure at least 1/8 inch, with an anti-slip treatment applied after installation, so the same dense pattern that gives the floor its scale also supports wet-service performance.

### Pools and Spas

All three of our mosaic paths hold pool and spa ratings: Roman Mosaics, Glass Mosaics, and zellige mosaics. For marble, that rating belongs to the mosaic format specifically, which makes the 5/8-inch squares the route for submerged marble surfaces. Glass Mosaics pair the pool rating with exterior suitability in any climate, and zellige brings its dynamic glazes to spa walls where water and light move together.

## Scale, Pattern, and Grout

Two decisions govern how a mosaic reads: the pattern itself and the grout around it. Solid fields settle into a room as texture, while Ventaglio fans and the checks announce themselves as pattern. The decision comes down to whether the surface should recede into material depth or carry the room as geometry.

Grout follows the same logic. Color-matching the grout to the tile lets each sheet read as one continuous surface, while a contrasting grout draws the grid and makes each square distinct. The Installation Guide covers grout joints and sealing for each material.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is mosaic tile?

Mosaic tile is tile composed of small pieces, called tesserae, typically two inches or smaller, arranged into a pattern and mounted on mesh sheets for installation. Marble, glass, ceramic, and glazed clay all appear in mosaic form. The small scale defines the category.

### Is mosaic tile a good choice for shower floors?

Mosaic is well suited to shower floors. The dense grout joints give the floor grip underfoot, and the small pieces follow the slope of the pan toward the drain without cutting. Stone and zellige mosaics require sealing in showers, and wet commercial floors call for grout joints of at least 1/8 inch plus an anti-slip treatment after installation.
