About
Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta in 18 colorways (Arroyo, Baja, Albar, Puebla, Dorado, Creosote, Sayulita, Azul, and Cacao among them), across squares, rectangles, hexagons, and the same geometric forms as our unglazed Cotto line. 


The glaze sits over a handmade clay body, so expect tile-to-tile variation in shape, size, and color, plus crazing as a defining characteristic of glazed terra cotta. Lay out the tiles on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any tile.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto Allende, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. 


The full installation procedure is in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made
Cotto Allende is handmade in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, using the same clay base as standard Cotto with a glaze applied before firing. The result is a sealed surface that opens up applications such as pools, spas, and steam showers, which aren't appropriate for unglazed terra cotta.


Natural clay is pressed into molds and sun-dried, then glazed in either gloss or satin finish before high-firing. The glaze develops fine hairline cracks during cooling, known as crazing, which is a normal characteristic of glazed clay tile and reflects how the glaze contracts on the clay body as temperature drops.


Color saturation is more controlled than unglazed Cotto, while the underlying clay body still produces handmade variation across the line. The finish determines how the tile reads in light, with each formulation producing a distinct surface character.

Frequently Asked Questions
What colorway is Puebla Cotto Allende?
Puebla is a satin-finish colorway in the Cotto Allende line, one of 18 glazed terra cotta colorways handmade in San Miguel de Allende. The Puebla colorway carries the tonal variation that comes from how the glaze applies and fires across each handmade clay body, with piece-to-piece differences in tone, crazing pattern, and glaze depth expected across every box.
What variation should I expect across Puebla 4x4 Cotto Allende tiles?
Puebla carries the variation inherent to glazed terra cotta: tonal shifts within the colorway, crazing (fine hairline cracks in the glaze), and minor differences in size and shape are all normal characteristics of the handmade clay body. 


Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and lay out the tiles on a flat surface to confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any piece.
Is Puebla 4x4 suitable for shower walls and wet areas?
Yes. Puebla 4x4 Cotto Allende is suited to shower wall and floor installations. Cotto Allende has a DCOF rating of 0.48, above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. For commercial wet-floor applications, a minimum 1/8" grout joint is required, and an anti-slip product may be applied after installation if further traction is desired. 


Sealing is required for all wet applications; apply 511 Porous Plus pre-grout and re-seal with a second pass after grouting for wet areas. The full sealing protocol is in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide.
Can Puebla 4x4 Cotto Allende be used in a pool or spa?
Yes. Cotto Allende is approved for pool waterline, spa interior, and fountain applications in both residential and commercial settings. Apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer before grouting as both a grout release and penetrating sealer, then apply a second pass of 511 Porous Plus after grouting is complete to protect grout lines exposed to continuous moisture.
Does Puebla 4x4 Cotto Allende require sealing?
Yes. Cotto Allende requires a pre-grout penetrating sealer applied before grouting, which also acts as a grout release. After the grout cures, wet installations (showers, pools, spas) require an additional sealing pass with 511 Porous Plus as the final step. Acidic cleaners must be avoided as they will damage the tile surface. Full instructions are in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide.
What is the difference between Cotto Allende and unglazed Cotto?
Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta; unglazed Cotto is fired clay without a glaze. The glaze on Cotto Allende opens up pool, spa, and steam shower applications that unglazed Cotto is not rated for. 


In the other direction, unglazed Cotto is approved for freeze/thaw exterior floors, while Cotto Allende is not. Both are handmade in San Miguel de Allende and require sealing, but the protocols differ: Cotto Allende uses 511 Porous Plus as a pre-grout sealer and grout release, with a second post-grout pass for wet areas.
Does Puebla 4x4 come in other formats, and can it be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways?
Cotto Allende is available across squares, rectangles, hexagons, and geometric forms. Puebla can be combined with other Cotto Allende colorways within the same installation; each piece shares the same handmade clay body from San Miguel de Allende, so material character and thickness are consistent across the line. Confirm the layout balance across colorways and finishes on a flat surface before setting any piece.
