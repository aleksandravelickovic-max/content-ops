About
Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.


Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.


Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.


The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile?
Desert Grey is a warm grey cement tile at the mid-range of the grey spectrum, with warm undertones that keep the color from reading as cool or industrial. Individual tiles vary within that range through the hand-pouring and curing process, distributing as a softly complex matte field across the installation when blended from all boxes before the dry layout begins.
Does Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile require sealing?
Yes. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. The sealer must cure fully before grouting begins. For interior installations, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the final top coat after the grout has cured. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time. Reseal every one to two years based on traffic and moisture exposure. See the Cement Installation Guide for the complete sealing sequence.
Can Desert Grey 2x8 be used in a shower?
Yes, on both shower walls and floors. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1. The 2x8 format can be installed in horizontal or vertical orientation on shower walls to suit the room. Seal before grouting, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax after the grout has cured, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch on shower floors.
Can Desert Grey 2x8 be used on an exterior floor?
Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates only. Desert Grey 2x8 is suitable for exterior floors and walls in climates that do not experience freeze/thaw conditions. Use 511 Porous Plus as both the pre-grout sealer and the final top coat. Do not use Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax outdoors.
Can Desert Grey 2x8 be mixed with other colorways or formats?
Yes. Desert Grey 2x8 combines with other colorways and formats in Zia's cement tile line without sizing conflicts. Desert Grey's mid-range warm grey sits between the cooler and warmer ends of the colorway spectrum. Confirm how it reads against adjacent tiles during the dry layout to verify the tonal balance before adhesive is applied. Blend tiles from all boxes within each colorway before setting begins.
How do I install Desert Grey 2x8 cement tile?
Blend tiles from all boxes and arrange a full dry layout before applying any adhesive. The 2x8 format produces different visual results in horizontal running bond, vertical stack, and herringbone arrangements. Establish the intended orientation during the dry stage, as the decision affects how the warm grey reads across the full field. For contrasting grout, apply with a grout bag rather than a float to prevent the porous cement surface from absorbing grout and leaving a lasting haze.
