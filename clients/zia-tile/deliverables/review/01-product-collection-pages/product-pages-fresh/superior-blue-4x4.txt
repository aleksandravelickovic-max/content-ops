We recommend ordering 15–25% overage so the installation has room for hand-selection, color blending, and the tile breakage inherent to the material. Before laying any piece, unpack every box and mix the tiles on a flat surface to plan the layout and confirm the color balance.


Zellige tile installation is labor-intensive, often likened more to masonry than traditional tiling due to the irregular nature of the handmade material. Plan for installations 2–3 times longer than standard ceramic. Every piece needs to be sorted, soaked, arranged, and hand-adjusted for size.

About
No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.


Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Contact Zia's team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170 with any material or installation questions.

How It's Made
Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.


The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.


Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Superior Blue zellige?
Superior Blue zellige is a deep, richly saturated blue with tonal variation built into each tile through the hand-dipping and kiln-firing process. Individual tiles hold different positions within the glaze's range, and those differences distribute across the installation as the field grows. Blending tiles from all boxes before the dry layout is essential for even tonal distribution across the finished surface.
What layout patterns work best with Superior Blue 4x4 zellige?
Superior Blue 4x4 can be installed in stack bond, offset bond, herringbone, or basket weave. Stack bond aligns tiles in a straight grid, making the pattern geometry and individual tile variation equally visible across the surface. Offset bond staggers joints half a unit in horizontal or vertical orientation, creating a more dynamic rhythm across the field. Herringbone introduces directional movement and brings out tonal differences between adjacent tiles by presenting them at opposing angles. In all patterns, confirm the full layout during the dry stage before setting begins, as the pattern only resolves correctly when the full field is visible. See the Zellige Installation Guide for layout and installation guidance.
Can Superior Blue 4x4 zellige be used in a pool?
Yes. Superior Blue 4x4 is suitable for pool and spa applications in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required for all zellige installations; for pools and spas, apply 511 Porous Plus Penetrating Sealer after grouting is complete and allow it to cure fully before the installation is exposed to water. Consult the Zellige Installation Guide for application steps and re-sealing intervals.
Can Superior Blue 4x4 be used in a shower?
Yes. Superior Blue 4x4 can be used on shower walls, shower floors, and steam showers. Sealing is required for all shower applications. Glazed zellige has a DCOF of 0.16, below the 0.42 wet-floor threshold for level interior floors. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation to increase slip resistance on shower floors. 
What grout color works best with Superior Blue zellige?
A grout matched to the mid-range of the glaze or a shade slightly lighter creates a continuous surface where the tonal variation in the tiles reads as the defining feature. White grout introduces a clear contrast and makes individual tile edges more prominent, emphasizing the handmade character of each piece. Dark charcoal or near-black grout minimizes the visibility of grout lines and allows the blue field to read as more continuous across the surface. 
Can Superior Blue be mixed with other zellige colorways?
Yes. Superior Blue can be mixed with other colorways in Zia's zellige collection. All zellige colorways are produced using the same traditional methods and share consistent surface character and thickness across the line. When mixing colorways, plan the full layout during the dry stage before setting any tile, and blend tiles from all boxes within each colorway to confirm even tonal distribution. For questions about colorway combinations, contact us at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
