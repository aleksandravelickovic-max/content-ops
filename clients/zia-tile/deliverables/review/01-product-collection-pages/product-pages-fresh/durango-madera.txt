We recommend 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and tile-to-tile variation. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange the layout dry to disperse variation evenly. Wash the tile immediately after cutting to prevent the cutting paste from drying on the surface.


Cotto requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout sealer applied as a grout release, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. Subfloors must be fully cured (minimum 30 days) to prevent efflorescence from depositing on the tile surface.

About
Each Cotto tile is made from locally-sourced clays. Our handcrafted Cotto tiles are made just outside of historic San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.


The tiles' clay formulation is mixed with water and blended to the ideal consistency, then pressed in a mold to dry for 1–2 weeks until the proper moisture level is achieved for firing. At this point, wood ash is added to the surface of our 'Fired Earth' tiles to give them their distinctive surface coloration.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cotto, derived from the Italian terra cotta or "baked earth," is part of a long lineage of fired clays that date back to the dawn of civilization itself. From Egyptian water jugs to Chinese roof tiles to Roman bricks, terra cotta has been part of the human story for as long as we've had an alphabet. The materials in Cotto tile haven't changed much over the millennia, while the firing process has evolved: from kiln furniture to mold designs to firing method, our handmade and high-fired Cotto is our modern take on an ancient tradition.


Our Cotto is produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, where natural clay is pressed into molds, sun-dried, and fired in kilns by local artisans. The unglazed surface is what gives Cotto its character at installation and over time. With proper sealing, Cotto handles indoor floors, exterior floors in any climate, and freeze/thaw exposure, and the surface wears like a natural stone product as use deepens its tone.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Durango + Madera Cotto made of?
Durango + Madera Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende. The clay is refined and mixed with water, then hand-pressed into the Durango specialty mold. After one to two weeks of sun-drying, each tile is kiln-fired. Heat variation and mineral interaction during firing produce the rust-brown tonal range and surface character. Natural variation in tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness is inherent to the material.
What paint colors pair well with Durango + Madera?
Durango + Madera works with paint colors that balance or contrast the rust-brown palette. Warm off-whites and cream create a neutral field that lets the sculptural shape lead. Olive, sage, and muted greens align with the earthy range. Darker colors like charcoal, navy, or black introduce stronger contrast and shift the composition. The choice depends on whether you want the tile to anchor the room or integrate into a broader palette.
Can Durango + Madera be used in a kitchen?
Yes. Durango + Madera Cotto works on kitchen floors and walls. Proper installation and sealing are essential to manage the natural porosity of unglazed terra cotta. Apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting to protect the surface, then finish with Fila Matte Wax for interior installations. Maintain a minimum 1/8" grout joint. In areas exposed to grease or moisture like behind a range, ongoing resealing prevents absorption and staining.
How do I clean terra cotta tile?
Use pH-neutral cleaner with warm water and avoid acidic or abrasive products. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit that can wear the surface. Clean with a damp cloth or mop rather than excess water, and dry the surface afterward to prevent residue buildup. Periodic resealing maintains long-term performance.
How does the Durango specialty shape install?
The Durango specialty shape is a curved, sculptural format that interlocks with adjacent pieces to build an architectural composition across the surface. Because the geometry depends on consistent orientation, a full dry layout before installation confirms how the shape resolves across the field. The shape requires more cuts than standard squares or rectangles, which is why increasing overage beyond the standard 15% is recommended for Durango orders.
