About 
Zia's limestone is sourced from two regions: Honed Limestone quarried near Fez in three colorways (Buff, Monument, and Basilica) across 6x6, 4x12, 12x12, and 12x24 formats; and French Limestone quarried in Bordeaux in 12-inch and 20-inch varying-length tiles plus French Cobblestone. Basilica is also offered in a single bush-hammered 4x12 variant. Variation in color, tone, and natural inclusions across pieces is inherent to the material; lay out the stone on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any piece.
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. The full installation procedure is in the Limestone Installation Guide. How It's Made
Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.
The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.
Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is honed limestone?
Honed limestone is ground smooth with fine abrasives to create a matte finish. The process removes surface irregularities without adding shine, leaving the stone's natural color and markings visible. The matte surface offers better traction than polished stone, making it suitable for floors and walls.
What is the difference between honed and bush-hammered limestone?
Honed limestone is ground smooth, producing a consistent, flat, matte surface. Bush-hammered limestone is textured with a high-impact machine that uniformly pits the surface, creating a rough, tactile finish. Honed works across a wide range of interior applications. Bush-hammered is chosen where a more textured, rustic surface is preferred.
How do I seal limestone tile?
Ensure the surface is clean and dry before applying 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the stone is fully saturated. Wipe off excess before it dries to prevent uneven film. Allow the sealer to cure fully before grouting. For interior installations, follow with Fila Matte Protective Wax as a top coat. For exterior applications, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus instead of wax, since UV exposure discolors wax.
How do I re-seal limestone tile?
Re-seal limestone as often as needed, depending on traffic, wear, and moisture exposure. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers, wiping off excess before it dries. For interior installations, finish with Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax. For outdoor installations, use 511 Porous Plus only — do not use Fila Matte Finish outdoors, as UV rays cause discoloration.
How do I clean limestone tile?
Use a microfiber mop with warm water and pH-neutral cleaner for routine maintenance. Vacuum or sweep regularly to remove grit and debris. Blot spills immediately rather than wiping. For heavier staining, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Avoid vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach — these etch and damage the surface over time.
What kind of variation should I expect across limestone tiles?
Limestone carries natural stone variation in tone, veining, and surface character. Color ranges from light cream to deeper buff tones depending on the quarry source. Veining patterns are unique to each piece. Subtle differences in thickness and edge profile occur naturally in quarried stone. Before installation, open and mix tiles from all boxes to distribute variation evenly throughout the design.
