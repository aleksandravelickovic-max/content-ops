About
Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta in 18 colorways (Arroyo, Baja, Albar, Puebla, Dorado, Creosote, Sayulita, Azul, and Cacao among them), across squares, rectangles, hexagons, and the same geometric forms as our unglazed Cotto line. 


The glaze sits over a handmade clay body, so expect tile-to-tile variation in shape, size, and color, plus crazing as a defining characteristic of glazed terra cotta. Lay out the tiles on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any tile.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto Allende, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. 


The full installation procedure is in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made
Cotto Allende is handmade in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, using the same clay base as standard Cotto with a glaze applied before firing. The result is a sealed surface that opens up applications such as pools, spas, and steam showers, which aren't appropriate for unglazed terra cotta.


Natural clay is pressed into molds and sun-dried, then glazed in either gloss or satin finish before high-firing. The glaze develops fine hairline cracks during cooling, known as crazing, which is a normal characteristic of glazed clay tile and reflects how the glaze contracts on the clay body as temperature drops.


Color saturation is more controlled than unglazed Cotto, while the underlying clay body still produces handmade variation across the line. The finish determines how the tile reads in light, with each formulation producing a distinct surface character.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Peyote 4x4 Cotto Allende?
Peyote is a muted blue-green Cotto Allende in satin finish. The glaze produces tonal variation tile to tile as a result of the hand-dipping and high-fire kiln process, so individual pieces hold different positions within the colorway's range. The satin finish gives the color a soft presence without the full reflectivity of a glossy glaze.
What is the difference between the satin and glossy finishes in Cotto Allende?
The satin finish reflects less light than glossy and reads as softer in the field. Compared to glossy Cotto Allende, satin does not produce the same specular highlight but the glaze color and tonal depth are consistent across both finishes. The choice is primarily aesthetic. Satin suits spaces where lower reflectivity is preferred. Glossy suits spaces where more light bounce is part of the design intent.
Is Peyote 4x4 suitable for pools and spas?
Yes. Peyote 4x4 Cotto Allende is rated for pool and spa applications in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting as a grout release, then apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus after the grout has fully cured to protect grout lines under continuous moisture exposure. Do not use Fila Matte Wax on Cotto Allende. 511 Porous Plus is the only recommended sealer for this line.
Is Peyote 4x4 suitable for flooring?
Yes. Peyote 4x4 is suitable for residential and commercial floors. Cotto Allende has a DCOF rating of 0.48, which meets the threshold for level interior floor applications. For wet floor installations, including shower floors, use a minimum 1/8" grout joint; slip resistance can be further increased with an anti-slip product applied after installation.
What is the difference between Cotto Allende and Cotto?
Cotto Allende is glazed, and Cotto is unglazed. The glaze on Cotto Allende makes it suitable for pools, spas, and saltwater or chlorine environments that are not appropriate for unglazed Cotto. Unglazed Cotto handles freeze/thaw climates. Cotto Allende does not. Choose Cotto Allende for wet and aquatic applications in non-freeze/thaw climates, and Cotto for exterior floors in cold climates where freeze/thaw resistance is required.
Can Peyote 4x4 be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways or formats?
Yes. All Cotto Allende colorways share the same handmade clay body and firing process, so surface character and tile dimensions are consistent across the line. Peyote 4x4 can be mixed with other colorways or with other Cotto Allende formats within the same installation. Plan any multi-colorway or mixed-format layout during the dry stage to confirm the color balance reads as intended before setting begins.
