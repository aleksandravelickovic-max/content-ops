How It's Made
Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.


The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.


Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is French Cobblestone, and where is it quarried?
French Cobblestone is a natural limestone quarried near Bordeaux, in the Dordogne region of France. It belongs to Zia's French limestone line alongside the 12-inch and 20-inch varying-length formats, all drawn from the same regional quarry source. 


The stone carries the tonal character specific to the French formation: a palette that reads through beige, tan, and light grey with fossil impressions and oxidation marks distinct from Zia's Moroccan honed limestone range.
What variation should I expect across French Cobblestone pieces?
French Cobblestone carries the variation inherent to natural limestone: tonal differences across pieces, fossil impressions, subtle fissures, and oxidation marks that shift from one stone to the next. 


Before installation, lay out all pieces on a flat surface and blend across boxes to confirm the color balance reads evenly throughout the installation. There is a face and an underside to each limestone piece; they are not reversible, so ensure each stone is installed face side up.
Can French Cobblestone be used outdoors in a freeze/thaw climate?
No. French Cobblestone limestone is not rated for exterior floor installations in freeze/thaw climates. Limestone is susceptible to freeze/thaw cycling, which can cause the stone to crack or spall over time under repeated expansion and contraction. 


For outdoor floor applications, French Cobblestone is suited to non-freeze/thaw climates only: patios, courtyards, and pool surrounds in mild-weather regions. For exterior wall use, apply a penetrating sealer before and after grouting.
Is French Cobblestone suitable for shower floors and wet areas?
Yes. French Cobblestone is suited to shower wall and floor installations. Honed limestone has a DCOF rating of 0.50, above the 0.42 wet-floor threshold for level interior floors. For commercial wet-floor applications, increase grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation. Sealing is required for all wet applications; the full sealing protocol is in the Limestone Installation Guide. 
Does French Cobblestone require sealing?
Yes. All limestone requires a two-stage sealing sequence. Before grouting, apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer in thin layers until the stone is fully saturated, then allow it to cure. After the grout cures, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for interior installations or a second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior applications. 


Acidic cleaners must be avoided at every stage (before, during, and after sealing) as they will damage the stone. Full instructions are in the Limestone Installation Guide.
How does French Cobblestone relate to the rest of Zia's limestone collection?
French Cobblestone sits within Zia's French limestone line, quarried near Bordeaux, alongside the 12-inch and 20-inch varying-length formats. The cobblestone format is smaller and more irregular in outline than the rectangular slab formats, which makes it suited to surfaces where the cut pattern carries more of the design intent. 


Zia's Moroccan honed limestone line (Buff, Monument, and Basilica) is quarried near Fez and produces a more uniform, rectilinear appearance alongside the French line's warmer, more organic tonal character. Both lines require the same sealing protocol and carry the same application ratings.
