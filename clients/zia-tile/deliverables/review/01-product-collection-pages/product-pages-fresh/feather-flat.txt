About
Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts. 
The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters. 
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.
Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made 
Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.
Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.
Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

Frequently Asked Questions 
What is Field Trip: Japan?
Field Trip: Japan is a sub-line within Zia's ceramics collection, produced in Japan using high-temperature firing. The result is a tile with three-dimensional surface texture in Flat, Etched, or Furrowed finish, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.
What does Feather Flat look like?
Feather is a soft, warm, off-white textured ceramic with a Flat finish that reads as a consistent off-white across the installation. The Flat finish is smooth but not reflective. Slight tonal movement is present tile to tile and distributes evenly across the full installation.
Does Feather Flat require sealing before grouting?
Yes. Feather Flat is a textured ceramic and requires a pre-grout penetrating sealer applied as a grout release before grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in a thin, even layer after laying the tile and allow it to cure fully before grouting. Refer to the Installation Guide for the full installation and sealing sequence.
Can Feather Flat be used on a shower floor?
Yes, with slip resistance measures in place. Feather Flat has a DCOF of 0.40, just below the 0.42 wet-floor threshold for level interior floors. For shower floor installations, size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete.
Can Feather Flat be installed on an exterior floor?
No. Feather Flat is not suitable for exterior floor installations in either freeze/thaw or non-freeze/thaw climates. It can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For material and application questions, contact us at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
Can Feather Flat be mixed with other Field Trip: Japan colorways or finishes?
Yes. Feather Flat shares the same 9.45" x 2.36" format and clay construction as all other Field Trip: Japan colorways and can be combined with any colorway in the series. Mixing colorways within the same Flat finish keeps surface texture and DCOF consistent across the field. When mixing finishes, note that DCOF ratings differ across Flat (0.40), Etched (0.45), and Furrowed (0.37), and slip resistance measures should be applied accordingly. Plan the full layout during the dry stage before setting begins.
