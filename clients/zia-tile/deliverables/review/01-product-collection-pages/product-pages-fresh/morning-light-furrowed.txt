About
Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to our handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts. 
The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2x8 and 2x4 rectangles and 4x4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters. 
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.
Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made 
Ceramic tile is designed in Los Angeles and made in Japan, where the line is produced using high-temperature firing and precision glaze application. The result is a clean, precisely sized tile with consistent color across boxes, a different product category from handmade clay tile.
Clay is molded into shape, dried, glazed, and fired at high temperature. The matte, satin, and gloss finishes are achieved through different glaze formulations, each producing distinct surface character and slip-resistance performance.
Each format is produced to consistent dimensions across the line, which allows for predictable installation and clean grout lines. Subtle variation in color and surface is normal and reflects the hand-finishing step in production.

Frequently Asked Questions 
What does Morning Light Furrowed look like?
Morning Light is a pale blue-grey ceramic in Furrowed finish. The parallel ridges running the length of each tile cast their own shadow, which registers as a slightly deeper tone against the lighter face of the ridge, creating a surface that reads differently in direct light versus ambient or raking light. Tonal variation is present tile to tile and distributes across the installation when tiles from all boxes are blended before the dry layout.
Does Morning Light Furrowed require sealing before grouting?
Yes. Morning Light Furrowed requires a penetrating sealer applied as a grout release before grouting. The furrowed surface is especially important to saturate fully. The parallel ridges create channels where grout will settle and bond to the tile body if the surface is not protected before the grout passes over it. Apply 511 Porous Plus in a thin, even layer after laying the tile and allow it to cure fully before grouting begins. See the Japanese Brick Ceramics Installation Guide for the full sealing and installation sequence.
What is the difference between the Flat, Etched, and Furrowed finishes in Field Trip: Japan?
The three finishes produce progressively more surface depth from Flat to Etched to Furrowed. Flat is a smooth face. Etched adds linear surface scoring. Furrowed produces deep ridges running the full tile length. Slip resistance differs across the three: Flat 0.40, Etched 0.45, Furrowed 0.37. For all three finishes in wet-area applications, size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation. Both Etched and Furrowed require a grout bag during installation to prevent grout from settling into the textured surface.
Can Morning Light Furrowed be used in a shower?
Yes, with slip resistance measures in place. Morning Light Furrowed has a DCOF of 0.37, below the 0.42 wet-floor threshold for level interior floors. For shower floor installations, size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8 inch and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete. Apply a penetrating sealer before grouting for all wet-area installations and use a grout bag to apply grout rather than a float to keep the furrowed channels clean. 
Why does Morning Light Furrowed require a grout bag during installation?
The furrowed ridges trap grout spread from a rubber float, filling the channels rather than the joints. Removal from deep channels after curing risks surface damage. A grout bag applies grout directly to the joints without contacting the tile face. This requirement applies to all Furrowed installations regardless of orientation. For complete installation requirements, see the Japanese Brick Ceramics Installation Guide.
Can Morning Light Furrowed be mixed with other Field Trip: Japan colorways or finishes?
Yes. Morning Light Furrowed can be combined with other colorways and finishes in the Field Trip: Japan series. All tiles share the same ceramic body and consistent format dimensions. When mixing colorways, confirm the tonal balance during the dry layout before adhesive is applied. When mixing Furrowed with Flat or Etched sections, the grout bag requirement applies to Furrowed and Etched areas. Plan the grouting sequence so those sections are worked separately from any Flat areas.
