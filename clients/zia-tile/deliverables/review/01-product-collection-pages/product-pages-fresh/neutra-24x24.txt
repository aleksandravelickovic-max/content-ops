Order Details + Installation
We recommend a 15–20% overage on all terrazzo orders to account for any cuts needed. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange a layout to evenly disperse aggregate distribution. Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. 


Installers will need to slightly adjust grout joint spacing (shim) or use wedge spacers to maintain straight grout lines. Where necessary, tile edges may be trimmed with a wet saw to assist with alignment.

About
Each terrazzo tile is a cement-based composite with crushed marble and stone aggregates ground and polished to a matte surface. Aggregate distribution varies tile to tile. Blend tiles from all boxes before installation and arrange a layout to disperse aggregate evenly.


Perhaps the most versatile tile Zia produces, with endless colorways, patterns, and shapes, Zia strives to offer the world's best curated collection of encaustic terrazzo tiles. While generally consistent in appearance, each handmade tile varies in aggregate distribution and surface character. Durable and versatile, matte terrazzo tile is an easy choice for many installations.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with terrazzo, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.


Proper installation and protection of terrazzo tile is key, so please ensure you work with an installer who has experience specifically installing terrazzo tile, and who has carefully reviewed Zia's installation guide.

How It's Made
Terrazzo is produced using techniques refined by Venetian stone masons since the 15th century, when leftover marble offcuts were combined with cement to floor courtyards and palazzo halls. The same craft is preserved in the workshops where Zia's terrazzo is produced today.


Crushed marble and stone aggregates are arranged in steel molds and covered with liquid cement, then hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once cured, the surface is ground and polished to the matte finish that defines the line.


Each tile is finished by hand, which produces the subtle tonal variation, occasional crazing, and slight edge irregularity that separate hand-pressed terrazzo from machine-set composite tile. The cement matrix and the visible aggregate are what define terrazzo as a material; the press and the finish are what carry that definition into the floor.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the Neutra colorway?
Neutra is the aggregate blend and cement matrix that defines this terrazzo tile: the colorway is produced by the specific combination of crushed marble and stone used in the Neutra formulation, ground and polished to the matte surface finish. Aggregate distribution varies tile to tile, which means the colorway reads across the full installation as a composite of all pieces rather than as a single repeating unit.
What is the difference between the 12x12 and 24x24 terrazzo formats, and where does each suit best?
The 12x12 square suits smaller rooms and installations where the grout joint pattern plays a visual role in the design. The 24x24 format reduces the number of grout joints across a given area, which allows the aggregate pattern of the tile itself to read at a larger scale. 


The 24x24 is well-suited to open-plan floors, large-format wall fields, and commercial interiors where a continuous material surface is the design intent.
How does aggregate distribution vary across Neutra tiles, and how should I blend across boxes?
Aggregate distribution is inherent to the hand-pressed production process and varies from tile to tile within a single order. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange a dry layout across the full installation area to confirm that the variation distributes evenly across the field. Tiles from a single box will share a closer production run and may cluster in tone if not blended with tiles from other boxes.
Is Neutra 24x24 suitable for shower floors and wet areas?
Yes. Neutra 24x24 is suited to shower wall and floor installations. Terrazzo has a DCOF rating of 0.51, above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. For commercial or high-traffic wet-floor applications, an anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation. Sealing is required for all wet applications; the full protocol is in Zia's installation guide.
Does Neutra terrazzo require sealing?
Yes. Terrazzo requires a two-stage sealing sequence. Before grouting, apply 511 Porous Plus penetrating sealer in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated, then allow it to cure. After the grout cures, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for interior installations or a second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior applications. Acidic cleaners must be avoided, as they will damage the tile surface. Full instructions are in Zia's installation guide.
Can Neutra 24x24 terrazzo be used outdoors?
Neutra 24x24 is suited to exterior wall installations and exterior floor installations in non-freeze/thaw climates. Terrazzo is not rated for exterior floors in freeze/thaw regions, as the cement matrix is susceptible to damage from repeated expansion and contraction cycles. 


For outdoor floor applications in mild climates, apply 511 Porous Plus sealer before and after grouting. For outdoor applications, use the 511 Porous Plus top coat rather than Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax, since UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time.
What is the history behind terrazzo, and how does Zia's production connect to that tradition?
Terrazzo originated with Venetian stone masons in the 15th century, who combined leftover marble offcuts with cement to surface the floors of courtyards and palazzo halls. The material has been produced continuously since then, with the core technique unchanged: aggregate set into a cement base, pressed, cured, and ground to a matte finish. 


Zia's terrazzo is produced using the same hydraulic pressing and hand-finishing methods refined by those original workshops, with the aggregate selection and colorway formulation developed by Zia's design team.
