Before installation, lay out all pieces and use a heavy spray to remove production dust; allow them to dry thoroughly before setting. Blend pieces from all boxes to disperse variation evenly. Limestone has a face and an underside (they are not reversible), so install the face side up.
Limestone requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout penetrating sealer, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). About 
Zia's limestone is sourced from two regions: Honed Limestone quarried near Fez in three colorways (Buff, Monument, and Basilica) across 6x6, 4x12, 12x12, and 12x24 formats; and French Limestone quarried in Bordeaux in 12-inch and 20-inch varying-length tiles plus French Cobblestone. Basilica is also offered in a single bush-hammered 4x12 variant. Variation in color, tone, and natural inclusions across pieces is inherent to the material; lay out the stone on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any piece.
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. The full installation procedure is in the Limestone Installation Guide. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made
Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.
The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.
Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does honed limestone mean?
Honed limestone is stone ground smooth with fine abrasives to create a matte finish. The process removes surface irregularities without adding shine, leaving the stone's natural color and markings visible. The result is a practical surface suited to floors and walls.
Is honed limestone suitable for a bathroom floor?
Yes. Honed limestone is suitable for bathroom floors. The matte finish provides better traction than polished stone. Sealing is required before grouting and should be maintained as often as needed, depending on traffic and moisture exposure. For wet floor applications, grout joints should measure at least 1/8" and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation.
How do I seal honed limestone?
Clean and dry the surface thoroughly before applying 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the stone is fully saturated. Wipe off excess before it dries to prevent uneven film. Allow the sealer to cure fully before grouting. For interior installations, follow with Fila Matte Protective Wax. For exterior applications, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus instead of wax.
How do I clean honed limestone?
Mop with warm water and pH-neutral cleaner for routine maintenance. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove grit and debris that can scratch the surface. Blot spills immediately rather than wiping them across the stone. For stubborn staining, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Never use acidic cleaners, including vinegar, lemon juice, and bleach, because these etch and permanently damage the surface.
How does the 12x24 format change the visual appearance of limestone?
The 12x24 format produces a more continuous, expansive surface than smaller formats. Fewer grout joints means the stone's tonal variation reads across a wider field, which works particularly well on large floors and floor-to-ceiling wall cladding. The format reduces installation time on large surfaces. With a large format tile, tonal balance across the field is especially important to assess during the dry layout stage before setting begins.
What overage should I order for honed limestone 12x24?
For a standard layout, order 15-20% overage to account for curation and cuts. The consistent sizing of limestone means less overage is typically needed compared to handmade tile. For large-format installations covering expansive surfaces, slightly higher overage can account for any necessary adjustments during the dry layout phase.
