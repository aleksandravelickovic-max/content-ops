About
Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta in 18 colorways (Arroyo, Baja, Albar, Puebla, Dorado, Creosote, Sayulita, Azul, and Cacao among them), across squares, rectangles, hexagons, and the same geometric forms as our unglazed Cotto line. 


The glaze sits over a handmade clay body, so expect tile-to-tile variation in shape, size, and color, plus crazing as a defining characteristic of glazed terra cotta. Lay out the tiles on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any tile.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto Allende, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. 


The full installation procedure is in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made
Cotto Allende is handmade in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, using the same clay base as standard Cotto with a glaze applied before firing. The result is a sealed surface that opens up applications such as pools, spas, and steam showers, which aren't appropriate for unglazed terra cotta.


Natural clay is pressed into molds and sun-dried, then glazed in either gloss or satin finish before high-firing. The glaze develops fine hairline cracks during cooling, known as crazing, which is a normal characteristic of glazed clay tile and reflects how the glaze contracts on the clay body as temperature drops.


Color saturation is more controlled than unglazed Cotto, while the underlying clay body still produces handmade variation across the line. The finish determines how the tile reads in light, with each formulation producing a distinct surface character.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does the Piedra colorway look like?
Piedra is a dark blue glaze with subtle shade variations that shift in tone depending on light and angle. The glossy finish introduces a degree of reflectivity that the warmer, earthier colorways in the Cotto Allende line do not produce, and the depth of the colorway reads clearly at close range alongside natural stone countertops, white fixtures, and metal-finished cabinetry.
What is crazing, and how does it read on Piedra tiles?
Crazing is the network of fine hairline cracks that develops in the glaze during the cooling phase after firing. It is a defining characteristic of glazed terra cotta and reflects how the glaze contracts on the clay body as the temperature drops. 


Grout color selection affects how crazing reads in the finished installation: a tonal grout that closely matches the Piedra colorway integrates the crazing into the overall surface, while a contrasting grout makes the pattern more visible. Either approach is a legitimate design decision.
Is Piedra Cotto Allende suitable for shower floors and wet areas?
Yes. Piedra 4x4 is suited to shower wall and floor installations. The glazed surface has a DCOF of 0.48, above the 0.42 wet-floor slip-resistance threshold for level interior floors. For commercial wet-floor applications, grout joints should measure at least 1/8" and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation. Wet-area installations require an additional sealing pass with 511 Porous Plus after grouting; refer to the Cotto Allende Installation Guide for full instructions.
Can Piedra 4x4 be used in a pool or spa?
Yes. Piedra Cotto Allende is approved for pool waterline, spa interior, and fountain applications in both residential and commercial settings. Sealing is required; apply a penetrating sealer after grouting is complete for all wet-area installations. For project-specific guidance, contact info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
Is Piedra Cotto Allende rated for outdoor installations?
Piedra 4x4 is suited to exterior wall installations and exterior floor installations in non-freeze/thaw climates only. Cotto Allende is not rated for exterior floors in freeze/thaw regions, as the glaze is susceptible to spalling under freeze/thaw cycling. For outdoor use in mild climates, apply a penetrating sealer before and after grouting and re-seal as needed depending on exposure. Full installation guidance is in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide.
What sealing protocol does Piedra Cotto Allende require?
Piedra Cotto Allende requires a pre-grout penetrating sealer, which also acts as a grout release. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tile is fully saturated, wipe off any excess before it dries, and allow it to cure before grouting. Wet-area installations — showers, pools, and spas — require an additional sealing pass with 511 Porous Plus after grouting is complete. Full instructions are in the Cotto Allende Installation Guide.
What is the difference between Cotto Allende and unglazed Cotto?
Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta; unglazed Cotto is left raw from the kiln. The glaze on Cotto Allende opens up applications like pools, spas, and steam showers in a way that unglazed Cotto is not rated for. 


The trade-off is that Cotto Allende is not approved for freeze/thaw exterior floors, while unglazed Cotto is. Crazing is a defining characteristic of Cotto Allende's glazed surface; unglazed Cotto develops a natural matte patina over time instead.
Can Piedra be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways or formats?
Yes. Piedra 4x4 can be combined with other colorways and formats across the Cotto Allende line. Each piece is formed from the same clay body in San Miguel de Allende, so thickness and material character are consistent across the range. 


Piedra's cool blue palette pairs most naturally with the neutral and warm-earth colorways in the collection, like Arroyo, Albar, or Sayulita, where the cooler tone reads in deliberate contrast against a warmer surrounding field.
