Before installation, lay out all pieces and use a heavy spray to remove production dust; allow them to dry thoroughly before setting. Blend pieces from all boxes to disperse variation evenly. Limestone has a face and an underside (they are not reversible), so install the face side up.
Limestone requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout penetrating sealer, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). About 
Zia's limestone is sourced from two regions: Honed Limestone quarried near Fez in three colorways (Buff, Monument, and Basilica) across 6x6, 4x12, 12x12, and 12x24 formats; and French Limestone quarried in Bordeaux in 12-inch and 20-inch varying-length tiles plus French Cobblestone. Basilica is also offered in a single bush-hammered 4x12 variant. Variation in color, tone, and natural inclusions across pieces is inherent to the material; lay out the stone on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any piece.
Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with limestone, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips. The full installation procedure is in the Limestone Installation Guide. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com.

How It's Made
Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.
The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.
Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

Frequently Asked Questions
What are the natural characteristics of limestone tile?
Limestone is a sedimentary stone formed over millennia from compressed mineral deposits. Color, tone, surface texture, and markings vary from tile to tile because no two sections of stone share the same geological history. These differences are inherent to the material and should be expected across an order. With use and exposure over time, the surface develops a patina that is part of how the stone matures.
Can honed limestone 12x12 be used in a minimalist interior?
Yes. The smooth matte surface and consistent buff tone produce a neutral field that does not introduce pattern or color competing with a restrained composition. The natural variation in the stone adds material depth without demanding attention. The 12x12 square format produces a gridded, structured layout that suits minimalist interiors well.
How does limestone compare to marble?
Both limestone and marble are natural stones requiring sealing and pH-neutral cleaning, but they differ in formation and character. Limestone is sedimentary with a consistent neutral palette and moderate variation. Marble is metamorphic with more pronounced veining and a broader color range. Marble etches more easily from acidic cleaners. Both materials develop patina with use.
How do I cut limestone on site?
All cuts must be made with a water-cooled tile saw with a diamond edge blade. This applies to straight cuts and cuts around fixtures and perimeter adjustments. If extensive cutting is required, work outdoors or in a well-ventilated area in accordance with California Prop 65 safety guidelines. Installers should always wear protective face covering and eye protection when cutting limestone.
What kind of variation should I expect across honed limestone 12x12 tiles?
Honed limestone 12x12 carries natural stone variation in tone, veining, and surface character. The honing process reveals these characteristics rather than concealing them. Veining patterns are unique to each piece. Before installation, open and mix tiles from all boxes to distribute variation evenly throughout the design.
Can honed limestone 12x12 be used outdoors?
Yes. Honed limestone 12x12 can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. For outdoor applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus only — do not use Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax outdoors, as UV exposure causes discoloration.
