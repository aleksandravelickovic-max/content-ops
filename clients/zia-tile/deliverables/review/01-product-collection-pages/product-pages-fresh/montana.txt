About
Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.


Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

How It's Made
Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.


Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.


The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

Frequently Asked Questions
What color is Montana cement tile?
Montana is a warm grey-brown cement tile that sits between the warmer end of the grey family and the lighter end of the brown range. Pigments in this mid-register carry moderate tonal shifts tile to tile and is enough to create depth across the field without the broader internal contrast found in the line's deeper colorways. Blending tiles from all boxes before the dry layout distributes those shifts evenly across the finished surface.
Does Montana cement tile require sealing?
Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries; the sealer must cure fully before grouting begins. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the final top coat for interior installations. 


For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax: UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time outdoors. Reseal every one to two years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure. See the Cement Installation Guide for the complete sealing sequence.
Can Montana cement tile be used in a shower?
Yes. Montana cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing strong slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before grouting and apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as the top coat after grout has cured for interior wet areas. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" for shower floor applications. An anti-slip product applied after installation can further increase traction for high-exposure shower floor surfaces. 
See the Cement Installation Guide for the full wet-area sealing sequence.
Can Montana cement tile be used on an exterior floor?
Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Montana cement tile is suitable for exterior floors and walls in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. For all exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before grouting and apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat after grout has cured. Do not use Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax for outdoor installations. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for the outdoor sealing sequence.
Can Montana cement tile be mixed with other colorways or formats?
Yes. Montana's warm grey-brown sits between the warm and cool ends of the cement colorway spectrum, making it a transitional tone in mixed-colorway installations. All solid-color cement tiles share consistent dimensions within each format group, so combining Montana with other colorways or formats creates no sizing conflicts. Blend tiles from all boxes within each colorway before setting to ensure even tonal distribution, and plan the full layout dry before adhesive is applied. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for complete installation requirements.
What grout color works best with Montana cement tile?
Grout color has a meaningful impact on how Montana reads across a full installation. A grout matched to the warm grey-brown range keeps the joints receding into the field; a lighter neutral grout opens the pattern at the joint level without introducing strong contrast. Contrasting grout on matte cement carries a risk of permanent haze: apply with a grout bag rather than a float and do not spread it over the tile face. 


See the Cement Installation Guide for grout selection and application guidance.
