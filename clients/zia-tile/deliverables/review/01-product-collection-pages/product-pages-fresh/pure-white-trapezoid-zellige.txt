About
No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.


Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

How It's Made
Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.


The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.


Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can Pure White trapezoid be mixed with other formats?
Yes. Pure White trapezoid can be mixed with other Pure White formats, including the 2x2 and 4x4, or with other colorways in Zia's zellige collection. Each format is produced using the same hand-pressing and kiln-firing methods. A common approach uses the trapezoid as a feature area against a square or Bejmat field tile in a coordinating or contrasting color.
Where should I start the layout for trapezoid zellige?
Begin the dry layout from the center of the wall or floor and work outward so the pattern distributes symmetrically and edge cuts fall at the perimeter. Confirm the orientation of each tile during the dry layout stage, as the interlocking geometry requires consistent direction throughout. Map corners and edge cuts before setting any tile, as the angled sides mean perimeter cuts are more complex than square or rectangular formats.
How does orientation affect the Pure White trapezoid pattern?
Horizontal orientation with alternating rows produces a layered, scale-like pattern. Vertical orientation with the narrow end facing up reads as pointed, fan-like forms. The glaze variation across tiles interacts differently with each pattern. Plan the orientation during the dry layout stage and view the arrangement from the intended viewing distance before setting tile to confirm the result.
Does Pure White trapezoid zellige work in a pool or spa?
Yes. Pure White trapezoid is suitable for pool and spa applications in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required after grouting for all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture. Refer to the Zellige Installation Guide for specific sealing instructions, or reach out to info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170 with project-specific questions.
How should I handle cuts at corners and edges with trapezoid zellige?
Corner and edge cuts require angled measurements that match the trapezoid geometry. Plan all perimeter cuts during the dry layout stage before setting any tile. Because each tile varies slightly in size, cuts must be measured tile-by-tile. Work with an installer experienced in zellige installation, as the angled cuts are more complex than standard rectangular formats. Order 20 to 25% overage to account for the additional complexity.
Does Pure White trapezoid work on floors as well as walls?
Yes. Pure White trapezoid is suitable for interior floors in both low- and high-traffic areas. The glaze is durable under foot traffic. For floor installations, a minimum of 1/8" grout joint is recommended to allow for tile thickness variation. For wet floor applications like shower floors, increase grout joints to 1/8" and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation.
