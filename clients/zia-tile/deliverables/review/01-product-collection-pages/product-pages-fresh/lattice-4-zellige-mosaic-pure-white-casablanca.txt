About
No two zellige mosaic sheets read alike. Tiles vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct. Open every box in your shipment and arrange a trial layout on a flat surface before setting tile, so the color balance and variation settle into the layout you want.


Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with California Prop 65. Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.


Seal the zellige mosaic according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at info@ziatile.com with any material or installation questions.

How It's Made
Zellige mosaic is handmade in Morocco using the same traditional techniques as field tile, refined by artisans whose families have practiced the craft for generations. The mosaic format is hand-placed on mesh backing for installation as designed sheets.


The process begins with raw clay mined from local Moroccan hillsides. Artisans soak the clay in water and filter it for impurities, then hand-press the resulting paste into molds before laying the tiles to dry in the Moroccan sun. Each tile is flattened and shaped by hand before glazing.


Artisans hand-dip each tile in glaze and arrange them inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat distribution creates the dynamic glazes and tonal variation that define traditional zellige. Once fired, tiles are hand-placed on mesh sheets. Every sheet is an expression of the artisan's work that day, beautifully imperfect and unique.

Frequently Asked Questions
What colorways make up the Lattice 4 mosaic?
The Lattice 4 mosaic combines two colorways from Zia's zellige collection: Pure White and Casablanca, arranged in a lattice pattern on an 8x8 mesh sheet. Both are light neutrals produced using the same traditional Moroccan methods, and each carries its own subtle tonal range that shifts with changing light. The two glazes sit close enough in tone to read as cohesive while remaining distinct enough to define the lattice pattern.
What is the Lattice pattern?
The Lattice 4 is a geometric lattice composition formed by the arrangement of zellige tiles on an 8x8 mesh sheet. The interlocking geometry references the traditional Moroccan mosaic tradition of installing zellige in decorative geometric and floral arrangements.
How different are Pure White and Casablanca zellige in the Lattice 4 mosaic?
Pure White and Casablanca sit close enough in tone for the lattice pattern to read as subtle geometry rather than high-contrast composition. Because both glazes are applied by hand, the degree of contrast also shifts from tile to tile and sheet to sheet, introducing organic variation into the pattern itself.
Does the Lattice 4 mosaic work alongside other neutral zellige colorways?
Yes. The Lattice 4 Pure White + Casablanca mosaic pairs well with other neutral zellige colorways in the same installation. Both component colorways occupy the neutral range, so the mosaic integrates naturally with the broader Zia zellige collection.
What is a zellige mosaic?
A zellige mosaic is a pre-arranged composition of handcrafted zellige tiles mounted on a mesh backing sheet. Each tile is produced using the same traditional Moroccan process as standard zellige. The mosaic format fixes a pattern such as the lattice composition, allowing it to install consistently across a surface without requiring the installer to arrange individual tiles on site.
How does a mosaic installation differ from a single-tile installation?
Single zellige tiles are placed individually, with the installer controlling spacing and layout piece by piece. Mesh-mounted mosaics arrive with pattern and spacing already established, and the installer sets the sheet as a unit. Before installation, separate sheets from multiple boxes and mix them to distribute variation evenly. Individual tiles can be removed from the mesh backing if edge or perimeter adjustments are needed.
