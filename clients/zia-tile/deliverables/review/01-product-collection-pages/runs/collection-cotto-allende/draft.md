# Cotto Allende

## Our Take on Glazed Terra Cotta

Zia's Cotto Allende is a glazed terra cotta tile crafted in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. It begins with the same clay body as our unglazed Cotto, hand-hewn and high-fired, then hand-glazed in pigments that carry color and texture beyond the spectrum of natural clay. The name references both its origin near San Miguel de Allende and the Spanish word for "beyond."

The line features eighteen colorways with finishes that range from near-matte to glossy, each carrying speckling, subtle crazing, and color shifts that vary by glaze. The glaze protects the clay body while keeping the tonal variation coveted in handmade tile, which makes Cotto Allende suitable for pools, spas, and showers as well as interior floors and walls.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do Cotto Allende tiles come from?

Our Cotto Allende tiles are made near San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, a UNESCO World Heritage city with a long tradition of art and ceramic production. The line shares its clay foundation with our unglazed Cotto, drawn from the same regional terra cotta and finished with pigmented glazes for a more colorful take on the classic material.

### What colorways, shapes, and formats does Cotto Allende come in?

Cotto Allende features eighteen colorways with names that reference Mexican geography and flora, including Sayulita, Cacao, Creosote, Peyote, Arroyo, Condesa, and Pedregal. Finishes range from near-matte to glossy, with speckling and subtle crazing that shift by glaze.

The line comes in four shapes:

- **4x4 square:** the standard format, suited to continuous fields and grid layouts
- **4x8 rectangle:** a brick-format option for stack-bond and running-bond walls
- **Triangle:** a geometric format for directional and patterned installations
- **1.5x8 mini bar:** the narrowest format, useful for linear or stack-bond layouts

### How are Cotto Allende tiles made?

Our Cotto Allende begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, hand-pressed and high-fired in the same way as unglazed Cotto, then hand-glazed. The hand-glazing introduces pigment over the fired clay body, producing the speckling, subtle crazing, and color shifts that vary from tile to tile. The result is a glazed surface that holds color and sheen while keeping the variation of handmade tile.

### Does Cotto Allende tile need special care?

Yes. Always seal Cotto Allende according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. Cotto Allende requires sealing after grouting in areas exposed to continuous moisture. Use only pH-neutral cleaning products, and avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners that can damage the glaze.

### What is the difference between Cotto and Cotto Allende, and where does each one belong?

Cotto and Cotto Allende are both made from the same Mexican clay body, but the finish defines their appearance and application. Cotto remains unglazed; Cotto Allende is finished with distinctive glazes. Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas. Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates.

### How do I install Cotto Allende tile?

Installing Cotto Allende requires a professional installer experienced with glazed terra cotta. Each tile has its own [installation guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cotto-allende-installation-guide), and the sealing protocol differs from unglazed Cotto, so the two should never follow a combined instruction set. ### How much Cotto Allende tile do I need for my project?

Start with the total square footage of the surface. Measure the length and width of each area in feet, then multiply for the surface area, and add all sections together for multi-surface projects. From that total, add a minimum of 15 to 20% overage to account for curation and cuts. For a more uniform look, plan for 20 to 25% across the full order.

For project-specific quantity questions, contact the Zia team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### Can Cotto Allende be installed in a pool or spa?

Yes. Glazed Cotto Allende is approved for pools and spas. The glaze seals the clay body against continuous moisture, which unglazed Cotto cannot match. Cotto Allende should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates, so confirm the climate of the installation before specifying it for an exterior pool or spa.
