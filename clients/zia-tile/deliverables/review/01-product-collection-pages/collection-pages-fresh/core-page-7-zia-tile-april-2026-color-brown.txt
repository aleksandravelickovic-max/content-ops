Brown Tile
Brown tile at Zia moves through clay, cement, stone, and glaze, so the color changes in each material. Cotto and Cotto Allende carry brown through fired clay, while Cantera stone, cement, terrazzo, ceramics, and zellige each bring a different surface and finish. Zellige shows inherent variation in tone, along with chips, pits, and crazing. Cement and terrazzo offer a more measured field, while Cotto and Cantera bring color from the body of the material itself.

Frequently Asked Questions
Can brown bathroom tile be used in a shower?
Yes, brown bathroom tile can be used in a shower when the material is suited for wet areas. Glazed brown zellige tile works on shower walls but carries a DCOF of 0.16, so it is best kept off shower floors. Cotto Allende handles both shower walls and floors with proper sealing and a minimum 1/8" grout joint. Matte ceramic with a DCOF of 0.74 suits both walls and floors. Seal all of these materials after grouting and reseal every one to two years, more often in shower environments.
How do you clean and maintain brown tile?
Regular sweeping or vacuuming removes grit from the surface and does more to protect the tile than heavy cleaning after the fact. Use a pH-neutral cleaner and avoid acidic products like vinegar or bleach, which can damage glaze and natural stone. Cotto, Cotto Allende, Cantera stone, and zellige require sealing — follow the schedule in the installation guides, and plan on more frequent sealing in showers and other wet areas.
Where can brown tiles be used in a home?
Brown tiles work across floors and walls, depending on the material. Cotto, cement, and terrazzo are common for interior floors, including kitchens, entryways, and living spaces. Zellige and ceramic are often used on backsplashes and bathroom walls, where surface variation or consistency can be more visible. In showers, glazed zellige works on walls, while Cotto Allende and matte ceramic handle both walls and floors with a minimum 1/8" grout joint and proper sealing.
Do brown tiles vary in tone or texture?
Yes, brown tiles vary in tone and texture, but the level depends on the material. Zellige shows the most movement, with shifts in tone and surface along with chips, pits, and crazing. Cotto and Cantera vary based on the clay or stone composition, so color can shift across a batch. Cement and terrazzo are more controlled but still show subtle differences. Texture follows the same pattern, from the uneven surface of handmade tile to the smoother finish of ceramic.
Is ordering samples recommended before placing a full order?
Yes, ordering samples before placing a full order is recommended. Brown tile can read differently depending on the material and the lighting in the space. Viewing samples in the installation area helps confirm tone, texture, and variation before committing. Samples can be added from any tile page by selecting the color and size. How much brown tile is needed for a project?
Measure each surface individually, multiply length by width in feet, and add all surfaces together for the total square footage. Add a minimum of 15% overage to account for cuts and variation. For handmade materials like zellige and Cotto, 15 to 20% overage is recommended, with 25% for a more uniform result.
