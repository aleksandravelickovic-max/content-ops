Frequently Asked Questions
What are rectangular tiles called?
Rectangular tiles are typically referred to by their sizes, such as 2x6, 4x12, or 12x24 inches, though some formats carry specific category names tied to their proportions and origin. The most widely recognized is the subway tile, a rectangular format named after the glazed white tiles installed in the New York City subway system, a look that can be easily achieved with our 2x8 ceramics or cement solids. In Moroccan zellige, the elongated 2x6 rectangular brick is known as Bejmat, a traditional tile with centuries of use in North African architecture and interiors.
What is a Bejmat zellige tile?
Bejmat is the traditional Moroccan name for an elongated rectangular zellige tile brick, most commonly produced in a 2x6 size. Like all zellige, each Bejmat tile is hand-pressed from clay, sun-dried, hand-shaped, and hand-dipped in glaze before being fired in an earthen kiln. The result is a tile with the same characteristic chips, pits, and crazing as square zellige formats, but whose length allows for a wider range of layout options, including horizontal, vertical, offset, or herringbone designs. 
Are square or rectangular tiles better?
Square versus rectangular tile is not a question of quality but of spatial intention. Rectangular formats create directionality, elongating a surface along the longer axis. Square formats distribute visual weight evenly and let the material's color and texture lead. The better choice depends on the room's proportions and the desired design direction.
Can a rectangular tile be used on both floors and walls?
Yes, Zia’s collection of rectangular tiles can be used on floors, walls, and any tiled surface. The specific material considerations determine the scope of approved applications more than the scale or shape itself. Zellige, cement, ceramic, Cotto, limestone, Cantera stone, terrazzo, and marble are all approved for floor and wall use in their rectangular formats. Material-specific sealing requirements and slip resistance guidance apply to wet floor applications, and outdoor installation selections are limited in freeze/thaw climates. 
What size rectangular tile should I choose?
Rectangular tile size should be proportional to the specific tiled surface and the overall design of the space. Smaller formats like 2x6 and 2x8 suit backsplashes and shower walls where the grout joint pattern contributes to the design. Mid-range formats like 4x8 and 4x12 work well across bathroom floors, kitchen walls, and feature walls where a less interrupted surface is preferred. Larger formats like 12x24 anchor expansive floors and walls in both residential and commercial settings. As a general rule, the tile size should be proportional to the surface it covers, though less common designs, such as the Cotto Allende Mini Bars, make an intentionally eye-catching statement.
What layout patterns work best with rectangular tile?
Three of the most common layouts for rectangular tile are running bond, stack bond, and herringbone. Running bond, where each tile is offset by half the length of the tile above or below it, is the most traditional setting and visually widens a surface. A vertical stack bond reads taller and more contemporary. Herringbone adds geometry and works particularly well with smaller formats like 2x6 Bejmat zellige and 2x8 cement. For more dynamic handmade materials like zellige, a dry layout before setting is essential to distribute variation evenly across the surface.
What are the best applications for rectangular tile?
Rectangular tile performs best in applications where guiding the eye is a design goal: kitchen backsplashes, shower walls, bathroom floors, fireplace surrounds, and exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates provide opportunities to transform a surface with texture and color. A vertical stack bond on a shower wall reads taller. A horizontal running bond on a backsplash reads wider. Material determines the final scope of approved uses, and sealing is required in wet applications for zellige, cement, Cotto, and natural stone tiles.
How do I maintain rectangular tile?
Our guidance for ongoing care for rectangular tile is specific to the material itself, rather than the tile size. All tiles should be cleaned with a pH-neutral cleaner, while acidic, bleach-based, or abrasive products that damage glazes, strip sealers, or etch natural stone should be avoided. Sealing is advised for most installations, and specific guidance can be found in our installation guides. Resealing is required on a schedule appropriate to traffic and moisture exposure, to protect both the tile and the grout over time. Grout lines in rectangular installations are long and continuous, so regular sweeping and/or vacuuming and prompt attention to spills, along with regular resealing in wet areas, is particularly important.
What should I know about contrasting grout with rectangular tile?
Contrasting grout with rectangular tile makes the geometric grid really pop. In a running bond or stack bond layout, the continuous horizontal and vertical lines of a contrasting grout become a graphic element in the design. Against porous unglazed materials like Cotto and cement tile, contrasting grout must be applied carefully by hand or with a grout bag to avoid staining. On glazed surfaces, including zellige and ceramic, sealing before grouting is required in wet areas regardless of grout color.
