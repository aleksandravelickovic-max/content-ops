Bathroom Tiles
Bathroom tile has to do a lot of work. It sheds water, handles humidity, and needs to hold up underfoot while still setting the tone for a room that often runs small and low on natural light. Color matters here more than in most spaces. A lighter palette opens a compact bath. A darker one gives a larger room weight and presence. At Zia, bathroom tile options draw from the full range of collections, including handmade zellige and encaustic cement.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is DCOF, and why does it matter in a bathroom?
DCOF stands for Dynamic Coefficient of Friction. It measures how much resistance a tile surface provides underfoot when wet. The higher the rating, the more traction the tile provides. In bathrooms, particularly on shower floors and wet room floors, DCOF matters because a low rating increases the risk of slipping on a wet surface.
How should bathroom tile be cleaned?
All Zia tiles should be cleaned with a pH-neutral cleaner. For routine use, a pH-neutral tile and stone cleaner handles most surfaces. For heavier stains, a non-acidic heavy-duty cleaner works well. For more specific guidance, contact the Zia team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
Which tile is best for bathroom installations?
Start with a design direction and find the tile that best meets it. Slip resistance, aesthetics, and scale will help narrow the options. Glazed tiles are easy to wipe clean, but matte cement and stone options perform well in bathrooms too. Specification sheets are available for a closer look at how each tile performs.
Does bathroom tile need to be sealed?
It depends on the tile. Consult the installation guide for the specific material to find sealer selection and application guidance. Plan to reseal over time. The quickest test is whether water beads on the surface. If it absorbs, it is time to reseal.
What is the best tile for a shower?
Shower walls and floors have different requirements. Almost any Zia material works on shower walls: glazed zellige, honed marble, matte ceramic, and glass mosaic. Floors need more grip. Glass mosaic and small-format hexagons work well, as does matte ceramic with a DCOF of 0.42 or higher. Glazed zellige rates DCOF 0.16 and is suited for walls but not shower floors. On any shower floor, hold a minimum 1/8" grout joint and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation. Seal anything porous before and after grouting.
Is zellige a good choice for a bathroom tile?
Yes. The hand-glazed surface handles water well, and the variation from tile to tile, including chips, pits, and crazing, is a characteristic of the material. Zia's zellige is rated for shower walls, backsplashes, and feature walls. With a DCOF of 0.16, it is best kept off shower floors. Seal according to Zia's installation guide for wet area installations.
What is the best marble for a bathroom?
The choice of marble for a bathroom comes down to palette and maintenance preference. Carrara has classic white and grey veining and works across most palettes. Grigio Carnico carries cooler grey tones. Nero Marquina is black with bold white veining. Every marble bathroom tile requires sealing with a penetrating sealer before and after grouting, plus periodic resealing.
Can the same tile be used on bathroom walls and floors?
Yes, in most cases. The main consideration is slip resistance on the floor. A tile that works well on a wall may not carry a high enough DCOF rating for a wet floor. Check the DCOF rating of the specific tile before specifying it for both surfaces. On any wet floor, hold a minimum 1/8" grout joint and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation.
What is the difference between glazed and unglazed tile in a bathroom?
Glazed tile has a layer of glass-like coating applied before firing, which makes the surface easier to clean and more resistant to staining. Unglazed tile carries the color of the raw material itself and tends to have more natural traction underfoot. In a bathroom, glazed tile is common on walls and backsplashes. Unglazed or matte tile is often specified for floors where slip resistance is a priority.
