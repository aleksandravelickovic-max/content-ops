Blue Tile
Blue tile shifts more with light than most colors. In zellige, the glaze reflects across the surface, showing variation in tone along with chips, pits, and crazing. In ceramics, the color reads more even and controlled. Cement and terrazzo hold blue in a matte field. The same blue reads differently depending on the material, which is why it works across backsplashes, bathroom walls, pools, and floors.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the best blue tile for a pool?
Pool applications require materials rated for submerged and wet environments. Blue glass mosaic tile is suitable for pools and spas and requires no sealing. For all pool applications, confirm the specific DCOF rating before installation. For guidance, contact the Zia team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
How do you clean and maintain blue tile?
Regular sweeping or vacuuming prevents surface abrasion and does more to protect the tile than heavy cleaning after the fact. Use a pH-neutral cleaner and avoid bleach, vinegar, and acidic products, which etch glazes and damage stone. Seal blue cement tile and zellige on the schedule specified in Zia's installation guides for each material. Glass mosaic tile requires no sealing.
What causes variation and movement in blue glazed tiles?
Variation in blue glazed tiles comes from the glazing and firing process. In handmade materials like zellige and Cotto Allende, glaze is applied by hand and fired in kilns with uneven heat, which creates shifts in tone from tile to tile. The surface also shows chips, pits, and crazing. This combination produces the movement visible across the installation.
Is blue tile a good choice for a bathroom?
Yes, blue tile works well in a bathroom across several materials. Blue zellige and blue ceramic tiles suit shower walls. On shower floors, specify a matte or textured finish and hold a minimum 1/8" grout joint for slip resistance. Apply an anti-slip treatment after installation for additional traction. Seal zellige and cement tile every one to two years, and more often in shower environments.
How much variation should be expected in glazed blue tiles?
The level of variation in glazed blue tile depends on the material. Zellige carries the most, with shifts in tone and surface from tile to tile, along with chips, pits, and crazing as inherent characteristics. Ceramic glazes are applied more uniformly, producing a more consistent color across the surface. Cement tile sits between the two, with subtle color shifts from the hand-pouring process but without the surface variation of zellige.
How much blue tile is needed for a project?
Measure each surface individually, multiply length by width in feet, and add all surfaces together for the total square footage. Add a minimum of 15% overage to account for cuts and variation. For zellige and Cotto Allende, 15 to 20% overage is recommended, with 25% for a more uniform result. For project-specific guidance, contact the Zia team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.
