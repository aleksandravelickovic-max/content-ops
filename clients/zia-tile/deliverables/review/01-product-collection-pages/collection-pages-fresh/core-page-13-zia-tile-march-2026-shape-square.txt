Frequently Asked Questions
What is considered a square tile?
A square tile is any tile whose length and width are equal. The shape has been crafted in ceramic, zellige, stone, cement, and glass mosaic for centuries and covers a wide range of sizes, from small square tiles at 2x2 to large-format 24x24 field tiles. Each material brings a distinct color, finish, and design perspective to a square dimension.
Where can square tiles be used?
Square tile works across nearly every residential and commercial application. Common uses include floors, bathroom walls, shower walls, fireplace surrounds, and square backsplash tile in kitchens. For exterior floor applications, suitability depends on the material and climate. Not all square tile formats are approved for outdoor use or are suitable for freeze/thaw climates. Smaller formats like 2x2 or 4x4 tiles suit shower floors and detail work. Larger formats, like 12x12 and 24x24, anchor open floors and expansive walls.
Is square tile timeless, or does it read as a trend?
Square tile is timeless because the format carries no single style; it appears in Roman bath complexes, Moroccan riads, and mid-century modern interiors. Zia’s curated yet expansive collection features materials that have served as staples for centuries, proving that great design is truly timeless.
Square tile vs. rectangular tile: which is right for my space?
Square tile versus rectangular tile is not a performance question. Both formats work equally well on floors and walls. Rectangular formats elongate a surface and direct the eye along the longer axis. Square formats distribute visual weight evenly and let the material's color and texture carry the room. The right choice depends on the proportions of the space and the visual effect you want to achieve.
What should I know about using contrasting grout with square tile?
Contrasting grout amplifies the grid. The effect ranges from a quiet tonal shift to a graphic pattern depending on the specific grout color choice. On porous unglazed materials like Cotto and cement tile, contrasting grout carries a staining risk and should be applied carefully by hand or with a grout bag. On glazed surfaces, sealing before grouting is required in wet areas regardless of grout color. This applies to 4x4 zellige and all other glazed square formats in the collection.
What are small square tiles called?
A small square tile at or below 2x2 inches is called a square mosaic tile. When the individual units are hand-cut stone, the traditional term is tesserae, from the Latin word for the small square pieces used in Roman and Byzantine mosaic work. Zia's Roman Mosaic collection uses Italian marble hand-cut into 5/8-inch tesserae squares by artisans in Italy, backed on mesh sheets for installation. We also offer zellige solids and checkerboards in 2x2-inch squares, and our newest collection of 1x1-inch recycled glass mosaics. 
Should square tiles be set in a grid or staggered?
Whether to set a square tile grid-on-grid or staggered depends on the material and the space. Grid-on-grid suits contemporary interiors and emphasizes the regularity of the format. A staggered offset reads as more artisanal and helps distribute natural size variation in handmade materials. Regardless of pattern, blend tiles from multiple boxes and lay the full installation out dry before setting to assess the composition.
How do I choose the right square tile size for my space?
Small square tile formats like 2x2 and 4x4 work well in compact spaces, on shower floors, and as detail or accent work. The smaller sizes are also easier to install on any curved surfaces. Mid-range formats around 8x8-inch squares are versatile across floors and walls in both residential and commercial settings. Larger formats like 12x12 and 24x24 suit open floors and expansive walls, where fewer grout lines read as cleaner and more minimal. As a general rule, the tile size should be proportional to the surface it covers, though a larger installation of smaller tiles makes a stunning statement.

How do I calculate how many square tiles I need?
Start by measuring the length and width of the surface in feet and multiplying to get the total square footage. For a floor, measure the room. For a wall, measure each section separately and add them together. Once you have the total square footage, add overage to account for cuts, natural variation, and any tiles set aside during installation. 
For most square tile materials, we recommend a minimum of 15% overage. For handmade materials like zellige, where variation in size and surface is inherent to the tile, 15 to 20% overage is recommended, with 25% if you are seeking a more uniform result.
