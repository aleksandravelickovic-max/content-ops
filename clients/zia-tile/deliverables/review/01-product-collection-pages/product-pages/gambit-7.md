## How It's Made

Our Gambit 7 is produced in Fez, Morocco, through the same fully manual process that defines all traditional zellige. Raw clay is pulled from local hillsides, soaked in water, filtered for impurities, and worked by hand until it reaches the right consistency for pressing into molds. Each element of the mosaic is then shaped again by hand to refine its edges and surface.

  

After drying in the Moroccan sun, each element is hand-dipped in one of three glazes: Absinthe, Tidepool, or Portuguese Blue. Because the glaze is applied by hand, coverage varies within each colorway, and that variation produces the tonal range visible across the finished multi-color Moroccan mosaic tile. The elements are then hand-assembled onto mesh backing, forming the 8x8 sheets that arrive on site ready for installation.

  

The final stage is the kiln. Earthen kilns do not distribute heat evenly, and the results of that unevenness are not incidental. The chips, pits, crazing, and shifting tones that emerge from the firing process are what make each Gambit 7 mosaic its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Gambit 7 zellige good for floors?

Yes, Gambit 7 zellige is suitable for floors, including high-traffic areas such as kitchens, primary bathrooms, and entryways. The mosaic format contributes to slip resistance through higher grout joint density, which makes it particularly appropriate for shower floors and wet-area installations. For wet floor applications, size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" and/or apply an anti-slip product after installation is complete.

### What grout color works best with Gambit 7 zellige?

The best grout color for Gambit 7 zellige is a light neutral, such as soft white, light grey, or cream. These tones allow the Absinthe, Tidepool, and Portuguese Blue palette to remain the focal point while minimizing visual separation between elements. A darker grey grout can be used where the intention is to emphasize the hand-formed edges and surface irregularity of each piece, though the three-color composition generally reads more clearly against a neutral grout.

### Do zellige tiles require grout?

Yes, zellige installations require grout between tiles. Traditional installations can appear closely set, but grout is necessary to protect tile edges and support long-term durability. For Gambit 7, grout also plays a design role by shaping how the three colorways transition across the field, particularly where saturated Portuguese Blue meets the cooler Tidepool range.

### Can Gambit 7 zellige be used on exterior walls?

Yes, Gambit 7 zellige can be used on exterior walls in non-freeze/thaw climates. The mosaic format performs reliably on vertical surfaces where the glazed finish and variation are protected from direct structural impact. It is not suitable for outdoor installations in freeze/thaw climates. Sealing is required for any exterior installation to maintain surface integrity over time.

### Why do designers choose zellige tile?

Designers choose zellige for its handcrafted surface, inherent material variation, and light-responsive glaze. Each tile is individually formed, dipped, and fired, producing the chips, pits, and crazing that define its surface character. The result is a living installation where a zellige backsplash or feature wall shifts with light and viewing angle rather than reading as static or uniform.

### Can I mix Gambit 7 with other zellige colors in the collection?

Yes, you can mix Gambit 7 with other colors in Zia's zellige collection. Each colorway in the line is produced using the same traditional methods, so tiles and mosaic sheets share consistent surface character across the range. Gambit 7 works particularly well alongside neutral whites and softer tones, where the three-color mosaic reads as a focal point against a quieter surrounding palette.
