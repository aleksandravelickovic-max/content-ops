## How It's Made — \[ACCORDION\]

Production starts at ground level. Artisans source raw clay from local Moroccan hillsides, soak it in water, and filter it for impurities until it reaches a consistency suitable for hand-pressing into molds.



The trapezoid form is hand-shaped after the initial pressing, which is where the slight variation in edge and dimension comes from. Once shaped, the tiles dry in the Moroccan sun before each piece is dipped individually in glaze. The Pure White glaze settles differently on each tile, producing the tonal range that defines the colorway. No two dips produce identical results.



The glazed tiles are arranged in earthen kilns and fired. Heat distributes unevenly through the chamber, and that unevenness is what produces the chips, pits, crazing, and tonal shifts that make each Pure White Trapezoid entirely its own.

## Frequently Asked Questions — \[ACCORDION\]

### Can I mix Pure White Trapezoid zellige with other formats in the collection?

Yes, the Pure White Trapezoid can be mixed with other formats in Zia's zellige collection. Each format is produced using the same traditional methods and shares consistent material character across the range. The trapezoid works well as a field tile or accent alongside square and rectangular zellige formats, where it introduces geometric detail without competing with the surrounding surface.

### Why choose a trapezoid format over a square or rectangle?

The trapezoid format introduces a geometric logic to the surface that square and rectangular formats do not produce. Where a square or rectangular tile creates a grid, the trapezoid creates an interlocking pattern that draws the eye across the surface rather than stopping it at each tile edge.

### How do I start the layout for a trapezoid installation?

To start the layout for a trapezoid installation, begin at the center of the surface and work outward. Establishing a center point allows the pattern to remain balanced and symmetrical across the field, with cuts distributed evenly at the perimeter. Lay out the full dry composition before setting any tile, as the interlocking nature of the trapezoid format means adjustments are easier to make before thinset is involved.

### How do I handle corners and edges with a trapezoid tile?

Corners and edges with a trapezoid tile require careful planning before cutting begins. The angled edges of the trapezoid mean that perimeter cuts will not be straight across, and the angle of the cut will depend on the orientation of the tile at that point in the pattern. Work with your installer to map out edge cuts in advance. All cuts must be made with a water-cooled tile saw fitted with a diamond edge blade.

### Does the interlocking trapezoid pattern require a specific tile orientation?

Yes, the interlocking trapezoid pattern requires consistent orientation to read correctly across the surface. The standard approach alternates each tile by flipping it, which creates the interlocking effect. Deviating from this orientation breaks the pattern and can create gaps or misalignments that are difficult to correct once the thinset has cured. Establishing the orientation before installation begins and confirming it with your installer is an important step in the preparation process.

### What is the visual difference between a tight and a wider grout joint with the trapezoid format?

A tight grout joint with the trapezoid format emphasizes the geometric pattern itself, allowing the interlocking shape to read as a continuous, unbroken composition. A wider grout joint draws more attention to the individual tile and the variation in each piece, including chips, pits, and tonal shifts. For wall installations, the traditional approach uses no standard grout joint, with tiles laid edge to edge or with a 1/16" spacing. For floors, a minimum of 1/8" is recommended, and the wider joint also contributes to slip resistance in wet applications.
