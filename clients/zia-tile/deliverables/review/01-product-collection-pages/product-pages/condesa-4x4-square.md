## How It's Made

Our Condesa 4x4 begins with natural Mexican clay drawn from the hills around San Miguel de Allende, where terracotta production has carried on for centuries. The clay is refined through soaking and filtering to preserve its mineral composition while achieving a workable consistency for forming. Each tile is then hand-pressed into a square mold, where the geometry takes shape based on material response rather than mechanical uniformity.

  

After forming, each tile is left to dry in open-air conditions for one to two weeks. This gradual drying releases moisture slowly and stabilizes the structure before firing. Glaze is then applied before the tile enters the kiln, where it interacts with the clay body during high-temperature firing. Variations in kiln heat and airflow produce the shifts in tone, glaze density, and surface texture that give Condesa its deep, earthy range.

  

Crazing, chips, and subtle surface movement develop during this process and remain part of the finished tile rather than a condition to be corrected. Because the palette is shaped by the clay and the firing rather than a uniform surface treatment, no two Condesa 4x4 tiles carry the same tonal profile, and that variation is what distinguishes handmade Cotto Allende from manufactured alternatives.

## Frequently Asked Questions 

### What is the Cotto Allende?

Cotto Allende is a glazed terracotta tile made from natural clay in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. The clay is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired with a glaze applied before firing. This process produces a smoother, more sealed surface than unglazed cotto, with inherent variation in tone and the chips, pits, and crazing that develop as the glaze settles across the clay body during firing.

### Is Condesa 4x4 Cotto Allende suitable for pools and spas?

Yes, Condesa 4x4 Cotto Allende is suitable for both saltwater and chlorine pool and spa installations. Sealing with 511 Porous Plus is required before and after grouting to protect both the tile surface and grout under continuous water exposure. Do not use Fila Matte Wax on Cotto Allende; 511 Porous Plus is the only recommended finish for this line.

### Is the Condesa 4x4 square good for terra cotta tile flooring?

Yes, Condesa 4x4 is suitable for flooring in both residential and commercial settings. A minimum 1/8" grout joint is required to support alignment and installation accuracy across the field. In wet floor applications, slip resistance can be increased by maintaining this grout joint size and/or applying an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete.

### How do you clean the Cotto Allende terra cotta tiles?

To clean Cotto Allende, use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water and a soft cloth. Avoid acidic or abrasive products, which can affect the glazed surface and make crazing more visible over time. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove abrasive debris. Resealing with 511 Porous Plus is required in wet and high-traffic areas to maintain surface performance.

### How long do terra cotta tiles last?

Terracotta tile lasts for decades when installed and maintained correctly. Cotto Allende develops a natural patina over time while maintaining its structural integrity under regular use. Long-term performance depends on proper substrate preparation, consistent maintenance, and adherence to sealing requirements at each stage of installation.

### Can Condesa 4x4 be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways?

Condesa 4x4 can be combined with other colorways in the Cotto Allende collection. Each colorway is produced using the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the range. The deep brown of Condesa pairs well against lighter colorways such as Puebla or Cacao to create contrast across the field.
