## How It's Made

Our Cacao 4x4 begins with natural Mexican clay sourced from the hills around San Miguel de Allende, a region that has produced terra cotta for centuries. Artisans press the clay into molds, then set the formed tiles out to dry in the sun before firing them at high temperatures in traditional kilns. The clay body that emerges carries the density and tonal range that define cotto production in this part of Mexico.

  

What distinguishes Cotto Allende from traditional cotto is the glaze. Before firing, each tile is coated in a pigmented glaze that sets the color and creates a smoother, more sealed surface than the unglazed cotto line. The Cacao glaze is where the cool blue-brown tones take form, deepening against the warm terra cotta body to produce the contrast that defines the colorway.

  

Crazing is part of the process. As the glaze settles across the clay body during firing, fine hairline lines develop across the surface. These are not defects. They are the conditions under which a glazed terra cotta tile earns its character.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Cotto Allende?

Cotto Allende is a glazed terra cotta tile produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. It begins with the same natural Mexican clay as our unglazed cotto line and adds a pigmented glaze before firing, which produces a smoother, more sealed surface with more defined color saturation. Subtle crazing and tonal variation are inherent characteristics of the line.

### How does Cacao Cotto Allende differ from unglazed cotto?

Cacao Cotto Allende differs from unglazed cotto in three ways that affect both aesthetics and application. The glaze produces a smoother, less porous surface with more consistent color than the matte, porous finish of unglazed cotto. Cacao is suitable for pool and spa installations, which unglazed cotto is not. And Cotto Allende is sealed with 511 Porous Plus only, where unglazed cotto is finished with a wax topcoat indoors.

### Why does my Cacao tile have fine surface lines?

Fine surface lines on Cacao, known as crazing, occur naturally during the firing process as the glaze settles across the terra cotta body. Crazing is an inherent characteristic of Cotto Allende and does not compromise the structural integrity of the tile. Matching grout color closely to the tile helps integrate crazing into the overall composition rather than emphasizing it.

### How do I seal Cacao Cotto Allende?

To seal Cacao Cotto Allende, apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers before grouting until the tile is fully saturated, wiping off any excess before it dries. Allow the sealer to cure fully before proceeding to grout. After the grout has cured, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus to protect both the tile and the grout lines. Do not use Fila Matte Wax on Cotto Allende; 511 Porous Plus is the only recommended finish for this line.

### Can Cacao be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways?

Cacao 4x4 can be mixed with other colorways in the Cotto Allende collection. Each colorway is produced using the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the range. Mixing Cacao with warmer colorways produces a layered composition where the cool blue-brown sits in deliberate contrast against the earthier tones of the line.

### How is Cacao Cotto Allende cleaned?

Clean Cacao Cotto Allende with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Tile and Stone Cleaner, warm water, and a soft cloth or mop. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove abrasive debris that can affect the glazed surface over time. Avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners, which can damage the glaze and make crazing more visible. For heavier staining, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) is recommended.
