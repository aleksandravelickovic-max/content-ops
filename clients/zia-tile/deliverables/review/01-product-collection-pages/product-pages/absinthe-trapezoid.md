## How It's Made

Our Absinthe Trapezoid reflects the working traditions of Fez, Morocco, where handcrafted zellige production stays true to artisanal practices refined over centuries. Artisans begin with raw clay pulled from local hillsides, soaking and filtering it to remove impurities before pressing it into molds. The trapezoid geometry is established at this stage, where each piece is hand-shaped into its final angular form rather than extruded or mechanically cut.

  

Once partially dried under the Moroccan sun, each tile is hand-dipped in an aqua-hued glaze. The semi-transparent application is what allows the clay body underneath to influence the final tone, producing a layered surface rather than a flat color. Because the glaze is applied by hand, coverage varies tile to tile, and that variation is what creates the range between soft mint and deeper water-toned greens visible across a full installation.

  

The final stage is the kiln. Tiles are fired in earthen kilns where heat moves unevenly through the chamber. This firing process is what produces the dynamic tonal shifts, along with the chips, pits, and crazing that appear as part of the surface rather than as a condition to be corrected. Each Absinthe Trapezoid records the conditions of its making.
