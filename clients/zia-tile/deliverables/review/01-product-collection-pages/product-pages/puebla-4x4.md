## How It's Made

Our Puebla 4x4 begins with natural Mexican clay drawn from the hills around San Miguel de Allende, where terra cotta production has carried on for centuries. Artisans press the clay into molds, set the formed tiles out to dry in the sun, then fire them at high temperatures in traditional kilns. The dense clay body that emerges holds the tonal range and durability that define cotto production in this part of Mexico.

The glaze is what distinguishes the Puebla colorway. Before firing, each tile is coated in a pigmented satin glaze that produces the speckled white surface characteristic of the line. The speckling references the slip-glazed pottery traditions of central Mexico, where hand-fired ceramics carry similar pigment behavior across the surface. Because the glaze is applied by hand, no two tiles carry the same distribution of speckling, and the full character of the installation only emerges once the tiles are set together.

Crazing is part of the process. As the glaze settles across the clay body during firing, fine hairline lines develop across the surface. These lines are not defects. They are what separates a handcrafted glazed terra cotta tile from a manufactured ceramic substitute.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What gives Puebla 4x4 its speckled appearance?

The speckled appearance of Puebla 4x4 comes from the satin glaze, which carries pigment that settles across the surface during firing to produce the fine speckling characteristic of the line. The effect references the slip-glazed pottery traditions of central Mexico, where hand-fired ceramics develop similar pigment behavior. Because the glaze is hand-applied and fired in traditional kilns, each tile carries its own distribution of speckling.

### How does Puebla differ from Cacao and other Cotto Allende colorways?

Puebla differs from Cacao and other Cotto Allende colorways in both finish and tonal direction. Where Cacao presents a cool blue-brown glaze over the terra cotta body, Puebla carries a satin white with fine speckling that reads lighter and more textured. All Cotto Allende tiles share the same clay body, firing process, and approved applications, so the practical difference across the line is visual.

### Can I use Puebla 4x4 in a pool or spa?

Yes, Puebla 4x4 is suitable for saltwater and chlorine pool and spa installations. Sealing is required before and after grouting to protect both the tile and the grout from continuous moisture exposure. Apply 511 Porous Plus at both stages; do not use Fila Matte Wax on Cotto Allende. Refer to the Zia Tile Installation Guide for full sealing instructions.

### How do I seal Puebla Cotto Allende?

To seal Puebla Cotto Allende, apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers before grouting until the tile is fully saturated, wiping off any excess before it dries. Allow the sealer to cure fully, then grout in sections no larger than 2'x2' and wipe away excess immediately. Once the grout has cured, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus. Do not use Fila Matte Wax on Cotto Allende; 511 Porous Plus is the only recommended finish for this line.

### Can Puebla be mixed with warmer Cotto Allende colorways?

Puebla 4x4 can be mixed with warmer colorways in the Cotto Allende line. Each colorway is produced using the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent thickness, format, and surface character across the range. Mixing the speckled white of Puebla with the warmer, more saturated colorways produces a layered composition where the satin surface reads as a lighter counterpoint against the earthier tones.

### How is Puebla Cotto Allende cleaned?

Clean Puebla Cotto Allende with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Tile and Stone Cleaner, warm water, and a soft cloth or mop. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove abrasive debris that can wear down the satin surface over time. Avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners, which can damage the glaze and make crazing more visible. For heavier staining, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) is recommended.
