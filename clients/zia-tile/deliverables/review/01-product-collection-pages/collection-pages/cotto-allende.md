# Cotto Allende

## Terra Cotta With a Top Coat

Cotto Allende is Zia's glazed terra cotta tile, handmade near San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. Named for the historic city where it is produced, Allende also means "beyond" in Spanish, reflecting the glazed finishes that extend the material beyond the natural spectrum of unglazed clay. Each tile is hand-formed, high-fired, and finished with a pigmented glaze, producing a smoother surface with subtle crazing and enhanced tonal variation across each piece.

Cotto Allende shares the same clay foundation as Zia's unglazed Cotto, with glazed finishes ranging from near-matte to glossy and speckling that shifts by colorway. Eighteen colorways reference Mexican geography and flora: Sayulita, Cacao, Creosote, Peyote, Arroyo, Condesa, Pedregal, and others. Four shapes include the 4x4 square, 4x8 rectangle, triangle, and the 1.5x8 mini bar. Cotto Allende is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where does Cotto Allende come from?

Cotto Allende is produced near San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, a UNESCO World Heritage city with a long tradition of ceramic and clay production. The collection takes its name from the city. Allende also means "beyond" in Spanish, reflecting the glazed finish that distinguishes this line from Zia's unglazed Cotto. Local clay and established kiln practices shape the density, tonal range, and surface character of each tile.

### What colorways and formats does Cotto Allende come in?

Cotto Allende features eighteen colorways, each named for Mexican geography and flora: Sayulita, Cacao, Creosote, Peyote, Arroyo, Condesa, Pedregal, and others. Glazes range from near-matte to glossy, with speckling that varies by colorway. Four shapes are available: the 4x4 square, 4x8 rectangle, triangle, and the 1.5x8 mini bar — the narrowest format in the Cotto family, suited to linear and stack-bond layouts. For the full current range, browse the [collection](https://ziatile.com/collections/cotto-tile-allende) or contact the Zia team at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### How is Cotto Allende made?

Cotto Allende begins with natural Mexican clay pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired in kilns. Before firing, each tile is finished with a pigmented glaze, which creates a smoother, more sealed surface with subtle crazing and defined color saturation, while retaining the inherent variation expected from handmade clay tile.

### Does Cotto Allende need special care?

Yes. Cotto Allende requires sealing in areas exposed to continuous moisture, including showers, pools, and spas. For proper care:

  - Seal with a penetrating sealer in moisture-exposed areas after grouting
  - Reseal regularly, more frequently in high-traffic or moisture-exposed areas
  - Use only pH-neutral cleaning products
  - Avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners, which can damage the glaze or accentuate crazing

Over time, the surface develops a natural patina as a material response to use and environment. This is a defining characteristic of glazed clay tile.

### How to clean Cotto Allende

Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove debris. For wet cleaning, use warm water with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Tile and Stone Cleaner, applied with a soft cloth or mop. For stubborn residue, use a non-acidic cleaner such as Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Avoid abrasive tools or pads, which can scratch the glazed surface and make crazing more visible.

### How do I install Cotto Allende?

Cotto Allende requires a professional installer experienced with handmade glazed clay materials. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and dry-lay the full layout to distribute variation evenly.

During installation:

  - Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset mortar for proper adhesion
  - Wet the back of each tile before setting to reduce suction
  - Set tiles with 1/8" grout joints using Mapei Flexcolor CQ grout

Sealing and grouting:

  - Apply a penetrating sealer after grouting in moisture-exposed areas

For full instructions, refer to the [Cotto Allende Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cotto-allende-installation-guide). For project-specific installation questions, the Zia team is available at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### What is Cotto Allende used for?

As a glazed terra cotta tile, Cotto Allende is suited for floors, walls, and wet spaces. It performs well in interior living spaces, kitchens, and high-traffic areas when properly sealed. Glazed Cotto Allende is approved for pools and spas. Cotto Allende is not suitable for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates.

Common applications:

  - Interior floors and walls across residential and commercial projects
  - Shower walls and floors, with proper sealing and installation
  - Pools and spas, where the glazed surface supports continuous moisture exposure
  - Exterior walls and floors in non-freeze/thaw climates

### How does Cotto Allende hold up over time?

Cotto Allende performs well when installed correctly and maintained through a consistent sealing schedule. The fired clay body provides structural stability under regular use. Seal with a penetrating sealer in moisture-exposed areas after grouting and reseal regularly in high-traffic and wet areas. Use pH-neutral cleaners to avoid surface damage. Subtle crazing is an inherent characteristic of the material and does not affect structural integrity. Over time, the tile develops a natural patina that reflects use.

### What is the difference between Cotto and Cotto Allende?

Cotto and Cotto Allende begin with the same clay foundation, hand-formed and high-fired near San Miguel de Allende. The distinction is the glaze. Cotto is left unglazed, producing a matte, porous surface that develops a natural patina with use. Unglazed Cotto is suitable for exterior floors in any climate, including freeze/thaw environments, but is not suitable for pools or spas.

Cotto Allende is finished with a pigmented glaze before firing, producing a smoother surface with subtle crazing and a broader range of color. Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates.
