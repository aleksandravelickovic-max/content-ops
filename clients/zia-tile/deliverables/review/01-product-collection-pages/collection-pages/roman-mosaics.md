# Roman Mosaics

## The Timelessness of Tesserae

From Hellenistic Greece to the Roman Empire, marble mosaics have defined the art of detail for thousands of years. Zia's Roman mosaics are sourced from marble quarries in Italy and Spain and finished in Italy, where each block is hand-clipped by artisans into 5/8-inch tesserae squares using traditional tooling methods that preserve the natural integrity of the stone.

Each tessera carries the irregularity of the hand-clipping process, producing edges that are slightly organic rather than mechanically uniform. Set on mesh in solid, fan, and checkerboard configurations, these small-format mosaics bring refined, graphic character to interior walls and floors, showers, and pools and spas.

### How are Zia's Roman mosaic tiles made?

Zia's Roman mosaic tiles begin as quarried marble blocks, cut and then hand-clipped by Italian artisans into 5/8-inch tesserae squares. Each piece is shaped individually rather than machine-cut, resulting in edges that remain slightly irregular and true to the hand-finishing process. After clipping, the tesserae are arranged into mesh-backed sheets in solid, fan, and checkerboard configurations, allowing for precise alignment during installation while maintaining the visual variation created by natural stone and handwork.

### Do Roman mosaic tiles need special care?

Yes, Roman mosaic tiles require sealing and ongoing maintenance consistent with natural marble surfaces. The material is porous and must be sealed before and after grouting to protect against moisture and staining. In wet areas — including shower walls, shower floors, and pool and spa installations — sealing is required as part of the installation process and must be maintained over time. Use only pH-neutral products for regular cleaning to preserve the stone surface and prevent etching.

### How to clean Roman mosaic tile

Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove debris that may scratch the surface. Wipe with clean water or a soft cloth and address spills immediately to prevent staining. For deeper cleaning, use a pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid acidic or abrasive products, as these will damage the marble surface.

### How do I install Roman mosaic tile?

Roman mosaic tile requires installers familiar with mesh-backed marble systems and the material behavior of natural stone during placement. Zia recommends professional installation.

Before installation:

  - Inspect all material upon receipt before installation begins
  - Blend sheets from all boxes to distribute variation evenly
  - Plan for 15 to 20% overage; for a more uniform look, increase to 20 to 25%

During installation:

  - Use a high-quality medium-bed thinset mortar for floor applications
  - Wet the back of each sheet before setting
  - Apply thinset in a consistent direction to avoid air pockets
  - Press sheets into place using only the palm of the hand
  - Install in sections no larger than 2x2 and clean excess thinset immediately

After placement:

  - Allow the thinset to cure for at least 48 hours before sealing
  - Apply a penetrating sealer before grouting and allow full curing
  - Grout in small sections and wipe excess immediately to prevent staining
  - Apply a final topcoat sealer after the grout has cured

All cuts must be made with a water-cooled tile saw. For exposed edges, miter at 45 degrees or finish with Schluter strips. Bring drywall flush with the finished stone surface where applicable. Installers must wear proper protective equipment in accordance with California Prop 65 guidelines. For detailed instructions, refer to the [Roman Mosaics Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/roman-mosaics-installation-guide). ### How does Roman mosaic tile hold up over time?

Roman mosaic tiles are made from dense natural marble and perform well over time when installed and maintained correctly. Longevity depends on proper substrate preparation, professional installation, sealing before and after grouting, and ongoing maintenance with pH-neutral cleaning products. When these conditions are met, Roman mosaic tiles maintain structural integrity and surface clarity.

Start with the total square footage: measure each surface in feet, multiply length by width, and add all sections together. From that total, add a minimum of 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts, natural variation, and any sheets set aside during installation. For a more uniform look, increase overage to 20 to 25%. ### What is the difference between Roman mosaic tile and marble tile?

Roman mosaic tile and marble tile are both sourced from natural marble but differ in format, production method, installation approach, and application range. Marble tile is cut from solid stone blocks into larger formats — such as 4x4, 12x12, or 24x24 — with each tile displaying the full veining and tonal range of the block. Roman mosaic tile is hand-clipped into 5/8-inch tesserae and assembled on mesh-backed sheets, distributing veining and tonal variation across many small pieces rather than a single large surface. Roman mosaics are also approved for pools and spas, while marble tile is not — making Roman mosaics the appropriate choice for pool tile applications within the Zia collection.
