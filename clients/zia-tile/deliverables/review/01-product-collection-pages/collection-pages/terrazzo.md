# Terrazzo

## Aggregates, Composed

Terrazzo has shaped architectural surfaces for over 500 years, with origins in Venetian interiors, where crushed marble and stone aggregates were set into cement to create durable floors with natural variation. Zia's collection continues that tradition through a modern process: crushed marble, quartz, and stone aggregates are hydraulically pressed into a cement base, then ground to reveal a dense, layered surface with visible aggregate distribution across each tile.

Available in 12x12 and 24x24 formats across speckled solid tones and patterned configurations, this terrazzo suits classic, mid-century modern, and contemporary minimalist interiors. Aggregate scale and distribution shift from tile to tile, producing subtle variation in tone and density that defines the material's character. Terrazzo is not suitable for exterior installation in freeze/thaw climates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where do terrazzo tiles come from?

Terrazzo originated in Venice, where stone fragments were embedded into cement surfaces to create durable flooring. The material evolved through Renaissance interiors and later became a defining surface in Art Deco and mid-century modern architecture. Zia's terrazzo collection continues this tradition through a modern manufacturing process that preserves the material's structural identity while refining its finish for contemporary use.

### How are Zia's terrazzo tiles made?

Zia's terrazzo tiles are produced through a controlled molding and pressing process. Crushed natural marble and stone aggregates are set into a steel mold, liquid cement is poured over the aggregate layer, and the tile is hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once cured, the surface is ground to expose the aggregate structure, then polished to a smooth finish with visible stone composition. This process produces variation at the aggregate level — no two tiles share identical distribution patterns.

### Does terrazzo tile need special care?

Terrazzo tiles are porous and must be sealed before and after grouting to protect the cement matrix against moisture and staining. Seal with a penetrating sealer before grouting and apply a matte protective wax as a topcoat for interior installations. For exterior installations in non-freeze/thaw climates, apply the penetrating sealer only; wax is not recommended for exterior use. Refer to the [Terrazzo Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/terrazzo-installation-guide) for product selection and full sequencing.

Terrazzo should be re-sealed at least every one to two years, or more frequently in high-traffic or moisture-exposed areas. Use only pH-neutral cleaners and avoid acidic or abrasive products.

### How to clean terrazzo tile

Regular sweeping or vacuuming prevents surface abrasion from debris. Clean spills immediately to prevent absorption into the cement matrix. For routine cleaning, use warm water with a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For heavier buildup, use Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute). Do not use bleach, vinegar, or acidic cleaners, as these dull the surface and weaken the sealer over time.

### How do I install terrazzo tile?

Terrazzo tile requires professional installation due to the material's weight, rigidity, and sealing requirements.

Before installation:

  - Blend tiles from all boxes to evenly distribute aggregate variation

During installation:

  - Set tiles using a high-quality medium-bed thinset over a properly cured substrate
  - Wet the back of each tile before setting to support adhesion
  - Press tiles into place by hand only — do not use mechanical impact tools
  - Allow full curing before sealing and grouting

Sealing and grouting:

  - Apply a penetrating sealer before grouting
  - Grout in controlled 2x2 sections and wipe clean as work progresses
  - For interior installations, apply a matte protective wax as a final topcoat
  - For exterior installations in non-freeze/thaw climates, apply an additional coat of penetrating sealer instead of wax
  - Allow all products to fully cure before moving to the next installation step

For full sequencing, refer to the [Terrazzo Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/terrazzo-installation-guide). For project-specific installation questions, the Zia team is available at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### How does terrazzo tile hold up over time?

Terrazzo is a long-lasting architectural flooring material when correctly installed and maintained. Stone aggregate bound in cement creates structural stability under regular residential and commercial use. With proper installation and a consistent sealing schedule, terrazzo maintains its surface integrity over decades. ### How do I choose between 12x12 and 24x24 terrazzo?

The choice depends on the scale of the surface and the desired visual effect. The 12x12 format suits compact spaces, feature walls, and surfaces where patterned compositions such as Highball, Collins, and Bossa Nova are the focal point. The 24x24 format suits open floors and expansive walls where fewer grout lines produce a cleaner, less interrupted surface.

### Is terrazzo suitable for a kitchen floor?

Terrazzo performs well on a kitchen floor when properly installed and sealed. Stone aggregates bound in cement create a hard-wearing surface that holds up consistently in high-traffic environments. Sealing before and after grouting is required to protect the cement matrix against moisture and staining, particularly in a kitchen environment. Re-seal every one to two years, or more frequently depending on traffic and wear.

### How much terrazzo tile is needed for a project?

Start with the total square footage of the surface. Measure the length and width of each surface in feet and multiply them together; for walls or multi-surface floors, add all sections. Plan for a minimum of 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and any tiles set aside during installation. ### What is the difference between terrazzo tile and marble tile?

Terrazzo tile and marble tile are both stone-based surfaces, but differ in composition and appearance. Terrazzo is produced by embedding crushed marble and stone aggregates into a cement matrix, hydraulically pressing the tile, and grinding the surface to reveal the aggregate distribution. Marble is a naturally occurring stone, quarried and cut directly from solid blocks, with variation determined entirely by the stone's geological composition and natural veining.
