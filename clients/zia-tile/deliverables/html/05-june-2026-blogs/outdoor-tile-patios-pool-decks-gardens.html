Outdoor Tile for Patios, Pool Decks and Gardens

Outdoor patio tile sets the character of a garden before the first plant arrives. The choice begins with climate, but it does not end there: Cotto brings sun-dried Mexican clay to patios in any region, glazed Cotto Allende and zellige carry color into pools and mild-climate courtyards, and cement tile, Cantera stone, and limestone widen the palette wherever winters stay above freezing.

Zia’s outdoor collection gathers those materials by climate and application, from a courtyard in Los Angeles to a farmhouse patio in Vermont.

Outdoor Tile Requirements

Three things decide whether a tile belongs outdoors: the clay body, the firing, and the surface. A dense, high-fired body takes on less water than a soft one, and the surface, glazed or unglazed, shapes how the tile behaves once temperatures drop.

Freezing climates add a second test. Fired clay absorbs some moisture, and water expands as it freezes, so year-round exterior use asks for a material matched to that cycle. Our unglazed Cotto meets it in any climate. Our glazed tiles, zellige and Cotto Allende, belong outdoors only where frost never settles.

Mild climates open the field considerably. Where winters stay above freezing, cement tile, Cantera stone, and limestone join the terra cotta lines outdoors, each with a distinct surface and range of color.

Outdoor Patio Tile at Zia

Our outdoor range spans clay and stone. Unglazed Cotto suits any climate, while Cotto Allende, zellige, cement tile, Cantera stone, and limestone suit non-freeze/thaw exteriors. Each material brings a different surface logic to the same garden: clay that deepens with wear, glaze that catches water and sun, pressed pattern, volcanic aggregate, or quarried stone.

Cotto

Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from near San Miguel de Allende, hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried in open courtyards, and high-fired. It is unglazed, and it suits exterior use in any climate, including freeze/thaw regions, where the clay surface can carry a patio through weather and wear. The one boundary is water: Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas.

The collection features six colorways, a classic range of Adobe, Fired Earth, and Red Clay alongside the unexpected tones of Blanco, Madera, and Oscura. Use and time deepen the tone, so each installation begins one way and settles into another.

Cotto Allende

Cotto Allende is our glazed terra cotta tile collection, hand-hewn from the same San Miguel de Allende clay, high-fired, then hand-glazed. It features eighteen colorways whose names reference Mexican geography and flora, in four shapes: a 4x4 square, a 4x8 rectangle, a triangle, and a 1.5x8 mini bar. Finishes range from matte to glossy, with speckling that varies by colorway.

Glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools. Outdoors, it belongs on patios and pool decks where winters stay mild, and it is not suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates.

Zellige

Zellige is handcrafted Moroccan clay tile with more than 800 years of tradition behind it, hand-shaped and hand-dipped in glaze. Chips, pits, and crazing are characteristics of each tile, and the dynamic glazes shift in color and sheen as light moves across the surface.

Glazed zellige is rated for pools and spas, an application it has served in Moroccan fountains and courtyard pools for centuries. In mild climates, colorways such as Pure White and Tidepool from the outdoor collection bring those shifting glazes into the garden. Freeze/thaw exterior installations call for a different material.

Cement Tile

Our cement tile is made in Vietnam by a nineteenth-century European method: mineral pigments and cement pressed hydraulically, then cured without kiln firing. The surface is matte across 68 colors, including the Cairo White Black pattern featured in the outdoor collection.

Cement tile belongs outdoors only in non-freeze/thaw climates. The pigment sits pressed into the surface, so the pattern keeps its matte face underfoot. That matte face holds its tone in direct sun with no glare, and beside rough garden stone the pressed pattern reads as a quiet graphic counterpoint.

Cantera Stone

Cantera is volcanic stone quarried in Mexico, with visible aggregate and mineral inclusions that mark each piece as stone. It suits interiors as well as exteriors in non-freeze/thaw climates, in earthy tones that sit naturally among planting.

Our Sierra format appears in the outdoor collection, broad enough to cover a garden terrace at a calm, even scale. In low afternoon sun, the aggregate catches shadow across each face, so the terrace gains a fine mineral texture as the light drops. Cantera is not suitable for pools or spas.

Limestone

Our limestone is quarried in solid blocks from Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France, then finished honed for a smooth face or bush-hammered for a textured one. It develops a natural patina over time, a record of the years it spends underfoot.

Limestone belongs outside in non-freeze/thaw climates, away from pools and spas. Along a mild-climate path or terrace, it strikes a quieter register than glazed tile. The finish sets the character underfoot, honed stone running smooth along the path while a bush-hammered face breaks the light into a fine grain of shadow.

Patios

For open patios, our Cotto sets a generous scale in 13x13 squares or Big Alcazar, a 13x13 arrowhead whose tiles lock into a geometric star that reads from across the garden. The 8x9 hexagon sits at a middle scale, and the outdoor collection features it in Adobe. At thirteen inches to the tile, the 13x13 formats hold their scale from a distance, and the joints mark a slow, even rhythm down the length of the patio.

Where frost never arrives, the choices widen. Cement tile carries pressed pattern across the patio, limestone puts quarried stone underfoot, and Cotto Allende adds glazed color from the same range that serves pools.

Pool Decks and Pools

Pool tile divides into two surfaces: the submerged interior and the deck that surrounds it. For the pool itself, glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools. Glazed zellige is rated for pools and spas as well, and Moroccan fountains have carried its glazes for centuries.

The deck is an exterior floor, so the climate rules apply. In mild climates, the pairing can run glazed tile in the water and Cotto or Cotto Allende across the deck. In freeze/thaw climates, the deck calls for unglazed Cotto, which suits exterior floors in any climate but not full submersion.

Wet floors carry a specification of their own. Our Cotto has a DCOF of 0.98, well above the 0.42 threshold for level interior floors. For wet commercial floors, grout joints should measure at least 1/8 inch, with an anti-slip treatment applied after installation, so the surface has a defined traction plan instead of relying on material texture alone.

Garden Paths and Courtyards

Garden tile rewards pattern at a small scale. Our Stars & Cross, a 6x6 eight-pointed star paired with a cross, follows a Moorish pattern found in courtyards from Granada to Puebla, and the 6x6 Alcazar interlocks curved and straight edges at the same compact size. Both are unglazed Cotto, so a Spanish courtyard set in either shape can sit in any climate.

For broader garden terraces in mild climates, Cantera earns its place. Our Sierra format covers ground evenly, and the visible aggregate reads as stone among greenery. Limestone, honed or bush-hammered, suits the same settings along paths and low garden steps.

Whatever the material, sealing applies in all installations, including dry spaces, and the requirements differ by material. Our tile installation guides cover preparation, setting, and sealer selection material by material.

How Climate Narrows the Field

A project in frost country resolves quickly: among these materials, unglazed Cotto is the one suited to exterior use in any climate, in six colorways across shapes from 4x4 squares to the 13x13 Big Alcazar. That range covers patios, paths, courtyards, and pool decks, letting the outdoor room stay in clay even where the pool interior needs glazed tile in a milder climate.

A mild-climate project chooses among all of these lines, so the decision shifts from climate to surface. Glazed color comes from Cotto Allende or zellige, graphic pattern from cement tile, and quarried stone from Cantera or limestone. Wherever water fully submerges the tile, the field narrows again to the glazed lines, Cotto Allende and zellige.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tile can be installed outdoors in a freeze/thaw climate?

Our unglazed Cotto is the freeze/thaw exterior option in this group. Glazed zellige and glazed Cotto Allende stay out of freeze/thaw exterior installations. Cement tile, Cantera stone, and limestone likewise belong in non-freeze/thaw exteriors.

Which tile suits a pool?

Glazed Cotto Allende tile is rated for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates. Glazed zellige carries the same pool and spa rating and the same freeze/thaw limit, bringing its shifting surface to water only where the climate allows it. Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas, so unglazed Cotto belongs on the deck rather than inside the pool.

Do outdoor tiles need to be sealed?

Sealing applies in all installations, including dry spaces. The requirements differ by material, and Cotto and Cotto Allende follow separate instructions, so our installation guides cover sealer selection material by material.
