# Limestone Tile: Honed vs. Brushed Finishes and Care

Limestone tile is quarried stone, cut from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France rather than pressed or fired. Because the tile is sliced from the block whole, each piece keeps the mineral character of its bed: soft shifts in tone, natural stone variation, and a surface that develops a natural patina over time. The finish decides how that character reads in a room. Honed limestone is ground smooth and matte. Bush-hammered limestone, the textured finish shoppers often search as brushed limestone, carries a fine, stippled grain that catches light and adds grip underfoot.

Our limestone collection offers both finishes across formats from 4x12 and 6x6 tiles up to 24x24 squares and long-plank runs, in tones including Buff, Monument, and Basilica. The pages that follow the finish decision, where the stone belongs in a home, and how it stays in good condition make up the rest of this guide.

## What Is Limestone Tile?

Limestone tile is natural stone cut from sedimentary rock composed largely of calcium carbonate, the same chemistry that makes it responsive to acids and gives it a soft, chalky depth of color. Unlike ceramic or cement tile, nothing is molded or fired. The [limestone collection at Zia](https://ziatile.com/collections/limestone-tile) is quarried near Fez, Morocco and Bordeaux, France, two regions with long histories of building in pale stone.

Formats run from 4x12 and 6x6 through 12x12, 12x24, and 24x24, with plank formats in varying lengths at 12 and 20 inches wide. Tones range from the light, sanded warmth of Buff to the deeper grey of Monument and the soft mid-range of Basilica. Each tile differs from its neighbor in tone and surface, and that natural stone variation is inherent to quarried limestone, not a flaw to correct.

## Honed vs. Brushed Limestone Tile

The difference between honed and brushed limestone is surface texture, and it changes how the same stone looks, feels, and performs. Honing grinds the surface flat and matte. Brushing and bush-hammering leave a worked texture that reads older, adds traction, and carries the marks of daily traffic without showing them.

### Honed limestone

Honed limestone is ground with abrasives until the surface sits flat, smooth, and matte, with no reflective sheen. The finish suits contemporary interiors because it lets the tone of the stone lead: light moves evenly across a honed floor, and grout joints recede into the field. It is the finish to reach for on kitchen floors, bathroom walls, and open living areas where the stone should read calm and continuous.

A honed surface also shows the stone's chemistry most plainly. Etching from acidic spills sits visibly on a smooth matte field, so honed installations reward prompt cleanup and pH-neutral care. Sealed and maintained, a honed limestone floor keeps its even, quiet surface for decades.

### Brushed limestone and our bush-hammered finish

Brushed limestone, as most suppliers use the term, is stone textured with wire brushes until the softer material lifts away and a gentle, undulating surface remains. Our collection takes the textured path further: the finish is bush-hammered, struck with a multi-pointed hammer that leaves a fine, stippled grain across the face of each tile. A search for brushed limestone and our Bush Hammered listings answer the same design intent, a matte stone surface with visible texture and real grip.

The texture earns its place in entries, hallways, and any floor that sees water or grit. It also softens the stone visually, giving a new installation the settled look of a floor that has been in place for generations. Where honed limestone reads crisp, bush-hammered limestone reads weathered in the best sense.

### How to choose between the two finishes

The room and the surface decide the finish. Honed limestone belongs where smoothness matters: kitchen and bath walls, low-traffic floors, and rooms where the design calls for an even, matte plane. Bush-hammered limestone belongs where texture matters: entries, mudrooms, exterior floors in mild climates, and wet floors where traction counts.

Many projects use both. A honed field on the walls with a bush-hammered floor keeps the palette identical while giving each surface the finish its job requires. Ordering a few samples of each finish in the same tone makes the comparison concrete before a full order ships.

## Where Limestone Flooring Works

Limestone flooring performs across most interior rooms and in exterior settings without hard freezes. The formats matter as much as the finish, so it helps to match the size of the tile to the scale of the space.

### Kitchens and living areas

Large formats suit open rooms. Our 12x24 and 24x24 tiles stretch across a kitchen or great room with few grout joints, and the varying-length planks in 12 and 20 inch widths set in offset rows give a room the rhythm of a traditional stone floor. In kitchens, sealing keeps spills sitting at the surface long enough to wipe away, and pH-neutral cleaner handles the daily pass.

### Bathrooms and showers

Limestone suits shower walls and bathroom floors when it is sealed according to our installation instructions. Always seal limestone, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as showers. For wet floors, grout joints should measure at least 1/8", with an anti-slip treatment applied after installation. Smaller formats such as 6x6 work well on shower floors, where tighter grout joints add footing.

### Exteriors in mild climates

Our limestone suits exterior floors, patios, and courtyards in non-freeze/thaw climates, where the bush-hammered finish gives an outdoor surface honest texture. It is not suitable for exterior installation in freeze/thaw regions, and it is not suitable for pools or spas. In the right climate, an exterior limestone floor develops its patina in the open air, deepening with wear and time.

## How to Care for Limestone Tile

Care for limestone tile rests on two habits: sealing on schedule and cleaning with the right chemistry. Limestone is porous, so each installation is sealed before and after grouting. Our [limestone installation guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/installation-guides) covers the full two-step pre-grout sealing and top-coat protocol, along with the correct materials for each setting.

Cleaning is a matter of what to avoid as much as what to do. Use a pH-neutral stone cleaner and a soft mop or cloth. Acidic or abrasive cleaners, including vinegar and citrus-based formulas, etch the calcium carbonate surface and dull the finish. Sweeping or vacuuming with a soft attachment before mopping keeps grit from scratching the stone.

Resealing depends on traffic and exposure rather than a fixed calendar. When water stops beading on the surface and starts soaking in quickly, the floor is ready for a fresh coat. Between sealings, the stone quietly does what limestone does: the tone deepens with wear and time, and that patina is the point.

## Limestone Tile FAQ

### What is the difference between honed and brushed limestone?

Honed limestone is ground flat, smooth, and matte, while brushed limestone is textured with wire brushes for a soft, worked surface. Our textured finish is bush-hammered rather than brushed: the face of each tile is struck with a multi-pointed hammer, producing a fine, stippled grain with similar traction and character. Both answers to the finish question start from the same quarried stone.

### Can limestone tile be used in a shower?

Yes, limestone works on shower walls and shower floors when sealed according to our installation instructions. Sealing before and after grouting keeps the porous stone protected in wet conditions, and pH-neutral cleaner preserves the surface. On shower floors, smaller formats with grout joints of at least 1/8" and an anti-slip treatment after installation give the finish reliable footing.

### How often does limestone tile need to be sealed?

Limestone is sealed before and after grouting at installation, then resealed as traffic and moisture wear the coat down over the years. The water test gives the honest answer: when droplets soak in instead of beading, the floor needs resealing. High-use areas such as kitchens and showers reach that point sooner than quiet rooms.

### How should limestone tile be cleaned?

Limestone should be cleaned with a pH-neutral stone cleaner, a soft mop, and prompt attention to spills. Vinegar, ammonia, citrus cleaners, and abrasive pads all damage the surface, either by etching the calcium carbonate or by scratching the finish. A soft-bristle sweep before mopping removes the grit that causes most wear.

### Can limestone tile be installed outdoors or around a pool?

Our limestone suits exterior installation in non-freeze/thaw climates only, and it is not suitable for pools or spas. In mild climates, bush-hammered limestone makes a textured, grounded surface for patios and courtyards. Homes in freezing regions should keep limestone indoors, where the stone performs in any room of the house.
