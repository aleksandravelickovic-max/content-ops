## About

Each Red Clay 8x8 Cotto tile is hand-pressed in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, from natural Mexican clay drawn from local beds. The artisan tradition there reaches back four centuries, where European clay-firing methods met older regional pottery traditions. The same hand-pressing, sun-drying, and high-firing techniques shape every tile Zia ships from the region today.

Red Clay sits in the classic Cotto range alongside Adobe and Fired Earth, the warm palette that has defined terra cotta for centuries. The deep, traditional red of this colorway reads closest to the raw material itself, the most direct expression of unglazed terra cotta in the line. It pairs naturally with classic hues for residential floors, walls, and outdoor surfaces, and anchors commercial installations in restaurants, hotels, and retail interiors where the lived-in character of aging clay is part of the design.

## Inherent Variation

Three characteristics define Red Clay 8x8 and should be expected across every order:

- **Tone:** depth of red shifts subtly from piece to piece, reflecting the iron content in the clay and conditions inside the kiln. The variation builds the depth that gives an installed Cotto floor its character.
- **Shape and edge:** each tile is hand-pressed, so dimensions and edge profiles vary slightly. Edges may be softer or more defined depending on how the clay was handled before firing.
- **Thickness:** small variations in thickness are normal in unglazed handmade tile. These do not affect installation when tiles are dry-laid and the substrate is properly prepared.

Variation should be embraced, not corrected. The same qualities that distinguish handmade Cotto from machine-pressed ceramics are what give the finished installation its depth.

## Preparation and Curation

Before installation, open boxes from across the full order and mix tiles together. Cotto varies in tone and dimension from piece to piece, and laying out the installation dry, across the surface and in the final orientation, is what distributes that variation evenly. A considered finished floor depends on this step.

Order a minimum of 15 to 20% overage to account for curation and cuts. For a more uniform look or for installations that incorporate specialty formats like Durango or Tornillo alongside the 8x8 square, plan for 20 to 25% across the full order, as perimeter cuts and pattern balancing typically require additional material.

## Installation and Finishing

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings).

For exposed edges, bring drywall flush with the finished tile surface, or finish with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Always seal Red Clay Cotto 8x8 according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. The pre-grout sealer doubles as a grout release, and grouting before it has cured will pull stains into the clay permanently. Interior and exterior installations use different finish coats; full instructions are available in the [Cotto Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cotto-installation-guide).

## How It's Made

Our Red Clay 8x8 begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende. The clay is mixed with water to the right consistency, hand-pressed into a square mold, and sun-dried in open courtyards for one to two weeks. Once cured to the proper moisture level, tiles are high-fired in traditional kilns.

The unglazed surface develops natural patina with use and time, a defining feature of Cotto and part of what gives a finished installation its depth. Tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness vary subtly from piece to piece, inherent characteristics of handmade unglazed clay.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What kind of variation should I expect across the Red Clay 8×8 tiles?

Red Clay 8×8 Cotto carries the natural variation that defines all handmade Cotto tile. Subtle differences in tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness from tile to tile are all part of its character. Before installation, open and mix tiles from all boxes, and dry-lay the installation to ensure variation is distributed evenly.

### What's the difference between Cotto and Cotto Allende, and where does each one belong?

Cotto and Cotto Allende are both made from the same Mexican clay body, but the finish defines their appearance and application. While Cotto remains unglazed, [Cotto Allende](https://ziatile.com/collections/cotto-tile-allende) is finished with distinctive glazes. Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas. Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates.

### Are the Red Clay 8×8 tiles suitable for shower floors and wet areas?

Yes. The Red Clay Cotto 8×8 tiles are suitable for shower floors and wet areas, with a Dynamic Coefficient of Friction (DCOF) of 0.98, above the 0.42 wet-floor safety threshold. For commercial wet-floor applications, grout joints should measure at least 1/8", and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation. Always seal Red Clay Cotto 8x8 according to our installation instructions for any wet-area installation.

### What's the sealing protocol for Red Clay 8×8 tiles?

Always seal Red Clay Cotto 8x8 according to our installation instructions, whether in dry spaces or in areas exposed to moisture, such as pools, spas, and showers. The sealing protocol calls for two separate applications: a pre-grout coat that doubles as a grout release, then a post-grout finish coat appropriate to the installation (interior or exterior). Allow the pre-grout sealer to fully cure before grouting. Full instructions are available in the [Cotto Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/cotto-installation-guide), and the Zia team is available at info@ziatile.com or 310-844-1170.

### Can the Red Clay 8×8 be mixed with other Cotto colorways and formats?

Yes. Red Clay Cotto can be combined with other colorways and formats throughout the Cotto collection. Each Cotto tile is formed from the same San Miguel de Allende clay body and fired through the same kiln process, creating consistency across the line. Red Clay pairs naturally with classic hues such as Adobe and Fired Earth, and contrasts with the unexpected tones of Blanco, Madera, and Oscura. Some variation in size and shape across handmade tiles is normal and not affected by colorway. When incorporating specialty geometric formats, increase overage to 20 to 25%, as perimeter cuts typically require additional material.

For a standard layout, order 15 to 20% overage to account for curation and cuts. For projects that incorporate specialty formats, plan for 20 to 25% across the full order, as edge cuts can affect the number of pieces needed.
