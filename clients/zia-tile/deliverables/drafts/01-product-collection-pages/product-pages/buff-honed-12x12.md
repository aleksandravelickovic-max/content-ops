## How It's Made

Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.

The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.

Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the natural characteristics of limestone tile?

Limestone is a sedimentary stone, and its characteristics come directly from how it forms. Color, tone, surface texture, and markings vary from tile to tile because no two sections of stone share the same geological history. These differences are inherent to the material and should be expected across an order. With use and exposure over time, the surface develops a patina that is part of the stone's long-term behavior.

### Can honed limestone be used in a minimalist interior?

Yes. The smooth matte surface and consistent buff tone produce a neutral field that does not introduce pattern or color that would compete with a restrained composition. The natural variation in the stone adds material depth without demanding attention, and the 12x12 square format produces a gridded, structured layout that suits minimalist interiors well.

### How does limestone compare to marble?

Both limestone and marble are natural stones that require sealing and pH-neutral cleaning products, but they differ in formation and surface character. Limestone is sedimentary, with a consistent neutral palette and moderate variation. Marble is metamorphic, with more pronounced veining and a broader color range. Marble is more susceptible to etching from acidic cleaners. Both materials develop patina with use.

### What design styles suit honed limestone?

Honed limestone is a neutral material that does not direct a space toward a specific aesthetic. It works in interiors where natural materials carry the design, and equally in spaces where the architecture does the work and the surfaces are kept quiet. The buff tone and matte surface are compatible with Mediterranean, contemporary, and rustic design directions.

### How do I remove water spots from honed limestone?

To remove water spots from honed limestone, wipe the surface with a damp cloth and a pH-neutral cleaner such as Miracle Sealants pH Neutral Tile and Stone Cleaner. For more stubborn water spots, Miracle Sealants Heavy Duty Cleaner (Acid Substitute) is the recommended product. Avoid acidic cleaners, including vinegar and lemon juice, as these will etch and permanently damage the limestone surface. Keeping the stone properly sealed reduces the likelihood of water spots penetrating the surface.

### How do I cut limestone on site?

To cut limestone on site, all cuts must be made with a water-cooled tile saw with a diamond edge blade. This applies to both straight cuts and cuts around fixtures and perimeter adjustments. If extensive cutting is required, work outdoors or in a well-ventilated area in accordance with California Prop 65 safety guidelines. Installers should always wear a protective face covering and eye protection when cutting limestone.
