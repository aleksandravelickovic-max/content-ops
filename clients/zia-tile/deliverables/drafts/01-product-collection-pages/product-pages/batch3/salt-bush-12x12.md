## How It's Made

Terrazzo is produced using techniques refined by Venetian stone masons since the 15th century, when leftover marble offcuts were combined with cement to floor courtyards and palazzo halls. The same craft is preserved in the workshops where Zia's terrazzo is produced today.

Crushed marble and stone aggregates are arranged in steel molds and covered with liquid cement, then hydraulically pressed into a powdered concrete base. Once cured, the surface is ground and polished to the matte finish that defines the line.

Each tile is finished by hand, which produces the subtle tonal variation, occasional crazing, and slight edge irregularity that separate hand-pressed terrazzo from machine-set composite tile. The cement matrix and the visible aggregate are what define terrazzo as a material; the press and the finish are what carry that definition into the floor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the aggregate composition of Salt Bush — what colors and materials make up the stone and marble mix?

Salt Bush is built on a grey cement base with a mix of crushed stone and marble aggregate in muted tones — grey, off-white, and occasional pale mineral flecks — distributed through the matrix. The aggregate is neither densely packed nor sparse; it reads as scattered stone detail across the grey field. The tonal character of the base comes from the cement formulation and the mineral character of the aggregate, not from added pigment.

### How does the 12x12 format work in a Salt Bush terrazzo installation?

The 12x12 format produces a continuous grey field with fewer grout joints than smaller formats, which allows the aggregate distribution to resolve across a wider surface area. At this scale, the speckled composition becomes the primary visual character of the floor rather than individual tile units. Blending tiles from all boxes and arranging a dry layout before setting is essential — aggregate density varies tile to tile, and the 12x12 scale makes that variation more apparent in the finished installation. A 1/8" grout joint is recommended, with minor shimming anticipated to keep lines straight across the field.

### How does Salt Bush compare to Red Rocks in the terrazzo range?

Salt Bush and Red Rocks are the two contrasting reads within Zia's terrazzo line. Salt Bush is a cool grey base with muted stone aggregate — restrained in palette, reading as a quiet neutral. Red Rocks is a warm pink base with cream and earth-tone aggregate — warmer in register and more assertive in tone. Both are 12x12 cement terrazzo with the same DCOF rating and installation requirements. The choice depends on whether the project draws on a cool, mineral palette or a warm, earth-toned one.

### What is the DCOF rating for Salt Bush, and what does it mean for wet floor applications?

Salt Bush 12x12 terrazzo carries a DCOF of 0.51, which meets the wet area slip resistance threshold of 0.42 required for interior wet floors. This means Salt Bush is suited for shower floors and other wet applications. The 1/8" minimum grout joint is required for shower floor applications, and an anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation to increase traction further in high-exposure wet areas.

### Can Salt Bush 12x12 be used on a shower floor or wall?

Yes. Salt Bush 12x12 is suited for shower floors and shower walls. Seal the tile before and after grouting per the installation guide, and size grout joints to a minimum of ⅛". The DCOF of 0.51 meets the wet floor threshold. An anti-slip product can be applied after installation for additional traction in high-use wet floor applications.

### Does Salt Bush 12x12 terrazzo require sealing?

Yes. Cement terrazzo requires sealing before and after grouting. Apply the penetrating sealer in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. For interior installations, a matte wax top coat is applied after grouting. For exterior installations in non-freeze/thaw climates, apply a second coat of penetrating sealer in place of wax, as UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time. Refer to Zia's terrazzo installation guide for the full sequence.

### What does blending tiles from multiple boxes involve on site?

Before setting, each box in the order should be opened and the tiles intermixed across the full delivery. The aggregate distribution in terrazzo varies from tile to tile, and pulling pieces from a single box creates clusters of similar aggregate density on the installed floor. Dry-laying the full arrangement — or as much of it as the site allows — before setting gives the installer the chance to balance the speckled composition across the field and identify where individual tiles need repositioning.

### What causes crazing on terrazzo, and is it a structural concern?

Crazing — a fine network of surface-level cracks in the cement matrix — is a characteristic of hand-pressed cement terrazzo that results from the curing and pressing process. It does not affect the structural integrity of the tile or the bond to the substrate when properly installed. Crazing is visible at close range on some tiles and less apparent on others; it is part of what distinguishes hand-pressed terrazzo from machine-manufactured composite tile.

### Can a Salt Bush tile be replaced individually if one is cracked or chipped?

Yes. A cracked or chipped terrazzo tile can be replaced individually without disturbing the surrounding installation, provided overage was retained from the original order. Zia recommends retaining extra tiles from the original batch for this reason — aggregate composition and base tone can vary between production runs, and a tile from the same order will provide the closest match. ### Is Salt Bush terrazzo suitable for a freeze/thaw climate?

No. Cement terrazzo is not rated for outdoor installation in freeze/thaw climates. Water can penetrate the porous cement matrix and expand during freeze cycles, causing surface damage. In non-freeze/thaw climates, Salt Bush 12x12 is suited for exterior floors, courtyards, and covered outdoor spaces with appropriate sealing.
