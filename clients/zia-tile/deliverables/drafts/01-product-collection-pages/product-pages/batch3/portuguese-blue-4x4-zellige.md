## About

No two zellige tiles are the same. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige.

Seal zellige according to our installation instructions, whether the application is dry or exposed to moisture (pools, spas, showers). Reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made

Zellige is hand-formed in Morocco, where the technique has been refined for more than 800 years and passed across generations of artisans.

The process begins with raw clay, which is mined from local hillsides, soaked, filtered for impurities, and hand-pressed into molds. The tiles are laid to dry in the Moroccan sun, flattened and shaped by hand, then dried further before glazing.

Each tile is hand-dipped in glaze and arranged inside an earthen kiln, where uneven heat creates the tonal variation that defines zellige. No two pieces leave the kiln the same, and the handmade nature and variation are hallmarks of this material. To prepare for installation, open and mix all boxes before setting them to achieve an ideal layout. The tiles will carry the marks of the day they were fired.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does Portuguese Blue zellige look like in a room?

Zia's Portuguese Blue reads as a classic, resolved blue — a mid-range tone with the same quality found in traditional azulejo panels from Lisbon, where the blue glaze carries depth and history without veering into navy or sky. The glaze variation across the field means individual tiles carry tonal range from a slightly more saturated blue to a paler, hazier register.

### How does the azulejo tradition show up in the Portuguese Blue glaze character?

The azulejo tradition of the Iberian Peninsula is built on a particular quality of blue — softer and more resolved than the concentrated blues of Moroccan zellige, carrying the settled quality of a glaze applied over centuries rather than the immediacy of a bold contemporary color choice. Portuguese Blue reflects that quality in its mid-range saturation and the slightly soft, European register of the glaze. The reference is in the colorway's position in the blue spectrum, not a decorative pattern.

### How does Portuguese Blue compare to Moroccan Blue and Glacier Blue?

Portuguese Blue sits between the two. Moroccan Blue carries more saturation and depth — closer to the concentrated blues of traditional North African tilework — and reads with more intensity across a surface. Glacier Blue is the lightest and coolest of the three, a clear, airy blue at the pale end of the range. Portuguese Blue occupies the mid-range: more depth than Glacier Blue, a softer, more Iberian quality than Moroccan Blue. Ordering samples of multiple colorways is the most reliable way to see how each reads in the specific context.

### Is Portuguese Blue available in other formats besides 4x4?

Yes. Portuguese Blue is also available in 2x2, 2x6, and hexagon formats. Each format is produced using the same traditional methods, so surface character and material quality remain consistent across the range. The 2x2 is mesh-mounted on 8" × 8" sheets, suited to mosaic details, niches, and border work within a larger 4x4 field.

### Can Portuguese Blue 4x4 zellige be used in a shower?

Yes. Zia's Portuguese Blue 4x4 is suited for shower walls, shower floors, and steam showers. Sealing is required for all shower applications. On shower floors, grout joints should measure a minimum of ⅛" and an anti-slip treatment should be applied after installation is complete. For steam shower applications, the substrate must be fully waterproofed before tile is set. ### Is Portuguese Blue zellige approved for pools and spas?

Yes. Portuguese Blue zellige is approved for pool waterlines, spa interiors, and fountains in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required per the installation instructions. ### What grout works with Portuguese Blue zellige?

A white or cream grout creates a clean contrast with the blue field, drawing on the same visual pattern found in traditional azulejo tilework. A grout matched closely to the blue glaze — a slate or blue-grey — allows the surface to read as a continuous field of color, with the tile-to-tile variation carrying the interest. The choice depends on whether the goal is a two-tone surface with a clear grid or an immersive blue field.

### What grout joint width does Zia recommend for Portuguese Blue 4x4?

For wall applications, a joint as tight as 1/16" is achievable, though ⅛" is more practical given the inherent size variation of handmade zellige. For shower floors and other wet floor applications, ⅛" is the minimum required, with anti-slip treatment applied after installation. Wedge spacers help maintain consistent spacing. Contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170 with installation-specific questions.

### What thinset does Zia recommend for Portuguese Blue 4x4 zellige?

White polymer-modified thinset is the correct choice. On a mid-range blue glaze like Portuguese Blue, white thinset prevents visible bleed at the chamfered edges and through the grout joints. Full back-butter coverage is required to support the irregular backs of handmade pieces. Refer to the Zellige Installation Guide for the complete application sequence.

### How thick is Portuguese Blue 4x4 zellige?

Portuguese Blue 4x4 zellige is approximately ⅜" thick, consistent with the standard for Zia's handmade zellige collection. Inherent thickness variation from tile to tile is characteristic of the material — the thinset setting bed accommodates this during installation. Contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170 with questions about substrate depth.

### What does variation look like across a full Portuguese Blue installation?

Across a field, Portuguese Blue ranges from tiles with a slightly more saturated, cleaner blue to those with a paler, hazier register. The chips, pits, and hairline crazing characteristic of zellige are present at the surface, and the chamfered edges create shadow lines between tiles. Mixing tiles from all boxes during the dry layout ensures the tonal range disperses evenly rather than clustering by production sequence.
