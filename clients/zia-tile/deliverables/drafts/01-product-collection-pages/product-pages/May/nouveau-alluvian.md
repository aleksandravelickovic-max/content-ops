## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Nouveau Alluvian cement tile?

Nouveau Alluvian is a warm sandy grey cement tile — an earthy, mineral-toned neutral that draws from the alluvial landscape: the sandy grey of riverbeds, silted flats, and naturally deposited earth. The tone sits between warm grey and sandy beige without pulling strongly toward either, with a particulate, layered warmth that makes it feel more natural and grounded than a flat neutral grey. The tonal variation of hand-poured cement distributes the sandy grey across individual tiles as a richly complex, sediment-like field.

### Does Nouveau Alluvian cement tile require sealing?

Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax. Reseal every 1–2 years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure.

### Can Nouveau Alluvian cement tile be used in a shower?

Yes. Nouveau Alluvian cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing excellent slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before and after grouting, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". An anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation for additional traction on shower floor surfaces.

### Can Nouveau Alluvian cement tile be used on an exterior floor?

Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Nouveau Alluvian cement tile is suitable for exterior floors in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. It is not rated for freeze/thaw cycling. For exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat.

### Are there special installation considerations for the Nouveau Alluvian specialty shape?

Yes. The specialty format of Nouveau Alluvian requires a dry layout before setting to verify the geometry reads correctly and to assess the distribution of tonal variation across the field. Arrange the full installation footprint on a flat surface, blend tiles from multiple boxes, and check the layout against walls, focal points, and transitions before applying adhesive. The curved or sculpted tile edges create organic geometry in the grout joint pattern — planning the starting point carefully ensures the field reads as intended from the room's primary sightlines.

### What pairs well with Nouveau Alluvian cement tile?

Nouveau Alluvian's warm sandy grey works naturally alongside earthy, organic, and Mediterranean-inflected palettes. Plaster walls in warm white or greige extend the mineral, sedimentary character of the palette. Natural linen, jute, and raw cotton textiles complement the earthy, alluvial tone without competing. Warm wood in oak, olive, and walnut reinforce the natural warmth of the sandy grey. Aged brass and blackened iron fixtures both suit the palette — brass drawing out the warmth, iron aligning with the mineral, grounded character of the alluvian field.
