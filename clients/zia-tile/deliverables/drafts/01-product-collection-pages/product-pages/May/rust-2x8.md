## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is laid. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway, with the deeper pigments carrying broader internal contrast than the lighter cream and bone tones.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile. Cement tile carries a 10 to 12 week lead time on special order, so plan curation against the project schedule before placing the order.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color, which is part of the reason 19th-century cement tile floors are still in service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Rust 2x8 cement tile?

Rust is a warm orange-brown cement tile — a deep, saturated tone drawn from the iron oxide pigments that define the earthy, warm end of the natural color spectrum. The rust sits between deep ochre and reddish brown, varying subtly tile to tile in the manner of hand-poured cement. Across a full installation, the range within the rust palette distributes as a warm, complex surface rather than a flat solid color, with the deeper pigments producing the most tonal movement within the colorway.

### Does Rust 2x8 cement tile require sealing?

Yes. Cement tile must be sealed before and after grouting. Apply 511 Porous Plus in thin layers until the tiles are fully saturated before grouting, wiping off any excess before it dries. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure fully, apply Fila Matte Finish Protective Wax as a final top coat for interior installations. For outdoor installations, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax — UV exposure causes wax to discolor outdoors. Reseal every 1–2 years or as needed based on traffic and moisture exposure.

### Can Rust 2x8 cement tile be used in a shower?

Yes. Rust 2x8 cement tile can be used on shower floors and shower walls. Cement tile has a DCOF greater than 1, providing excellent slip resistance in wet conditions. Seal before and after grouting, and size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8". An anti-slip treatment can be applied after installation for additional traction on shower floor surfaces.

### Can Rust 2x8 be used on an exterior floor?

Yes, in non-freeze/thaw climates. Rust 2x8 cement tile is suitable for exterior floors in climates that do not experience freezing temperatures. It is not rated for freeze/thaw cycling. For exterior applications, seal with 511 Porous Plus before and after grouting, and use a second coat of 511 Porous Plus as the final top coat.

### What grout color works best with Rust 2x8 cement tile?

For a traditional look, a neutral sand or warm grey grout allows the rust color to lead without introducing strong contrast at the joints. Matching the grout closely to the rust tones creates a more continuous surface where the joints recede into the field. Avoid dark contrasting grouts on saturated cement colors — contrasting grout can leave haze that is difficult to remove from the matte cement surface. Using a grout bag in areas with contrasting grout minimizes direct contact between grout and the tile face.

### What pairs well with Rust 2x8 cement tile?

The warm orange-brown of Rust pairs naturally with earthy materials and warm neutrals. Cream and off-white walls provide a light backdrop that lets the rust lead. Natural wood tones — particularly warm oak and walnut — complement the orange-brown without competing. Sage, olive, and forest green textiles or accents create a natural complementary pairing in the earthy color family. Aged brass fixtures reinforce the warm metallic warmth of the rust palette.
