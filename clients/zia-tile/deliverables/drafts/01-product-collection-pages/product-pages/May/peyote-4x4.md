## About

Cotto Allende is glazed terra cotta in 18 colorways (Arroyo, Baja, Albar, Puebla, Dorado, Creosote, Sayulita, Azul, and Cacao among them), across squares, rectangles, hexagons, and the same geometric forms as our unglazed Cotto line. Lay out the tiles on a flat surface and confirm the color balance reads the way you want before setting any tile.

## How It's Made

Cotto Allende is handmade in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, using the same clay base as standard Cotto with a glaze applied before firing. The result is a sealed surface that opens up applications such as pools, spas, and steam showers, which aren't appropriate for unglazed terra cotta.

Natural clay is pressed into molds and sun-dried, then glazed in either gloss or satin finish before high-firing. The glaze develops fine hairline cracks during cooling, known as crazing, which is a normal characteristic of glazed clay tile and not a defect.

Color saturation is more controlled than unglazed Cotto, while the underlying clay body still produces handmade variation across the line. The finish determines how the tile reads in light, with each formulation producing a distinct surface character.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Peyote 4x4 Cotto Allende?

Peyote is a muted blue-green Cotto Allende with a satin glaze that shifts between soft sage, blue-grey, and deeper teal tones depending on the light and viewing angle. The colorway occupies the quiet end of the blue-green family — neither a bright turquoise nor a dark teal — and the natural variation in the glaze across individual tiles produces a surface with subtle tonal movement. The satin finish gives the color a soft luminosity without the full reflectivity of a glossy glaze.

### What is the difference between the satin and glossy finish in Cotto Allende?

The satin finish sits between the high sheen of a glossy glaze and the flatness of a matte surface. Compared to glossy Cotto Allende, a satin finish reflects less light and reads as softer and less reflective — the color is present but the surface does not produce the same degree of glare or specular highlight. In wet areas, the satin finish still performs fully and is appropriate for pools, spas, and showers. The choice between satin and glossy is primarily aesthetic and affects how the glaze reads in the light conditions of a specific space.

### Is Peyote 4x4 suitable for pools and spas?

Yes, Peyote 4x4 Cotto Allende is suitable for both saltwater and chlorine pool and spa installations. Sealing with 511 Porous Plus is required before and after grouting to protect both the tile surface and grout under continuous water exposure. Do not use Fila Matte Wax on Cotto Allende — 511 Porous Plus is the only recommended sealer for this line.

### Is Peyote 4x4 suitable for flooring?

Yes, Peyote 4x4 is suitable for flooring in both residential and commercial settings. A minimum 1/8" grout joint is required to support alignment and installation accuracy across the field. Cotto Allende has a DCOF of 0.48. In wet floor applications, slip resistance can be increased by maintaining this grout joint size and/or applying an anti-slip treatment after installation is complete.

### What pairs well with Peyote Cotto Allende?

The muted blue-green of Peyote pairs naturally with warm neutrals that allow the soft, complex glaze to lead. Warm white, cream, and off-white walls and cabinetry keep the surroundings open and light-filled while the tile provides the color. Natural wood and rattan complement the earthy quality of the terra cotta body beneath the glaze. Unlacquered brass fixtures introduce a warm metallic that sits naturally alongside the blue-green. Deep navy or forest green used in adjacent areas reinforces the cool tonal family of the glaze.

### Can Peyote 4x4 be mixed with other Cotto Allende colorways?

Yes. Peyote 4x4 can be combined with other colorways in the Cotto Allende collection. The muted blue-green of Peyote pairs well with lighter neutral colorways such as Cacao or Puebla as a field tile alongside Peyote accents, or with deeper saturated colorways like Condesa for stronger contrast.
