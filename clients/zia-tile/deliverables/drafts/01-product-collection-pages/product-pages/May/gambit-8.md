## About

No two zellige tiles are the same. The Gambit 8 mosaic uses Plum, Purple Iris, and Portuguese Blue — three colorways in the deep purple and blue-purple range, each carrying its own glaze character and tonal variation. Pieces vary in size, surface, and glaze across each box, with hairline crazing and chamfered edges that come from how the material is hand-formed rather than flaws to correct.

Before setting any sheet, separate and mix sheets from multiple boxes to distribute tonal variation evenly across the full layout. Lay out the composition on a flat surface before setting to confirm how the three glazes distribute and how the pattern reads. Individual tiles can be removed from the mesh backing if adjustments are needed at edges or perimeters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with zellige, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered cuts or Schluter strips. Traditional trim and edge pieces do not exist for zellige. Seal Gambit 8 according to our installation instructions, whether in dry areas or areas exposed to moisture. Reach out to [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) with any material or installation questions.

## How It's Made

Gambit 8 begins in the same place as all traditional zellige. The clay is pulled from the hillsides surrounding Fez, Morocco, soaked in water, and filtered for impurities before being worked by hand until it reaches the right consistency for pressing into molds. For Gambit 8, the molds shape the small geometric elements that make up the mosaic composition.

After drying in the Moroccan sun, each element is dipped individually in Plum, Purple Iris, or Portuguese Blue glaze. Because the glaze is applied by hand, coverage varies from piece to piece, and that variation is what produces the tonal depth visible within each colorway across the finished mosaic. Each element is then hand-assembled onto a mesh backing, forming the sheets that arrive on site ready for installation.

The final stage is the kiln. Earthen kilns do not distribute heat evenly, and the results of that unevenness are not incidental. The chips, pits, crazing, and shifting tones that emerge from the firing process are what give Gambit 8 its jewel-toned depth and ensure that no two installed panels read exactly alike.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What colors are in the Gambit 8 zellige mosaic?

Gambit 8 uses three glazes: Plum, Purple Iris, and Portuguese Blue. Plum is the deepest tone in the palette, reading as a rich, dark purple with the depth and variation of a jewel-toned glaze. Purple Iris sits in the mid-range of the purple family, carrying more brightness and slightly more red-leaning warmth than Plum. Portuguese Blue shifts the palette toward the blue-purple end of the range, introducing a cooler, more blue-toned counterpoint that keeps the composition from reading as fully purple. All three carry tonal variation tile to tile as a result of the hand-dipping process and uneven kiln heat.

### What pairs well with Gambit 8 zellige mosaic?

The jewel-toned purple and blue-purple palette of Gambit 8 pairs best with materials that allow the saturated glaze to lead. Crisp white walls and cabinetry provide the clearest contrast and amplify the depth of the three glazes. Warm natural stone or wood creates an earthy counterpoint that grounds the richness of the color. Matte black fixtures and hardware reinforce the deep tones without competing. In the surrounding field, a single solid zellige colorway — Portuguese Blue or a neutral white — used as the backdrop allows Gambit 8 to function as the focal feature of the installation.

### Can Gambit 8 be used in a shower?

Yes. Gambit 8 can be used on shower walls, shower floors, and steam showers. Sealing is required for all shower applications. For shower floors, slip resistance can be increased by sizing grout joints to a minimum of ⅛" and applying an anti-slip treatment after installation. The mosaic format contributes to surface traction through higher grout coverage per square foot. Refer to the [Zellige Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/zellige-installation-guide) for full sealing instructions.

### Can Gambit 8 be used in a pool?

Yes. Gambit 8 is suitable for pool and spa applications in both saltwater and chlorine environments. Sealing is required; apply sealer to all tiles and grout lines exposed to continuous moisture after grouting is complete. The deep purple and blue-purple glaze palette reads with particular intensity at pool waterlines, where the jewel tones interact with reflected water light. Consult the [Zellige Installation Guide](https://ziatile.com/pages/zellige-installation-guide) for specific sealing instructions.

### What grout color works best with Gambit 8 zellige mosaic?

Grout color shapes how the three-glaze palette reads across the mosaic. Dark purple or charcoal grout integrates with the deeper Plum elements and creates a continuous field where the geometric pattern reads through subtle contrast rather than sharp grout line definition. White or light grout introduces high contrast and makes individual tile edges and the geometric structure more prominent — appropriate when the pattern itself is the intended focal point. A neutral mid-grey grout creates a middle-ground approach where the mosaic reads as balanced between the light and dark glazes.

### How do I install Gambit 8 zellige mosaic sheets?

Gambit 8 arrives on site as mesh-mounted sheets. Before setting any sheet, separate and mix from multiple boxes to distribute the Plum, Purple Iris, and Portuguese Blue elements evenly across the full layout. Lay the full composition out on a flat surface to confirm how the three glazes distribute and how the pattern resolves before setting any tile. Individual tiles can be removed from the mesh backing for edge and perimeter adjustments. Use wedge spacers to maintain consistent spacing between sheets and plan for two to three times the installation time of a standard ceramic project.
