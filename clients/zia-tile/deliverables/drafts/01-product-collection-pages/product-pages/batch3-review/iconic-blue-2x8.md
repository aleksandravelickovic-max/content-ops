## About

Zia's ceramics are the design counterpoint to handmade zellige. They were developed with a Japanese potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-firing extends back more than 1,000 years, to produce a tile precise enough for tight grout joints and clean layouts.

The collection spans matte, satin, and glossy finishes plus the textured Field Trip: Japan series, in three core formats: 2×8 and 2×4 rectangles and 4×4 squares. Color and tone vary slightly across high-fired ceramics — less than handmade zellige, but enough that mixing boxes before the set still matters.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with ceramic, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://ziatile.com/pages/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

Sealing requirements vary by finish: matte ceramics must be sealed before grouting as a grout release; satin and glossy finishes do not require pre-grout sealing. For any material or installation questions, reach out to our team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com).

## How It's Made

Zia's ceramics are produced in a potters' village outside Nagoya, where high-temperature ceramic firing has been practiced for more than 1,000 years. The collection was developed through a direct collaboration with these workshops to bring architectural-grade precision to Zia's tile range.

Each tile is molded and glazed, then fired at high temperature in a single pass. The high-firing process produces a dense, durable body with very minor variation in tone and dimension — consistent enough for tight grout joint layouts while still retaining the character of individually fired pieces.

Matte finishes are applied as a flat glaze that diffuses light evenly and requires a penetrating sealer before grouting. Satin and glossy finishes carry more reflectivity and do not require pre-grout sealing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does the 2x8 format affect the way Iconic Blue reads on a backsplash versus a shower wall?

On a kitchen backsplash set in a horizontal stack, the 2×8 format creates a strong directional coursing across the wall — the length of each tile and the grout lines work together to draw the eye horizontally, and the deep blue reads as an anchored field behind the counter. On a shower wall, the same horizontal stack produces a similar effect across a larger continuous surface; a vertical stack or herringbone layout in the shower shifts the register and adds movement to the deep blue field. The format choice — orientation and pattern — changes the composition more significantly with a deep, saturated color like Iconic Blue than it would with a pale neutral.

### How does Iconic Blue compare to the same colorway in a 4x4 format?

The Iconic Blue colorway is available in 2×4 and 4×4 formats in addition to the 2×8. The 4×4 square produces a balanced grid where horizontal and vertical grout lines carry equal visual weight — the deep blue field reads as a more settled, even surface without the directional pull of the 2×8 subway. The 2×8 is inherently directional; the 4×4 is not. The finish and color are the same across formats; the format choice determines how the blue registers in the space.

### Does Iconic Blue 2x8 require sealing before installation?

Yes. The Iconic Blue 2x8 is a matte finish and requires a penetrating sealer applied before grouting as a grout release. This prevents grout haze from bonding permanently to the matte surface during installation. Apply the sealer in a thin, even layer after laying the tile and allow it to cure fully before grouting. For questions about sealer selection, contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### Can Iconic Blue 2x8 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Zia's Iconic Blue 2x8 is suited for shower walls and shower floors. Seal the tile before grouting as required for all matte ceramics. Size grout joints to a minimum of 1/8" to support traction on shower floor surfaces, and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation in high-exposure wet areas.

### Can Iconic Blue 2x8 be installed in a pool or spa?

Yes. Zia's matte-finish ceramics are approved for pool and spa applications, including pool waterline tile and spa interiors. The high-fired ceramic body is compatible with saltwater and chlorine environments.

### What is the DCOF rating for Iconic Blue 2x8, and what does the matte finish mean for floor use?

Matte ceramics carry a DCOF of 0.74, which exceeds the 0.42 minimum threshold for level interior floor applications. Of the three ceramic finish types Zia carries, matte has the highest slip resistance rating — making it the preferred choice when the tile will be used on a wet floor like a shower floor. Plan for a minimum 1/8" grout joint on shower floors and apply an anti-slip treatment after installation in high-exposure wet areas.

### What layout orientations work well with Iconic Blue 2x8?

The 2×8 rectangle supports four main layout configurations: horizontal stack, vertical stack, offset at 50%, and herringbone. A horizontal stack emphasizes width; a vertical stack draws the eye upward; herringbone introduces movement across the field. With a deep color like Iconic Blue, herringbone on a full wall reads as a graphic pattern. A dry run before setting confirms the layout direction before the tile is fixed.

### How does the matte finish on Iconic Blue compare to the satin finish on other Zia ceramics?

The matte surface diffuses light evenly across the deep blue ground, holding the color as a non-reflective, consistent field. A satin finish in the same deep blue would return a low-level sheen and carry slightly more surface variation under directional light. The matte finish requires a pre-grout sealer that satin ceramics do not, and its DCOF of 0.74 is higher than the 0.45 DCOF of satin ceramics and well above the 0.42 universal wet-floor threshold — making matte the better choice for floor applications where slip resistance is a priority.

### How does tonal variation distribute in a full Iconic Blue installation?

High-fired ceramics carry very minor variation in size, color, tone, and surface texture. In Iconic Blue, this expresses as subtle shifts within the deep blue range — individual tiles may read slightly deeper or slightly lighter. Mixing tiles from all boxes thoroughly before installation and arranging a dry layout before setting distributes these tonal differences evenly across the field.

### What grout choices work well with Iconic Blue 2x8?

For a grout approach, a tone-on-tone deep blue or charcoal grey keeps the surface unified and allows the deep blue field to read without interruption. A light or near-white grout creates a strong graphic contrast against the deep blue tile and makes the 2×8 coursing pattern a more prominent visual element. Ordering grout samples alongside tile samples is the recommended approach to confirm the relationship in the actual installation conditions. Contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170 for sample ordering.

### Can Iconic Blue 2x8 be mixed with other Iconic Blue formats in the same installation?

Yes. The Iconic Blue colorway is available in 2×4 and 4×4 formats, all sharing the same matte finish and color. Combining the 2×8 with a smaller format — a 2×8 field on a shower wall with a 2×4 border or floor — introduces a format change without a color shift. Lay out the mixed formats dry before setting to confirm the proportional relationship across the two tile sizes.
