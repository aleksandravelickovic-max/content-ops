## About

Each cement tile carries the hand of the maker in the pigment lay, the press, and the cure: slight irregularities at the edge and small color drifts that read as a single soft matte field once the floor is set. Color shifts subtly tile to tile within every colorway.

Expect tonal variation across the box, surface character that softens with patina, and small pits or air pockets in the surface, all natural to a hand-poured pigmented cement tile.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with cement tile, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cement tile is hand-poured in Vietnam using techniques refined by French and Catalan craftspeople in the 1850s, when pigmented cement was developed as a more affordable alternative to stone mosaic. The Vietnamese workshops preserve the original method exactly: one tile at a time, by hand.

Each tile begins with mineral pigments laid by hand into a steel pattern frame, one color at a time, on top of a powdered concrete base inside a steel mold. The mold is closed, and the tile is hydraulically pressed at 3,000 lbs psi, soaked in water, and cured on drying racks for seven days. The tile is never fired, which is what distinguishes cement tile from ceramic tile.

The 3 mm pigmented surface layer is what gives cement tile its longevity and its characteristic patina. There is real color depth in the surface, not just a topcoat, which means the tile can wear and re-seal over decades without losing its color.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What distinguishes Taupe from White and Black in the cement range — where does Taupe sit in the warm/cool spectrum?

White is a neutral-toned cement tile, with a pale, crisp tone that reads clearly as light and neutral. Black sits at the opposite end — a deep, matte, non-reflective dark. Taupe occupies the middle ground between grey and beige, carrying a low level of warmth that neither the white nor the black has. Against the same materials — pale plaster, natural wood, stone — Taupe reads as an earth neutral rather than a color statement. The warmth is present but not strong, which is what separates it from a cool grey.

### Is the Taupe colorway available in other formats within the cement collection?

Yes. Taupe is also available in the 4x4 square and the 2x8 subway format within Zia's cement range. The 4x4 produces a denser grout grid and is a common choice for shower walls and backsplash applications where a smaller module suits the scale. The 2x8 can be set in a running bond or stacked layout. The 8x8 is the largest of the three Taupe formats and produces the fewest grout joints per square foot.

### How does the 8x8 format read differently from a 4x4 grid in the Taupe colorway?

The 8x8 format covers four times the area of a single 4x4 tile, which reduces the number of grout joints by the same factor. With 1/16" joints, the grid becomes minimal across the floor — the taupe reads as a near-continuous surface rather than a pattern of tiles. In a 4x4 layout, the closer grid introduces more visual structure and the joint lines read as a more active element. The 8x8 suits spaces where the intent is a quiet, open-feeling floor plane; the 4x4 suits spaces where a more defined grid is desirable.

### What grout color choices change how Taupe reads across the installation?

Grout close in tone to the taupe — a warm grey or sand — keeps the grid minimal and allows the floor to read as a single surface. A lighter grout, closer to white, draws the grid into visibility and produces a stronger geometric pattern. A darker grout — charcoal or dark grey — creates contrast and defines the tile format more sharply. The 8x8 format amplifies all three effects: fewer joints mean each grout line covers more relative visual area than it would in a 4x4 layout. For wet commercial floor applications, grout joints should measure at least 1/8". Contact [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170 for project-specific guidance.

### How does Taupe cement compare to the Sage colorway in the range — both are muted neutrals?

Sage is a muted green-grey cement tile — a cooler neutral with a clear green undertone. Taupe sits in the warm neutral range between grey and beige, with no green shift. The two colorways both read as quiet and muted, but Sage's green undertone ties it to natural materials like stone and aged wood through color affinity, while Taupe's beige warmth tends to read as an earth tone. In the same space, Sage pulls slightly cooler and more mineral; Taupe pulls slightly warmer and more grounded. The two can work together in adjacent areas where a tonal shift between spaces is the intent.

### Can Taupe 8x8 be used in a shower?

Yes. Zia's Taupe 8x8 is suited for shower walls and shower floors. Seal the tile before grouting and again after, following the cement installation guide. For shower floor applications, apply an anti-slip treatment after installation to support traction in wet conditions. Because cement tile is porous, consistent sealing in shower environments is particularly important to maintain the surface and manage moisture over time.

### Can Taupe 8x8 be installed outdoors?

Taupe 8x8 cement tile is suited for exterior floors and walls in non-freeze/thaw climates — patios, courtyards, and covered outdoor spaces in mild climates. It is not suitable for installation in climates where the surface may be subject to freeze/thaw cycles. In outdoor applications, a sealer with appropriate UV and moisture protection is recommended; the cement installation guide covers the outdoor sealing sequence.

### What materials pair well with Taupe 8x8?

The warm neutral of Taupe suits pairings across a wide range of materials. Against pale oak or walnut cabinetry, the taupe reads as a coherent earth-tone ground. Against Carrara marble or honed limestone, the warmth in the taupe ties the natural stone and the floor together. The Taupe 8x8 also suits exteriors in mild climates — a courtyard or covered patio in taupe cement reads as a material extension of the interior rather than a hard shift in palette.

### What causes efflorescence in cement tile, and how is it prevented?

Efflorescence is a white, powdery deposit that can appear on cement tile when moisture migrates through the tile and carries soluble salts to the surface. It is most common when tile is installed over a substrate with residual moisture, or when the tile is not properly sealed before grouting. Sealing before grouting is the primary prevention measure: it closes the porous surface so that moisture and salts cannot migrate through. If efflorescence appears after installation, consult the cement installation guide or contact the team at [info@ziatile.com](mailto:info@ziatile.com) or 310-844-1170.

### How is cement tile different from ceramic or porcelain tile?

Cement tile is never fired. Each tile is hydraulically pressed from a pigmented cement surface layer and a powdered concrete base, then cured on drying racks for seven days. The absence of kiln firing is what gives cement tile its matte surface, its porosity, and its characteristic patina behavior — none of which are present in fired ceramic or porcelain. Ceramic and porcelain tiles are fired at high temperature, which vitrifies the clay body and produces a denser, less porous surface. Cement tile requires sealing that ceramic and porcelain do not; in return, it develops a depth of surface character over time that fired tile cannot.

### Can Taupe 8x8 cement tile be used with underfloor radiant heating?

Yes. Taupe 8x8 is compatible with all heated floor systems. Cement tile conducts and releases heat efficiently, making it a considered choice for radiant heating installations. Allow the substrate and radiant system to fully cure and stabilize before tile installation, and follow the heating system manufacturer's guidelines for temperature ramp-up after installation is complete.
