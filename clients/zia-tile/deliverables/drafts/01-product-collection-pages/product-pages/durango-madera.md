#### Order Details \+ Installation \[Locked\]

We recommend 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and tile-to-tile variation; because the Durango is a specialty shape, the additional cuts required during layout often warrant increasing to 20% or more. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange the layout dry to disperse variation evenly. Wash the tile immediately after cutting to prevent the cutting paste from drying on the surface.

Cotto requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout sealer applied as a grout release, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. Subfloors must be fully cured (minimum 30 days) to prevent efflorescence from depositing on the tile surface.

## About

Each Cotto tile is made from locally-sourced clays. Our handcrafted Cotto tiles are made just outside of historic San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.

The tiles' clay formulation is mixed with water and blended to the ideal consistency, then pressed in a mold to dry for 1–2 weeks until the proper moisture level is achieved for firing.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cotto, derived from the Italian terra cotta or "baked earth," is part of a long lineage of fired clays that date back to the dawn of civilization itself. From Egyptian water jugs to Chinese roof tiles to Roman bricks, terra cotta has been part of the human story for as long as we've had an alphabet. The materials in Cotto tile haven't changed much over the millennia, while the firing process has evolved: from kiln furniture to mold designs to firing method, our handmade and high-fired Cotto is our modern take on an ancient tradition.

Our Cotto is produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, where natural clay is pressed into molds, sun-dried, and fired in kilns by local artisans. The unglazed surface is what gives cotto its character at installation and over time. With proper sealing, cotto handles indoor floors, exterior floors in any climate, and freeze/thaw exposure, and the surface wears like a natural stone product as use deepens its tone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Durango + Madera cotto made of?

The Durango + Madera cotto is made from natural Mexican clay sourced near San Miguel de Allende, Mexico. The clay is refined, mixed with water, and hand-pressed into the Durango specialty mold before drying for one to two weeks. Each tile is then kiln-fired, and the heat variation and mineral interaction during firing produce the rust-brown tonal range and surface character of the finished tile. Natural variation in tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness is inherent to the material.

### What paint colors pair well with Durango + Madera?

Durango + Madera pairs with paint colors that either balance or contrast the rust-brown palette. Warm off-whites and cream tones create a neutral surrounding field that lets the sculptural shape lead. Olive, sage, and muted greens align with the earthy tonal range. Deeper colors such as charcoal, navy, or black introduce stronger contrast and shift the overall composition. The choice depends on whether the tile should lead the room or integrate into a broader palette.

### Can Durango + Madera be used in a kitchen?

Yes, Durango + Madera cotto can be used in a kitchen on both floors and walls. Proper installation and sealing are required to manage the natural porosity of unglazed terra cotta. Apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting to protect the surface, then finish with Fila Matte Wax for interior installations. Maintain a minimum 1/8" grout joint. In areas exposed to grease or moisture, such as behind a range, ongoing resealing prevents absorption and staining.

### How do I clean terra cotta tile?

To clean terra cotta tile, use a pH-neutral cleaner with warm water and avoid acidic or abrasive products. Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove abrasive debris that can wear the surface over time. Clean with a damp cloth or mop rather than excess water, and dry the surface afterward to prevent residue buildup. Periodic resealing is required to maintain long-term performance.

### How does the Durango specialty shape install?

The Durango specialty shape is a curved, sculptural format that interlocks with adjacent pieces to build an architectural composition across the surface. Because the geometry depends on consistent orientation, a full dry layout before installation is essential to confirm how the shape resolves across the field. The shape also requires more cuts than a standard square or rectangle, which is why increasing overage beyond the standard 15% is recommended for Durango orders.

### Can Durango + Madera be mixed with other cotto colorways?

Yes. Durango + Madera can be mixed with other cotto colorways in the Zia collection. Each colorway is produced using the same clay body and firing process, so tiles share consistent surface character across the range. The rust-brown Madera works well alongside lighter cotto colorways where the tonal contrast creates a layered composition across the sculptural Durango field.
