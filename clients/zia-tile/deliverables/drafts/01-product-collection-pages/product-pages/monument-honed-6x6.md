## How It's Made

Limestone is quarried from solid blocks near Fez, Morocco, and around Bordeaux, France, in workshops where the material has been cut and honed for centuries. Each block is sized down to the format dimensions and finished by stone masons at the source.

The honed finish is achieved by gently smoothing the surface to a clean, soft matte. The bush-hammered finish is achieved by striking the face with a textured hammer head, which uniformly pits the surface for a more rustic, aged character. Both finishes preserve the natural mineral signature of the stone.

Limestone is a living material. Subtle tonal variation across pieces is inherent to natural stone, and no two tiles are identical. The soft patina that develops over years of use is a defining feature of the material and the reason limestone has carried so many of the floors that survive from antiquity into the present.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is honed limestone?

Honed limestone is a natural stone finished through controlled mechanical grinding to produce a flat, matte, gently smoothed surface. In Monument 6x6, the honing process preserves the stone's natural mineral variation and subtle fossilization patterns while creating a consistent low-sheen plane suited to horizontal and vertical applications. Honed limestone is commonly used where a controlled, non-reflective stone surface is part of the design intent, across both flooring and wall installations.

### How do I care for honed limestone?

To care for honed limestone, seal the stone before and after grouting to reduce absorption and protect against staining. Ongoing maintenance requires pH-neutral stone cleaners and regular dry removal of dust and grit to prevent surface abrasion. Avoid acidic and abrasive products, including vinegar, bleach, and citrus-based cleaners, which can etch the stone permanently. Periodic resealing is required based on traffic and exposure, particularly for floors in high-use areas.

### What design styles suit Monument 6x6?

Monument 6x6 suits restrained architectural palettes where material tone defines the space rather than surface pattern. The honed limestone tile works particularly well in contemporary, modern rustic, and Mediterranean-influenced interiors where continuity across floors or wall planes is part of the design direction. The neutral grey palette also supports checkerboard layouts when paired with a contrasting light stone, as well as monochrome installations where the stone carries the full visual field.

### Can Monument 6x6 be used on a shower floor?

Yes. Monument 6x6 can be used on shower floors, shower walls, and steam showers. Sealing is required for all shower applications to protect the natural stone from continuous moisture exposure. Slip resistance can be increased by sizing grout joints to a minimum of ⅛" and applying an anti-slip product after installation. The 6x6 format and honed finish together produce a shower surface with consistent traction underfoot.

### Can Monument 6x6 be installed in a checkerboard layout?

Yes. Monument 6x6 installs well in a checkerboard layout. The 6x6 square format is particularly suited to this pattern, and the soft grey of Monument pairs naturally with a lighter contrasting stone — such as a warmer limestone, a white marble, or a cream-toned terrazzo — to produce the classic checkerboard rhythm across a floor or wall. A full dry layout is recommended before setting to confirm alignment and the visual balance of the two stones.

### How does Monument differ from Buff limestone?

Monument and Buff are the same material — natural sedimentary limestone quarried near Fez, Morocco — but the two colorways carry different tonal ranges. Buff reads as a warm neutral with cream to tan undertones; Monument reads as a soft neutral grey with cooler mineral tones. Both are finished to the same honed matte surface and follow the same installation and sealing protocol. The choice between the two is a question of whether a warmer or cooler neutral suits the design direction.
