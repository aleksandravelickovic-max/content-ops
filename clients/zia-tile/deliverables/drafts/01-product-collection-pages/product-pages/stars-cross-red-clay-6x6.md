We recommend 15 to 20% overage to account for cuts and tile-to-tile variation; because the Stars & Cross is a specialty shape, the additional cuts required during layout often warrant increasing to 20% or more. Before installation, blend tiles from all boxes and arrange the layout dry to disperse variation evenly. Wash the tile immediately after cutting to prevent the cutting paste from drying on the surface.

Cotto requires a two-stage sealing sequence: a pre-grout sealer applied as a grout release, and a post-grout top coat (a matte wax for interior installations, or a second pass of the pre-grout sealer for exterior, since UV exposure rules out wax outside). Use 1/8" tile spacers for the recommended grout joint. Subfloors must be fully cured (minimum 30 days) to prevent efflorescence from depositing on the tile surface.

## About

Each Cotto tile is made from locally-sourced clays. Our handcrafted Cotto tiles are made just outside of historic San Miguel de Allende, Mexico.

The tiles' clay formulation is mixed with water and blended to the ideal consistency, then pressed in a mold to dry for 1–2 weeks until the proper moisture level is achieved for firing.

Installers must wear protective face and eye coverings when working with Cotto, thinset, sealants, and grout, in accordance with [California Prop 65](https://www.ziatile.com/proposition-65-warnings). Bring drywall flush with the finished surface of the tile, or finish exposed edges with mitered edges or Schluter strips.

## How It's Made

Cotto, derived from the Italian terra cotta or "baked earth," is part of a long lineage of fired clays that date back to the dawn of civilization itself. From Egyptian water jugs to Chinese roof tiles to Roman bricks, terra cotta has been part of the human story for as long as we've had an alphabet. The materials in Cotto tile haven't changed much over the millennia, while the firing process has evolved: from kiln furniture to mold designs to firing method, our handmade and high-fired Cotto is our modern take on an ancient tradition.

Our Cotto is produced in San Miguel de Allende, Mexico, where natural clay is pressed into molds, sun-dried, and fired in kilns by local artisans. The unglazed surface is what gives cotto its character at installation and over time. With proper sealing, cotto handles indoor floors, exterior floors in any climate, and freeze/thaw exposure, and the surface wears like a natural stone product as use deepens its tone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What color is Stars & Cross + Red Clay terra cotta tile?

The Stars & Cross + Red Clay terra cotta tile features a warm red clay base that shifts across earthy red, brick, and orange-brown tones. Each handmade terra cotta tile carries natural firing variation, so no two pieces read exactly the same once installed. The overall effect across the installation is a continuous field of warm, sun-baked clay color with subtle tonal movement built into the patterned surface.

### What paint color goes with Stars & Cross + Red Clay terra cotta tile?

Stars & Cross + Red Clay pairs best with paint colors that either soften or contrast its warm clay base. White, cream, and off-white expand light and keep the tile visually grounded. Soft beige and muted grey maintain a neutral field that lets the pattern lead. Navy, charcoal, and deep green create higher contrast that sharpens the geometry of the interlocking shape and draws more attention to the pattern itself.

### Can Stars & Cross + Red Clay be used in a kitchen?

Yes, Stars & Cross + Red Clay can be used in a kitchen for both floors and walls. The tile performs well in backsplash and flooring applications when properly sealed to protect against moisture and staining. Apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting, then finish with Fila Matte Wax for interior installations. A minimum 1/8" grout joint is recommended for kitchen floor installations.

### What are the natural characteristics of terra cotta tile?

Zia terra cotta is formed from natural clay that is shaped, dried, and kiln-fired, producing visible variation in tone, shape, edge profile, and thickness. Each handmade tile carries inherent differences in color and surface density, and these differences become more pronounced across a full installation. This variation defines the material's surface behavior rather than indicating defects.

### How do I seal Stars & Cross + Red Clay terra cotta tile?

To seal Stars & Cross + Red Clay, apply 511 Porous Plus before grouting to saturate the surface and reduce absorption. After grouting and allowing the grout to cure, apply a second coat. For interior applications, finish with Fila Matte Wax as a final protective coat. For exterior applications, apply a second coat of 511 Porous Plus in place of wax — UV exposure causes wax to discolor over time outdoors. Periodic resealing is required to maintain resistance to moisture and staining.

### How does the Stars & Cross specialty shape install?

The Stars & Cross specialty shape interlocks across tile edges, so the pattern resolves across the field only when each piece is oriented consistently. A full dry layout before setting is essential to confirm how the geometry reads across the space and to identify where cuts are needed at the perimeter. The additional cuts required for the interlocking edges are why increasing overage beyond the standard 15–20% recommendation is advisable for Stars & Cross orders.
