# Cotto Allende — material rules

Cotto Allende is Zia's glazed terra cotta tile line. Always capitalized. Never call it a "sub-line" or "sub-category" — use "glazed terra cotta tile" or "glazed line." Same clay foundation as Cotto, then hand-glazed. Eighteen colorways (write "eighteen," not "18").

## Freeze/thaw — NOT SUITABLE

NOT suitable for outdoor use in freeze/thaw climates. Same rule as glazed zellige. State affirmatively. (§4.1)

## Pools & spas — SUITABLE

Approved for pools and spas. (§4.2)

> Exact language from Jamie's edit (§4.2): "Glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools."
> Approved FAQ wording (§8.3): "Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates."

## Sealing — profile: cotto-allende-glazed (§4.3)

- Only requires post-grout sealing with 511 Porous Plus in areas exposed to continuous moisture.
- Sealing still applies in all installations, including dry spaces.
- NEVER name sealer products in customer-facing copy. Direct readers to the Installation Guide.
- Cotto and Cotto Allende sealing instructions must NEVER be combined into a single mixed instruction set.

## Variation language

- Use: matte-to-glossy finish, speckling that varies by colorway, subtle crazing, color shifts.
- Do not apply the unglazed Cotto variation set (tone/shape/edge/thickness only) — Cotto Allende is glazed and can show crazing.

## Cross-product disambiguation (critical)

Cotto and Cotto Allende are the most common mix-up on Zia pages. Whenever pools/spas, freeze/thaw, or sealing appear, confirm the rule matches THIS material, not unglazed Cotto. They are opposite on pools (unglazed = no; glazed = yes) and opposite on the selling point (unglazed = any climate; glazed = not freeze/thaw).
