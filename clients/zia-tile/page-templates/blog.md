# Blog post template — Zia Tile

Skeleton for an editorial blog post. The golden reference is `campaigns/02-march-april-updates/drafts/blog-4-cotto.md`. The bar is Architectural Digest / Wallpaper* / Remodelista, not SEO blog copy (§2.5). Load the matching `materials/{material}.md` before writing.

---

## Opening paragraph (§6.1) — the highest-leverage section
- Lead with the material, the place, or the feeling. NOT the production process.
- Do NOT open with "families pressing clay into molds" or any process-first framing (Jamie: generic, present in every terra cotta brand's copy).
- Anchor on person + place, or a single specific material detail (§2.5 Leads).

> Jamie's approved Blog Post 4 opener (Cotto): "Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques. The result is a terra cotta defined by tonal variation, surface character, and the inherent details that give handmade tile its appeal. Over time, the unglazed surface develops a natural patina that reflects the material's connection to place, process, and age."

## Introduce the collection early (§6.2)
Name Zia's specific collection and colorways in the opening or first body section. Do not make the reader wait several sections.

## Body sections (§6.3)
- Each H2/H3 approaches the material from a different angle: history, performance, application, care, design context.
- Do not repeat the same information in different words.
- Word audit: if a noun or descriptor appears more than twice in a section, vary it.
- Each section header introduces a new dimension, not a restatement of the intro.

## Applications (§6.6)
Reference specific Zia shapes/formats in context (e.g., 13x13 squares, Big Alcazar for open layouts; Stars & Cross and 6x6 Alcazar for compact rooms). Generic application descriptions with no product names are a recurring gap.

## Patina and aging (§6.5)
Patina is a feature, not a warning. Frame as desirable and intentional. Reference use and time as drivers — NOT light exposure (unless confirmed).

## Concluding paragraph (§6.3)
Every blog post needs a proper conclusion. Do not end abruptly. Close on the maker's intent, the homeowner's words, or a practical aside — never a CTA or generic takeaway (§2.5).

---

## Person consistency (§6.4) — flagged in every review
Choose first person ("our Cotto") or third person ("Zia's Cotto") and hold it for the entire piece. No switching within a paragraph or between sections. No second person (you/your) anywhere. Pipeline gates this via person-consistency.
