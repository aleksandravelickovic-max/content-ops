## Verified sources used

- Roto-Rooter Water Damage Restoration: https://www.rotorooter.com/water-damage-restoration/
  - 24/7, 365 service positioning.
  - Clean, gray, and black water categories.
  - IICRC-certified water restoration technicians.
  - Process: containment, extraction, removal of unsalvageable contents, sanitation, drying, electronic moisture sensors, final inspection.
  - Insurance documentation with photos, sketches, and drying logs.

- Roto-Rooter Sewer Camera Inspection: https://www.rotorooter.com/plumbing/video-camera-line-inspection/
  - Waterproof video camera inspection.
  - Identifies blockages, leaks, structural problems, root intrusion, cracks, corrosion, obstructions, pipe separation, and offsets.

- Roto-Rooter Mold Remediation: https://www.rotorooter.com/water-damage-restoration/mold-remediation/
  - IICRC and AMRT protocol language.
  - Mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours after water intrusion.
  - HEPA vacuums, air scrubbers, dehumidifiers, antimicrobial treatments, insurance documentation.

- EPA, A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home: https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home
  - Dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours.
  - Mold control depends on moisture control.
  - Hidden mold may occur behind drywall, wallpaper, paneling, ceiling tiles, carpets, and around leaking or condensing pipes.

- CDC, Mold: https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html
  - Mold grows where moisture is present.
  - Clean up and dry out a home within 24 to 48 hours after a flood.
  - Mold can cause health effects, especially for people with asthma, allergies, immune compromise, or chronic lung disease.

- IICRC S500: https://iicrc.org/s500/
  - ANSI/IICRC S500 is the Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration.
  - It covers procedures, precautions, drying technology, equipment, documentation, inspection, structural restoration, and related restoration components.
