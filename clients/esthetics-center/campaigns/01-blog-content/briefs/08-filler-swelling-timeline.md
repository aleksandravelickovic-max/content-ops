## SERP Analysis

**Search intent:** Informational / post-treatment reassurance — patients who just received filler seeking to confirm their experience is normal. High anxiety-driven "is this normal" intent.
**Dominant format:** Chronological day-by-day or week-by-week healing timeline, often combined with normal-vs-warning-signs section and aftercare tips. Competitors all use the same core format with minor variation — differentiation comes from depth and area-specific detail.

### What competitors cover
- Why swelling occurs (needle trauma, HA water-absorption, inflammatory response)
- Peak swelling timing: 24–48 hours post-injection
- Day-by-day: Day 1–2 (peak), Days 3–5 (improvement), Days 6–7 (significant reduction), Week 2 (settling), Weeks 3–4 (final result)
- Area-specific variation mentioned briefly
- Normal symptoms: asymmetry, firmness, bruising, mild lumpiness
- Warning signs: vascular occlusion, infection, hard nodules
- Aftercare tips: cold compresses, head elevation, sodium reduction, avoiding heat/alcohol/exercise
- When to contact injector

### Content gaps we exploit (our differentiators)
- **Filler type differences in swelling pattern** — denser, high-G-prime products (Volux for jawline) cause different swelling than softer lip fillers; competitors treat all HA fillers identically
- **First-timer vs. repeat patient** — repeat patients often swell less; not addressed anywhere
- **The "ugly duckling" phase validated** — Days 2–5 are psychologically hard; competitors barely acknowledge it; an empathetic, honest section builds trust
- **Medication and supplement specifics with timing windows** — blood thinners, NSAIDs, fish oil, vitamin E: stop X days before, restart X days after; competitors mention this vaguely
- **Lymphatic drainage massage guidance** — mentioned in passing by 1–2 competitors; no one gives clear, specific guidance on when, how, and whether
- **Delayed swelling / inflammatory reactions** — filler granuloma, delayed hypersensitivity reaction weeks or months later; almost entirely absent from competitor content

### H2 Structure

**H2 1: Why Filler Causes Swelling (The Biology Behind It)**
- Micro-queries: "why is my face swollen after filler," "does hyaluronic acid cause swelling"
- Brief and accessible: needle trauma triggers an inflammatory response; HA fillers attract and hold water (hydrophilic); the body needs time to integrate the new volume. This is normal, expected, and temporary — not a sign the filler was placed incorrectly.

**H2 2: The Filler Swelling Timeline — Week by Week**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #1 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "when does filler swelling peak," "how long until filler swelling goes down"
- The core timeline section. Day 1–2: peak swelling, possible bruising, asymmetry normal. Days 3–5: noticeable improvement, bruising shifting to yellow-green. Days 6–7: most swelling resolved, filler beginning to integrate. Week 2: settling and softening. Weeks 3–4: final result visible for most patients.
- Note: this is the average timeline; individual variation is real. Dr. Rice's team context: medically supervised post-treatment assessment.

**H2 3: Swelling by Treatment Area — Lips, Cheeks, Jawline, Under-Eye**
- Micro-queries: "how long does lip filler swelling last," "jawline filler swelling timeline," "cheek filler swelling days"
- Lips: most reactive area; peak swelling can be dramatic (Days 1–3); tends to resolve faster (Days 5–7 most visible improvement). Cheeks: moderate swelling; can feel firm and "high" at first; settles over 2–3 weeks. Jawline: denser filler = firmer, longer swelling period; full integration at 3–4 weeks. Under-eye: most sensitive area; longest swelling window; often subtle but persistent.

**H2 4: First-Time vs. Repeat Filler Patients**
- Micro-queries: "does filler swelling get better each time," "why do I swell more with filler than my friend"
- Repeat patients typically swell less as the tissue has prior experience with filler integration. First-timers should expect more pronounced and slightly longer swelling. Individual factors: age (older patients may have slower lymphatic drainage), skin thickness, baseline inflammation.

**H2 5: Normal Symptoms vs. Warning Signs**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #2 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "is hard lump after filler normal," "what are signs of filler complication"
- Normal: asymmetry in first 2 weeks, firmness (especially high-G-prime products), mild lumpiness, bruising, tenderness to touch. Warning signs: blanching (vascular compression — call immediately), progressive redness/warmth/pain worsening after day 3 (possible infection), persistent hard nodule not improving after 4 weeks, vision changes (extremely rare, seek emergency care). 25+ years, 12,000+ patients: Dr. Rice's team has complication protocols and same-day response capability.

**H2 6: How to Reduce Swelling After Filler**
- Micro-queries: "how to reduce filler swelling fast," "does ice help filler swelling"
- Evidence-based aftercare:
  - Cold compresses (not ice directly) in first 24 hrs
  - Head elevation while sleeping for 2–3 nights
  - Reduce sodium intake (sodium promotes water retention)
  - Avoid alcohol for 24–48 hrs
  - Avoid exercise and heat for 24–48 hrs
  - Arnica topical or oral supplement (pre and post; discuss with provider)
  - Antihistamine: may help mild histamine-related swelling; consult provider before using

**H2 7: Medications, Supplements, and Habits That Make Swelling Worse**
- Micro-queries: "what medications worsen filler swelling," "should I stop taking fish oil before filler"
- Specific culprits with windows: ibuprofen and NSAIDs (stop 7–10 days before if possible), aspirin (same), vitamin E supplements (stop 1 week before), fish oil / omega-3 (stop 5–7 days before), alcohol (avoid 24–48 hrs before and after). Resume: most supplements safe to restart 24–48 hrs after treatment. Always confirm timing with your injector.

**H2 8: When Swelling Returns — Delayed Reactions and What to Watch For**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #3 / CTA placement)*
- Micro-queries: "filler swelling months later," "can filler cause delayed reaction"
- Delayed inflammatory reactions (DIRs): swelling that returns weeks or months after treatment, often triggered by illness or immune activation. Filler granulomas: rare firm nodules forming around filler as a foreign body reaction; treatment is different from normal swelling. Neither is common, but both are real — and addressing them here, when competitors don't, builds credibility.
