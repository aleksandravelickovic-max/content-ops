## SERP Analysis

**Search intent:** Informational with transactional undertone — pre-booking research; readers are evaluating providers and want to feel empowered before the appointment. Strong "vetting the provider" angle alongside the "what to ask" angle.
**Dominant format:** Numbered listicle of 10–15 questions with explanatory paragraphs. Some hybrid Q&A + checklist formats also perform. Local medspa blogs trend toward SEO-optimized listicles with embedded CTAs.

### What competitors cover
- Provider credentials, training, and experience verification
- Number of units needed and per-unit pricing
- Treatment areas and injection site mapping
- Expected results timeline and duration (3–4 months)
- Risks and side effects
- Botox vs. alternative neurotoxins (Dysport, Xeomin, Jeuveau)
- Aftercare instructions overview
- What to do if results are unsatisfactory
- Medical history and contraindications
- Natural-looking vs. frozen appearance concerns
- Follow-up appointment scheduling

### Content gaps we exploit (our differentiators)
- **Red flags during the consultation itself** — no competitor frames the consultation as a two-way evaluation; patients don't know what a rushed, upselling, or evasive provider looks like
- **Questions about the specific product brand and why** — patients should ask which neurotoxin is being used and why; competitors don't explain why this matters
- **How to prepare before the consultation** — what to bring, medications to disclose, photos of desired results; competitors start at the consultation, not before it
- **What "informed consent" actually covers** — it exists but patients don't know what to read carefully or push back on
- **Post-consultation next steps** — what happens between saying yes and the actual appointment

## Outline

### Opening hook direction
Address the reader going into their first Botox consultation feeling unprepared — they don't want to ask a "dumb" question or seem high-maintenance, but they also don't know if the provider they're seeing is actually the right fit. Make them feel like asking thorough questions is the smart move, not the difficult one.

### H2 Structure

**H2 1: Why the Consultation Is the Most Important Appointment**
- Micro-queries: "what happens at a botox consultation," "how long does a botox consultation take"
- This is where provider fit is determined — not just what you'll get, but whether the person doing it has the training, experience, and judgment to do it well. A consultation that feels rushed or overly focused on upselling is itself diagnostic information.

**H2 2: Questions to Ask About Credentials and Experience**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #1 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "what qualifications should a botox provider have," "is a nurse injector or doctor better for botox"
- What to ask: Are you licensed in California? Who is the medical director? What is your specific training in facial injectables? How many Botox treatments do you perform per month? What credentials does the supervising physician hold?
- Dr. Rice context: double board-certified in facial plastic and reconstructive surgery; team includes licensed cosmetic nurse injectors, PAs, and aesthetic nurse practitioners working under physician oversight.

**H2 3: Questions to Ask About the Treatment Plan**
- Micro-queries: "how many units of botox for forehead," "botox cost per unit or per area"
- What to ask: How many units are you recommending and for which areas? What brand of neurotoxin will you use, and why that one? Will the injection mapping be customized to my anatomy? Is there a two-week follow-up included?
- Why it matters: vague answers on units or refusal to name the product are quality signals.

**H2 4: Questions to Ask About Realistic Results**
- Micro-queries: "how do I avoid looking frozen with botox," "how to tell your injector you want natural results"
- What to ask: Can I see before-and-after photos of patients with similar anatomy and concerns? What does "natural-looking" mean in your approach? How do you handle asymmetry or results I'm not happy with?
- The "frozen" fear: a skilled injector will proactively address this and explain dosing philosophy without being asked.

**H2 5: Questions to Ask About Risks, Side Effects, and Complications**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #2 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "what are the risks of botox i should know," "what happens if botox goes wrong"
- What to ask: What are the most common side effects for the areas we're treating? What's your protocol if I develop ptosis or asymmetry? Do you keep reversal agents on-site? Who do I contact if I have a concern post-treatment?
- A provider who answers these questions directly and without defensiveness is a green flag. 25+ years, 12,000+ patients framing: experienced providers have complication protocols because complications happen — transparency is the standard.

**H2 6: Questions to Ask About Pricing and What's Included**
- Micro-queries: "is botox priced per unit or per area," "what is included in botox consultation cost"
- What to ask: Is the consultation fee applied to the treatment? Are you pricing by unit or by area? Is the two-week follow-up included or additional? Are there packages or touch-up policies?
- Red flag: providers who won't give unit counts or who push "per area" pricing without explaining what's included.

**H2 7: Red Flags to Watch for During Any Botox Consultation**
- Micro-queries: "how to tell if a botox provider is not qualified," "bad botox consultation warning signs"
- Specific red flags (what competitors never list in one place):
  - No clear answer on the medical director's credentials
  - Pressure to book or add treatments during the consultation
  - No discussion of contraindications or medical history
  - Vague or evasive answers about units and product
  - No mention of follow-up appointment
  - "Consultations" conducted over text or social media without an in-person assessment

**H2 8: How to Prepare Before Your Consultation**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #3 / CTA placement)*
- Micro-queries: "what to bring to a botox consultation," "how to prepare for a botox consultation"
- What to bring: list of current medications and supplements (especially blood thinners, NSAIDs, vitamin E, fish oil), photos of expressions that bother you (and photos of your neutral face), reference images if helpful — and realistic expectations about what they are and aren't.
- Stop blood-thinning supplements 7–10 days before treatment if approved by your doctor.
- After saying yes: what happens next (pre-treatment instructions, appointment confirmation, what to avoid day-of).
