**Dominant format:** Long-form educational guide, often with bulleted candidacy checklists and FAQ. Overview-then-detail structure: what it is → how it works → who qualifies → what to expect.

### What competitors cover
- What non-surgical body contouring is (not a weight-loss solution)
- Main modalities: cryolipolysis (CoolSculpting), laser lipolysis (SculpSure), RF (Morpheus Body, truSculpt), ultrasound, injection lipolysis (Kybella), Emsculpt NEO

### Content gaps we exploit (our differentiators)
- **Disqualifiers consolidated** — most pages list positive criteria only; contraindications (Raynaud's, implanted metal/pacemaker for RF, active infection, pregnancy, BMI over 35) are almost never listed in one place
- **Skin laxity framework** — mild/moderate/severe laxity and which treatment tier maps to each; major differentiator for medically credible content
- **Postpartum and post-weight-loss patients** — common patient profile, almost entirely absent from competitor content
- **Combination protocols** — when providers combine modalities (cryo + RF for fat + skin tightening) and why; competitors don't explain the logic
- **Visceral vs. subcutaneous fat distinction** — briefly mentioned everywhere, never fully explained; patients with deep belly fat need to understand why body contouring addresses only subcutaneous fat

## Outline

### Opening hook direction
Address the reader who is already at or near their goal weight but has a stubborn area that won't respond to diet or exercise. They've heard about body contouring but don't know if they qualify. Make them feel seen before providing the criteria.

### H2 Structure

**H2 1: What Non-Surgical Body Sculpting Actually Is (and Isn't)**
- Micro-queries: "what is non-surgical body contouring," "is body sculpting the same as weight loss"
- Critical distinction up front: fat *reduction* in specific areas, not weight loss. Body weight may not change. Visceral fat is not treatable. Sets accurate expectations before candidacy discussion.

**H2 2: The Main Treatment Options and How They Work**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #1 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "what types of non-surgical body contouring are there," "CoolSculpting vs Emsculpt vs SculpSure which is better"
- Brief table (see structural requirements). Keep descriptions to 1–2 sentences per modality. Not a deep dive — enough to frame the candidacy discussion. Dr. Rice credential: medically supervised setting where treatment selection is matched to anatomy.

**H2 3: Signs You Are a Strong Candidate**
- Micro-queries: "what makes someone a good candidate for body contouring," "do I qualify for CoolSculpting"
- Positive criteria: BMI under 30 (or near goal weight), localized subcutaneous fat pocket that is pinchable, good skin tone/elasticity, stable weight for 6+ months, realistic expectations. Note: these are general criteria; final assessment happens at consultation.

**H2 4: Signs Non-Surgical Body Contouring May Not Be Right for You**
- Micro-queries: "who should not get non-surgical body contouring," "CoolSculpting contraindications"
- Explicit disqualifiers list: Raynaud's disease or cold agglutinin disease (for cryo), implanted metal or electronic devices in treatment area (for RF/ultrasound), active infection or open skin in treatment area, pregnancy or breastfeeding, BMI significantly over 30 with diffuse fat distribution, severe skin laxity (surgical territory), cryoglobulinemia.
- Also: if the goal is weight loss rather than spot reduction, body contouring is the wrong tool.

**H2 5: The Skin Laxity Factor — Why It Changes Everything**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #2 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "does non-surgical body contouring tighten loose skin," "body contouring for loose skin after weight loss"
- Mild laxity: RF or ultrasound devices can address fat and stimulate collagen simultaneously. Moderate: combination protocols may help. Severe: surgical intervention is often the more appropriate path. 25+ years, 12,000+ patients context for surgical-level candidacy assessment.

**H2 6: Special Considerations — Postpartum, Post-Weight-Loss, and Older Patients**
- Micro-queries: "can I get body contouring after having a baby," "body contouring after weight loss skin"
- Postpartum: timing (typically after breastfeeding ends and weight stabilizes), diastasis recti consideration (Emsculpt NEO can address muscle component). Post-weight-loss: skin laxity assessment is critical. Older patients: slower collagen response means RF/ultrasound modalities may need more sessions.

**H2 7: What to Realistically Expect — Results, Timeline, and Sessions**
- Micro-queries: "how long does non-surgical body contouring take to show results," "how many body contouring sessions do I need"
- General timeline: 8–12 weeks for initial results; some modalities show earlier. Session count: 1–4 per area depending on modality and fat volume. Do not invent device-specific protocols — use ranges and note that personalized plans are determined at consultation.

**H2 8: How to Know for Sure — What Happens at a Consultation**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #3 / CTA placement)*
- Micro-queries: "what happens at a body contouring consultation," "how do I find out if I'm a candidate"
- What the assessment covers: medical history, skin quality evaluation, fat distribution, treatment area measurement, goal discussion, modality matching. Not a sales appointment — a clinical fit evaluation.
