## SERP Analysis

**Search intent:** Informational — pre-op patients planning their lives around the procedure, post-op patients checking whether their experience is normal. High-reassurance-seeking intent throughout.
**Dominant format:** Chronological long-form guide. Day-by-day for week 1, then weekly milestones through month 2, then monthly summaries through month 12. Swelling-percentage callouts are common.

### What competitors cover
- Days 1–2: swelling peaks, bruising onset, rest, head elevation
- Days 3–5: swelling plateaus, congestion, pain management
- Days 6–10: splint removal, first follow-up, stitches out
- Week 2: bruising fades, return to social activities
- Weeks 3–4: light activity, most patients back to work
- Month 2+: gradual tip refinement, final results framing
- Recovery tips (cold compresses, sleep positioning)

### Content gaps we exploit (our differentiators)
- **The "mirror discouragement" phase** — mentioned once by one competitor, never developed; the psychological experience of looking worse before better is real and unaddressed
- **Specific dietary guidance** — anti-inflammatory foods, salt restriction to reduce swelling, hydration protocols; almost entirely absent from competitor content
- **Complication red flags** — when to call your surgeon; infection signs, asymmetry concerns, breathing obstruction barely covered
- **Activity restriction specificity** — exact return-to-exercise milestones (walking, light cardio, weightlifting) are vague; we can be concrete
- **Open vs. closed rhinoplasty recovery differences** — alluded to but never explained by competitors; this is a frequent patient question
- **What "final results" looks like** — how to read your own healing at 6 months vs. 12 months; patients don't know what to look for

### H2 Structure

**H2 1: How Long Does Rhinoplasty Recovery Actually Take?**
- Micro-queries: "how long does rhinoplasty recovery take," "when do you look normal after rhinoplasty"
- Honest framing: most patients look presentable by week 2–3, socially confident by month 1–2, but full results take 12 months. Tip swelling is the last to resolve. Sets realistic expectations before the timeline begins.

**H2 2: Days 1–3: The First 72 Hours at Home**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #1 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "what does the first day after rhinoplasty feel like," "rhinoplasty day 1 pain level"
- Splint or cast in place, nasal packing if used, peak swelling and bruising onset, periorbital bruising common. Head elevation. Pain is typically manageable — describe the sensation honestly. Congestion is significant. Sleeping position. Dr. Rice credential: double board-certified in facial plastic surgery; post-op care protocols are surgical-level.

**H2 3: Days 4–7: Peak Swelling and Managing Discomfort**
- Micro-queries: "when does rhinoplasty swelling peak," "rhinoplasty day 5 swelling"
- Swelling peaks around days 2–3 and holds through day 5 for most patients. Bruising color shifts from purple to yellow-green (normal progression). Congestion continues. Cold compresses around (not on) the nose. Most patients still not presentable in public — normalize this.

**H2 4: Splint Removal and Your First Look**
- Micro-queries: "what happens when rhinoplasty splint comes off," "can i go out after rhinoplasty cast is removed"
- Typically day 7–10. The nose will still look swollen — manage expectations here strongly. This is not the final result. First look often causes anxiety; this is the "mirror discouragement" window. Stitches out (external) typically same visit for open rhinoplasty.

**H2 5: Week 2–3: When You Start to Feel Human Again**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #2 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "when can I go back to work after rhinoplasty," "week 2 rhinoplasty what to expect"
- Most patients comfortable returning to desk work or remote work. Physical activity: walking only. Social confidence varies; some patients comfortable, others still self-conscious. Bruising mostly gone. 25+ years, 12,000+ patients — experienced surgical oversight throughout recovery.

**H2 6: Month 1–3: Visible Refinement Begins**
- Micro-queries: "rhinoplasty 1 month results," "why is my nose still swollen 2 months after rhinoplasty"
- Most social downtime behind the patient. Light cardio cleared around week 3–4; more strenuous activity cleared around week 6 (confirm specific clearances with surgeon). The nose continues to change — tip swelling resolves slowly. Patients often anxious at this stage; normalize the timeline.

**H2 7: Months 4–12: The Path to Final Results**
- Micro-queries: "rhinoplasty tip swelling how long," "rhinoplasty 6 months vs 12 months results"
- Tip definition improving through month 6; subtle changes continue through month 12. What to look for: overall shape refines, skin softens and redrapes. Final photography / documentation typically at 12 months. Open vs. closed recovery difference: open rhinoplasty (external incision) tends to have slightly longer tip swelling resolution.

**H2 8: Recovery Tips, Warning Signs, and the Emotional Side**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #3 / CTA placement)*
- Micro-queries: "how to recover faster from rhinoplasty," "signs of rhinoplasty complication"
- Tips: anti-inflammatory diet (reduce sodium, increase hydration, avoid alcohol), arnica, head elevation for first 2 weeks, no glasses on nose bridge until cleared, sun protection on incision. Warning signs: increasing pain (not decreasing), signs of infection (fever, unusual discharge, rapidly worsening swelling), breathing obstruction.
- Emotional note: looking worse before better is well-documented. The discouragement window is typically weeks 1–3. It passes.
