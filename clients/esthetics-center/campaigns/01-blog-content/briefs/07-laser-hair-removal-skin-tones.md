## Outline

### Opening hook direction
Speak directly to the reader who has been told — or assumed — that laser hair removal isn't for their skin. Acknowledge that this was true with older technology. Lead with the shift, not the limitation.

### H2 Structure

**H2 1: Why Skin Tone Matters in Laser Hair Removal**
- Micro-queries: "why doesn't laser hair removal work on dark skin," "how does skin tone affect laser hair removal"
- The core science in plain English: laser targets melanin (pigment) in the hair follicle. Darker skin has more melanin in the surrounding tissue, which older lasers couldn't distinguish from follicle melanin — leading to burns. Longer wavelengths bypass surface melanin and reach the follicle safely. This is the premise everything else builds on.

**H2 2: The Fitzpatrick Scale — Which Type Are You?**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #1 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "what is the Fitzpatrick scale," "how do I know my Fitzpatrick skin type"
- Brief table: Types I–VI with descriptions (skin tone, hair color, sun response). Keep it practical and self-diagnostic. Note: self-typing is a starting point; clinical assessment may differ. Dr. Rice framing: skin tone assessment at Esthetics Center is done by licensed professionals in a medically supervised setting.

**H2 3: Laser Types Matched to Skin Tone**
- Micro-queries: "what laser is safest for dark skin," "Nd:YAG vs diode laser for dark skin"
- Three main options:
  - Alexandrite (755nm): most effective for Types I–III; not appropriate for darker skin due to high melanin absorption
  - Diode (810nm): effective for Types III–V; solid middle-ground wavelength
  - Nd:YAG (1064nm): the gold standard for Types V–VI; longest wavelength, bypasses surface melanin most effectively
- Distinguish IPL: not a laser; lower precision, not recommended for darker skin.

**H2 4: Fitzpatrick Types I–IV — From Fair to Medium-Olive**
- Micro-queries: "laser hair removal for olive skin," "is laser safe for Fitzpatrick type 4"
- Types I–III: most treatment-eligible, widest device selection, fewest sessions typically needed. Type IV (the ambiguous middle): eligible for Diode or Nd:YAG, not Alexandrite; benefits from conservative fluence settings; providers should perform a patch test regardless. This group is underserved in competitor content — own this section.

**H2 5: Fitzpatrick Types V–VI — Dark and Deep Skin Tones**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #2 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "is laser hair removal safe for Black skin," "laser hair removal dark brown skin results"
- Nd:YAG 1064nm is the appropriate choice. Contact cooling is essential (ICE tip, cryogen cooling). More sessions typically required (8–10 vs. 4–6 for lighter skin). Risks: hypopigmentation (lighter spots) is the more common risk for very dark skin, not just hyperpigmentation. 25+ years, 12,000+ patients context — experienced providers adjust fluence and pulse duration based on real-time tissue response.

**H2 6: Tanned Skin — A Special Note for Outdoor Lifestyles**
- Micro-queries: "can you get laser hair removal with a tan," "how long to wait after sun exposure for laser"
- Artificial and natural tans both increase surface melanin, raising burn risk. General guidance: wait until the tan fully fades before treatment — typically 4+ weeks for a moderate natural tan. NorCal-specific: outdoor activities, hiking, summer skin exposure are relevant. Practical: schedule fall/winter sessions to manage this naturally.

**H2 7: Risks to Understand — Hyperpigmentation, Hypopigmentation, and Burns**
- Micro-queries: "can laser hair removal cause dark spots," "does laser cause hypopigmentation"
- Clearly distinguish: hyperpigmentation (darker spots) is more common in medium-dark skin and typically temporary; hypopigmentation (lighter spots) is more common in very dark skin and can be longer-lasting. Burns (blistering, scarring) are rare with appropriate wavelength and provider training but are the most serious risk. All three risks decrease significantly with the right technology and provider.

**H2 8: The Patch Test, Choosing Your Provider, and What to Ask**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #3 / CTA placement)*
- Micro-queries: "what is a patch test for laser hair removal," "how to choose a laser hair removal provider for dark skin"
- Patch test process: small area treated first (48–72 hrs before full treatment), monitor for adverse reaction. Step-by-step: what to expect, what a reaction looks like vs. normal redness, when to proceed vs. pause.
- What to ask your provider: which laser will you use on my skin type and why, what fluence settings, do you have experience treating my Fitzpatrick type, is a patch test included?
