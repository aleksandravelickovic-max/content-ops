### H2 Structure

**H2 1: What a Brow Lift Actually Addresses**
- Micro-queries: "what does a brow lift fix," "is a brow lift the same as a forehead lift"
- Specific concerns: brow ptosis (descent below the orbital rim), lateral hooding (brows drooping toward outer corners), forehead lines (created by muscles compensating for descended brows), a heavy or tired appearance regardless of how rested you are. A brow lift addresses position and volume — not skin texture or under-eye hollowing.

**H2 2: Signs You May Be a Candidate**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #1 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "am I a candidate for a brow lift," "what age should you get a brow lift"
- Strong indicators: brow position at or below the orbital rim, lateral brow descent that creates hooding of the upper eyelid, forehead lines caused primarily by brow compensation (muscles working to lift descended brows), good overall skin elasticity. Also: patients who have considered upper blepharoplasty but whose primary issue is brow position, not excess eyelid skin.
- Dr. Rice credential: double board-certified in facial plastic and reconstructive surgery — candidacy assessment requires surgical-level anatomy evaluation.

**H2 3: Brow Lift Types — Endoscopic, Temporal, and Coronal Compared**
- Micro-queries: "endoscopic brow lift vs coronal brow lift," "what is a temporal brow lift"
- Concise comparison: Endoscopic (3–5 small incisions within hairline, endoscope-guided, gold standard for most patients, minimal scarring, faster recovery). Temporal/Limited Incision (small incisions at temples, addresses lateral brow descent, good for patients needing targeted lift rather than full forehead elevation). Coronal (longer incision across scalp, used less frequently, suited for very high brow descent or specific anatomical needs, longer recovery). Which one suits whom: this is the decision framework competitors skip.

**H2 4: The Endoscopic Brow Lift — How It Works and Why It's the Standard**
- Micro-queries: "how is endoscopic brow lift done," "endoscopic brow lift recovery time"
- Step-by-step process: small incisions within the hairline, endoscope allows visualization, underlying muscles and tissue are repositioned (not just skin pulled), fixation with bioabsorbable anchors or sutures. Result: natural elevation with preserved hairline, minimal visible scarring.

**H2 5: Non-Surgical Alternatives — Botox, Thread Lifts, and Filler**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #2 placement here)*
- Micro-queries: "Botox brow lift vs surgery results," "does a thread lift work as well as brow lift surgery"
- Honest comparison table (see structural requirements): Botox brow lift (2–4mm lift, 3–4 months duration, no downtime, repeatable, best for mild descent). Thread lifts (PDO threads, 3–5mm lift possible, 12–18 months duration, minimal downtime, variable longevity). Surgery (10–15mm lift possible, 5–10+ years duration, 2-week recovery). Don't oversell surgery or dismiss non-surgical — be accurate. 25+ years, 12,000+ patients context: Dr. Rice recommends based on anatomy, not preference for surgical volume.

**H2 6: Brow Lift vs. Upper Eyelid Surgery — Which One Do You Need?**
- Micro-queries: "brow lift or blepharoplasty which do I need," "how do I know if I need a brow lift or eyelid lift"
- This is one of the most common patient confusions. Key distinction: if the heaviness is coming from the brow position, blepharoplasty alone may not fully address it — and may create an over-operated look if the brow remains low. The test: manually lift the brow and assess whether the upper eyelid issue improves. Many patients need both, sequenced correctly. A surgical consultation is the only way to know for certain.

**H2 7: Recovery — Week-by-Week Timeline**
- Micro-queries: "how long is brow lift recovery," "brow lift swelling week by week"
- Week 1: swelling, bruising, numbness at incision sites (temporary — sensory nerves affected during surgery), head elevation, light activity only. Week 2: swelling significantly improved, bruising fading, most patients comfortable appearing in public. Weeks 3–4: return to normal activity, light exercise cleared. Month 2+: full activity, final results emerging. Hairline and incision sites: healing continues for several months; discuss expectations for scar maturation.

**H2 8: How Long Results Last — and How to Extend Them**
*(→ Dr. Rice trust signal #3 / CTA placement)*
- Micro-queries: "how long does a brow lift last," "will brow lift results fade"
- Surgical results: typically 5–10 years, depending on age at procedure, skin quality, and genetics. Aging continues — the goal is to reset the clock, not stop it. Maintenance: Botox in the forehead and glabella can extend surgical results by preventing the muscle action that contributes to brow descent. Repeat surgery: possible in appropriate candidates after results have significantly faded, typically 8–10+ years later.
