---

## How Each Method Actually Works

Waxing removes hair at the shaft. The follicle stays intact and healthy, which is why hair grows back on the same schedule every time. No matter how many years you wax, the follicle keeps doing its job.

Laser hair removal works differently. The laser targets melanin — the pigment — inside the hair follicle itself. The energy damages the follicle's ability to regrow hair, reducing production over a series of sessions. Because the follicle is the actual target, results don't reset between appointments the way waxing does.

That's the fundamental difference. Waxing manages regrowth. Laser interrupts it at the source.

This is also why results from the two methods are so different in the long run. One is a monthly appointment you'll keep making indefinitely. The other is a course of treatment with an end point.

## What Laser Hair Removal Actually Costs

Laser hair removal is priced differently than waxing. Most providers charge per session or by package, and cost varies based on the treatment area, the number of sessions needed, and the provider's clinical level.

Most patients complete a full course in 6–8 sessions, spaced 4–8 weeks apart depending on the area. After the initial course, most people schedule touch-up sessions every one to two years to maintain results.

The break-even point — where lifetime laser costs fall below cumulative waxing costs — typically happens within two years for patients treating medium to large areas.

## Pain, Comfort, and What Each Method Feels Like

Waxing pain is immediate and consistent. The sharp pull on each strip is the same every visit, because the hair grows back to the same thickness every time. There's no improvement in how it feels session to session.

Laser feels different — typically described as a warm snap, often with a cooling mechanism built into the device. It can be uncomfortable, especially on the first session. But here's the difference that most people don't realize: laser gets more comfortable with each session, not less.

As the hair thins and the follicle weakens over the course of treatment, each subsequent session treats less and less hair. By sessions four or five, most patients report noticeably less discomfort than at the start.

The two methods have opposite pain curves. With waxing, discomfort stays constant for years. With laser, it decreases as treatment progresses. That's not a small distinction — it's a different relationship with the appointment entirely.

## Results and Permanence — What "Long-Lasting" Actually Means

Laser hair removal is regulated by the FDA as a method of "permanent hair reduction" — not permanent hair removal. That distinction matters.

Most patients see a 70–90% reduction in hair growth after completing a full course of sessions. Some hair may return over time, particularly in areas affected by hormonal changes. Annual or biannual touch-up sessions maintain results for most people.

Waxing, by contrast, offers no reduction in regrowth. Results last two to six weeks per session — consistently, indefinitely. That consistency is the point, and it's also the limitation.

For most regular waxers, laser delivers more reduction in total hair over time with fewer total appointments. The results are not identical between patients, but the trajectory is clear.

## Who Is (and Isn't) a Candidate for Laser

Laser works by targeting melanin in the hair follicle. That means the contrast between hair color and skin tone affects how well the treatment works.

**Good candidates for laser hair removal:**
- Dark hair (black or brown) on any skin tone
- Medium-dark hair on lighter to medium skin
- Patients with consistent hair regrowth cycles
- Anyone who waxes regularly and wants to reduce long-term hair growth

**Candidates who may see limited results:**
- Gray, white, or blonde hair — very low melanin means the laser has little to target
- Red hair — similarly low melanin content; results are less predictable
- Patients with PCOS or other hormonal conditions — laser can still work, but more sessions may be needed and results may require ongoing maintenance as hormonal hair stimulation continues

A note on darker skin tones: modern technology — specifically Nd:YAG lasers operating at longer wavelengths — has expanded eligibility for patients with deeper skin tones. Eligibility is not the same as it was with older technology. If you were told years ago that laser wasn't an option for your skin, that assessment may no longer apply.

## The Best Time to Start Laser — and How to Transition from Waxing

The practical side of switching from waxing to laser trips people up. Here's what to know.

**Fall and winter are the ideal starting window.** Laser-treated skin needs to stay out of direct sun for several weeks post-session. Starting in fall or winter means your sun exposure is naturally lower, you don't have to modify outdoor plans as much, and it's easier to avoid the UV-related complications that can affect treatment and healing.

For patients in Northern California — where summer activities run long — this timing is especially practical.

**Transition checklist from waxing to laser:**

- Stop waxing, threading, and plucking at least 4–6 weeks before your first laser session (the follicle must be intact for the laser to target it)
- Shaving is fine — and required — before laser sessions; shave the area 24 hours before each appointment
- Avoid sun exposure and tanning (including self-tanner) for 2–4 weeks before treatment
- Avoid retinoids and active exfoliants for 1–2 weeks before treatment
- Between laser sessions, you can shave as needed — waxing and plucking must stop for the duration of your treatment course

Many patients don't realize that shaving between sessions is not only allowed but expected. You don't have to live with full regrowth while you wait for your next appointment.
