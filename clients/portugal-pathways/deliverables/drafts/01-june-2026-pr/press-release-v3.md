## Two Independent Decisions: Residency Planning and Luxury New Homes in Portugal

**LISBON, PORTUGAL** — Among internationally mobile families and high-net-worth principals considering Portugal, two questions frequently surface together but rarely resolve the same way. The first is how to establish residency or residency optionality — preserving freedom of movement, future flexibility, and long-term structural resilience. The second is whether to acquire a luxury new home. They are independent decisions, driven by different objectives, structured through different professional relationships, and carrying no qualifying link between them.

Portugal Pathways, an integrated information, introductions and advisory platform supporting international clients with residency and relocation planning in Portugal, works with families across both decisions. In its experience, conflating the two is one of the most consistent sources of confusion among incoming clients — and one that matters in practice, because the decisions carry different timelines, different structures, and different implications.

### Who Is Looking at Portugal

The profiles are varied, but the underlying logic is consistent.

Principals and families from markets experiencing political volatility, currency risk, or regulatory uncertainty typically approach Portugal as a structural question — a way to build geopolitical resilience and preserve future options for themselves and their families. Financially sophisticated individuals operating across multiple jurisdictions treat Portugal as a stable, well-regulated European consideration within a broader international position, with long-term optionality as the primary goal rather than immediate relocation. Families with children in or approaching education ages are focused on European access, continuity across schooling systems, and long-term family security. For globally mobile founders and executives, the ability to access Portugal and travel within the Schengen Area with a low physical-presence requirement is the central consideration.

What unites them is a preference for flexibility and future-proofing over relocation for its own sake — the ability to adapt as circumstances, regulatory environments, and family priorities evolve.

### Residency Planning: Two Distinct Structures

For non-EU nationals, residency in Portugal generally routes through either investment-linked residency or income and activity-based residency. The two operate on entirely different terms.

The Portuguese Golden Visa is a residency-by-investment programme. Qualifying investment routes currently include subscription of at least €500,000 in a CMVM-regulated investment fund, certain business creation and capital investment routes, and cultural or artistic production donations from €250,000. The qualifying investment serves as the mechanism for participation in the programme; it is not the primary benefit. What many applicants are seeking is the residency structure itself — the rights, the family inclusion provisions, and the mobility it enables.

Under current legislation, Golden Visa holders are generally required to spend on average approximately seven days per year in Portugal — broadly around fourteen days across the initial two-year residence period. This is the low-presence structure that globally mobile families and internationally distributed principals refer to when they describe residency optionality in Portugal. It is specific to the Portuguese Golden Visa. It is not a general feature of Portuguese residency, and it applies to no other visa route.

Since the end of 2023, direct residential property purchase is not a qualifying investment route under the Golden Visa programme. Luxury new homes and Golden Visa residency are entirely separate matters.

One further distinction that Portugal Pathways raises consistently with incoming clients: holding a Portuguese Golden Visa does not automatically make an individual a Portuguese tax resident. Tax residency is determined separately, according to Portuguese tax law and individual circumstances.

The D7 (passive income), D8 (digital nomad), and D2 (entrepreneur) visas operate on an entirely different basis. These require genuine physical presence in Portugal — typically six consecutive months, or eight months across a year. They are suited to families and individuals planning a substantive relocation, not to those seeking a low-presence structure. The approximately seven-day annual presence figure does not apply to these routes. Residency approval under any pathway is discretionary and subject to review by AIMA, Portugal's immigration authority.

### Luxury New Homes: A Separate Decision Entirely

Luxury new home acquisitions in Portugal follow their own logic. They do not confer residency or any low-presence benefit — that link was removed when residential property was withdrawn as a Golden Visa qualifying route at the end of 2023. Families who want Golden Visa residency and want to acquire a luxury new home in Portugal may pursue both, but through two entirely independent processes with no structural connection between them.

For high-net-worth buyers, new-build, off-plan and off-market luxury new homes in Lisbon's established prime districts, Porto's riverside neighbourhoods, and the Algarve's Golden Triangle are assessed on their own terms: family use and access, proximity to international schooling and private amenities, and long-term position within a broader portfolio. New supply in prime locations is structurally constrained by planning controls and heritage protections. Portugal does not operate a nationwide multiple listing service, and many off-plan and off-market luxury new homes are not widely advertised on mainstream portals. Portugal Pathways holds buyer and investment mandates with more than 250 developers, architects, builders and designers across Portugal, providing clients with access to selected new-build, off-plan and off-market luxury new homes — including homes not openly marketed.

### Lifestyle Enters Last

For most of the families Portugal Pathways advises, lifestyle factors — climate, culture, access to private schooling, and the established international communities in Lisbon, Porto, and the Algarve — tend to confirm a structural decision rather than initiate one. A Portuguese residency permit also provides visa-free travel within the Schengen Area for short stays, generally up to 90 days in any 180-day period under current rules. These factors matter. They enter the conversation once the structural framework is in place.
