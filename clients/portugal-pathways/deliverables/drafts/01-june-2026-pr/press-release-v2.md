## Residency and Property in Portugal: Why Internationally Mobile Families Treat Them as Two Separate Decisions

**LISBON, PORTUGAL** — For high-net-worth families and internationally mobile principals considering Portugal, two questions frequently surface together but rarely resolve the same way: how to establish residency or residency optionality, and whether to acquire prime property. They are structurally independent decisions, driven by different priorities, and increasingly understood as such by both advisers and sophisticated clients.

Portugal Pathways, an advisory firm supporting international clients with residency and relocation planning in Portugal, works with families across both decisions. In its experience, conflating them is one of the most common sources of confusion early in the planning process.

### Why Internationally Mobile Families Look at Portugal

The profiles vary. Some are principals and families from markets experiencing political or currency instability, who view a European residency primarily as structural insurance — a means of protecting future mobility and preserving options for their families. Others hold interests across more than one jurisdiction and are assessing Portugal as a stable, well-regulated addition to an international footprint. A third group is focused on their children's long-term access to European education systems and family security across generations. For globally mobile founders and executives, the ability to access Europe with minimal physical presence is the central consideration.

What unites them is a preference for optionality rather than relocation for its own sake — the ability to adapt as circumstances, regulation, and family priorities evolve.

### Residency Planning: Two Distinct Tracks

For non-EU nationals, residency in Portugal routes through either investment-linked residency or income and activity-based residency. The two operate on entirely different terms.

The Golden Visa is Portugal's residency-by-investment programme. It requires a minimum investment of €500,000 in a CMVM-regulated alternative investment fund. For qualifying applicants, the physical presence requirement is a minimum of seven days per year. This is the low-presence structure that globally mobile families and internationally distributed principals reference when they describe residency optionality in Portugal. It is specific to the Golden Visa. It is not a general feature of Portuguese residency.

One clarification that Portugal Pathways raises consistently with incoming clients: since the end of 2023, direct property purchase is no longer a qualifying investment route under the Golden Visa programme. The fund investment pathway is what remains. Property and Golden Visa residency are now entirely separate matters.

The D7 (passive income), D8 (digital nomad), and D2 (entrepreneur) visas operate on a different basis. These require the applicant to demonstrate genuine physical presence in Portugal — typically six consecutive months, or eight months across a year. They are suited to families and individuals planning a substantive relocation to Portugal, not to those seeking a low-presence structure. The seven-day presence advantage does not apply to these routes.

Residency approval under any pathway is discretionary and subject to review by AIMA, Portugal's immigration authority.

"Despite geopolitical issues worldwide, Portugal continues to be stable, safe, tax-efficient, and economically resilient. These qualities remain key drivers for individuals and families pursuing a secure and vibrant future in the country," said Paul Stannard, Chairman and Founder of Portugal Pathways.

### Property Acquisition: A Separate Decision Entirely

Property decisions in Portugal follow their own logic. Direct property purchase does not confer residency or any low-presence benefit — that link was severed when the real-estate route closed at the end of 2023. Families who want Golden Visa residency and want to own property in Portugal pursue both, but through two independent processes with no structural connection between them.

For high-net-worth buyers, acquisitions in Lisbon's established prime districts, Porto's riverside neighbourhoods, and the Algarve's Golden Triangle are assessed on their own terms: family use and access, connection to international schooling and private amenities, and long-term asset allocation within a wider portfolio. New supply in prime locations is structurally limited by planning controls and heritage protections. Much of what becomes available in these areas moves through off-market channels rather than public listings.

Portugal Pathways has access to over 250 developers and estimates that more than 35% of luxury off-market and off-plan opportunities in prime Portuguese locations are not publicly listed.

### Lifestyle Enters at the End

For most families Portugal Pathways advises, lifestyle factors — climate, culture, access to private schooling and established international communities — tend to confirm a structural decision rather than initiate one. Portugal's coastal cities and prime resort areas have developed reliable private service infrastructure over years of international buyer activity. That infrastructure matters to clients. It enters the conversation once the structural framework is established.
