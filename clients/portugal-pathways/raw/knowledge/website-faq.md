## Residency Programmes

### Golden Visa

**Who is eligible?**
Non-EU, non-EEA, and non-Swiss citizens aged 18+, with a clean criminal record, who make a qualifying investment under current rules. The current qualifying route is a minimum €500,000 investment in a CMVM-regulated alternative investment fund. Direct property purchase is no longer a qualifying route following regulatory changes in 2023.

Minimum residency requirement: an average of 7 days per year in Portugal. Renewable residency card every two years. Access to 29 Schengen region countries. Potential eligibility for Portuguese citizenship after a 10-year qualifying period. Family members (spouse, dependent children, dependent parents) can be included.

**Do CMVM-regulated funds guarantee Golden Visa approval?**
No. CMVM regulation refers to the regulatory framework for fund governance. Approval depends on individual eligibility, documentation, and discretionary review by the relevant authorities.

**Does purchasing luxury property qualify for a Golden Visa?**
No. Portugal's Golden Visa currently centres solely on regulated alternative investment funds. Property is no longer an eligible investment route as of 2023.

### D7 Visa (Passive Income / Retirement Visa)

**How do I qualify?**
Applicants must demonstrate income meets the minimum threshold, provide proof of accommodation in Portugal, pass a clean criminal background check, and intend to reside in Portugal long-term. Holders can live, work, and study in Portugal; access healthcare; travel freely within the Schengen area; and apply for permanent residency or citizenship after the qualifying residency period.

### D2 Visa (Entrepreneur Visa)

**What is the D2 Visa?**
A residence visa for non-EU/EEA nationals who want to start or invest in a business in Portugal. Requires a viable business plan demonstrating economic potential.

**How long is it valid?**
Initially allows entry and application for a residence permit, typically issued for 1–2 years. Renewable if the business remains active and financial/legal requirements are met. After 5 years, holders may apply for permanent residency or citizenship if residency and language requirements are met.

**Do I need to set up a company before applying?**
In most cases, yes — or at least show clear intent. Either incorporate a company in Portugal or present a detailed business plan with proof of financial means.

**How long does processing take?**
D2 visa processing typically takes 2–4 months, depending on consulate and documentation. Company formation can take as little as 1–2 days in simple cases.

**What types of businesses qualify?**
No strict limitation, but Portuguese authorities assess whether the business has economic, social, and cultural relevance. Tech start-ups, tourism ventures, professional service providers, restaurants, and traditional businesses are common examples. The business should ideally contribute to job creation and economic growth.

**Can it be renewed?**
Yes, if the business remains active and the applicant continues to meet financial and legal requirements.

**What if the D2 application is rejected?**
The consulate will provide reasons. Common reasons: insufficient business plan, insufficient financial proof, or missing documents. Applicants can appeal or reapply with improved documentation.

**Does business failure affect permanent residency?**
Business failure alone does not automatically disqualify, but you should be able to demonstrate active operation, contribution to the Portuguese economy, legal compliance, and financial self-sufficiency. Transitioning to another activity may still support residency renewal.

### D8 Visa (Digital Nomad Visa)

A special visa allowing remote workers to live and work in Portugal for up to one year. Eligible applicants must prove remote work capability, sufficient financial means, and valid health insurance coverage. Clean criminal record and no outstanding debt or tax obligations in Portugal required.

### IFICI (NHR 2.0) — Incentivised Tax Regime

**Who is eligible?**
Individuals who become Portuguese tax residents and have not been tax residents in Portugal for the past 5 years. Must earn employment or self-employment income in eligible activities: highly qualified professions (doctors, IT and communication experts, industrial and equipment designers), job positions in certified start-ups, or teaching in higher education and scientific research.

**Which professions qualify under the highly qualified route?**
General and executive managers, medical doctors, IT and communication specialists, managers of administrative and commercial services, production and specialised services managers, experts in physics/mathematics/engineering and related technical disciplines, industrial and equipment designers, university and higher education professors.

**Where to find official guidelines?**
Portuguese Tax Authorities, AICEP (Portuguese Trade & Investment Agency), or IAPMEI (Institute for Competitiveness and Innovation). Consulting a tax advisor is recommended.

### NHR (Non-Habitual Residency)

The NHR programme ended for new entrants on 31 December 2023. Applicants who had applied to secure NHR status by that date must be able to prove they took serious steps to demonstrate intent to reside and pay tax in Portugal under the NHR regime.

Once NHR ends (after the 10-year period), foreign-sourced interest and dividends are typically taxed at a flat rate, with the option to aggregate with other earnings if progressive scales result in lower overall liability. Double tax treaties continue to apply.

**When to start planning for NHR expiry?**
Ideally during years 1–7, while flexibility exists to implement tax-efficient structures. Late planning (years 8–10) limits options.

## Luxury Property

### General

**Can international buyers purchase property without residency?**
Yes. There are no restrictions on foreign ownership in Portugal. International buyers can acquire freehold apartments, villas, development plots, and entire buildings. Ownership rights are identical for Portuguese and non-Portuguese nationals.

**What taxes and fees apply when buying luxury property?**
Key acquisition costs: IMT (Property Transfer Tax — progressive rates for luxury properties), Stamp Duty (0.8%), notary and registration fees, legal fees. High-value assets may attract AIMI (additional annual property tax) depending on ownership structure. Structuring via corporate entities may alter tax exposure.

**Can luxury property be financed in Portugal?**
Yes, subject to bank criteria. Options include standard mortgage, and leveraged asset lending (based on listed stocks and bonds). Financing structures are more conservative for international borrowers. Foreign income documentation required.

### Off-Plan and Off-Market

**What is an off-plan property?**
A new build purchased before or during construction. Buyers secure units at an early stage, often benefiting from preferential pricing, prime positioning, and staged payment structures.

**What are typical payment terms?**
Common framework: reservation deposit, 10–30% at CPCV (Contrato-Promessa de Compra e Venda / promissory contract), staged construction payments, balance at completion (Escritura). Structures vary by developer.

**What is off-market property?**
Properties not advertised on property portals or traditional agency channels — discreet villa developments, architect-led private residences, pre-launch developer inventory, entire buildings via private networks. Access is relationship-driven.

**Why do sophisticated buyers prefer off-market access?**
Privacy, early access, strategic negotiation positioning, brand-level discretion, avoiding public price anchoring.

**How does Portugal Pathways access off-market opportunities?**
Portugal Pathways works directly with leading developers, builders, architects, designers, and private sellers. Access is based on long-term relationships. More than 35% of luxury off-market and off-plan opportunities in Portugal are not available on property portals. Portugal Pathways has access to over 250 developers, builders, architects, and designers with unique mandates.

**Can buyers customise finishes in off-plan developments?**
Depends on the development. In bespoke luxury developments, customisation may include flooring materials, kitchen specification, smart home systems, pool and landscaping options, interior design packages. Later-stage purchases generally allow less flexibility.

**How long does completion take for off-plan luxury homes?**
Typically 12–36 months depending on scale and project stage. Larger masterplan communities may extend beyond this. Construction risk and inflation exposure should be assessed during contract review.

**How does capital appreciation work with off-plan properties?**
Appreciation may occur between reservation and completion, completion and resale, or with area infrastructure upgrades. Returns are market-driven and not guaranteed. Strongest in locations with structural land limitations and international demand.

### Due Diligence

Professional due diligence should include: title and land registry verification, planning approval confirmation, construction licensing, developer financial standing, independent legal review, tax structuring review. High-value buyers should also consider succession and estate planning.

### Why is supply constrained in luxury areas?

Coastal and nature protection laws, environmental zoning, heritage overlays, limited development land, strict planning controls. These constraints underpin long-term scarcity in prime micro-locations.

## Healthcare

**Do I need private health insurance for residency applications?**
In most cases yes. Private health insurance meeting Portuguese visa requirements is essential for D7, D2, and digital nomad visa applications.

**Can I access both public and private systems?**
Yes. Once resident, you can register for the public SNS (Serviço Nacional de Saúde) while also holding private insurance.

**What does private healthcare cover?**
Typically: general medicine, specialist appointments, surgery, diagnostics, and sometimes dental, vision, and international coverage depending on the provider. Shorter wait times, more specialist choices, and English-speaking doctors compared to the SNS.

**Are pre-existing conditions covered?**
Coverage varies by provider. Some exclude initially, some offer partial coverage after a waiting period. Advisors can identify the most flexible providers.
