## 1. Brand & Audience

TrustLayer is an AI-powered third-party risk management platform. It automates the collection, verification, and management of compliance documents — primarily certificates of insurance (COIs) — replacing manual email, spreadsheet, and phone workflows with an automated pipeline: request, collect, validate, store, and monitor.

**Critical platform fact:** TrustLayer verifies and tracks compliance documents. It does NOT issue, underwrite, or guarantee insurance. Every piece of content must maintain this distinction. See §4 for enforcement rules.

Website: https://trustlayer.io/

**What TrustLayer is:**
- A third-party risk management platform powered by AI
- A compliance document automation tool (COIs, W-9s, business licenses, contracts)
- A monitoring and gap-detection system for vendor compliance

**What TrustLayer is NOT — never position as:**
- Insurance software
- An insurance issuer or underwriter
- A platform that guarantees compliance (it verifies and tracks — it does not guarantee)
- A manual workflow tool

**Target audiences:**

| Segment | Content angle |
|---|---|
| Risk management teams | Replace manual COI review with automated collection and AI gap detection |
| Procurement teams | Verify vendor compliance at scale without added headcount |
| Compliance and legal departments | Real-time monitoring; audit trail; reduce liability exposure |
| Insurance professionals | COI management at volume; renewal tracking; automated reminders |
| Vendor management teams | Zero-friction vendor submission — no vendor sign-in required |

**Industries served:** Construction, property management, insurance, logistics, retail, distribution, finance, healthcare, events, franchises, equipment rental, workers compensation.

**Company scale context:** TrustLayer operates across a 517,000+ company network and processes 400,000+ COIs per month. Named clients: EL Ad, Grenadier Homes, PayneWest Insurance. Do not reference other client names unless provided in raw/knowledge/.

## 2. Voice & Tone

Voice: casual, confident, approachable. TrustLayer leans into being the "not boring compliance software" brand. Content should feel direct and human — not stuffy or corporate. They use humor and personality where appropriate, but dial it back on liability and compliance specifics.

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Casual | Write the way a smart colleague would explain this. No legal-speak unless quoting the product. |
| Confident | State what the platform does clearly. No hedging unless accuracy requires it. |
| Approachable | Avoid corporate jargon. Use plain language over compliance-world formality. |
| Calibrated | Dial humor down on liability-sensitive topics. Accuracy matters here. |
| Outcome-first | Lead with what the user stops doing (manual review, chasing vendors) then explain how. |

### 2.1 Tagline

**"Third-party risk management powered by AI"**

Use for brand-level positioning. Do not force it into every piece.

### 2.2 Brand voice in practice

Do this:
- "TrustLayer collects, verifies, and stores COIs automatically — no manual follow-up required."
- "Vendors submit documents directly. No account creation, no sign-in, no friction on their end."
- "The platform flags missing or non-compliant coverage before it becomes your problem."

Not this:
- "TrustLayer is innovative, cutting-edge insurance software." — wrong category; banned terms
- "Our comprehensive, seamless compliance ecosystem..." — anti-slop violations
- "TrustLayer guarantees your vendors are compliant." — accuracy violation; see §4
- "TrustLayer issues and underwrites vendor insurance." — factual error; banned claim

### 2.3 Sentence and paragraph rules

- Paragraphs: 2–4 lines maximum. One idea per sentence.
- First sentence of every section directly answers the heading.
- No em dashes. Restructure or use a period.
- Lead with outcomes; follow with mechanism.
- Do not start sections or paragraphs with a hyperlink.

### 2.4 Humor and personality

TrustLayer uses phrases like "Squad goals," "Captain Compliance," "Risk Crunching Robots," and "Smart AI that does your busywork" in brand contexts. In content:
- Light personality is appropriate in blog posts and marketing copy
- Keep it factual on compliance specifics — humor does not override accuracy
- Do not invent brand personality phrases; adapt from confirmed brand voice or keep it straight

### 3.3 Banned phrases (throat-clearing, hedges, closers)

Delete entirely:

- "It's important to note that..." / "It's worth mentioning that..."
- "In today's [X] landscape..."
- "Let's dive/delve into..."
- "In this article, we will..."
- "To be fair..." / "To be honest..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "In terms of..."
- "In conclusion..." / "To sum up..." / "To wrap up..."
- "Feel free to reach out" / "Don't hesitate to..."
- "Moving forward" / "Going forward"
- "Due to the fact that" → because
- "In order to" → to
- "Has the ability to" → can
- "With that in mind..." / "Building on this foundation..." / "That being said..."
- "Imagine a world where..." / "Picture this:" / "Have you ever wondered..."
- "Here are X things you need to know about Y"

### 3.4 Banned structures

**The "Not just X — it's Y" pattern:** Never frame something as more than it is by negating a lesser version.
- Bad: "TrustLayer isn't just compliance software — it's a risk management revolution."
- Good: State what it is and what it does.

**Rule of three:** Vary groupings — use one, two, or four. Match the number to what you have to say.

**Synonym stacking:** Do not list near-synonyms as padding.
- Bad: "a full, complete, and thorough compliance verification process"
- Good: "a compliance verification process" (or describe what makes it thorough)

**Guarantee constructions:** Never write "TrustLayer guarantees compliance" or any variant. The platform verifies and monitors — it does not guarantee. See §4.

## 4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

### 4.1 Platform category rule (critical)

TrustLayer is a third-party risk management platform. It is NOT insurance software. Writers who describe it as "insurance software" misrepresent the product category. Every piece must hold this distinction.

**Prohibited constructions (critical — do not use):**
- "insurance software"
- "TrustLayer issues insurance"
- "TrustLayer underwrites [coverage/policies/anything]"
- "guaranteed compliance"
- "compliance guaranteed"
- "TrustLayer ensures your vendors are compliant" (use: "TrustLayer monitors vendor compliance" or "flags non-compliant coverage")

**Correct framing:**
- "third-party risk management platform"
- "compliance document management platform"
- "TrustLayer automates COI collection and verification"
- "the platform flags gaps in coverage"
- "TrustLayer monitors vendor compliance in real time"

### 4.2 What TrustLayer does NOT do

Writers must keep these boundaries clear:

- TrustLayer does NOT issue insurance policies
- TrustLayer does NOT underwrite coverage
- TrustLayer does NOT guarantee that vendors are compliant — it verifies, tracks, and alerts
- TrustLayer does NOT require vendors to create an account to submit documents (zero sign-in is a feature, not a limitation)

### 4.3 Scale and metric accuracy

- **517,000+ company network** — confirmed; use this figure as stated
- **400,000+ COIs/month** — confirmed; use this figure as stated
- **Named clients:** EL Ad, Grenadier Homes, PayneWest Insurance — only reference these names
- **G2 badge:** "High Performer" — use only this designation; do not invent G2 ratings or rankings
- **TrustScore** — proprietary metric; describe it as a risk scoring tool, not as an insurance rating

Do not invent coverage limits, pricing tiers, feature specifications, or additional client names.

### 4.4 COI terminology

- Always spell out "certificate of insurance (COI)" on first use in a piece
- After first use, "COI" is acceptable
- "COIs" (plural) — acceptable
- Do not confuse a COI with an insurance policy. A COI is proof-of-insurance documentation — it does not confer coverage.

### 4.5 Vendor submission experience

Zero vendor sign-in is a confirmed and key differentiator. Vendors submit compliance documents directly without creating an account. Writers may state this as a fact. Do not qualify it or hedge it.

## 5. Blog Post Rules

### 5.1 Opening hook (first 3–5 lines must)

1. Identify the specific problem or situation the reader is dealing with
2. Make the reader feel understood — not lectured
3. Transition to what this piece will help them do or decide

Do not open with: a definition of COIs, a generic statement about compliance being hard, a rhetorical question, or a background-statistics paragraph.

### 5.2 Blog structure

- Up to 8 H2 sections
- H3 only when necessary; if used, include a short bridge sentence between the H2 heading and first H3
- No H4
- Capitalize headings per AP style
- No section over 300 words
- At least one table or structured list where the topic supports it
- 2–3 lists per article

### 5.3 Blog CTA requirements

Every blog post ends with a CTA directed toward the reader's next step with TrustLayer.

- Use the confirmed CTA elements from contact-block.md
- Tone: direct and practical — not a hard sell
- Do not end a post with "learn more" or an open-ended "contact us" without a specific action

See §5 for full rules. Summary:

## 7. SEO Standards

**Before writing any piece:** Run a real SERP analysis. Do not simulate it. Steps:
1. Access the internet and search the primary keyword (language: English, location: United States)
2. Analyze the top 5 ranking pages for: search intent, LSI terms, content format, subtopics, micro-queries, content gaps
3. Identify which gaps deserve full sections vs. brief paragraphs
4. Present findings before proceeding to the outline

**Meta deliverables (provide before writing the introduction):**
- 3 meta title options: 55–60 characters, include primary keyword, drive clicks
- 3 meta description options: 130–150 characters, include primary keyword, state a benefit or outcome
- 3 H1 title options: SEO-oriented, CTR-driven

**Outline requirements:**
- Build only after SERP analysis
- Cover all critical topics from competitor pages; expand beyond their depth where the content supports it
- Include 2–3 micro-queries under each H2 (internal structure only — not visible in output)
- 1 table per article where the topic supports comparison or structured data
- 2–3 lists per article

**Keyword placement:**
- Primary keyword in the first sentence
- Use semantic variants in headings; avoid repeating the exact keyword phrase in every H2
- "Certificate of insurance" and "COI" are both valid keyword targets — use naturally
