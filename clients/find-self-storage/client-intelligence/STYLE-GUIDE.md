## 1. Brand & Audience

FindSelfStorage.com is a self-storage comparison marketplace and editorial resource. It aggregates 10,000+ storage facilities nationwide. Users search, compare, and reserve storage units online — no credit card required, no booking fee.

**Critical platform fact:** FindSelfStorage.com is a marketplace aggregator. It does not own, operate, or manage any storage facility. Every piece of content must maintain this distinction. See §4 for enforcement rules.

### Primary audience

FindSelfStorage.com serves a single consumer audience. There is no B2B content tier (unlike sister marketplace platforms). Identify the specific consumer situation before writing.

**Audience segments:**
- Individuals and families needing extra space (seasonal overflow, life transitions)
- Renters and homeowners between moves or during renovations
- College students seeking short-term or summer storage
- Retirees downsizing
- Small business owners storing inventory, equipment, or records
- RV, boat, motorcycle, and vehicle owners who need off-property storage
- People facing HOA or municipal restrictions on residential vehicle parking

### Mission framing (client-stated)

Simplify the storage rental process by offering transparent pricing, flexible options, and convenient online booking — helping customers save time and money while finding the right storage space near them.

## 2. Voice & Tone

**Client-stated register:** Friendly, clear, and trustworthy. Focused on making storage simple, affordable, and stress-free. Practical and helpful language.

**What this means in practice:** Write as a knowledgeable, reassuring guide — not a salesperson. Lead with what the reader needs to do or know. Keep language concrete and direct. Avoid urgency tactics, hype, and filler.

**Current site copy is NOT the voice model.** The existing website copy is functional but stiff, keyword-stuffed, and impersonal. New content should be warmer and more direct while staying practical. See §4 for the accuracy constraints that must hold regardless of register.

### 3.3 Banned phrases (throat-clearing, hedges, closers)

Delete entirely:

- "In order to" → to
- "Has the ability to" → can
- "Moving forward" / "Going forward"
- "Imagine a world where..." / "Picture this:" / "Have you ever wondered..."
- "Here are X things you need to know about Y"

### 3.4 Banned structures

**Ownership-implying constructions:** Any sentence structured as "our [storage noun]" is banned. This includes "our facilities," "our units," "our storage options," "our climate-controlled storage," "our drive-up units." The platform does not own any storage — it is a marketplace. See §4.1 for the full enforcement rule.

**The "Not just X — it's Y" pattern:** Never frame something as more than it is by negating a lesser version.
- Bad: "FindSelfStorage.com isn't just a search tool — it's your complete storage resource."
- Good: State what it is and what it does.

**Rule of three:** AI defaults to grouping in threes. Vary it — use one, two, or four. Match the number to what you actually have to say.

**Synonym stacking:** Do not list near-synonyms as padding.
- Bad: "a full, complete, and thorough comparison of available facilities"
- Good: "a side-by-side comparison of available facilities" (or describe the specific attributes compared)

### 3.5 Tone and rhythm tells

- **Uniform sentence length:** Mix short and long. Let some run. Cut others hard.
- **Participial phrases:** "Leveraging its nationwide network, the platform..." is an AI fingerprint. Restructure or state the result directly.
- **"Ensuring" clauses:** "...ensuring you find the best price." Trailing clauses are filler. If the claim matters, give it its own sentence.
- **"Paramount" and "cannot be overstated":** Lifted from current site copy — do not repeat. These are filler intensifiers dressed as urgency.

## 4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

### 4.1 Marketplace ownership rule (critical)

FindSelfStorage.com is a marketplace aggregator. It does not own, operate, or manage any storage facility. Writers who blur this line create false advertising exposure.

**Prohibited constructions — do not use:**
- "our storage facilities" / "our facilities"
- "our storage units" / "our units"
- "our climate-controlled storage"
- "our drive-up units"
- "FindSelfStorage.com's facilities"
- Any sentence implying the platform stores belongings or operates physical locations

**Correct framing:**
- "facilities in the network"
- "storage facilities available through FindSelfStorage.com"
- "facilities listed on the platform"
- "storage units available in your area" (when combined with search context)
- "find climate-controlled facilities near [city]"

### 4.2 Pricing accuracy

The price ranges published on FindSelfStorage.com category pages ($30–$300+/month depending on type) are editorial estimates — not live data from facility listings. No sourcing methodology is stated on-site.

Rules:
- Do not state specific prices or present ranges as guaranteed figures.
- Always pair pricing references with the correct caveat: pricing varies by facility, location, unit size, and availability.
- Do not imply FindSelfStorage.com sets or controls pricing — facilities set their own prices.
- Correct framing: "Compare current pricing from facilities in your area" or "monthly pricing varies by facility, location, and unit size."

### 4.3 Availability accuracy

- Do not assert that specific units are currently available.
- Correct framing: "check real-time availability" or "see current availability at facilities near you."

### 4.4 Network size claim

- **10,000+** storage facilities — the confirmed site claim; use this exact figure
- Do not inflate to "tens of thousands" or a higher number
- Do not state a specific unit count — no confirmed figure exists

## 5. Blog Post Rules

### 5.1 Opening hook pattern (first 3–5 sentences must)

1. Identify the specific situation or problem the reader is in
2. Make the reader feel understood — not lectured
3. Transition to what this piece will help them do or decide

Do not open with: a definition of self-storage, a generic statement about storage being stressful, a rhetorical question, or an industry fact.

### 5.2 Structure

- Up to 8 H2 sections
- H3 only when necessary; include a short bridge sentence between the H2 and first H3 when used
- No H4
- Capitalize headings per AP style
- No section over 300 words
- At least one table or structured comparison where the topic supports it (unit size comparisons, storage type decision matrix, RV storage option breakdown, etc.)
- 2–3 lists per article

### 5.3 CTA requirements

Every blog post ends with a CTA directing the reader to search and reserve on FindSelfStorage.com.

- Direct the reader to search by city or ZIP code
- Mention the free online reservation (no credit card, no obligation)
- Mention the ability to compare pricing and availability
- Tone: helpful and practical — not a hard sell
- Do not end with a generic "contact us" or open-ended "learn more"

## 6. Page Structure

### 6.1 Storage type / category landing pages

These pages target a specific storage type (climate-controlled, drive-up, business, boat, RV, etc.). They are functional, not editorial.

Structure:
1. H1: primary keyword (e.g., "Climate-Controlled Storage Units")
2. Short intro: what this storage type is and who needs it — 2–3 sentences max
3. Use cases for this storage type (who benefits, what they store)
4. What to look for when choosing this type
5. Unit size guidance relevant to this type (link to or embed a brief size table)
6. Inline FAQ — embed relevant FAQ questions on the page
7. CTA: search/compare/reserve — free online reservation

Rules:
- Do not invent specific facility names, addresses, pricing, or availability
- Do not make claims about the number of facilities offering a specific type unless confirmed
- Do not use "our facilities" (see §4.1)

### 6.2 Blog posts

See §5 for full rules. Summary:
- Up to 8 H2 sections; no H4
- Opening hook: situation → reader feels understood → transition
- 300 words per section max
- End with a platform CTA (search, compare, reserve)

### 6.3 City/state/location landing pages

Structure:
1. H1: primary keyword (e.g., "Self Storage in [City], [State]")
2. Short intro: what the user can do here (search, compare, reserve) — 2–3 sentences max
3. Overview of storage options typically available in this area
4. Why comparing on FindSelfStorage.com is useful (platform value props)
5. Unit size guide section or brief size table
6. CTA: search/compare/reserve — free online reservation

Rules:
- Lead with the location keyword in the H1 and first sentence
- Do not invent specific facility names, addresses, pricing, or availability for that city
- Do not claim how many facilities are in a specific city unless confirmed

## 7. SEO Standards

**Before writing any piece:** Run a real SERP analysis. Do not simulate it.

Steps:
1. Search the primary keyword (language: English, location: United States)
2. Analyze top 5 ranking pages: search intent, LSI terms, content format, subtopics, micro-queries, content gaps
3. Identify gaps that deserve full sections vs. brief paragraphs
4. Present findings before proceeding to the outline

**Meta deliverables (provide before writing the introduction):**
- 3 meta title options: 55–60 characters, include primary keyword, drive clicks
- 3 meta description options: 130–150 characters, include primary keyword, state a benefit or utility, highlight a platform advantage (compare, free reservation, 10,000+ facilities)
- 3 H1 title options: SEO-oriented, CTR-driven

**Outline requirements:**
- Build only after SERP analysis
- Cover critical topics from competitor pages; expand beyond their depth where the content supports it
- Mark where coverage matches competitors vs. where it adds new value
- Include 2–3 micro-queries under each H2 (internal structure — not visible in output)
- Each paragraph answers one micro-query
- 1 table per article where the topic supports comparison or structured data
- 2–3 lists per article

**Keyword placement:**
- Primary keyword in the first sentence
- Use location keywords naturally in city/state page content — do not force them into every paragraph
- Use semantic variants in blog posts; avoid repeating the exact keyword phrase in every heading
