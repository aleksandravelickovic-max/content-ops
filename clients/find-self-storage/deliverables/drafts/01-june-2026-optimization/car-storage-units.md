## Types of Car Storage Units

The three main types of car storage offer different levels of protection at different price points. Your choice should match your vehicle's value, storage duration, and local climate.

### Enclosed (Indoor) Car Storage

Fully enclosed units work like a private garage. The vehicle sits inside a sealed drive-up unit with a roll-up door, protected from rain, hail, UV rays, dust, rodents, and temperature extremes. This is the highest-protection option and the right choice for anything valuable or stored long-term.

**Best for:** Classic and collector cars, luxury vehicles, winter storage in cold climates, and any vehicle where paint, interior, or mechanical condition matters.

| Unit Size | Dimensions | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Small | 10' x 10' to 10' x 15' | Motorcycles, compact cars, sports cars |
| Medium | 10' x 20' | Sedans, SUVs, minivans |
| Large | 10' x 25' to 10' x 30' | Full-size pickups, cargo vans, or one car plus extra items |

**Sizing note:** If your vehicle has a lift kit, roof rack, or oversize tires, measure actual vehicle height and compare against both the unit's interior clearance and door opening height, as these are often different measurements.

### Covered Car Storage

Covered storage parks your vehicle under a standalone canopy roof with open sides. You get overhead protection from rain, hail, and direct sun without the fully enclosed unit cost.

**Best for:** Daily drivers or lightly used vehicles where budget matters more than maximum protection.

**Limitations:** Open sides mean limited protection from rodents, temperature extremes, wind-driven rain, or flooding. Typically secured with perimeter fencing and gated entry.

### Outdoor Car Storage

A secured, open parking lot with perimeter fencing and surveillance. No weather protection, but access is easy and pricing is the lowest of the three options.

**Best for:** RVs, trailers, and vehicles built for outdoor exposure. Also practical for short-term storage in mild climates.

## Should You Look for Climate Controlled Car Storage?

Climate controlled car storage (also called temperature-controlled or heated/cooled storage) keeps the interior of the unit at a stable temperature year-round, typically between 55 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit.

Not all facilities offer this for vehicles, and availability varies by location. When searching for car storage, check individual facility listings to see whether this option is available near you.

It is worth seeking out if any of the following apply:

- You're in a region with extreme heat, hard freezes, or high year-round humidity
- Storage duration is 3 months or longer
- The vehicle has leather upholstery, sensitive electronics, or significant collector value
- You're concerned about battery discharge in freezing temperatures or fluid thickening in extreme cold

What climate control protects against:

- Paint oxidation and cracking from heat spikes
- Rubber seal deterioration from UV and temperature cycling
- Mold and condensation buildup from humidity
- Battery failure in freezing conditions
- Fluid thickening (oil, brake fluid, coolant) in extreme cold

For a modern daily driver stored short-term in a mild climate, covered or standard enclosed storage is usually adequate. For anything valuable, long-term, or in a harsh climate, climate control is worth checking availability for when you compare facilities.

## Car Storage Security Features to Look For

When comparing facilities, look for these security features before committing to a unit:

- 24/7 video surveillance with recorded footage

- On-site management or regular staffed hours

Ask facilities specifically about overnight, weekend, and holiday security protocols, not just what is listed on the website.

## How to Prepare Your Car for Long-Term Storage

Stored cars develop predictable problems — dead batteries, flat-spotted tires, and degraded fuel that gums up injectors and carburetors. Taking a few hours before storage prevents most of them.

**Change the oil and oil filter:** Used engine oil contains acidic byproducts that cause internal corrosion when left sitting. Change the oil immediately before storage and do not defer to the next regular interval.

**Drain and replace the cooling system:** Use a 50/50 antifreeze-to-water mix rated for your climate's lowest expected temperature. During summer months, it's common to run on straight water, so switch to the proper mix before winter storage.

**Top off transmission, steering, and differential fluids:** Full fluid reservoirs reduce oxidation and confirm proper lubrication when the vehicle is restarted after a long idle period. Always use the fluid types specified in your owner's manual.

**Check and top off brake fluid:** A dry brake system is a safety hazard. Top off the reservoir before storage and inspect for any existing leaks.

**Fill the fuel tank and add fuel stabilizer:** A full tank prevents moisture and condensation inside the fuel system. Add a fuel stabilizer (such as Sta-Bil) for any storage period over 30 days to prevent fuel degradation and varnish buildup in injectors and carburetors.

**Inflate tires to the correct PSI:** Tires develop flat spots under prolonged static load. Inflate to the vehicle manufacturer's specification, or slightly above. For storage exceeding six months, consider tire cradles or flat-spot prevention stands.

**Wash, wax, and cover the exterior:** Dirt and contaminants accelerate paint oxidation. Wash the vehicle, apply a coat of wax, and treat rubber trim with a UV protectant. Inside an enclosed unit, use a breathable fabric car cover. Avoid non-breathable plastic covers, which trap condensation against the paint.

**Clean the interior thoroughly:** Vacuum the carpets and seats, wipe down all surfaces, and remove all food, beverages, and organic materials. Organic matter attracts pests and causes mold and odor during storage.

**Disconnect the battery or connect a maintainer:** Disconnect the negative terminal to prevent phantom drain from the clock, alarm system, and onboard electronics. For long-term storage, a battery maintainer (trickle charger) is the better option, as it keeps the battery at proper charge without overcharging. Clean terminal connectors with a baking soda and water solution before reinstalling.

**Seal the exhaust and air intake:** Use a clean rag, foam plug, or plastic bag (secured with a rubber band) to cover the exhaust tip and air intake opening. This prevents rodents and insects from nesting inside. Mark or tag these covers clearly so you do not start the vehicle without removing them first.

**Run the vehicle periodically:** If your facility permits it, return every two weeks to start the engine and run it for at least 15 to 20 minutes. Drive slowly within the facility if possible to prevent tire flat-spotting and keep all lubricated components in good condition. Reconnect the battery and remove exhaust plugs each time before starting.

## Car Storage FAQs

**How much does it cost to store a car per month?**
Expect to pay $25 to $75 per month for outdoor lot storage, $50 to $150 for covered parking, and $75 to $400 or more for enclosed units depending on size and whether climate control is available. Prices vary significantly by location, with urban markets running higher than suburban or rural areas.

**What size storage unit do I need for a car?**
A 10' x 15' unit fits most compact cars and sports cars. A 10' x 20' accommodates sedans, crossovers, and minivans. Full-size pickups and vans typically require a 10' x 25' or 10' x 30' unit. Always measure your vehicle's height and verify it clears the facility's door opening before renting.

**Can I store a car in a regular self storage unit?**
Yes. Many standard drive-up self storage units are large enough for a vehicle. You'll need at least a 10' x 15' for compact cars and a 10' x 20' or larger for full-size vehicles. Confirm with the facility that vehicle storage is permitted and that door clearance matches your car's height.

**Is climate controlled storage necessary for a car?**
It depends on the vehicle and the storage duration. Climate control is worth seeking for classic or collector cars, storage of 3 or more months, and regions with extreme temperatures or high humidity. It protects paint, rubber seals, electronics, and fluids from damage. Availability varies by facility, so check listings near you to see which locations offer this option.

**What is the difference between covered and enclosed car storage?**
Enclosed storage places your car inside a fully sealed drive-up unit with a roll-up door. It provides complete protection from weather, pests, dust, and temperature extremes. Covered storage parks your vehicle under a standalone roof with open sides. It protects from direct rain and sun but not from rodents, flooding, or temperature extremes.

**How do I prepare my car for long-term storage?**
Key steps: change the oil, fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer, top off all fluids, inflate tires, disconnect the battery or connect a maintainer, wash and wax the exterior, clean the interior, and plug the exhaust and air intake. Run the engine for 15 to 20 minutes every two weeks when possible.
