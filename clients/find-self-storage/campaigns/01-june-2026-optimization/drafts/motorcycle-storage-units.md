## Types of Motorcycle Storage Units

### Enclosed (Indoor) Motorcycle Storage

Fully enclosed storage places your motorcycle inside a lockable drive-up unit with a roll-up door. This is the highest-protection option, keeping the bike safe from weather, dust, pests, and temperature extremes.

Best for: Winter storage in cold or wet climates, classic and vintage bikes, and any motorcycle where paint, chrome, or mechanical condition matters.

Enclosed units are sized in the same range as standard self storage. A 5' x 10' unit comfortably fits most motorcycles with room for a helmet and riding gear. A 10' x 10' gives additional space for tools or maintenance work alongside the bike.

### Covered Motorcycle Storage

Covered storage parks your motorcycle under a roof structure with open sides. You get protection from rain, hail, and direct sun without the cost of a fully enclosed unit.

Best for: Riders in mild climates who want basic weather protection and flexible, frequent access at a lower price point.

Limitations: Open sides mean limited protection from wind-driven rain, temperature extremes, and pests.

### Outdoor Motorcycle Storage

An open, secured lot with perimeter fencing and surveillance cameras. No weather protection, but access is typically easy and pricing is lowest.

Best for: Short-term storage in mild climates, or as a budget option for a bike with a quality weatherproof motorcycle cover.

## Do You Need Climate Controlled Motorcycle Storage?

Climate control typically maintains a consistent temperature inside the unit of between 55 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit. It is not required for most motorcycles, but is worth considering if:

- You're in a region with extreme cold (below freezing) or intense summer heat
- The motorcycle has leather seats, custom paintwork, or chrome components that are vulnerable to temperature cycling
- Storage duration is 3 months or longer
- You own a vintage or collector bike with significant value

For most modern bikes stored through a standard winter in a moderate climate, an enclosed non-climate-controlled unit is adequate if the bike is properly prepared for storage. Availability of climate controlled motorcycle storage varies by facility so it is best to check individual listings near you to see what's offered.

## How to Prepare Your Motorcycle for Storage

Neglected bikes develop problems fast: carburetors seize from varnished fuel, batteries drain completely and won't hold a charge, tires develop flat spots under static weight for months, and chains corrode into stiff, rust-spotted links. The steps below prevent all of that.

**Take a final ride to warm up the engine:** Before preparing for storage, ride the motorcycle for at least 30 minutes to bring the engine up to full operating temperature. This burns off moisture from the engine internals and helps circulate fresh fluids.

**Add fuel stabilizer and fill the tank:** Add a fuel stabilizer to the gas tank, then fill the tank completely. Run the engine for 10 minutes to circulate the stabilizer through the fuel system. A full tank minimizes air space and prevents moisture condensation inside the tank. This step is essential for any storage period over 30 days.

**Change the oil if it is due:** If an oil change is approaching, do it now while the engine is hot. Old oil contains acidic byproducts that cause corrosion when left sitting. Fresh oil during storage is significantly better for engine internals than oil that has been in the engine for thousands of miles.

**Wash and wax the motorcycle:** Wash the entire motorcycle to remove dirt, bugs, road salt, and grime. Apply a coat of wax to painted surfaces and a metal protectant or light oil to chrome and bare metal parts. Clean and treat leather seats with a conditioner to prevent cracking.

**Lubricate the chain:** If your motorcycle has a chain drive, clean and lubricate it thoroughly before storage. A properly lubricated chain resists rust and corrosion far better than a dry one sitting for months.

**Check and inflate tires:** Inflate tires to the manufacturer's maximum recommended PSI. Tires lose pressure slowly during storage and can develop flat spots under prolonged static weight. Parking the bike on a paddock stand keeps weight off the tires entirely and is the best option for long-term storage.

**Disconnect the battery or connect a maintainer:** Disconnect the negative terminal to prevent phantom drain from electronics. For storage over 30 days, connect a motorcycle battery maintainer (not a basic trickle charger) to keep the battery at full charge without overcharging. Store the battery in a cool, dry place if removing it from the bike.

**Cover the exhaust and air intake:** Use a rag, foam plug, or purpose-made cover to seal the exhaust pipe and air intake opening. This prevents moisture and pests from entering the engine. Mark these covers visibly so you remember to remove them before starting.

**Cover the motorcycle:** Use a breathable motorcycle-specific cover. Avoid non-breathable plastic tarps, which trap condensation and promote rust. A good motorcycle cover keeps dust off the bike while allowing moisture to escape.

## Getting Your Motorcycle Ready to Ride After Storage

Before your first ride of the season, take these steps:

- Reconnect the battery and check for corrosion on the terminals
- Remove exhaust and intake covers
- Check all fluid levels (coolant, brake fluid, clutch fluid) and top off if needed
- Check tire pressure and inspect for cracking or flat spots
- Check the chain lubrication
- Start the engine and let it idle for several minutes
- Take a short, slow test ride before going on a long trip

## Motorcycle Storage FAQs

**How much does motorcycle storage cost per month?**
Costs typically range from $20 to $60 per month for outdoor lot storage, $40 to $100 for covered or enclosed 5' x 10' units, and $75 to $200 or more for larger enclosed or climate controlled units. Motorcycle storage is one of the most affordable vehicle storage options because bikes fit in small units.

**What size storage unit do I need for a motorcycle?**
Most motorcycles fit comfortably in a 5' x 10' drive-up unit. If you want space for tools, gear, or maintenance work alongside the bike, a 10' x 10' unit is more comfortable. Two bikes typically fit in a 10' x 10' to 10' x 15' unit.

**Do I need climate controlled storage for a motorcycle?**
Not for most bikes in moderate climates. A standard enclosed drive-up unit provides adequate protection for typical winter storage. Climate control is worth considering for classic or collector bikes, very long storage periods, or regions with extreme cold or heat. Availability varies by facility.

**Can I store a motorcycle outside?**
Yes, in a secured outdoor lot with a quality weatherproof motorcycle cover. Outdoor storage is the least expensive option and works well for mild-climate short-term storage. For winter storage or valuable bikes, enclosed storage is a better investment.

**What is the best way to store a motorcycle for winter?**
Fill the tank and add fuel stabilizer, change the oil if due, wash and wax the bike, lubricate the chain, inflate the tires, disconnect or maintain the battery, cover the exhaust and intake, and use a breathable motorcycle cover. Store in an enclosed or covered unit to protect against moisture and temperature extremes.
