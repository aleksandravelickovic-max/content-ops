## Who Needs RV Storage?

RV storage is a practical solution for a wide range of owners:

- Seasonal travelers: Motorhomes and campers parked for months at a time between trips
- HOA-restricted homeowners: Communities that prohibit RVs, trailers, and oversized vehicles in driveways or on streets

- RV owners protecting their investment: Keeping a vehicle valued at tens of thousands of dollars secure and protected from weather

## Types of RV Storage Units

RV storage comes in three main configurations. The right choice depends on your RV's size, how long you're storing, your budget, and how important weather protection is to you.

### Enclosed (Indoor) RV Storage

Enclosed RV storage places your vehicle inside a dedicated space with walls and a door. They are either individual drive-up units with a roll-up door, or spaces inside a larger enclosed building shared with other RVs.

Individual enclosed units give you a private space similar to a large garage. Your vehicle is the only one in the unit, eliminating the risk of accidental damage from neighboring vehicles. These typically accommodate smaller Class B and Class C motorhomes, travel trailers, and fifth-wheels.

Enclosed garage buildings have one or two entrances with individual marked parking spaces inside. Multiple owners share the same building but each RV has its own designated spot. Large buildings can accommodate Class A motorhomes depending on ceiling height. You gain full weather protection but share the space with other vehicle owners.

Best for: Long-term storage, Class A and B motorhomes, and owners who want maximum protection from weather, pests, and theft.

### Covered RV Storage

Covered storage parks your RV under a roof structure with open sides. It protects against direct rain, hail, snow accumulation, and UV damage without the cost of a fully enclosed option.

Best for: Owners on a moderate budget who want meaningful weather protection. A good middle-ground for Class C and travel trailers in most climates.

Limitations: Open sides mean wind-driven rain, temperature extremes, and pests can still reach the vehicle. Most covered facilities use perimeter fencing and gate access for security.

### Outdoor (Uncovered) RV Storage

An open, secured lot with fencing and surveillance. No weather protection, but pricing is lowest and access tends to be most flexible.

Best for: Budget-conscious owners, RVs with heavy-duty exterior covers, or short-term parking when weather risk is low.

Note: If choosing outdoor storage, a quality RV cover is a worthwhile investment to protect the roof, seals, and exterior from UV damage and precipitation.

## Do You Need Climate Controlled RV Storage?

Climate controlled RV storage maintains a stable interior temperature year-round — typically somewhere in the 55–85°F range — regardless of outside conditions. It is not standard for RV storage, and availability varies considerably by facility and region. When you search listings on FindSelfStorage.com, check individual facility pages to see whether climate control is offered.

For many RV owners, it is not necessary. Short-term storage in a mild climate, or covered storage with a good RV cover, is usually sufficient. But there are situations where climate control is worth seeking out.

It matters most when:

- You are storing in a region with extreme heat or cold
- The storage period will run three months or longer
- Your RV has components sensitive to temperature swings — leather upholstery, rubber roof seals, electronics, or a lithium battery bank

What climate control actually protects against:

- **Rubber seal deterioration**: Heat accelerates cracking in roof seams and door gaskets
- **Fiberglass oxidation**: Prolonged sun and heat exposure dulls and pits the exterior
- **Battery failure**: Freezing temperatures permanently reduce battery capacity, especially in AGM and lithium cells
- **Mold and mildew**: High-humidity regions without temperature regulation create conditions for interior growth even in a closed RV

For short-term storage or storage in temperate climates, a covered or enclosed unit without climate control is typically sufficient and costs less. For long-term storage in climates with hard winters, intense summer heat, or high humidity, filtering for climate controlled options when comparing facilities on FindSelfStorage.com is worthwhile.

## Security Features to Look For in RV Storage

RVs represent a significant financial investment. Before committing to a facility, verify these security measures:

- Perimeter fencing with controlled gate access (PIN or key fob)
- 24/7 HD video surveillance covering the entire lot and access points
- On-site management or regular staff patrols during access hours
- Well-lit access areas to deter theft and vandalism
- Individual unit alarms for enclosed options
- Drive-through or pull-through spaces so you can maneuver safely without tight reversing

Ask about overnight and weekend security specifically. A facility with daytime management only offers very different protection than one with 24/7 monitoring.

## How to Prepare Your RV for Storage

Skip the prep steps and the bill arrives later — blown water lines in January, seized slide mechanisms that need a shop visit, or a rodent nest discovered in the engine bay after six months in a lot. The work you do before parking saves far more time and money than it costs.

**Clean the exterior thoroughly:** Wash the entire exterior including the roof. Remove all bugs, road grime, and mildew before storage. Apply a UV protectant or wax to fiberglass and painted surfaces. Inspect and treat any rubber roof seams or seals with appropriate conditioner to prevent cracking.

**Clean and empty the interior:** Remove all food, beverages, and organic materials. Wipe down all surfaces, vacuum carpets, and clean upholstery. Leave cabinet doors and compartments slightly open to allow air circulation and prevent mildew.

**Drain and flush all water systems:** Drain the fresh water tank, water heater, and all water lines. If storing over winter in a cold climate, use RV antifreeze (propylene glycol, not automotive antifreeze) to protect all lines and the water pump from freezing. Drain and flush the black and grey tanks as well.

**Stabilize the fuel:** Fill the fuel tank to at least 95% to minimize condensation buildup. Add a fuel stabilizer and run the engine for 15 minutes to circulate it through the fuel system. This prevents varnish and deposits from forming in carburetors and injectors.

**Change the engine oil and check fluid levels:** Old oil is acidic and can cause corrosion during long storage periods. Change the oil and filter before parking. Top off coolant, brake fluid, and power steering fluid.

**Disconnect or maintain the battery:** Disconnect the house batteries and chassis battery to prevent phantom drain. For long-term storage, connect a battery maintainer (not a basic trickle charger) to keep batteries at full charge without overcharging.

**Protect the tires:** Inflate tires to the maximum recommended PSI. Park on a hard, level surface rather than grass or dirt to prevent flat spots and reduce moisture wicking into tire sidewalls. Use tire covers to protect against UV degradation. For storage exceeding 3 months, place boards or tire cradles under each tire to reduce flat-spotting.

**Cover or seal all openings:** Seal the exhaust pipe, air intake, and any exterior vents to prevent rodents and insects from nesting inside. Use foam plugs, mesh covers, or purpose-made vent covers. Place rodent deterrents (steel wool, peppermint sachets, or commercial repellents) inside compartments.

**Retract slide-outs and awnings:** Store the RV with all slide-outs fully retracted to reduce stress on the seals. Retract awnings fully and secure them. Extended awnings are vulnerable to wind damage and can collect debris that leads to mold.

**Check and address the propane:** Close the main valve at the tank before moving the RV into any enclosed or covered space. Propane is heavier than air — even a small leak in an enclosed structure creates an accumulation risk that can ignite from a single spark. Note the fuel level before storage so you are not caught short on the next trip. For storage lasting three months or more, consider removing the tanks entirely and storing them outdoors or in a well-ventilated area rather than leaving them attached. Never store an RV with propane appliances left in pilot or standing flame mode — this applies to furnaces, water heaters, and refrigerators.

## RV Storage FAQs

**How much does it cost to store an RV per month?**
Expect to pay $50 to $125 for outdoor lot storage, $100 to $250 for covered parking, and $150 to $450 or more for enclosed storage. Prices vary by RV size, location, and facility. Class A motorhomes at 40'+ cost significantly more to store than smaller travel trailers.

**What size storage space do I need for my RV?**
Measure your RV's total length including any tow hitch or front overhang, then add 3 to 5 feet of clearance on each end. Most travel trailers and Class C motorhomes fit in a 10' x 30' to 10' x 40' space. Large Class A coaches often require 10' x 45' or more. Always confirm ceiling height and door clearance with the facility.

**Can I store an RV in a regular self storage unit?**
Smaller Class B camper vans and compact travel trailers can sometimes fit in large drive-up self storage units. Class A and larger Class C motorhomes typically need a dedicated vehicle storage lot or large enclosed building. Measure carefully and confirm the facility accepts RVs before booking.

**Is covered or enclosed RV storage worth the extra cost?**
For long-term storage (3 months or more) or in regions with harsh winters, hail, or intense UV, covered or enclosed storage protects the roof seals, fiberglass, rubber trim, and interior from accelerated deterioration. For short-term or mild-climate storage, outdoor lots are often sufficient with a quality RV cover.

**How do I prepare my RV for long-term storage?**
Key steps: clean and dry the interior and exterior, drain and winterize all water systems, stabilize the fuel, change the engine oil, disconnect or maintain the batteries, cover exhaust and vents to block pests, retract slide-outs and awnings, and protect tires from flat-spotting and UV damage.

**Are there HOA or municipal restrictions on parking an RV at home?**
Many HOAs and municipalities restrict or prohibit parking RVs in driveways, on streets, or in front yards. Restrictions vary by location. If you are unsure about your local rules, check with your HOA or local planning department. Self storage facilities offer a compliant, secure alternative.
