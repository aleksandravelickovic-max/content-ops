## Who Needs Boat Storage?

Boat storage is a practical solution for a wide range of watercraft owners:

- **Seasonal boaters:** sailboats, fishing boats, and ski boats stored during winter months
- **Owners without driveway space:** boats and trailers that are too large to park at home
- **HOA-restricted homeowners:** communities that prohibit boat and trailer parking on residential lots
- **Saltwater boaters:** boats that benefit from enclosed protection between outings to prevent corrosion
- **Cabin cruiser and pontoon owners:** larger vessels that require dedicated sized spaces
- **Jet ski and PWC owners:** personal watercraft stored away from direct weather exposure

## Types of Boat Storage Units

Boat storage comes in four main configurations. The right choice depends on your boat's size, value, how often you use it, and your budget.

### Enclosed (Indoor) Boat Storage

Enclosed boat storage places your vessel inside a sealed drive-up unit with a roll-up door, similar to a private garage. It provides the highest level of protection from weather, UV damage, dust, and pests.

**Best for:** Boats stored for a full season, high-value watercraft, saltwater boats that benefit from drying out in a sealed environment, and boats with sensitive electronics or upholstery.

To use an enclosed unit, your boat and trailer must fit within the unit dimensions. Measure your boat's overall length on the trailer (including the hitch tongue), width, and height (including the motor if tilted) before comparing unit sizes.

### Covered Boat Storage

Covered storage parks your boat under a roof structure with open sides. You get protection from rain, hail, and direct sun without the cost of a fully enclosed unit.

**Best for:** Boaters who use their vessel regularly and want some weather protection between outings without paying for full enclosure. A good middle-ground option for mild climates.

**Limitations:** Limited protection from wind-driven rain, humidity, rodents, or temperature extremes. Most covered facilities use perimeter fencing and gated access for security.

### Outdoor (Open) Boat Storage

Outdoor storage is a secured, open-air lot with fencing and surveillance. No weather protection, but pricing is the most affordable option and access is typically flexible.

**Best for:** Boat owners on a tight budget, boats already designed for outdoor exposure, or short-term storage in mild-weather climates where precipitation is low.

If choosing outdoor storage, a quality boat cover is a worthwhile investment to protect the hull, cockpit, and motor from UV rays and moisture between uses.

### Dry Stack Boat Storage

Dry stack (also called stack storage or rack storage) is a specialized system where a marina or facility uses large forklift-style equipment to store boats on steel racks, stacked several units high inside a large covered or enclosed building. When you want to use your boat, staff retrieve it with the forklift and launch it for you.

**Best for:** Smaller to mid-size powerboats in coastal or lakefront markets. Ideal for boats stored for weeks or months at a time. Not practical for boaters who want spontaneous, self-service access.

**Advantages:** Excellent protection, efficient use of space, and many dry stack facilities are waterfront-adjacent, making launch and retrieval easy.

**Limitations:** Access requires advance notice and is dependent on facility staffing hours. Not suitable for boats over a certain size or weight. Less common inland.

## What Size Storage Unit Do You Need for a Boat?

Boat storage sizing is based on your vessel's total length on the trailer, not just the hull length. Always measure the complete rig: bow to stern, width at the widest point, and total height with the motor tilted up. The following are general industry sizing guidelines so it is best to confirm exact dimensions and door clearance with each facility before booking.

| Vessel Type | Typical Overall Length on Trailer | Recommended Unit Size |
|---|---|---|
| Jet ski / PWC | 10' to 14' | 10' x 15' |
| Small fishing boat or jon boat | 14' to 18' | 10' x 20' |
| Bass boat or ski boat | 18' to 22' | 10' x 25' |
| Pontoon or deck boat | 20' to 26' | 10' x 30' |
| Cabin cruiser or larger powerboat | 24'+ | 10' x 35'+ or outdoor lot |

**Important:** Boats with T-tops, towers, radar arches, or outboard motors add height. Measure total rig height with the motor tilted up and compare it against the facility's door opening height before committing to a unit. Some facilities accommodate non-standard lengths on a case-by-case basis. Always call ahead to confirm.

## Should You Look for Climate Controlled Boat Storage?

Climate controlled boat storage maintains a stable interior temperature year-round, typically between 55 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit. Availability varies by facility, so check individual listings near you to see which locations offer this option.

It is worth seeking out if any of the following apply:

- You're storing a boat with a fiberglass hull in a region with extreme temperature swings
- The vessel has leather upholstery, wood trim, or high-end electronics
- You're in a high-humidity coastal environment where mold and mildew are a concern
- Storage duration is 3 months or longer

What climate control protects against:

- Fiberglass osmotic blistering from freeze/thaw cycles
- Gelcoat fading and cracking from heat exposure
- Mold and mildew on upholstery, canvas, and carpet
- Battery discharge and fluid degradation in extreme cold
- Corrosion on metal hardware, wiring, and electronics

For a trailerable fishing boat or ski boat stored short-term in a moderate climate, covered or standard enclosed storage is typically adequate. For a higher-value vessel or long-term seasonal storage in a harsh climate, climate control is worth checking availability for when comparing facilities.

## Boat Storage Security Features to Look For

When comparing facilities, verify these security layers before committing to a unit:

- Perimeter fencing with gated access control (PIN pad or key fob)
- 24/7 video surveillance covering the lot, access points, and storage bays
- On-site management or regular staff presence during access hours
- Adequate lighting throughout the facility
- Individual unit alarms (for enclosed units)
- Drive-up or forklift access appropriate to your storage type

For outdoor and covered lots, also check whether the facility has security patrols and whether the perimeter fencing extends fully around the property with no open access points.

## How to Prepare Your Boat for Storage

Skipping prep costs more than the storage unit itself. Corrosion works through cooling passages over a single winter, osmotic blistering can compromise a fiberglass hull after one freeze/thaw cycle, and mold in upholstery and canvas rarely comes out cleanly.

**Flush the engine with fresh water:** Particularly critical for saltwater boats. Connect a flushing attachment to the lower unit and run the engine on fresh water for at least 10 minutes. This clears salt, sand, and debris from the cooling passages before they can cause corrosion.

**Change the engine oil and filter:** Old oil contains acids and combustion byproducts that corrode engine internals during long idle periods. Change the oil and filter immediately before storage, regardless of where you are in your normal service interval.

**Fog the engine cylinders:** With the engine running, spray fogging oil into the carburetor or throttle body. This coats cylinder walls with a protective film that prevents rust during storage. Shut the engine off while fogging so the oil remains on the cylinder walls.

**Stabilize the fuel:** Fill the tank to at least 95% capacity to minimize condensation buildup. Add a marine-grade fuel stabilizer (such as Sta-Bil Marine) and run the engine for 10 minutes to circulate it through the entire fuel system. This prevents varnish deposits in carburetors and injectors.

**Flush and service the cooling system:** Drain all water from the engine block, water pump, and any water lines. In cold climates, use marine antifreeze (propylene glycol) in the cooling system to prevent freeze damage. Do not skip this step if temperatures in your area drop below freezing.

**Disconnect or maintain the battery:** Disconnect the negative terminal to prevent phantom drain. For storage over 30 days, connect a marine battery maintainer to keep the battery at full charge without overcharging. Remove the battery entirely if storing outdoors or in an unheated space.

**Clean the hull and apply protective wax:** Wash the hull thoroughly, removing all algae, barnacles (if saltwater), waterline stains, and grime. Apply a coat of marine wax to the above-waterline gelcoat to protect the finish during storage. Do not leave bottom paint exposed to air longer than necessary before re-launching.

**Clean and protect the interior:** Remove all seat cushions, canvas covers, carpeting, and any porous materials that can trap moisture and grow mold. Wipe down all surfaces, treat vinyl upholstery with a UV protectant, and leave compartment hatches slightly open to allow airflow.

**Remove all electronics and valuables:** Remove fish finders, GPS units, stereo heads, and any portable electronics. Store these separately in a climate-controlled environment. This also reduces theft risk while the boat is in storage.

**Lubricate all fittings and moving parts:** Grease the trailer wheel bearings, lube the steering cable, and apply corrosion inhibitor to all metal fittings, hinges, latches, and clamps. This prevents seizing during the storage period.

**Cover the boat properly:** Use a breathable, boat-specific cover sized for your vessel. Secure it tightly to prevent wind from lifting it. Avoid non-breathable plastic tarps, which trap condensation and accelerate mold growth. In enclosed storage, a well-fitted cover still protects against dust.

**Check and inflate trailer tires:** Trailer tires are prone to flat-spotting and UV cracking. Inflate to the maximum recommended PSI and park the trailer on blocks or stands to relieve weight from the tires if storing for 3 months or more.

## Boat Storage FAQs

**How much does it cost to store a boat per month?**
Typical monthly costs run $50 to $150 for outdoor lot storage, $100 to $250 for covered parking, $150 to $350 or more for enclosed units, and $200 to $600 or more for dry stack depending on location and boat size. Coastal markets are significantly more expensive than inland areas.

**What size storage unit do I need for a boat?**
Measure your boat and trailer as a complete unit from hitch to bow. A jet ski typically fits in a 10' x 15'. A small fishing boat needs a 10' x 20'. A pontoon or deck boat usually requires a 10' x 30'. Larger vessels may need an oversized enclosed unit or outdoor lot. Always confirm the facility's door height clears your boat's highest point before renting.

**What is dry stack boat storage?**
Dry stack storage uses large rack systems inside a covered building to stack boats vertically, maximizing storage capacity. Staff retrieve your boat by forklift when you want to use it. It offers excellent protection and is common at marinas. The trade-off is that access requires advance notice and is limited to facility staffing hours.

**Is indoor boat storage worth it?**
For boats stored over winter or for extended periods, indoor enclosed storage is worth the premium. It protects fiberglass, upholstery, electronics, and paint from UV damage, temperature extremes, and moisture. For boats used regularly year-round in mild climates, covered or outdoor storage may be sufficient.

**Can I store a boat on its trailer in a storage unit?**
Yes. Most boat storage at self storage facilities is designed for boats on their trailers. This makes drop-off and pickup easy since you simply tow the boat in and out. Measure the full rig (boat plus trailer) for length, width, and height before selecting a unit size.

**How do I prepare a boat for long-term storage?**
Key steps: flush the engine with fresh water, change the oil, fog the cylinders, stabilize the fuel, drain and antifreeze the cooling system, disconnect the battery or connect a maintainer, clean and wax the hull, remove cushions and canvas, cover with a breathable boat cover, and lubricate trailer bearings. Run the engine every few weeks if possible during long storage periods.
