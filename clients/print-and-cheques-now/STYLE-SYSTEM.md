## 2. Voice & Tone

**Register:** Practical, trustworthy, and direct. Print & Cheques Now speaks plainly to business owners and their staff. Content should feel like advice from a straightforward supplier — not a promotional pitch.

**Not:** Fluffy, promotional, enthusiastic, or hyperbolic.
**Yes:** Specific, factual, useful, reassuring.

The company's positioning is built on price transparency and compliance credentials. Content must reinforce both — not oversell them.

| Situation | Not this | Use this |
|---|---|---|

| Reference compliance | "Our cheques work everywhere!" | "Cheques meet Payments Canada standards and carry the Bank Acceptance Guarantee." |

**Sentence and paragraph rules:**
- Short paragraphs: 2–4 lines maximum.
- First sentence of every section answers the heading directly.
- No em dashes. Restructure or use a period.
- Lead with the practical outcome; follow with the mechanism.
- Do not start a paragraph with a hyperlink.

### 3.4 Banned structures

**The "Not just X — it's Y" pattern:** Never frame something as more than it is by negating a lesser version.
- Bad: "Print & Cheques Now isn't just a printer — it's a compliance partner."
- Good: State what it is and what it does.

**Rule of three:** AI defaults to grouping in threes. Vary it — use one, two, or four. Match the number to what you have to say.

**Synonym stacking:** Do not list near-synonyms as padding.
- Bad: "a full, complete, and thorough order process"
- Good: "a straightforward order process" (or describe the specific steps)

**Banking-relationship constructions:** Never imply Print & Cheques Now owns or manages banking relationships. Do not use "our bank" or any phrase suggesting the company holds banking relationships with customers. The company prints cheques compliant with Payments Canada standards — it does not bank.

### 3.5 Dual-spelling SEO rule (CRITICAL — client-specific)

This is the most important terminology rule for this client. Both spellings of the core product term must appear naturally throughout each piece of content:

- **cheque / cheques** — the Canadian/British spelling; primary spelling used throughout
- **check / checks** — the American spelling; must also appear naturally

This is **not** a consistency error. It is an intentional dual-spelling SEO strategy to capture both Canadian search traffic (which uses "cheque") and American-adjacent search traffic (which uses "check"). The content remains in Canadian English — the "check" spelling appears as a natural alternate reference or in context where American readers might search.

**Rules for implementation:**
- Do NOT write "cheque check" as a compound phrase
- Do NOT write "cheques/checks" or "cheques (checks)" as a parenthetical explanation
- DO use both spellings as natural alternate references across the piece — e.g., a paragraph might use "cheques" and a later paragraph might use "checks" in a different context
- The primary spelling is always "cheque" — "check" appears as a natural supplement, not as a replacement
- Minimum: both spellings appear at least once in every blog post or page of meaningful length

**Example of correct implementation:**
"Print & Cheques Now prints personal cheques at up to 60% more volume than your bank charges for the same order. If you've been paying bank prices for checks, the difference adds up quickly."

**Example of incorrect implementation:**
"Our cheque/check printing service..." (parenthetical compound — banned)
"cheque check printing" (compound — banned)

## 5. Blog Post Rules

### 5.1 Opening hook pattern (first 3–5 lines must)

1. Identify the specific problem or situation the reader is in
2. Make the reader feel understood — not lectured
3. Transition to what this piece will help them do or decide

Do not open with: a definition of what a cheque is, a generic statement about business costs, a rhetorical question, or a statistic about the Canadian banking industry.

### 5.2 Structure

- Up to 8 H2 sections
- H3 only when necessary; if used, include a short bridge sentence between the H2 heading and first H3
- No H4
- Capitalize headings per AP style
- No section over 300 words
- At least one table or structured comparison where the topic supports it (product comparisons, software compatibility, bank list, etc.)
- 2–3 lists per article

### 5.3 CTA requirements

Every blog post ends with a CTA directing the reader to order or get a quote from Print & Cheques Now.

- Direct the reader to chequesnow.ca or the phone number (1-866-760-2661)
- Mention the Bank Acceptance Guarantee or the volume savings where relevant
- Tone: practical and direct — not a hard sell
- See contact-block.md for confirmed CTA elements and approved phrasing

Do not end a post with a generic "contact us" or open-ended "learn more." The CTA must give the reader a clear next step.

## 7. SEO Standards

**Before writing any piece:** Run a real SERP analysis. Do not simulate it. Steps:
1. Access the internet and search the primary keyword (language: English, location: Canada)
2. Analyze the top 5 ranking pages for: search intent, LSI terms, content format patterns, subtopics, micro-queries, content gaps
3. Identify which gaps deserve full sections vs. brief paragraphs
4. Present findings before proceeding to the outline

**Meta deliverables (provide before writing the introduction):**
- 3 meta title options: 55–60 characters, include primary keyword, drive clicks
- 3 meta description options: 130–150 characters, include primary keyword, state a benefit or credential, highlight a trust signal (CPA-certified, Bank Acceptance Guarantee, volume savings)
- 3 H1 title options: SEO-oriented, CTR-driven

**Outline requirements:**
- Build only after SERP analysis
- Cover all critical topics from competitor pages; expand beyond their depth where the content supports it
- Mark where coverage matches competitors vs. where it adds new value
- Include 2–3 micro-queries under each H2 (internal structure only — not visible in output)
- Each paragraph answers one micro-query
- 1 table per article where the topic supports comparison or structured data
- 2–3 lists per article

**Keyword placement:**
- Primary keyword in the first sentence
- Use both "cheque" and "check" spellings across the piece (see §3.5)
- For blog posts: use semantic variants; avoid repeating the exact keyword phrase in every heading

**Dual-spelling keyword note:**
The dual-spelling strategy (§3.5) is an SEO requirement. Both "cheque" and "check" should appear naturally across content, including in headings where natural. Do not force awkward phrasing — let both spellings occur in different sentence contexts.
