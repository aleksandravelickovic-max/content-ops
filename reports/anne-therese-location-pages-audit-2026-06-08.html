Critical Issues — Must Fix Before Publishing
✗ Critical
"Medical-grade" used for Hydrafacial without confirmation
Affects: All 4 Hydrafacial pages (Cape Coral, Bonita Springs, Lewis Center, Gahanna)
▼

All four Hydrafacial pages contain the phrase "Hydrafacial is a medical-grade facial treatment" in the "Why [Location] Patients Choose Hydrafacial" section. The Anne Therese style system explicitly prohibits "medical-grade" without client confirmation.

"Hydrafacial is a medical-grade facial treatment that fits [location] patients who want to address surface concerns…"

Additionally, the site's own Hydrafacial treatment page does not use "medical-grade" to describe the service. The featured provider listed on the Hydrafacial page is Krystle Anderson (LME, LT — an esthetician), which makes "medical-grade" an especially problematic descriptor since the service appears to be categorized as a skincare/aesthetic service, not a medical one.

Fix Required
Replace "medical-grade facial treatment" with approved language. Preferred options per style system: "medically supervised facial treatment" or "professional Hydrafacial at a medically guided practice." Confirm correct descriptor with Wendy (CSM) before final copy. If the client does not want to use "medically supervised" for Hydrafacial (since it's esthetician-performed), simply remove the modifier and let the practice's positioning carry the authority.
✗ Critical
Cape Coral Hydrafacial footer says "CoolSculpting patients"
Affects: Page 1 — Hydrafacial, Cape Coral only
▼

The "Visit Our Cape Coral Location" footer block at the bottom of the Cape Coral Hydrafacial page contains a copy-paste error. It reads:

"Our Cape Coral location serves CoolSculpting patients in Cape Coral and surrounding communities, including Fort Myers, North Fort Myers, Pine Island, and Matlacha."

Every other location page (Bonita Springs, Lewis Center, Gahanna) correctly reads "Hydrafacial patients." This is clearly a leftover from a CoolSculpting page template. The surrounding cities also need a quick check — Fort Myers, North Fort Myers, Pine Island, and Matlacha are appropriate Southwest Florida communities for Cape Coral, so those are fine.

Fix Required
Change "CoolSculpting patients" → "Hydrafacial patients" in the Cape Coral Hydrafacial footer.
✗ Critical
Clear + Brilliant appointment duration conflicts with the live site
Affects: All 4 Clear + Brilliant pages (Cape Coral, Bonita Springs, Lewis Center, Gahanna)
▼

All four Clear + Brilliant pages state the appointment is 45–60 minutes total, with 20–30 minutes of numbing before the 15–20 minute laser portion. However, the official Clear + Brilliant treatment page on the Anne Therese website states the appointment is 30–45 minutes.

The docs' math (20–30 min numbing + 15–20 min treatment + post-treatment review) does add up to ~45–60 minutes, so the logic is sound. But the conflict with the live site means one of two things: (a) the site's stated time is outdated, or (b) the docs are overstating. Publishing content that contradicts the live site on appointment duration will confuse patients and create scheduling issues.

Fix Required
Confirm the correct appointment duration with Wendy or the client before publishing. If 45–60 minutes is accurate, the website's treatment page should also be updated for consistency. Do not publish with a discrepancy between these pages and the site.

Affects: All 12 pages
▼

Every page across all 12 contains this identical sentence in the "Why [Location] Patients Choose" section:

"At Anne Therese Aesthetic Medicine, each [service] session is supervised by Dr. Anne Therese Stubbs. Dr. Stubbs is an MD and Founder & Medical Director who spent 14 years in emergency medicine before transitioning to aesthetic practice."

  • Hydrafacial → Krystle Anderson, LME, LT (esthetician — Cape Coral)
  • Clear + Brilliant → Kristina Slemker, RN BSN (aesthetic nurse injector — Lewis Center)
  • Dysport → Auri Resh, FNP, BSN (family nurse practitioner — treatment page)

If "supervised by" means medical director oversight (which is legally valid for a medical practice), the claim may be accurate in a compliance sense. But patients reading these pages may reasonably expect Dr. Stubbs to personally administer or attend every treatment at all four locations, which is not what the website shows.

Action Required
Confirm with Wendy (CSM): Is "supervised by Dr. Anne Therese Stubbs" approved client-side language for all three service types, or should these pages feature the actual location-specific providers? If the client wants Dr. Stubbs featured consistently as a brand trust signal, the phrasing should be softened — e.g., "treatments are performed under the medical direction of Dr. Anne Therese Stubbs" rather than "each session is supervised by."

⚠ Flag
Demographic population claims in Lewis Center and Gahanna C+B pages — unsourced
Affects: Page 8 — Clear + Brilliant, Lewis Center • Page 11 — Clear + Brilliant, Gahanna
▼

Both Ohio Clear + Brilliant pages make specific demographic statements about the local patient population:

Lewis Center: "Lewis Center's meaningfully diverse population, including a significant Asian community and a notable Black/African American population, means skin-tone eligibility framing is directly relevant to local patients."
Gahanna: "Gahanna's patient population is meaningfully diverse, with a significant Black and African American community, which means skin tone eligibility is a real factor in treatment planning here, not a footnote."

These claims are not sourced from raw/. The intent — ensuring light-based treatment candidacy is inclusive in the copy — is well-placed and clinically appropriate. The demographic descriptions are plausible for these Columbus-area suburbs. However, per the no-invention rule, any specific claim about the patient population needs to be either confirmed or reframed in general terms.

Action Required
Two options: (1) Confirm the demographic references are accurate and acceptable to the client, or (2) rephrase to remove the specificity — e.g., "Because the treatment is light-based, candidacy depends on Fitzpatrick skin type, and the provider calibrates accordingly for medium to darker skin tones." The clinical point stands either way; it's the unsourced demographic language that needs grounding.
