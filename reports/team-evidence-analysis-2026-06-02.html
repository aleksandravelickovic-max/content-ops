Why the Audit Methodology Undercounts This Team

The audit scores what is visible on screen during 60 sampled Hubstaff frames. Three structural problems apply specifically to this team.

  • Automation runs unattended. Mateus's 22-page batches run while he is not actively clicking. A frame captured during that run scores 0 agentic intensity. The output — 37,400 words in a day — is real.
  • Infrastructure work is invisible. Building a slash command, a QA agent, or a SERP analysis pipeline produces no finished content during that session. It produces 10× output in every session after. Screenshot scoring penalizes investment weeks.
  • Lead-level coordination looks like off-task. ClickUp task direction, Meet reviews, and cross-IC coordination all register as low or zero on an IC-designed rubric. The output those activities enable shows up in other people's scores, not the lead's.
60 frames is a narrow sample. The audit's own methodology section states it "may over- or under-represent any given phase of work." For Mateus, the captured window was a documented migration and infrastructure week — the lowest-output week in months. The audit treated it as representative.
