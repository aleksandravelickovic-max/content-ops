Press Releases

AI-written press releases distributed to news and media networks

What it is: You give Search Atlas a topic and a target URL. The platform writes the press release for you and then distributes it to a selection of media networks — think syndication sites, news wire services, and editorial outlets. You choose which networks to publish to based on their reach, tier, and cost.

Step-by-step workflow

1
Check your HDC balance
Each action costs Hyperdrive Credits (HDC). Writing a press release costs 1 HDC. Distribution costs additional HDC per channel. Check your balance first so you don't get partway through and stall.
2
Create the press release
Provide: the target URL (the page you want covered), the main topic (e.g. "Search Atlas launches AI-powered link building marketplace"), up to 3 target keywords, a content type (e.g. product launch, company news, partnership), and any tone or style instructions (professional, authoritative, etc.). The platform generates a draft from these inputs. You can trigger writing immediately or do it as a separate step.
3
Review the generated draft
The AI writes the full press release — headline, body, and summary. You must review it before publishing. If it needs changes, you can update the content and regenerate (costs 1 more HDC). Publishing without reviewing is blocked by the system.
4
Choose distribution channels
Pull the available networks — each shows its HDC cost, tier (standard vs. premium), approximate number of links it will generate, and expected publishing time. Pick the ones that match your goals and budget. Higher-tier channels reach larger audiences and cost more HDC.
5
Confirm cost and publish
The system will show you your current HDC balance vs. the total cost of your selected channels before any money moves. You must explicitly confirm. Once published, you can track distribution status per channel.
💡
Dynamic PR mode: When publishing, you can enable "Dynamic PR" — the platform generates a slightly different version of the press release for each distribution channel, so every publisher receives unique content instead of the same copy. This costs extra HDC but reduces the risk of duplicate-content issues across outlets.
⚠️
Cost confirmation cannot be skipped. The platform requires your explicit approval before charging HDC for publishing — even if you're running in automated mode. This is enforced at the system level, not just a courtesy check.

Tips for better results

Be specific with your topic
The more concrete your topic input ("Search Atlas integrates with Google Search Console for real-time indexing alerts"), the tighter and more credible the AI output. Vague topics produce generic press releases.
Use the Knowledge Graph
If the brand has a Knowledge Graph set up in Search Atlas, link the press release to it. The AI uses that company data — name, website, social profiles, description — to write a more accurate release with fewer errors to correct.
Match content type to the news
Press release content types (product launch, thought leadership, award, etc.) affect how the AI frames the story. Choosing the right type avoids having to heavily rewrite the generated draft.
Check distribution timing
Each channel shows an expected publishing time. For time-sensitive announcements, filter for faster-publishing channels first.

Link Lab

A publisher marketplace for paid editorial link placements

What it is: Link Lab is a marketplace where you can buy editorial placements on real publisher websites. You find a publication that fits your niche, write (or have them write) a 600–800 word article that includes a link back to your target page, pay for the placement in HDC, and the publisher posts it. It's a direct, transparent way to earn contextual backlinks without cold outreach.

Step-by-step workflow

1
Create a project
Projects are just organizational folders — group your placements by campaign, client, or quarter (e.g. "Q3 Search Atlas Link Building"). Everything you buy gets attached to a project so you can track orders in one place.
2
Browse the publisher marketplace
Filter publications by 20+ criteria to find the right placements. Key filters: Domain Rating (DR) — Ahrefs link authority score; Domain Authority (DA) — Moz's equivalent; Domain Power — Search Atlas's own score; organic traffic; price; category (Technology, Business, Marketing, etc.); country; spam score; and Google News indexed status. Sort results by price, DR, or traffic to find the best value.
3
Filter for LLM visibility (optional but valuable)
This is a standout feature: you can specifically filter for publications that are visible in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Google AI Overviews. If you're building links with AI visibility in mind — not just traditional SEO — this filter narrows the marketplace to only publications that LLMs are already citing.
4
Add publications to your cart
When you find a publication you want, add it to your cart. You can add multiple publications in one session and buy them together at checkout. The target URL and anchor text are set at checkout — not when you add to cart — so you can plan first and specify details at the end.
5
Write the placement article
You need an article for each placement. Requirements: 600–800 words, HTML format, includes your target URL wrapped in an anchor tag with your chosen anchor text, no more than 2 external reference links. You can write it yourself, have AI draft it here, or pay an extra +15 HDC per placement to use the publisher's own writing service. The article must be created before checkout — you reference its ID at purchase.
6
Checkout and confirm payment
At checkout you assign each cart item a project, a target URL, an anchor text, and an article ID. The system shows your HDC balance vs. total cost. You must confirm before charges are applied. Once submitted, orders go to the publisher for review and posting.
7
Track orders
After checkout, monitor order status through the orders list. You can check per-order details to see if a placement is under review, live, or needs attention.

Key marketplace filters — reference card

Domain Rating (DR)
Ahrefs link authority, 0–100. Target DR 40+ for meaningful link equity.
Domain Authority (DA)
Moz's equivalent score. Useful as a second signal alongside DR.
Domain Power
Search Atlas's own composite score.
Organic Traffic
Monthly estimated visitors. Filter for publications with real audiences.
Price (USD)
Set min/max to stay within budget. Higher DR = higher cost.
Category
Technology, Business, Marketing, Health, etc. Match to your niche.
Country
Filter by publisher country. Useful for geo-targeted campaigns.
Spam Score
Set a max. Lower is better — high spam score = risky placement.
Google News Indexed
Filters for publishers in Google News. Adds credibility signal.
Common Crawl Visible
Site is in Common Crawl — used to train LLMs. Boosts AI visibility.
ChatGPT Visible
Publisher is cited in OpenAI responses. Key for GEO/AEO strategy.
Gemini / AI Overview Visible
Publisher appears in Google's AI-generated answers.
💡
Marquee publications: Some publishers are flagged as Marquee — premium placements with higher authority and stricter editorial standards. Filter for is_marquee: true if you need a prestige placement for a specific campaign.

Tips for better placements

Match category tightly
A tech SaaS link on a marketing publication is fine. On a cooking or lifestyle site, it looks paid and signals low relevance to search engines. Stick to topically adjacent publishers.
Vary your anchor text
Don't use exact-match keyword anchors across every placement. Mix branded anchors ("Search Atlas"), partial-match ("SEO platform"), and natural phrases ("learn more about") to keep a healthy link profile.
Write the article before you browse
You need the article created before checkout. Writing it first means you can move faster once you've decided on publications.
Use LLM visibility filters for GEO work
If the goal is to get a brand or page cited in AI answers (not just traditional SERPs), filter for ChatGPT- and Gemini-visible publishers. These placements have a different ROI story than pure DR.

Digital PR Outreach

Find journalists covering your topics and pitch them at scale

What it is: The Digital PR tool automates the prospecting and emailing part of traditional PR outreach. You define who you want to reach (keywords, country, DR range), the platform discovers matching journalists and publications, and then you send personalized pitch emails through a connected email account — all tracked in one place. It's a CRM-lite for journalist relationships, built into Search Atlas.

Step-by-step workflow

1
Connect your send-from email account
Outreach emails are sent from a real email account you connect to Search Atlas — not a Search Atlas address. You'll need to link your email (e.g. your company Gmail or Outlook) before creating any campaigns. This is what journalists will see and reply to.
2
Create a campaign
Set the campaign name and targeting parameters: target keywords (topics you want journalists to cover), country codes (e.g. us, uk, ca), DR range (minimum and maximum domain authority of publications you want to reach), search depth (1–5; higher = more opportunities discovered, slower), domains to blacklist (competitors, irrelevant sites), and specific target domains if you already have publications in mind. You also set up your outreach email template here: subject line, body, and follow-up sequence with timing.
3
Review discovered opportunities
The platform returns a list of journalists and publications matching your criteria. For each one you can see: domain name, DR, organic traffic, referring domains, number of contacts found, and whether they've appeared in your previous campaigns. Filter by DR, traffic, or referring domains to prioritize the best targets. Sort by any metric to work the list efficiently.
4
Verify contact emails
Before sending, run email verification on the campaign. This checks whether the journalist email addresses are valid and deliverable. Important: contacts that fail verification are permanently deleted from the campaign — this is destructive and irreversible. Only run verification when you're ready to commit to a contact list. You'll need to confirm this step explicitly.
5
Schedule outreach in bulk
Select the opportunities you want to pitch and queue them for sending — up to 500 at a time. The platform sends emails using your campaign template and follow-up sequence. Timing of follow-ups is configured per campaign (e.g. follow up 72 hours after no reply).
6
Monitor replies and respond
Incoming journalist replies are collected in one inbox, organized by recency. You can filter by campaign, read/unread status, or search by contact name. Reply directly from the tool to keep the full conversation thread in one place rather than switching to your email client.
⚠️
Email verification is destructive. When you verify contacts, any email that bounces or is undeliverable is permanently removed from the campaign — there is no undo. Do not run verification until you've reviewed the contact list and are ready to send. If you need to re-verify already-verified contacts, you must explicitly force re-verification.
💡
Keyword expansion: When creating a campaign, you can enable keyword expansion. The platform automatically expands your target keywords with related terms to discover a wider set of journalists. Useful when your initial keyword list is narrow. Keep it off if you want tight, precise targeting.

Tips for better outreach

Write the template like a human pitch
The subject line and body are what journalists actually read. Personalize the subject, lead with what's relevant to them (not what you want), and keep the body short — 3–4 sentences max. Generic pitches go to trash.
Set a realistic DR floor
Setting DR minimum too high means few opportunities; too low means outreach to low-quality sites. For most campaigns, DR 30–70 is a productive middle range. Adjust based on the campaign goal.
Use the blacklist early
Add competitors, known irrelevant domains, and any publications you already have relationships with to the blacklist before discovery runs. It's harder to clean up after the list is built.
Check for duplicates across campaigns
The opportunity list flags contacts found in your other campaigns. Filter for found_in_other_campaigns: false to avoid pitching the same journalist from two different campaigns simultaneously — it looks disorganized and can hurt your sender reputation.
