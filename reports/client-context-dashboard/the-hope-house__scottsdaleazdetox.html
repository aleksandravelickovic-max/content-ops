1. Brand & Facility Overview

Scottsdale Detox is a licensed medical detox facility in Scottsdale, AZ, operating under The Hope House group of companies. Founded in 2013, it was the first entity in the group — The Hope House residential program followed in 2017. The facility accepts only 10 clients at a time, a founding philosophy carried forward from the group's earliest days.

Website: https://scottsdaleazdetox.com

**What Scottsdale Detox is:**

  • A medical detox facility providing 24/7 medically supervised withdrawal management

  • The first step in recovery — not a full treatment program

  • A low-census, high-privacy environment (10 clients maximum)

  • A Joint Commission accredited, LegitScript certified detox center

    • **What Scottsdale Detox is NOT — never position as:**

  • A residential rehab or full treatment program

  • A detox center that completes recovery (detox is step one)

  • A general addiction treatment center — it is specifically a medical detox facility

    • **Key accreditations and trust signals (confirmed):**

  • Joint Commission Gold Seal of Approval

  • AZ SafeClean Certified

  • LegitScript Certified Detox Center

  • "The Best Award 2023"

  • Named "Best Suboxone Treatment Center in Scottsdale"

    2. Brand Voice & Tone

    Voice: calm, clinically grounded, compassionate, direct. Scottsdale Detox speaks to people in acute need and their families. Content must balance medical precision with human acknowledgment of how difficult withdrawal is.

    | Attribute | Rule |
    |---|---|
    | Calm | Do not alarm; do not sensationalize withdrawal symptoms. Describe accurately and clearly. |
    | Clinically grounded | Ground every protocol and timeline claim in confirmed site data. Never invent. |
    | Compassionate | Acknowledge the difficulty of withdrawal without minimizing it or overdramatizing it. |
    | Direct | State what the facility offers, who it is for, and what to do next. No throat-clearing. |
    | Reassuring | Emphasize safety, supervision, and individualized care as the core value proposition. |

    2.1 Taglines

    **"Where Healing Begins"** — use in brand-level and introductory contexts. Sets a welcoming, hopeful tone without making outcome promises.

    **"Medical Detox Designed for Safety, Privacy, and Real Recovery"** — use when emphasizing clinical and privacy differentiators. More specific than the primary tagline.

    Do not force either tagline into clinical or procedural copy. Use where the framing is appropriate.

    2.2 Voice in practice

    Do this:

  • "Medical detox at Scottsdale Detox is supervised by physicians and nurse practitioners around the clock." — specific, accurate, reassuring

  • "Alcohol withdrawal can become life-threatening without medical supervision — 24/7 monitoring allows the team to respond immediately if symptoms escalate." — honest about risk, positions supervision as the solution

  • "Detox is the first step toward recovery, not the finish line. The clinical team helps clients stabilize so they can engage the work that follows." — frames detox correctly as step one

  • "Each client works through an individualized treatment plan tailored to their substance use history, medical background, and specific withdrawal patterns." — patient-centered, accurate

    • Not this:

  • "Our seamless detox process will have you feeling better in no time." — minimizes difficulty, uses banned language

  • "We guarantee a comfortable, symptom-free withdrawal." — prohibited outcome guarantee

  • "Detox at Scottsdale Detox is the comprehensive solution for addiction." — misframes detox as full treatment

  • "Our cutting-edge, holistic programs..." — anti-slop list; "holistic individualized treatment" is the one approved compound phrase

    • 2.3 Sentence and paragraph rules

  • Paragraphs: 2-4 lines maximum. One idea per sentence.

  • First sentence of every section directly answers the heading or states the topic.

  • No em dashes. Restructure or use a period.

  • Lead with the patient need or clinical reality; follow with how the facility addresses it.

  • Do not start sections or paragraphs with a hyperlink.

    • 2.4 Detox-first framing (required)

    Detox is the first step, not the full treatment. Every content piece that describes the detox process must include a clear framing — explicit or implicit — that positions medical detox as the beginning of recovery, not the end. Failure to frame this correctly sets a false expectation for patients and their families.

    Approved framing patterns:

  • "Medical detox is the first step toward recovery."

  • "Stabilizing through medical detox creates the foundation for the treatment that follows."

  • "After detox, clients work with the clinical team to identify next steps in their recovery, including residential rehab programs or outpatient programs."

    • When referencing step-down care, use "residential rehab programs" or "outpatient programs" generically. Do not name other companies in The Hope House group by name.

    2.5 Addiction metaphor rules

    Do not use "battle" or "fight" as metaphors for addiction. These frames imply failure if recovery is not linear.

    Use instead: address, work through, confront, stabilize, navigate, manage.

    2.6 Banned language

    | Category | Banned |
    |---|---|
    | Outcome guarantees | guarantee, guaranteed, symptom-free withdrawal, pain-free detox, comfortable withdrawal (as a promise) |
    | Minimizing language | quick detox, easy withdrawal, in no time, fast recovery |
    | Addiction metaphors | battle addiction, fight addiction, fight withdrawal |
    | Anti-slop | utilize, leverage, robust, seamless, comprehensive (as puff), cutting-edge, holistic (as a standalone puff word — "holistic individualized treatment" is the one approved exception), synergy, transformative, innovative, game-changing, revolutionary |

    3.2 MAT medications (confirmed list)

    Medication-Assisted Treatment at Scottsdale Detox uses: buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone, and benzodiazepines (for alcohol withdrawal management, tapered per protocol).

    Do not name any medication outside this confirmed list. Do not invent dosing specifics or protocols.

    3.3 Withdrawal timelines — confirmed data only

    All timelines below are confirmed from site content. Do not compress, alter, or extend them. Do not invent additional phases.

    **Alcohol detox timeline (confirmed):**

    | Phase | Timeframe | Symptoms |
    |---|---|---|
    | Early | 6-12 hours | Restlessness, anxiety |
    | Early-mid | 12-24 hours | Elevated heart rate, tremors |
    | Mid | 24-48 hours | Hallucinations, confusion, fever |
    | Peak | 48-72 hours | Intense shaking, mood changes |
    | Improving | 3-5 days | Improvement, fatigue |
    | Post-acute | 5+ days | Psychological cravings remain |

    Alcohol detox overall span: approximately 1-2 weeks with medication adjustments throughout. Benzodiazepines used in early stages, tapered over 4-7 days, continued through day 14.

    **Opioid detox timeline (confirmed):**

    | Phase | Timeframe | Symptoms |
    |---|---|---|
    | Early | 6-12 hours | Anxiety, muscle aches |
    | Peak | 1-3 days | Peak symptom intensity |
    | Late | 3-5 days | Symptom reduction |
    | Post-acute | Weeks to months | Mood and craving fluctuations |

    MAT (buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone) reduces cravings and withdrawal symptoms throughout opioid detox.

    **Benzo detox (confirmed):**

    Benzo detox uses a gradual dose reduction approach — approximately 25% per week. The process may extend 25+ weeks. Careful tapering with potential medication substitution is the confirmed protocol.

    3.4 Success rate statistic — restricted use

    One success rate reference appears on the site: "success rates of withdrawal after 12 months ranged from 24% to 51%."

    This statistic applies specifically to benzodiazepine withdrawal at the 12-month mark.

    Rules for use:

  • Only use in educational context (explaining benzo detox complexity, not as a brand achievement claim)

  • Always include the full qualifier: "after 12 months"

  • Never present as a standalone success claim

  • Never apply to alcohol, opioid, or general detox

    • Compliant example: "Research cited at Scottsdale Detox indicates that success rates for benzodiazepine withdrawal after 12 months ranged from 24% to 51%, reflecting the complexity of benzo dependence and the importance of professional supervision."

    Non-compliant example: "Scottsdale Detox has a 24-51% success rate." — prohibited.

    3.5 Medical risk language (required)

    Alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal can be life-threatening. Content about these substances must include language that:
    1. Notes unsupervised withdrawal is dangerous
    2. Positions medical supervision as a safety measure, not just a comfort measure

    Do not present detox as safe to attempt at home. Do not present withdrawal as mild or easily managed without medical oversight.

    Do not minimize how difficult withdrawal is. Do not present detox as universally successful or easy.
