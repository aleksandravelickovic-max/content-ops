1. Brand & Audience

US Self Storage (USSelfStorage.com) is a self-storage comparison marketplace and editorial resource. It aggregates 20,000+ storage facilities and 2,000,000+ storage units nationwide. Users search, compare, and reserve storage units online. The platform also operates a substantial editorial section covering storage, packing, moving, and organization topics.

**Critical platform fact:** USSelfStorage.com is a marketplace aggregator. It does not own, operate, or manage any storage facility. Every piece of content must maintain this distinction. See §4 for enforcement rules.

Dual audience

This client serves two distinct audiences. Identify which audience a piece targets before writing anything.

**Consumer audience** (marketplace pages, consumer blog, storage info hub):

  • People planning a move or mid-relocation

  • Homeowners storing seasonal or excess items

  • Renters between apartments needing short-term storage

  • People downsizing (long-term storage)

  • Small business owners storing inventory, equipment, or records

  • Vehicle owners seeking car, RV, or boat storage

  • Anyone seeking packing, moving, or organization guidance

    • **B2B audience** (Storage Promoter Blog only):

  • Storage facility owners and operators

  • Property managers overseeing storage facilities

  • Investors evaluating the self-storage market

  • Storage industry professionals seeking operational or marketing guidance

    • Do not mix B2B framing and consumer framing in a single piece. When writing the Storage Promoter Blog, treat the reader as a business operator — not as someone looking for storage.

    2. Voice & Tone

    **Register:** Helpful, educational, informative, and practical. More advisory and content-rich than purely transactional. The site functions as both a marketplace and an information resource. Writers should write as a knowledgeable guide, not a salesperson.

    **Consumer blog tone:** Warm and advisory. Educational without being condescending. Use "you" consistently when addressing the reader. Start with the user's problem — not with the platform. Keep practical advice front and center. Use active voice and direct sentences. Short sentences alongside fuller explanations.

    **Marketplace/landing page tone:** Clear, functional, and direct. Lead with utility. The reader wants to find a unit — do not add noise. State what the platform does, what the user can find, and why comparing here is worth their time.

    **B2B blog tone (Storage Promoter Blog):** Professional and operator-focused. Treats the reader as a business owner. Uses industry terminology. Emphasis is on revenue, occupancy, marketing ROI, and operational efficiency — not on finding a storage unit.

    3.4 Banned structures

    **The "Not just X — it's Y" pattern:** Never frame something as more than it is by negating a lesser version.

  • Bad: "USSelfStorage.com isn't just a search tool — it's a complete storage resource."

  • Good: State what it is and what it does.

    • **Rule of three:** AI defaults to grouping in threes. Vary it — use one, two, or four. Match the number to what you have to say.

    **Synonym stacking:** Do not list near-synonyms as padding.

  • Bad: "a full, complete, and thorough comparison of facilities"

  • Good: "a comparison of facilities" (or describe the specific attributes compared)

    • **Ownership-implying constructions:** Any sentence structured as "our [storage noun]" is banned. This includes "our facilities," "our units," "our storage options," "our climate-controlled storage." The platform does not own any storage — it is a marketplace. See §4 for all accuracy rules.

    3.5 Tone and rhythm tells

  • **Uniform sentence length:** Mix short and long. Let some run. Cut others hard.

  • **Participial phrases:** "Leveraging advanced search tools, the platform..." is an AI fingerprint. Restructure: "The platform's search tools let you filter by..." or just state the result.

  • **"Ensuring" clauses:** "...ensuring you find the best price." These trailing clauses are filler. If the claim is important, give it its own sentence with evidence.

    4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

    4.1 Marketplace ownership rule (critical)

    USSelfStorage.com is a marketplace aggregator. It does not own, operate, or manage any storage facility. This distinction must hold in every sentence. Writers who blur this line create false advertising exposure.

    **Prohibited constructions (critical — do not use):**

  • "our storage facilities" / "our facilities"

  • "our storage units" / "our units"

  • "our climate-controlled storage"

  • "our drive-up units"

  • "USSelfStorage.com's facilities"

  • Any sentence that implies the platform stores people's belongings or operates physical locations

    • **Correct framing:**

  • "facilities in the network"

  • "storage facilities available through USSelfStorage.com"

  • "facilities listed on the platform"

  • "storage units available in your area" (when combined with search context)

  • "find climate-controlled facilities near [city]"

    • 4.2 Pricing accuracy

  • Do not state specific prices, price ranges, or average costs for storage units.

  • Do not imply that USSelfStorage.com sets or controls pricing.

  • Correct framing: "Compare current pricing from facilities in your area" or "monthly pricing varies by facility, location, and unit size."

  • Never use "guaranteed lowest prices" — this is the sister site's (USStorageUnits.com) brand claim and does not belong to this platform.

    • 4.3 Availability accuracy

  • Do not assert that specific units are available.

  • Correct framing: "check real-time availability" or "see current availability at facilities near you."

    • 4.4 Unit size data

    The unit sizes and descriptions in §8 (and in `raw/knowledge/overview.md`) are confirmed. Writers may state these as facts. Do not invent additional unit sizes or modify the confirmed descriptions.

    4.5 Network size claims

  • 20,000+ facilities: confirmed, use as stated

  • 2,000,000+ storage units: confirmed, use as stated

  • Do not round these up, down, or substitute alternate figures.

    • 4.6 Sister site separation

    US Storage Units (USStorageUnits.com) is a separate platform. Do not reference, link to, or compare with it in content. Do not use USStorageUnits.com copy patterns or claims in USSelfStorage.com content. In particular: "guaranteed lowest prices" belongs to the sister site — never use this phrase here.

    4.7 B2B content accuracy

    Storage Promoter Blog content addresses facility operators. Do not invent statistics about the self-storage industry, occupancy rates, revenue figures, or operational benchmarks. Flag any statistics for CSM confirmation before publishing.

    5. Blog Post Rules

    5.1 Consumer blog: opening hook pattern (first 3–5 lines must)

    1. Identify the specific situation or problem the reader is in
    2. Make the reader feel understood — not lectured
    3. Transition to what this piece will help them do or decide

    Do not open with: a definition of self-storage, a generic statement about moving being stressful, a rhetorical question, or a fact about the industry.

    5.2 Consumer blog: structure

  • Up to 8 H2 sections

  • H3 only when necessary; if used, include a short bridge sentence between the H2 heading and first H3

  • No H4

  • Capitalize headings per AP style

  • No section over 300 words

  • At least one table or structured comparison where the topic supports it (unit size comparisons, climate-controlled vs. standard, etc.)

  • 2–3 lists per article

    • 5.3 Consumer blog: CTA requirements

    Every consumer blog post ends with a CTA directing the reader to search or reserve on USSelfStorage.com.

    • Direct the reader to search by city or ZIP code

    • Mention the free online reservation

    • Mention the ability to compare pricing, amenities, and reviews

    • Tone: helpful and practical — not a hard sell

    • See contact-block.md for confirmed CTA elements and approved phrasing

    Do not end a consumer post with a generic "contact us" or an open-ended "learn more." The CTA must give the reader a clear next step on the platform.

    5.4 Storage Promoter Blog (B2B): structure and rules

  • Audience is facility operators — write to their business objectives

  • Lead with an operational problem or opportunity, not consumer advice

  • Use industry metrics where they exist (occupancy, revenue per square foot) — but only cite figures from provided inputs, not invented ones

  • CTA directs operators to list their facility on USSelfStorage.com or to a relevant operator resource — not to search for a storage unit

  • Do not use consumer-facing copy patterns in B2B posts

  • Structure follows the same H2/H3 rules as consumer posts

    6. Page Structure

    6.1 City/state landing pages

    These pages serve consumers searching for storage in a specific location. They are functional, not editorial.

    Structure:
    1. H1: primary keyword (e.g., "Self Storage in [City], [State]")
    2. Short intro: what the user can do on this page (search, compare, reserve) — 2–3 sentences max
    3. Overview of storage options available in the area (types, unit sizes) — reference network, not owned facilities
    4. Why compare on USSelfStorage.com (platform value props)
    5. Unit size guide section (link to `/unit-size-guide` or embed a brief size table)
    6. CTA: search/compare/reserve — free online reservation

    Rules for city/state pages:

  • Lead with the location keyword in the H1 and first sentence

  • Do not invent specific facility names, addresses, pricing, or availability

  • Do not make claims about the number of facilities in a specific city unless confirmed

  • Do not use "our facilities" (see §4.1)

    • 6.2 Consumer blog posts

    See §5 for full rules. Summary:

  • Up to 8 H2 sections; no H4

  • Opening hook: situation → understanding → transition

  • 300 words per section max

  • End with a platform CTA (search, compare, reserve)

    • 6.3 Storage information pages (storage-info, storage-tips)

    Hub pages and standalone informational pieces. Structure follows the same H2/H3 rules as blog posts. These pages are not campaign content — they are durable reference resources.

    • Prioritize accuracy and usefulness over length

    • Answer the main question in the first paragraph

    • Link to relevant unit size guide, city pages, or blog content where appropriate

    • CTA: direct to search or relevant section of the platform

    6.4 Unit size guide page

    The unit size guide at `/unit-size-guide` uses the confirmed size data in §8. Structure:

    1. H1: Unit Size Guide (or variant with keyword)
    2. Brief intro: how to choose the right unit size
    3. Size-by-size breakdown using confirmed specs (table format preferred)
    4. Guidance section: what factors affect size selection (how long you're storing, what type of items, access frequency)
    5. CTA: search for available units by size in your area

    Do not add unit sizes not listed in §8. Do not modify confirmed descriptions.

    6.5 Storage Promoter Blog posts (B2B)

    See §5.4. These pages use the URL pattern `/storage-promoter-blog/{slug}` and are written entirely for facility operators. They should not resemble consumer content in tone, framing, or CTA.

    7. SEO Standards

    **Before writing any piece:** Run a real SERP analysis. Do not simulate it. Steps:
    1. Access the internet and search the primary keyword (language: English, location: United States)
    2. Analyze the top 5 ranking pages for: search intent, LSI terms, content format patterns, subtopics, micro-queries, content gaps
    3. Identify which gaps deserve full sections vs. brief paragraphs
    4. Present findings before proceeding to the outline

    **Meta deliverables (provide before writing the introduction):**

  • 3 meta title options: 55–60 characters, include primary keyword, drive clicks

  • 3 meta description options: 130–150 characters, include primary keyword, state a benefit or utility, highlight a platform advantage (compare, free reservation, review-based selection)

  • 3 H1 title options: SEO-oriented, CTR-driven

    • **Outline requirements:**

  • Build only after SERP analysis

  • Cover all critical topics from competitor pages; expand beyond their depth where the content supports it

  • Mark where coverage matches competitors vs. where it adds new value

  • Include 2–3 micro-queries under each H2 (internal structure only — not visible in output)

  • Each paragraph answers one micro-query

  • 1 table per article where the topic supports comparison or structured data

  • 2–3 lists per article

    • **Keyword placement:**

  • Primary keyword in the first sentence

  • Use location keywords naturally in city/state page content — do not force them into every paragraph

  • For blog posts: use semantic variants; avoid repeating the exact keyword phrase in every heading
