1. Brand & Audience

US Storage Units (USStorageUnits.com) is a self-storage comparison and reservation marketplace. It connects users with 10,000+ storage facilities and 2,000,000+ storage units across the United States. Users search by city or ZIP code, compare pricing, amenities, and unit sizes, then reserve online for free.

**The platform is a marketplace. It does not own or operate any storage facility.** Every piece of content must reflect this accurately. See §4 for the full accuracy ruleset on this point.

**Audience:** People with an active, near-term storage need. This includes people moving or downsizing, renters in between leases, homeowners clearing space, small business owners storing inventory or equipment, and vehicle owners looking for car, RV, or boat storage. These users are in decision mode — comparing prices and locations, ready to act.

**Content type:** No blog. No editorial. USStorageUnits.com is a marketplace with landing pages, service category pages, and city/location pages. All content is transactional and conversion-oriented.

---

2. Voice & Tone

**Register:** Utilitarian, direct, action-oriented. The goal of every page is to move a user from awareness to search to reservation. Copy does not need to build brand affinity — it needs to answer the user's question and reduce friction to the next step.

**Not a lifestyle brand.** Do not write warm, aspirational, or emotionally-driven copy. Do not treat storage as a meaningful life event. Treat it as a practical problem with a fast, obvious solution.

| Goal | Not this | Use this |
|---|---|---|
| Open a page | "Storing your belongings is an important decision..." | State what the page covers and why it matters to the user's search |
| Describe the CTA | "Discover your ideal storage solution today" | "Reserve a unit for free — no credit card required" |
| Reference the platform | "We offer thousands of storage units" | "Facilities in the network offer..." or "USStorageUnits.com lists..." |
| Describe pricing | "Competitive rates to fit every budget" | "Guaranteed lowest prices on the web — reserve online to lock in your rate" |
| Describe the process | "Begin your storage journey with us" | "Search by ZIP code, compare nearby facilities, and reserve in minutes" |
| Reference a feature | "Our state-of-the-art climate-controlled units" | "Climate-controlled units at listed facilities keep temperature-sensitive items protected" |

    **The "Not just X — it's Y" pattern:** Never frame something as more than it is by negating a lesser version.

  • Bad: "This isn't just a storage directory — it's a complete marketplace experience."

  • Good: Say what it actually does.

    • **Rule of three:** AI defaults to grouping in threes. Vary it — use one, two, or four. Match the number to what you actually have to say.

    **Synonym stacking:** Do not list near-synonyms as padding.

  • Bad: "a convenient, affordable, and flexible storage solution"

  • Good: "free to reserve, with month-to-month rentals and guaranteed lowest prices"

  • Bad: "We have 10,000 storage facilities."

  • Good: "USStorageUnits.com lists 10,000+ facilities."

    4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

    4.1 Marketplace framing — critical rule

    USStorageUnits.com is a marketplace aggregator. It does not own, operate, manage, or staff any storage facility. This distinction is not a stylistic preference — it is a factual and legal accuracy requirement.

    **Banned ownership language:**

  • "our storage units"

  • "our facilities"

  • "our storage locations"

  • "our climate-controlled units"

  • "we offer storage units"

  • Any phrasing that implies USStorageUnits.com operates the physical facility

    • **Required framing alternatives:**

  • "facilities in the network"

  • "partner facilities"

  • "listed facilities"

  • "storage providers in the network"

  • "facilities on USStorageUnits.com"

    • When describing features (climate control, drive-up access, 24-hour access), attribute them to facilities: "Climate-controlled units are available at facilities in the network" — not "Our climate-controlled units."

    4.2 Confirmed claims (use these; do not alter the wording)

    These claims appear on the site and can be used in copy:

    • "Guaranteed lowest prices on the web"

    • "Free to reserve storage units online"

    • "No credit card required, no obligation, cancel or change your reservation at any time"

    • "Only takes a few minutes"

    • 10,000+ facilities in the network

    • 2,000,000+ storage units available

    4.3 What to never invent

  • Specific pricing for any unit or facility

  • Specific availability at any facility

  • Feature details (access hours, security specifics, elevator availability) not sourced from `raw/`

  • Testimonials, reviews, or customer quotes

  • Any claim about a named third-party facility

    • 4.4 Unit size and specification data (confirmed)

    Standard unit sizes: 5×5, 5×10, 10×10, 10×15, 10×20, 10×30.
    Locker size: approx. 3×4 ft; ceiling height approx. 3–4 ft.
    Standard unit ceiling height: 8–9 ft.

    Vehicle storage sizing:

  • 10×10: small cars

  • 10×20: midsize cars, SUVs, trucks

  • 10×30: large trucks, oversized vehicles

    • Vehicle storage configurations: enclosed, covered (overhead only), uncovered.

    Do not invent or round these dimensions. Do not imply other unit sizes exist unless confirmed.

    4.5 The sister site distinction

    US Self Storage is a separate platform with 20,000+ facilities. Do not use the 20,000+ figure for USStorageUnits.com content. Always cite 10,000+ for this client.

    6. Page Structure

    6.1 Service category pages (e.g., /climate-controlled-storage, /rv-storage, /car-storage)

    These pages serve users searching for a specific storage type. Structure:

    1. **H1** — Primary keyword; direct and descriptive (e.g., "Climate-Controlled Storage Units")
    2. **Lead paragraph** — Define the storage type; state what the page offers; include primary keyword in the first sentence
    3. **What it is / how it works** — Explain the storage type for users who may not know the details (climate control mechanics, drive-up access explained, etc.)
    4. **Unit sizes and configurations** — Use a table where applicable; cite confirmed size data from §4.4
    5. **Who it's for** — Two to four specific use cases (not a generic list)
    6. **How to reserve** — Brief process description; emphasize free reservation, no credit card, month-to-month; link to the booking flow
    7. **CTA** — Reserve-focused; use confirmed claim language from §4.2

    No H4. H3 used only when a subsection genuinely requires subdivision. If used, include a short bridge sentence between the H2 and the first H3.

    6.2 City/location pages (e.g., /{state}/storage-units-{city})

    These pages serve users searching by location. Structure:

    1. **H1** — City + storage type keyword (e.g., "Storage Units in Austin, TX")
    2. **Lead paragraph** — Confirm location coverage; mention the network; include primary keyword in first sentence
    3. **Storage types available in the area** — Brief description of types available through listed facilities in that market
    4. **Unit sizes** — Table or list with confirmed size data
    5. **How to find and reserve** — Search by ZIP code; compare prices; reserve in minutes; free, no credit card
    6. **CTA** — Reserve-focused

    Do not invent facility names, counts, or specific pricing for a named city. Do not assert specific availability at a specific location unless data is confirmed.

    6.3 Near-me and national landing pages (e.g., /storage-units-near-me)

    1. **H1** — Direct keyword match
    2. **Lead paragraph** — What the page does; how location search works; primary keyword in first sentence
    3. **Network overview** — 10,000+ facilities, 2,000,000+ units, real-time pricing
    4. **Storage types covered** — Brief list with links to category pages
    5. **Process** — Search → compare → reserve; free reservation; no credit card
    6. **Value propositions** — Guaranteed lowest prices; month-to-month; cancel anytime
    7. **CTA**

    7. SEO Standards

    **Before writing any piece:** Run a real SERP analysis. Do not simulate it. Steps:
    1. Access the internet and search the primary keyword (language: English, location: United States)
    2. Analyze top 5 ranking pages for: search intent, LSI terms, content format patterns, subtopics, micro-queries, content gaps
    3. Identify which gaps deserve full sections vs. paragraphs
    4. Present findings before proceeding

    **Meta deliverables (provide before writing the introduction):**

  • 3 meta title options: 55–60 characters, include primary keyword, stimulate curiosity or state a benefit

  • 3 meta description options: 130–150 characters, include primary keyword, CTR-driven, state a benefit or differentiator

  • 3 H1 title options: SEO-oriented, intent-matched, conversion-aware

    • **Outline requirements:**

  • Build only after SERP analysis

  • Cover all critical competitor topics; expand beyond their depth where the platform's data supports it

  • Mark where content matches competitor coverage vs. where it expands

  • Include 2–3 micro-queries under each H2 (internal structure only — not visible in output)

  • Each paragraph answers one micro-query

  • 1 table per page where appropriate; 2–3 lists per page

    • **Location page SEO note:** Geo-modifier in H1, first paragraph, and at least one H2. Do not over-insert — once per section is enough.
