---

1. Brand & Audience

Pekas Smith is an Arizona law firm focused exclusively on Social Security Disability cases — Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) and Supplemental Security Income (SSI). The firm represents individuals who cannot work due to medical conditions and need help navigating the federal disability benefits system.

Website: https://disabilitylawyerarizona.com/

**Audience:** Arizona residents who are unable to work due to a disabling medical condition. Common situations: initial SSDI/SSI applications, denied claims, hearing preparation, appeals. Many clients are in difficult financial and medical circumstances — content must reflect that.

---

2. Voice & Tone

**Register:** Professional with empathy. Informative, respectful, and reassuring.

Content must feel approachable to someone under significant stress — not academic, not condescending, not sales-driven. The disability benefits process is complex; good content simplifies it without being patronizing.

| Goal | Not this | Use this |
|---|---|---|
| Explain the process | "Simply file an application" | "The SSA requires detailed medical evidence..." |
| Address denials | "Don't worry about a denial" | "A denial does not end the process — most claimants can request reconsideration or a hearing" |
| Describe the firm | "We fight for you" | "Pekas Smith represents claimants at every stage of the disability process" |
| Frame outcomes | "We'll get you approved" | "Pekas Smith helps clients prepare the strongest possible case" |

---

3. Terminology

3.1 Required terms

These terms must be used accurately and consistently. The client is very particular about terminology.

  • **Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)** — define at first use; use SSDI after

  • **Supplemental Security Income (SSI)** — define at first use; use SSI after

  • **Social Security Administration (SSA)** — define at first use; use SSA after

  • **Administrative Law Judge** — always capitalized (critical — see §3.2)

  • **disability benefits** — preferred over "disability payments" or "disability checks"

  • **disability hearing** — preferred for the ALJ hearing stage

  • **appeals process** — for the reconsideration and hearing stages collectively

  • **claimant** — preferred term for the individual seeking benefits

3.2 Strict avoidance list

| Term | Severity | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| administrative law judge (lowercase) | Critical | Always capitalized: Administrative Law Judge |
| "we'll get you approved" | Critical | Cannot promise outcomes in legal representation |
| "guaranteed approval" | Critical | Cannot guarantee legal outcomes |
| "easy" (describing the process) | Critical | The process is not easy — this undermines trust |
| "fight for you" | Warning | Aggressive framing — use "represent" or "advocate for" |
| "simple application" | Warning | Misleading — the process is complex |
| Informal abbreviations for SSA (e.g., "Social Security") when referring to the agency | Warning | Use SSA or full name — "Social Security" alone is ambiguous |

4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

**SSA process stages** (must be represented accurately):
1. Initial application
2. Reconsideration (first appeal)
3. ALJ hearing (Administrative Law Judge hearing)
4. Appeals Council review
5. Federal court (rare)

  • Do not invent approval statistics or success rates.

  • Do not promise outcomes or imply that any particular case will succeed.

  • Do not misrepresent the SSA's criteria. Eligibility requires both a qualifying medical condition and an inability to perform substantial gainful activity (SGA).

  • SSI and SSDI are different programs with different eligibility criteria — do not conflate them.

  • The firm represents claimants in Arizona. Do not imply national representation without confirmation.

5. Writing Rules (Algorithmic Authorship)

These rules are derived from the client's content authorship guidelines. They override universal formatting defaults where they conflict.

5.1 Paragraph structure

  • The first sentence of every paragraph must contain a direct answer to the question the paragraph addresses.

  • Bold the first sentence of every paragraph.

  • Paragraphs: 2–5 lines. One main idea per paragraph. No exceptions.

  • Do not use dashes — under any circumstances. Not em dashes, not hyphens as dashes. Rewrite the sentence.

  • H3 subsections: ensure a short bridge between the H2 and the first H3

    • 5.3 Lists

  • Bullet points for: types, features, attributes

  • Keep list grammar consistent within each list

    • 5.4 Voice and imperatives

  • For steps and actionable guidance: imperative voice

  • For introductory or explanatory sentences: natural sentence flow

  • Active voice preferred throughout. Passive voice only when the subject is unknown or irrelevant.

    • 5.5 AEO / entity optimization
