1. Brand & Audience

Anne Therese Aesthetic Medicine is a medical aesthetics practice offering non-surgical cosmetic treatments: injectables, laser treatments, facials, and body contouring. Treatments performed by licensed medical professionals and licensed estheticians using clinically guided protocols.

Locations: Lewis Center, Gahanna, Cape Coral, and Bonita Springs.

Services include: Botox, Hydrafacial, CoolSculpting, laser-based skin treatments, professional skincare.

**Audience:** Patients in communities surrounding the clinic locations. Typically individuals interested in non-surgical treatments, facial rejuvenation, injectable treatments, and medically guided aesthetic care. Many are focused on confidence and natural-looking results — not dramatic changes.

---

2. Voice & Tone

**Register:** A balance of medical professionalism and aesthetic sophistication. Credible and approachable, with a premium aesthetic feel.

Content should feel refined without being cold, and medically grounded without being clinical or technical. It should not feel sales-driven or promotional.

| Goal | Not this | Use this |
|---|---|---|
| Communicate results | "Dramatic transformation guaranteed" | "Subtle improvements designed around your goals" |
| Reference the team | "Experts" (vague) | "Licensed medical professionals and licensed estheticians" |
| Describe procedures | "Cutting-edge treatments" | "[Treatment name] for [specific skin concern]" |
| Frame outcomes | "Look 10 years younger" | "A refreshed, natural-looking result" |

---

3. Terminology

3.1 Required terms

  • **Licensed medical professionals** or **licensed estheticians** — use the correct descriptor for the staff performing the treatment

  • **Medically supervised** or **medically guided** — not "medical-grade" without confirmation

  • **Clinically guided protocols** — for describing the treatment approach

  • Reference treatment names by brand when appropriate: Botox, Hydrafacial, CoolSculpting

  • **Individualized treatment plan** — not "personalized" alone (overused)

    • 3.2 Strict avoidance list

    | Term | Reason |
    |---|---|
    | "guaranteed results" / "guarantee" | Cannot guarantee cosmetic outcomes |
    | "dramatic transformation" | Exaggerated claim — patients seek subtle improvements |
    | "look younger" (as outcome promise) | Not medically responsible |
    | "best medspa in [city]" | Unsupported superlative |
    | "revolutionary" / "cutting-edge" | Vague promotional language |
    | "aggressive marketing" phrasing | Contradicts the premium, approachable register |
    | "eliminate" (for a condition) | Treatments improve or reduce — not eliminate |

    4. Accuracy & Technical Standards

  • Do not promise specific outcomes for any procedure.

  • Do not present any treatment as universally effective or risk-free.

  • Treatments are performed by either licensed medical professionals or licensed estheticians — match the correct title to the treatment type. Injectables require medical professionals; facials may be performed by estheticians.

  • Do not make comparative claims against other practices.

  • When referencing specific treatments (Botox, Hydrafacial, CoolSculpting), use their correct brand names. Do not invent specifications.

  • Multiple locations span different states (Ohio: Lewis Center, Gahanna; Florida: Cape Coral, Bonita Springs). Do not imply all services are available at all locations without confirmation.

    • ---

    5. Specificity Rules

  • Name specific treatments rather than vague categories. "CoolSculpting for body contouring" is more credible than "body contouring procedures."

  • Reference specific locations when writing location-specific content. Do not apply Ohio content to Florida locations or vice versa.

  • Attribute outcomes to individual factors: skin type, treatment history, individual goals.

  • Frame confidence and appearance goals in terms of how the patient wants to feel — not how they will look.
