The benchmark in Jamie's own words

Copy should read closer to Architectural Digest than an AI content dump. A useful test: paste a paragraph into ChatGPT, prompt it to rewrite as Architectural Digest editorial. That should be closer than what was submitted. That is the floor, not the ceiling.

The five themes Jamie's edits cluster around

  1. Plainer than aspirational. Lifestyle marketing phrases replaced with declarative, fact-grounded sentences.
  2. Anti-filler. Words and clauses that don't earn their place get cut.
  3. Technical precision over softness. Vague guidance ("larger grout joints") replaced with specifics ("1/8" minimum").
  4. Material accuracy. Wrong claims (hand-pressed where it isn't, chips/pits/crazing on Cotto) corrected; product distinctions (Cotto vs. Cotto Allende) enforced everywhere.
  5. Operational ownership. Things Zia keeps doing — adding the contact line, killing Saltillo references, removing special-order callouts — Jamie wants the team to own without being asked.

4. Technical accuracy corrections

4.1 Cotto vs. Cotto Allende — applied everywhere pools/spas/outdoor are mentioned

  • Unglazed Cotto: suitable for outdoor floors, patios, and courtyards in any climate including freeze/thaw. NOT for pools or spas (full submersion).
  • Cotto Allende (glazed): approved for pools and spas. NOT for outdoor freeze/thaw climates.
Jamie's exact framing when comparing them in the same section: "Glazed Cotto Allende is ideal for wet spaces, including pools."

4.2 Sealing — Cotto and Cotto Allende are separate instruction sets

  • Cotto (unglazed): Seal before grouting with 511 Porous Plus. After grouting, Fila Matte Wax for interior; second coat of 511 Porous Plus for exterior.
  • Cotto Allende (glazed): Only requires post-grout sealing with 511 Porous Plus in continuously moist areas.
  • Do not combine into a single bullet list. Write as two separate, clearly labeled blocks.
  • Sealing applies in all installations, including dry. Don't write sealing guidance that only references moisture areas.

4.3 Variation language — Cotto has no crazing

  • Cotto (unglazed): tone, shape, edge profile, thickness.
  • Zellige (glazed): chips, pits, and crazing — as a trio.
  • Never apply zellige surface language to an unglazed Cotto page.

4.4 Slip resistance — specifics, not "consult the installer"

  • Cotto DCOF: 0.98 — well above the 0.42 threshold for level interior floors.
  • For wet commercial floors: grout joints at least 1/8", plus anti-slip treatment after installation.
  • This language belongs under the shower/wet area section, not under general interior floor bullets.

4.5 Freeze/thaw — material-by-material

  • Zellige (glazed): NOT freeze/thaw outdoors.
  • Unglazed Cotto: any climate including freeze/thaw. Selling point.
  • Cotto Allende (glazed): NOT freeze/thaw outdoors.
  • Marble: can be used outdoors in appropriate climates. Do not state it cannot.
  • Cement: non-freeze/thaw climates only.

4.6 Unverified claims Jamie removed

  • Specific kiln types (wood-fired, gas-fired) — not approved without written confirmation from Zia.
  • "Light exposure deepens patina" — wrong. Patina is driven by use and time, not light. Removed.
  • "Hand-pressed" claim where Zia doesn't hand-press — removed (Henry caught this Apr 28).
  • Red Clay 8x8 page actually displays a 13x13 tile — copy can't fix this; flagged as imagery problem.
  • "Zellige mosaic likened to masonry" analogy — flagged unacceptable Apr 24.

5. Operational changes Jamie wants the team to own

5.1 Contact line on every piece

Jamie has been adding the contact line herself every time. It must be included without being asked.
  • Email: info@ziatile.com
  • Phone: 310-844-1170
  • Appear together in the closing section and in FAQ answers where relevant — especially the quantity/overage FAQ and the installation FAQ.

5.2 Saltillo framing

Jamie's direct comment: "My concern is that Saltillo is positioned as a fine tile that we do not sell. Shouldn't we steer this a bit more generic/mass/negative, in order to comment on why we don't sell it? Why would we want someone to leave our site and go elsewhere for a tile we do not produce?"
  • Do not position Saltillo as a quality tile Zia simply doesn't carry. The comparison must subtly favor Cotto.
  • Frame Saltillo's characteristics as distinctions that explain why Cotto performs differently (lighter body, more porous, warmer mineral variation).
  • If there is no editorial reason to include Saltillo in a piece, consider removing entirely.
  • Never reference products Zia doesn't sell without a clear strategic reason. Confirm with Alex.

5.3 Special-order / lead-time references

  • Omit or minimize. Zia prefers not to draw attention to special order status.
  • This includes the Durango Blanco lead-time callout — Jamie removed.

5.4 Cotto Allende framing

  • Do not call it a "sub-line" — call it "glazed terra cotta tile" or "glazed line."
  • Do not reference it as "a sub-category in this collection" on the Cotto collection page — write it as its own line.

6. Structural patterns Jamie wants enforced

6.1 Blog post opening paragraphs

Jamie's approved Blog Post 4 opener: "Our Cotto begins with natural Mexican clay from San Miguel de Allende, which is hand-pressed into molds, sun-dried, and high-fired using traditional techniques. The result is a terra cotta defined by tonal variation, surface character, and the inherent details that give handmade tile its appeal. Over time, the unglazed surface develops a natural patina that reflects the material's connection to place, process, and age."
  • Lead with the material, the place, or the feeling — not the production process.
  • "Families pressing clay into molds" and similar process-first framing is generic; every terra cotta brand opens that way.
  • The second and third sentences of the original opener were strong and were preserved. Fix the entry point, keep what works.

6.2 Introduce the collection early in blog posts

Jamie's addition (placed inside "What Is Cotto Tile?", not a separate colorways section near the end): "Zia's Cotto line was born from our three traditional tones: Adobe, Fired Earth, and Red Clay, while our newer whitewashed Blanco, deep brown Madera, and matte black Oscura round out the earthy palette."

6.3 Repetition is a consistent flag

  • "Charming unglazed pieces add an earthy aesthetic" appeared twice on the Cotto collection page. Cut the second instance.
  • "Traditional" appeared twice in consecutive sentences. After one use, switch to "classic range."
  • Run a word frequency check before submitting any collection page.

6.4 Person and voice consistency

Choose first person ("our Cotto") or third person ("Zia's Cotto") and hold it for the entire piece. Do not switch within a paragraph or between sections. Flagged in every review session.

6.5 Overage — give two numbers with context, not one

  • Standard: 15–20%.
  • Specialty formats (e.g., Durango) or uniform-look projects: 20–25%.
  • Give both with the rationale for each. Don't write separate rationale sentences.
AvoidUse
"to account for cuts and the natural variation inherent to handmade tile""to account for curation and cuts"
"Installations that aim for a more curated appearance may require up to 25% overage, allowing for greater flexibility during the dry layout process.""For projects that incorporate specialty formats, plan for 20 to 25% across the full order as edge cuts can affect the number of pieces needed."

6.6 Cotto vs. Cotto Allende FAQ — Jamie's corrected version

"Cotto tiles are suited for exterior floors in any climate, but not for applications where they are fully submerged in water, like pools or spas. Glazed Cotto Allende tile is approved for pools and spas, but should not be installed outdoors in freeze/thaw climates."

6.7 Bathrooms section in blog posts

  • Cotto suitable with proper sealing. Smaller formats (4×4, 6×6 Alcazar) build more grout joints for slip resistance.
  • Jamie removed language that positioned Cotto Allende as the lower-maintenance bathroom alternative.

7. SEO notes from the May 13 review

  • Jamie added "terracotta" (one word) to the Blog Post 4 keyword list alongside "terra cotta" (two words). Both forms should be considered.
  • "Saltillo vs Cotto" is in the keyword list — keep the keyword, but the copy must steer toward Zia's product.
  • Meta title format: [Product Name] [Format] | Zia Tile — 50–60 characters.
  • Meta description: 140–160 characters; include primary keyword and at least two use cases.
